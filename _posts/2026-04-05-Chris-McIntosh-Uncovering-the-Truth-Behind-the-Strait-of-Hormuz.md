**March 31st, 2026** – In a candid discussion on Capital Kazm, host Danny welcomed Chris McIntosh, publisher of the Capitalist Exploits Newsletter, for a deep dive into the escalating crisis in the Strait of Hormuz and its far-reaching global implications. McIntosh, known for his incisive analysis, wasted no time in challenging conventional narratives, asserting that "the first casualty of war is the truth."

"Most humans will prefer the comfortable lie to the uncomfortable truth," McIntosh stated, setting the tone for an episode dedicated to uncovering the reality behind the noise by meticulously examining data.

## The Strait of Hormuz: Beyond the Headlines

The conversation quickly turned to the Strait of Hormuz, a critical choke point for global oil supplies. Danny noted the prevailing narrative that Iran had shut the strait, but McIntosh offered a crucial correction.

"It's not entirely accurate," McIntosh explained. "The moment this war broke out, the Strait of Hormuz was closed, and it had nothing to do with the Iranians per se, and it had everything to do with the Lloyds of London and insurance." Insurers, he elaborated, were unwilling to cover large vessels in such a high-risk zone.

The power dynamic, however, soon shifted. As the conflict progressed, Iran began providing guarantees, allowing insurers to re-engage. This subtle but significant change, McIntosh argued, is a "power shift that people haven't been discussing because it's certainly in the Western media, it's not a narrative that would play well."

While acknowledging a "massive curtailment of supply," McIntosh clarified that ships are indeed still moving through the strait, a fact verifiable through tracking actual ship movements.

## The Petrodollar Under Siege

The implications of Iran's newfound leverage extend beyond mere traffic control. McIntosh highlighted a critical development: Iran's demand that oil be traded in Yuan, coupled with a $1-2 million toll for passage. He drew a parallel to the Suez Canal, where Egypt charges a fee, suggesting Iran has recognized its strategic power.

"It's not just the tolling," McIntosh emphasized. "It's their demands that the oil no longer be traded in dollars, because they have realized that that's the... far greater weapon than any missile ever would be is moving that trade into Yuan."

This move, though currently small in terms of overall flow, represents a direct hit to the petrodollar system, which has long allowed the US to run significant deficits by recycling oil receipts into US treasuries and equity markets. "The fact that they could do it, and they have, and the US at this point, from what we can tell, can't do a thing about it, is a huge problem for the Treasury markets," McIntosh warned.

## Unprecedented Damage: Oil Production Offline

McIntosh then presented alarming data on the physical damage to oil infrastructure across the Middle East, asserting that "there's more crude now offline than at any time in the history of global oil markets." He estimated **11 to 13 million barrels a day** are currently offline across Saudi Arabia, UAE, Iraq, Kuwait, Qatar, Iran, Bahrain, and Oman.

Specific examples of the damage include:

*   **Saudi Arabia (Aramco):** Production down from 10 million to approximately 7 million barrels a day, a **30% decline**. The Rustoa refinery has been shut since March 2nd, and the Samur refinery at Yanbu has also been hit.
*   **UAE (ADNO):** About **1.5 million barrels a day** of production is shut, with 1.3 million barrels taken offline. The Ruas refinery, severely damaged by drone strikes, has taken out an estimated **400,000 barrels a day**. The Jibble Ali port in Dubai was also impacted.
*   **Qatar:** **17% of its natural gas supply** has been taken out. The CEO estimates it will take **up to 5 years** to bring the facility back to full production.

Despite this catastrophic damage, McIntosh noted a significant disconnect: "When you go into the futures market, they're not pricing that for a continuation." He believes the market is fundamentally misunderstanding the long-term implications.

## Market Blindness and Systemic Vulnerabilities

Restart timelines for damaged facilities are often optimistic and predicated on an immediate cessation of hostilities. Saudi Arabia, for instance, suggested some capacity could be restored within **10 weeks**, while Kuwait's KPC CEO stated **4 months** for full capacity. McIntosh found these estimates unrealistic given the ongoing conflict, suggesting that "it's probably worse than we've been told."

The current crisis is exacerbated by decades of operating on a "just-in-time" inventory model. This efficiency-driven approach, while boosting margins, has drastically reduced storage and resilience across industries, including energy. "We find entire countries that have got basically no SPRs [Strategic Petroleum Reserves]," McIntosh lamented.

This paradigm is now shifting to a "just-in-case" model, with governments and asset managers reevaluating their strategies:

*   **Nationalization and Export Controls:** China has shut off exports, and South Korea and Singapore have begun shuttering refined product exports to secure supplies for themselves.
*   **Asset Manager Reallocation:** Energy's historically low weighting in the S&P 500 (under 3%) is likely to increase, creating more demand.
*   **Global Inventory Build-Up:** Countries like Japan, which entered this crisis with 256 days of inventory due to historical lessons (e.g., Pearl Harbor), may become the new model, driving significant demand for oil to build up reserves worldwide.

## Beyond Energy: The Looming Famine

The energy crisis, McIntosh warned, is merely the tip of the iceberg. "Everything is downstream," he stated, explaining how disruptions in energy and logistics ripple through the entire global system.

A critical concern is the impending famine. "We are going to have a famine, like a 100%, 110% probability there," McIntosh asserted. The lack of natural gas, crucial for ammonia production, has led to a severe shortage of fertilizer just as planting season begins. This will have a "significant impact on crop yields."

Furthermore, the entire food production and distribution chain is under pressure, compounded by a massive lack of capital expenditure over the last decade in logistics, tankers, and agriculture. "It's not even just the ability to turn around and go, 'Okay, let's build some more ships.' It's the ability to build those ships because the shipyards don't exist anymore," he explained.

## The Unspoken Fears

The gravity of the situation was underscored by a quote from the Italian Defense Minister: "I am forced to know things about what could happen in the coming weeks and the effects... that no longer allow me to sleep." McIntosh also referenced a recently resigned government official who cited a decision to use a nuclear bomb on Iran as his reason for stepping down.

While not directly linking these statements, McIntosh suggested they reflect the catastrophic potential of the current trajectory. The combination of energy shortages, supply chain breakdowns, and the looming famine paints a grim picture, one that the market, particularly in its futures pricing, appears to be dangerously underestimating.

The discussion concluded with a stark reminder that while energy equities have performed well, they remain "underpriced" relative to the unprecedented crisis unfolding, suggesting that the market has yet to fully grasp the uncomfortable truth of what lies ahead.
