## Fundamentals: China's Dominant Buying Role

A central theme of the discussion is the fundamental role of Chinese demand. Lansancy presents data showing that the People's Bank of China (PBOC) has consistently bought gold during price dips. Significant spikes in PBOC purchases coincided with major market bottoms in late 2022, late 2023, and most recently in mid-2026. This pattern underscores China's strategy of accumulating gold during weakness.

Beyond official central bank purchases, hidden demand is substantial. London's over-the-counter (OTC) market data reveals nearly 900 tons of gold exports to China not attributed to official central bank buying over the past five years. This gold is likely held by Chinese proxies, including state-owned enterprises and military entities. Additionally, Chinese non-monetary imports—encompassing ETF, retail, and private investment buying—have surged. Chinese insurance companies are increasingly incorporating gold into their products, further expanding domestic demand.

Lansancy argues that China's aggressive accumulation is a primary catalyst for the current bottom. This demand is not driven by short-term trading but by a long-term strategy to diversify reserves and prepare for potential shifts in global financial architecture, including the use of gold as a high-quality liquid asset (HQLA) competing with U.S. Treasuries.

## Federal Reserve Policy and Yield Curve Dynamics

The evolving stance of the Federal Reserve is another key factor. Initial fears that new Fed Chair Walsh would adopt a hawkish stance, potentially raising rates and pressuring gold, have subsided. Recent economic data, including weaker employment figures, suggest the Fed may not need to hike rates. The market has largely discounted further tightening on the short end of the yield curve.

Long-term bond yields, however, remain under pressure. The 3-year yield has reached its highest level since 2007, with the 10-year yield following closely. Lansancy explains that the Fed is engaged in a form of "mini yield curve control" by intervening to support the Japanese yen. This involves printing dollars to buy yen, preventing Japan from selling U.S. Treasuries and pushing yields higher. While not officially labeled yield curve control, this strategy effectively suppresses long-term yields and increases the money supply.

Lansancy views this environment as bullish for gold. Gradual increases in long-term yields, combined with stable short-term rates, support precious metals. A sharp spike in yields could be bearish in the short term, but the current slow and controlled rise aligns with historical patterns that favor gold.
