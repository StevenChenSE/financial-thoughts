**Date:** March 20  
**Source:** Interview with Mike Mclo, Senior Commodity Strategist at Bloomberg Intelligence

Financial markets are in the midst of a deepening sell-off, and according to veteran strategist Mike Mclo, this may be the beginning of a severe economic downturn. In a recent interview, Mclo outlined a compelling case for a coming recession, triggered by geopolitical shocks and exacerbated by what he sees as dangerously overvalued asset prices.

## The Trigger: A Closed Strait and a "Fog of War"

The immediate catalyst for the current market anxiety is the closure of the Strait of Hormuz following military strikes in the Middle East. Mclo admits this was a surprise.

> "I did not expect that the strait would close... This is the biggest shock for the market and it has inklings of 2008, 9/11 before that, and 2022 with Russia’s invasion of Ukraine."

He believes this closure has created an "energy crisis for a global recession," sending shockwaves through commodities. Industrial metals like copper and silver, which were up significantly year-to-date, have now rolled over. This, Mclo argues, is a classic sign of economic demand cracking.

However, he expects a resolution soon, predicting the U.S. and allied forces will "pound and pound until Iran no longer has offensive capabilities," potentially reopening the vital waterway within weeks.

## The Deeper Problem: An Overextended Market Primed to Fall

Even before the Middle East conflict, markets were weakening. Mclo points to a more fundamental issue: extreme overvaluation.

> "The stock market’s bullishness at the beginning of this year... was the most extreme I’ve seen, similar to peaks in 2000 and 2007."

He highlights several alarming metrics:
*   The U.S. stock market capitalization is about 2.3 times GDP, a level not seen since 1928.
*   60-day market volatility recently hit a 10-year low before beginning to revert—a pattern that also preceded the 2008 crash.
*   Leading indicators like gold and cryptocurrencies had already begun parabolic rallies and subsequent collapses, signaling underlying stress.

"Everything's linked," Mclo states, noting that copper, the S&P 500, and other risk assets are all moving in lockstep. "They're all the same trade right now."

## Bold Predictions: Oil to Crash, Recession to Bite

Contrary to prediction markets that see a high probability of oil ending the year above $100/barrel, Mclo makes a starkly deflationary call. He draws parallels to 2008, when oil spiked to $147 only to crash to $32.

> "I think this is the year I finally get to my $40 barrel call in crude oil... By the end of the year, by the time we get the elections, crude oil is going to be closer to 50."

His reasoning is that high oil prices are their own worst enemy: they destroy demand, spur alternative energy investment, and bring on new supply, ultimately tipping the global economy into recession. He sees this "pump then dump" cycle already playing out in natural gas, silver, and copper this year.

For Mclo, the recession trigger is clear:
> "The economy is going into recession. The number one reason to go into a recession is the stock market going down. That is the economy now."

He warns that even a routine 10% market correction would have a massive impact given current valuation extremes.

## Outlook on Key Assets

*   **Gold & Silver:** "I'm not a fan of gold." Mclo believes gold's recent drop from ~$4,500 toward $4,000 reflects anticipation of a more secure world. He expects silver to fall back toward $50.
*   **Bitcoin:** "I still think Bitcoin is going to head towards 10,000." He views crypto's collapse as a leading indicator for broader risk assets.
*   **The Fed:** Mclo is critical of any talk of rate hikes, calling it an "oxymoron" in the current environment. He points to the European Central Bank's rate hike in 2008 as a mistake that accelerated the Great Recession. He expects the Fed to hold and eventually ease as inflation falls, potentially to near 0%.
*   **The Best Trade:** "The best trade this year... is the long bond." He advocates hiding in U.S. Treasuries.

## The Bottom Line: "This is the Big One"

Mclo concludes that 2024 is shaping up to be a pivotal year that will "make and break careers." He sees a combination of elements from the 2000 dot-com crash, 9/11, and the 2008 financial crisis—but with a stock market that is far more overvalued.

> "This is a scenario that's going to make 2008 look like *[sic]* stuff compared to what's happening because we're just so stretched."

His advice for investors is to "button down and act accordingly," preparing for significant volatility and a major market downturn as the year unfolds.
