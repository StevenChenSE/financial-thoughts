In a recent social media analysis, financial commentator Andy of Finding Value made a bold prediction: the Energy Select Sector SPDR Fund (XLE) could surge to $180 or higher, driven by long-term cyclical forces rather than short-term headlines.

## The Setup: More Than Just Geopolitics

Andy began by addressing a popular tweet highlighting a potential short squeeze in the energy sector, with XLE short interest at its highest level since 2008. While recent tensions in the Middle East have captured attention, Andy was quick to dismiss the idea that the war created the opportunity.

"This setup here is not created by the war. It was not created by some short-term market event," he stated. Instead, he pointed to a multi-year technical pattern on the XLE chart—a "three-hump consolidation" followed by a false breakdown—mirroring the configuration seen before the last major energy bull market.

Zooming out on the chart, he outlined his projection: "This is going to go way up probably, you know, up to here. $180 at least." He suggested that if the move replicates the prior cycle, XLE could even reach roughly $215.

## The Long-Term Cycle vs. Short-Term Noise

Andy emphasized that profitable investing hinges on identifying long-term market cycles, not reacting to daily news. "Why was I bullish oil before the war? And was the war the catalyst or was the war the kind of the outcome of what was going to be inevitable anyway? I think it's the latter."

He believes the structural backdrop is transformative: "I don't think oil is ever going to go back to $45 or $60 a barrel barring we don't have some ridiculous depression recession. I think it's going to be much higher than that."

This long-term view shapes his approach to entry points. He argued that obsessing over perfect timing can be counterproductive. Using a historical example, he noted that buying XLE during its deep trough—even if followed by a painful drawdown—could have ultimately yielded a "10-bagger" return over the full bull cycle.

"The traders will say this was a horrible buy. But if we go to where I think we're going... and you bought it at $16, you just made over a 10-bagger. Is getting a 10-bagger within a 10-year time frame a poor return? No, it's absolutely killer."

He critiqued the short-term mindset prevalent on social media, comparing it to youth hockey: "Everyone is just posting about crap that's going up... and then everyone just skates to where the action is. Oh, it's all here. It's breaking out. You little mites. All just little mites."

## The Macro Picture: Echoes of the 1970s

Andy connected the energy breakout to broader macroeconomic shifts, particularly in interest rates. He highlighted that if the 10-year Treasury yield breaks meaningfully above 5%, it could pressure the stock market and bond market simultaneously.

"The other time that occurred when we passed 5% was 1970. Do you know what happened in 1970? A gigantic bull market in precious metals and commodities and energy."

He believes we are at the start of a similar regime change, where "hard assets outperform financial assets potentially for the rest of my life." This would represent one of the largest historical shifts, as investors are conditioned to a decades-long bull market in financial assets fueled by falling rates.

Andy shared a striking chart ratio of gold to the S&P 500, suggesting the pattern could indicate gold rising to eight or more times the price of the S&P. "People are not ready for this, guys. They are not ready."

## Energy’s Domino Effect

The analysis extended to interconnected markets. Andy agreed with a tweet noting that an energy shock, combined with slowing growth, a Fed unable to cut rates, and a weakening dollar creates "three problems hitting at the same time."

"A softer dollar makes every commodity priced in dollars more expensive. Oil, copper, food inputs, all of it gets repriced higher," he explained. This environment would benefit domestic producers of hard assets—energy, metals, agriculture—whose revenues get a currency boost.

He anticipates the Fed will eventually be forced to respond to market pressure by lowering rates to support equities, "at the expense of the dollar." His positioning is clear: "We are positioned for the dollar to go down... and precious metals and commodities will rip."

## Final Thoughts: Focus on the Cycle

Andy concluded by reiterating the importance of a long-term, cycle-based perspective. He addressed conflicting headlines—one predicting only weeks of volatility followed by decades of stability, another warning oil could hit $200 if the Strait of Hormuz stays closed—by noting, "What we're reading is just opinions. That's all it is."

His strategy remains anchored in the chart patterns and macroeconomic alignment he sees. "Everything's in alignment for this to occur. Everything. It looks absolutely fantastic."

For investors, his message is to understand the larger cycle, build positions in undervalued assets, and avoid being swayed by short-term news, analyst upgrades, or market volatility. The goal isn't to trade the swings, but to capture the powerful, long-term wave he believes is just beginning.
