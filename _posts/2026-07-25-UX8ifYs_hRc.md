## Two Paths for America: Compete or Surrender?

In a candid discussion with *Capital* host Danny, investment strategist Clem Chambers outlines a stark geopolitical and economic binary for the United States. He argues that the nation faces a choice between the "America Trade"—aggressive re-industrialization, AI dominance, and monetary expansion to compete with China—or the "Surrender Monkey Trade," a passive approach that cedes global manufacturing and technological leadership to Beijing. Chambers warns that the latter path leads to economic hollowing out, similar to what he perceives has happened in Europe, while the former path guarantees a decade of elevated inflation but offers transformative wealth creation opportunities.

### The Geopolitical Stakes: Manufacturing and Critical Minerals

Chambers emphasizes that historical superpowers have always been the "workshop of the world." Britain ruled when it dominated manufacturing, and America rose to dominance by doing the same. Today, China controls approximately 80% of the world's critical minerals, including rare earth elements, sulfuric acid, and fluorite, and has effectively won the robotics race. China produces 85% of the world's humanoid robots and offers them at significantly lower costs than American alternatives.

> "If you don't compete with China, China will dominate the world within a generation... They've got America and the rest of the world by the throat in rare earth." — Clem Chambers

To counter this, Chambers argues the US must "onshore" production, rebuild its industrial base, and secure its supply chains. He points out that American political discourse often lacks the industrial focus required to challenge China's manufacturing prowess. Without aggressive action, he warns, the US risks becoming a "cargo cult" like Europe, dependent on foreign manufacturing and vulnerable to geopolitical leverage.

### The AI War: No Second Place

A central pillar of Chambers' "America Trade" thesis is the existential importance of Artificial Intelligence. He asserts that there is "no second place" in AI. Geopolitically, if China develops superior AI, it could potentially impose its political system on the world, ending democracy as we know it. Therefore, the US is compelled to go "all in" on AI development, regardless of the economic cost.

Chambers addresses concerns about Chinese AI competitors like Kimi K3 and DeepSeek. He explains that these models often rely on "distillation," a process where Chinese developers use thousands of hacked accounts to interrogate and steal training data from American models like OpenAI's or Anthropic's Claude. He predicts the US will soon firewall China off from American AI services to protect intellectual property, viewing AI not just as technology, but as the top of the chain in modern warfare.

### Inflation as a Feature, Not a Bug

To fund this massive re-industrialization and AI infrastructure buildup, Chambers predicts the US will enter a "wartime economy" mode. This involves printing money "like there's no tomorrow." He anticipates a period of 10 to 20 years with constant inflation ranging from 5% to 10%.

> "Inflation is going to be coming again and this time it's going to stay... People need to think about adjusting to a 7 or 8%, 10%, 5 or 6% constant inflation." — Clem Chambers

However, Chambers argues this inflation is manageable because the new money is flowing into "productive assets" rather than just consumer goods. The focus is on building infrastructure, data centers, and manufacturing capacity. This environment creates a "K-shaped economy," where passive investors lose purchasing power, while active investors positioned in the right sectors thrive.

### Investment Opportunities: Energy, Metals, and AI Infrastructure

For investors, Chambers identifies several key sectors poised to benefit from this shift:

*   **Mining and Critical Minerals:** Copper, in particular, is highlighted as a foundational element for AI transformers and energy grids. Chambers notes that while there is currently no copper deficit, one is inevitable due to surging demand. He expects copper prices to continue rising significantly.
*   **Energy and Industrial Equipment:** The AI boom requires massive amounts of energy and cooling. Companies producing air conditioning, bulldozers, and power generation equipment will see sustained demand.
*   **AI and Technology:** Chambers dismisses the idea of an "AI bubble," calling it a "boom" comparable to the automobile revolution. He argues that AI is a "bulldozer for the brain," exponentially increasing productivity. He notes that programmers are already using AI to produce 10 to 20 times more code, and this leverage will spread across the entire economy.

### The Psychological Battle: Optimism vs. Pessimism

Chambers concludes by addressing the psychological aspect of investing. He warns against the pervasive negativity on social media and in mainstream news, which he attributes partly to foreign propaganda efforts from Russia and China designed to divide and demoralize Americans. He urges viewers to be "active and optimistic," noting that since 2009, the best strategy has consistently been to remain invested and long.

> "Pessimists don't make money... If you're positive and active, you'll do really, really well. You'll do better than you can ever imagine." — Clem Chambers

Chambers predicts a strong market run through the following year, driven by IPOs and continued AI investment. He advises investors to look beyond short-term noise and focus on the long-term trend of American re-industrialization and technological supremacy. Those who understand and position themselves for this "America Trade" stand to gain significantly, while those who succumb to fear or passivity will face substantial opportunity costs.
