## The Global Bust: Worse Than 2008

Hunter’s primary warning concerns what follows the meltup. He predicts a global bust in the next year, characterized by a financial crisis worse than 2008–2009. He distinguishes between a bear market (stock prices) and a bust (economic and financial system collapse).

Key elements of his bust scenario include:
- Massive leverage: Global leverage is far higher than in 2008, meaning the unwind will be faster and more severe.
- Bank failures: Hunter expects major bank and financial institution failures worldwide.
- Deflation initially: As the economy contracts and inflation is already near 2%, the bust will quickly push prices into negative territory.

He cites examples of hidden leverage, such as overextended pension funds, private credit, and failed infrastructure projects in China and elsewhere, as evidence of systemic fragility.

Hunter praised new Fed Chair Kevin Warsh as one of the most qualified chairs in history, aligning with his view that the bond market, not the Fed, should set rates. He believes Warsh’s focus on trends over month-to-month data is a positive shift from the previous administration’s approach.

However, Hunter warns that Warsh’s reluctance to repeat the massive money printing of 2008–2009 and 2020 could delay the Fed’s response to a crisis. He argues that policymakers are “fighting the last war,” which may cause a deeper initial decline before they are forced to act decisively.
