## Brien Lundin on the Fed’s Dilemma, the End of Dollar Credibility, and the Next Monetary Reset

### Introduction

In a candid discussion regarding the current state of global finance, Brien Lundin, editor of the *Gold Newsletter* and host of the New Orleans Investment Conference, argues that the United States has entered a severe debt trap. With federal debt approaching $40 trillion and annual interest costs exceeding $1.2 trillion, the path forward for the Federal Reserve is fraught with peril. Lundin suggests that the traditional tools of monetary policy are failing, leading to a scenario where currency depreciation is inevitable, benefiting hard assets like gold, silver, and industrial commodities.

### The Fed’s Debt Trap and the Illusion of Rate Hikes

Despite prediction markets suggesting a near-certainty of Federal Reserve rate hikes, Lundin remains skeptical. He argues that current inflationary pressures are driven by supply-side shocks, such as oil price increases linked to geopolitical conflicts, rather than demand-side factors. Raising interest rates in such an environment would only exacerbate shortages and worsen the debt service burden.

Lundin points out that the appointment of Kevin Walsh as Fed Chair was intended to facilitate an easier money policy, despite initial hawkish rhetoric. He believes the Fed cannot afford higher rates because the cost of servicing the national debt is already unsustainable. As yields on the 10-year Treasury rise due to "bond vigilantes" demanding higher returns for depreciating currencies, the government is forced into a corner. The only viable exit strategy, Lundin asserts, is printing money, which leads to further currency debasement.

### Why This Gold Bull Market Is Different

Historically, gold bull markets have been followed by sharp corrections. However, Lundin argues that this cycle is distinct. Initially driven by central bank purchases, which kept mining stocks and silver lagging, the market has now shifted to include Western investors and algorithmic traders. This has introduced typical bull market volatility, including significant drawdowns like the one seen in late January.

Lundin notes that gold is no longer viewed as a short-term "safe haven trade" by Western traders, who lump it in with risk assets subject to Fed policy. However, over the long term, gold remains a safe haven *investment*. As fiat currencies lose credibility due to unmanageable debt loads, gold’s value will rise relative to paper money. He predicts that once the market realizes the Fed will not pursue a long-term hawkish stance—likely by September or October—gold could rally to the $4,400–$4,600 range by year-end.

### The Dollar’s Decline and the Rise of Gold

While the US dollar remains the global reserve currency due to the rule of law and network effects, its dominance is eroding. Lundin does not foresee another fiat currency, such as the yuan or ruble, replacing the dollar. Instead, he sees gold taking on a larger role in the global monetary system. As governments diversify away from the dollar, they are turning to gold. The ultimate solution to restore currency credibility, he believes, is a return to some form of gold-backed system, a process that may take decades but is already underway.

### Commodities: Uranium, Copper, and the Energy Transition

Beyond precious metals, Lundin highlights significant opportunities in the commodity sector, particularly in uranium and copper. He describes uranium as a "set it and forget it" investment due to steepening demand curves from AI data centers and a nuclear renaissance involving small modular reactors. Supply constraints are severe, with new mines taking 15 to 20 years to develop.

Similarly, copper is facing a supply deficit driven by electrification, grid expansion, and AI infrastructure. Lundin notes that copper cannot be easily substituted and that prices will need to rise significantly—potentially above $8 per pound—to bring new projects to market. He criticizes Germany’s abandonment of nuclear energy as a strategic error that has increased reliance on foreign natural gas and coal.

### Investing in Mining Stocks

For those interested in mining stocks, Lundin emphasizes the importance of due diligence. He looks for large-scale projects with strong management teams. In the current high-price environment for gold and silver, even junior explorers can achieve profitability. However, he cautions against going 100% into any single asset class, advocating for diversification while acknowledging that the precious metals sector offers inefficiencies that knowledgeable investors can exploit.

### Conclusion

Brien Lundin’s outlook is clear: the US is trapped in a cycle of debt and currency depreciation that will continue to erode the value of fiat money. While short-term volatility in gold is driven by speculative trading, the long-term trend is upward. Investors should consider positioning themselves in gold, silver, uranium, and copper to protect wealth against the impending monetary reset. As the credibility of fiat currencies fades, hard assets will remain the true store of value.
