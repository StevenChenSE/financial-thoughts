## A Macro Shift Driven by Technical Analysis and Policy

In his August 2026 "State of the Markets" webinar, Sid Norris of Elliott Wave Plus outlined a significant macroeconomic shift based on technical analysis, specifically ratio charts and cycle analysis. Norris argues that a grand multi-decade rotation is underway, marking the end of a century-long trend where equities outperformed commodities. Moving forward, he expects commodities to significantly outperform the stock market through mid-2027, supported by a weakening US dollar and active market manipulation by central authorities.

## The Commodities Versus Stock Market Ratio

The cornerstone of Norris’s outlook is the ratio of commodities to the stock market. He highlights the April 2020 low, when crude oil briefly went negative, as a massive cycle-degree bottom that ended a long-term five-wave decline starting in 1942. Since then, a new uptrend has emerged.

Using cycle analysis tools from the Foundation for the Study of Cycles (FSC) alongside Hurst cycle analysis, Norris projects that commodities priced against the S&P 500 (ES) are at a significant low and will trend higher until approximately May 2027. This aligns with his Elliott Wave count, which suggests the current move is the beginning of a higher-degree wave three.

Norris emphasizes that this shift is foundational. He notes that many traditional money managers are slow to adapt, having relied on financials and equities for decades. However, he asserts that the data indicates commodities and commodity stocks are now the primary opportunity for investors.

## The US Dollar: No Longer a Safe Haven

A critical component of this bullish commodity thesis is the US dollar. Norris argues that the dollar is losing its status as a safe haven and is entering a significant bear phase. His Elliott Wave count on the US Dollar Index (DXY) identifies a cycle-degree top in late 2022, followed by a five-wave decline and corrective patterns that have now concluded.

He believes the DXY is poised for a wave three decline, targeting as low as 80 by late 2027. Cycle analysis supports this, showing bearish pressure through late 2026 and into 2027. Norris explains that the Federal Reserve and Treasury are preoccupied with managing stock market stability through the midterms and controlling gasoline prices, leaving them unable or unwilling to defend the dollar. Consequently, he anticipates the implementation of yield curve control to manage interest rates, further weakening the currency.

## Market Manipulation and Mega-Cap Stocks

Norris describes the current stock market as a "Frankenstein market," heavily manipulated to remain elevated until the midterm elections. He points to the disproportionate influence of a handful of mega-cap stocks, such as Microsoft and Nvidia, which have seen sudden, massive spikes (e.g., Microsoft up 30% in a month) that defy traditional technical signals.

He theorizes that algorithmic trading is being used to rotate capital among these mega-caps in a round-robin fashion, artificially propping up indices. This manipulation extends to the oil market, where Norris believes authorities are actively suppressing prices to keep gasoline affordable for voters. As a result, he advises caution with oil and gas equities until the political pressure subsides, likely around September.

## Opportunities in Metals and Miners

Given the expected dollar weakness, Norris is highly bullish on metals. He highlights gold, silver, and their respective miners as top picks. Using sentiment analysis, he points to a "perfect storm" setup where commercial traders (miners) remain heavily long on gold and silver, while retail traders are bearish. Historically, such divergence favors the commercials.

Norris is particularly focused on silver miners, which cycle analysis suggests will outperform gold miners through the end of the year. He also identifies opportunities in palladium, platinum, lithium, and uranium. His Elliott Wave Plus "Buy Low" model portfolio, which has heavily weighted these sectors, has significantly outperformed the S&P 500 year-to-date, currently up 17.58% compared to the index’s 12.83%.

He advises investors to look beyond large-cap miners to mid-cap and junior miners, which can offer exponential gains during commodity bull markets, though he warns to screen for companies prone to dilution.

Sid Norris concludes that the most prudent strategy is to align with the forces currently at play: a weakening dollar, government intervention in oil prices, and the rotation toward hard assets. By focusing on metals, miners, and avoiding manipulated indices and oil equities until the political landscape shifts, investors can position themselves for the next leg of the market cycle.
