## The silver market has experienced one of its most dramatic runs in recent memory—rising from $35 to $125 in record time—only to pull back sharply to the $75 range. But beneath the price action lies a deeper story about the fragility of the derivatives-based pricing system, the growing chasm between Eastern and Western gold flows, and what these dynamics mean for stackers holding physical metal.

In a wide-ranging interview on Capital Kosm, two of the most respected voices in the precious metals space—**David Morgan** (known as "Mr. Silver") and **Bill Holter** (known as "Mr. Gold")—dove deep into the mechanics of the COMEX, the LBMA, and why the endgame for physical holders may look very different from what most investors expect.

---

## Understanding the COMEX and LBMA: Separating Myth from Reality

One of the most persistent misconceptions in the silver community is that the COMEX is a delivery market. Both Morgan and Holter were quick to dispel this notion.

**The COMEX in Numbers:**

- Approximately **850 million ounces** of silver mined annually
- **150 million ounces** from recycling
- Total supply: roughly **1 billion ounces per year**
- The COMEX represents **less than 1%** of futures contracts that actually go out for delivery
- Most contracts are rolled forward, closed for cash, or settled via warehouse warrant transfers where the metal never moves
- The exchange's contribution to real-world physical supply is **well under 5%**

"The COMEX is for futures hedging. It's paper gain. It obviously controls the price and has for years," Morgan explained. "The LBMA does clear, but it's mostly vault transfers. Most of this metal on these deliveries is reallocated, not moved."

### The LBMA: A Different Animal

The London Bullion Market Association operates differently than the COMEX:

- It's the **largest physical silver market in the world**
- Functions as an **over-the-counter market** for clearing and settlement
- Banks hold approximately **27,000 tons** (870 million ounces)—roughly equivalent to all silver held in ETFs
- Unlike the COMEX, it doesn't produce any silver; it's purely a **clearing, allocating, and redistribution center**

"The basic thing is 90–95% of silver goes from the refineries into the fabricators, the industrial users, the people that use it for pace, solar panels, etc., and investors," Morgan noted. "Those thousand-ounce bars get broken down into coins, medallions, one-ounce wafers, ten-ounce wafers, kilo bars to hundred-ounce bars."

## Recent COMEX Dynamics: A Shift in the Force?

While the COMEX was never designed as a delivery mechanism, Holter observed something unusual happening over the past 18 months:

**Recent COMEX Delivery Trends:**

- For 18 consecutive months, more silver was standing for delivery at the **end of each month** than at the beginning
- In January, **40 million ounces** were delivered
- February saw **25 million ounces**
- March began with **52 million ounces** standing for delivery against an inventory of approximately **73 million ounces**
- By month's end, inventory dropped to **40 million**—the first month in 18 months where delivery demand exceeded beginning inventory

But perhaps most remarkably, the **CME itself** has stood for delivery three times—a phenomenon Holter says he has never witnessed before.

"The COMEX was never intended to be a delivery market, and David's right—about 1% or thereabouts go for delivery," Holter stated. "But it's become different than what it was up until 18 months ago."

Morgan agreed, emphasizing the importance of watching these nuances: "If you're on the short side and you're forced to deliver, and you've got X amount asking for delivery, and then later in a month when the rubber really meets the road, those contracts are rolled over to the last possible extent, and there's still a bunch of metal there—and it's bigger than when it started with. That's worth noting."

## True Price Discovery: Was It Happening?

Both analysts believe the market was beginning to experience **genuine price discovery** at the end of 2025 and into early 2026.

"I think we were starting to see the beginnings of price discovery," Holter said. "Anything under $150 is still suppressed price. What you saw at the end of last year, early this year, was the supply and demand deficit—the structural deficit that's been in place now for over five years."

### What Changed?

The parabolic move from $50 to $125 in record time was met with aggressive intervention.

"The tail has been wagging the dog for three or four months," Holter explained. "Silver was relentless. It was doing exactly what it should do. And then all of a sudden, there was excitement. And the powers that be can read charts just as well or better than me. They could see what silver was doing, and what did they do? They held it. They exacerbated what should have been—I don't know—15–20% pullback."

Morgan elaborated on the mechanics: "We had an overextended market. The powers that be—the market didn't run from $35 to $125. It was a $90 run, and it was certainly due a pullback. Was it supposed to do a 50% pullback? Probably not. Was that pullback helped by the sale of paper contracts? I mean, that is the price suppression scheme."

### The Shanghai Silver Premium: An Unresolved Mystery

At the time of recording, silver in Shanghai was trading at **$84.48** compared to **$75 in Western spot markets**—a **12% premium** representing approximately **$9.50 per ounce**.

This arbitrage should not exist in an efficient market, yet it persists. Holter offered a possible explanation:

"The only thought I have is: You've got arbitrageurs who know they can sell silver for some, but they're not positive that they're going to get deliverable. So are you going to take the chance where if you're buying silver at $75, but you can't get it to deliver your sale at $85? Do you really want to be naked silver in Asia?"

The uncertainty around VAT taxes and delivery confirmations appears to be keeping arbitrageurs on the sidelines—suggesting the premium reflects genuine delivery risk rather than simple market inefficiency.

## The Fragility of the Financial System: Cracks in the Dam

Beyond the precious metals markets, both analysts see a system under severe stress.

"The entire credit system is deteriorating," Holter warned. "You've got private credit—that's a problem. You have student loans that are a problem. Auto loans, home loans, credit cards—the default rates, the delinquency rates are all rising. Bankruptcy rates are increasing."

### Private Credit: The Hidden Danger

Holter pointed specifically to the private credit sector as a growing concern:

"Look at the funds that the private credit hedge funds put out there. How many have been gated where you can't get any money for six months, a year, two years?"

### What Powers the System—And What's Wrong With It

Morgan offered a broader perspective on systemic fragility:

"It's on toothpicks. I mean, it's ready to go. It's just that they're able to paper over again and again and again, and they hide stuff. They don't have to put stuff on the balance sheet. That's the truth. Why tell the truth in the financial system when you can make these phony baloney accounting entries?"

The system is still functioning, Morgan acknowledged, because "as long as there's trust, faith, or confidence in the dollar between the banking system and the public, the game will go on." But the cracks are widening:

"There's more than cracks in the dam. I mean, there's water coming through a lot of holes, and there's not a lot of fingers left to plug those holes."

## Gold and Silver Outlook: Bull Market Intact?

Despite the recent correction, both analysts remain bullish on precious metals over the long term.

**Gold's Role:**

"Gold is money," Holter asserted. "Central banks are buying so much gold because they understand that gold is real money. Everything else is credit. Everything else is paper."

Morgan agreed: "Gold is the leader, and gold is more establishment. The banks keep buying it. The banks know what's happening."

**Silver's Potential:**

- Industrial demand comprises **60–65%** of the silver market
- AI will not work without silver
- Electric vehicles require significant silver content
- Robotics is an emerging demand driver
- Solar panels continue driving substantial demand
- The **critical mineral designation** may lead to U.S. government stockpiling
- **401(k)s are now eligible** to include gold and silver in investment options

**The Gold-Silver Ratio:**

"I think you're going to see silver outperform gold simply because of the ratio," Holter said. "I don't know what number it's going to get down to. Does it get down to 40, 30, 20? Nobody knows."

Morgan offered his technical outlook: "We're going to consolidate, probably go sideways through the summer. The metals have a seasonality where they're weak in August—weakest in August. Doesn't hold every year, but it's a good rule of thumb. So I think there's a little bit of patience required for current holders of silver or new purchases of silver, but it doesn't mean the bull market's over."

He also dismissed a Bloomberg analysis calling for silver to drop to $50 or even $34: "I'll go on record saying I don't agree with that. I still think the bull market's intact. And I think we have some work to do to get back to the $100 level."

### The Critical Distinction: Price vs. Value

Morgan emphasized that investors should think in terms of value, not just price:

"Gold can stay the same, but if real estate gets cut in half, what's gold worth in real estate terms? It's doubled. That's something people don't really think about often enough, but I think it bears repeating because that's something to really burn into your consciousness."

Holter reinforced this point: "A financial crisis could bring down the metals' prices temporarily. But again, if you're buying physical metal and you're stacking, you know what the endgame is. The sale of gold, the sale of silver at a lower price temporarily is just that—temporary. It's not the endgame. You should be stacking for the endgame. You're not stacking to make profit. You're stacking to survive financially."

## Mining Stocks: The Contrarian Opportunity

Both analysts see mining stocks as undervalued, even at current gold and silver prices.

"Mining shares are still your best value here. That's a fact," Morgan said. "The market doesn't believe the earnings that are going to be coming out in the next several quarters. It just doesn't want to buy into the fact that we've got $75 silver and $4,600 gold. And that's an opportunity."

Holter added: "The profit margin is much higher today than it was a year ago, even with the higher input costs. They're cheap. They're still very cheap."

If mining stocks break down technically, Morgan suggested it would represent a buying opportunity: "If that's true and they do break down from here, one, it'll be a buying opportunity. And two, it'll probably show that we do need more work to build and fill the gold and silver market—meaning going sideways, maybe trending down for a while."

## Protecting Your Wealth: Physical Metal and Brokerage Risks

Both analysts emphasized the importance of **holding physical metal** rather than paper derivatives.

### The Endgame Philosophy

"If you know what the endgame is, it doesn't matter how we got there," Holter said. "Whether silver went up, down, sideways doesn't matter. Once we reach the endgame and derivatives blow up, the only thing that's going to count is whether you have ounces—real physical ounces—in the end or not."

"This is the rally," he continued. "If you're buying metal physically, you do not sell it. And if you're buying silver, if you're buying gold, it should only be physical. Because if you're buying paper metal and there's a default, you're not going to get paid anyway. So just stack your metal, sit on it."

Morgan added a caveat: "I believe in the free market. If you've stacked silver for a certain amount of time and you want something, it's your money. I just finished the silver $100 silver party in Las Vegas for my members. One guy came up with almost tears in his eyes—and he showed me the boat he bought. His life dream was to own that boat, not to own a bunch of blocks of silver till he died. And I was happy for him. So in that case, the guy lived his dream, and silver helped him achieve it."

But he also backed Holter's core message: "The one thing I said when I was telling my people I wanted to take partial profits—go ahead, I think we're going to stall out in the $100-plus range—but don't sell it all, no matter what. Because as Bill says, the last thing you want in the world is for a true hyperinflation or a complete currency collapse, and you're left holding currency."

### Brokerage and Account Risks: The Great Taking

Holter raised concerns about what happens to brokerage accounts if the system breaks:

"Make sure you get your certificate—your stock certificate. If your broker goes under, that certificate is on their balance sheet for your benefit. It's not your asset. The broker is holding it as custodian. So it's on their balance sheet. Same thing with a bank account. It used to be you would put money into a bank, and you were a depositor. You're not a depositor anymore. The laws have changed. You are a lender. Read *The Great Taking*. It's real."

Morgan confirmed he had read the book and interviewed its author, David Rogers Webb: "I've written about it as well. The law is there. So we do have to consider it—how much. If you have the safeguard of having the certificate—for me, I've always thought that was the way to go. I used to take all my certs. I don't anymore."

His reasoning: when you have a certificate, you must put it back into the system electronically to sell. "When you mail that FedEx and put it into the system, who's going to open it? If it's already in the system and I put out an alert—sell out as much as you can because I see a brokerage failure happening—don't let yours be next. It's already in the system. You click your mouse and get out."

## The East-West Gold Divide: Power Shifting

A significant theme throughout the discussion was the **massive transfer of gold from West to East**.

Central banks in BRICS nations and other Eastern countries have been aggressively accumulating gold while Western central banks—particularly the U.S.—have been depleting reserves.

### Why the West is Unloading Gold

"The East wants free and fair trade with real settlement," Holter explained. "And whether or not these stable coins work is going to depend on how the East views whatever the new currency system is in the West. If the East says we want nothing to do with it, then game over. It doesn't work because they will have all the gold at that point."

Morgan added: "Power always follows gold, and gold has gone from West to East. And he who has the gold makes the rules."

### The Stable Coin Strategy

Both analysts discussed the Western plan to maintain dollar dominance through **stable coins**:

"This whole stable coin thing has to do with a new market for U.S. debt that no one else wants to buy," Morgan explained. "They're putting it into this stable coin thing. The stable coin buys treasuries—T-bills, T-bonds, T-notes, different interest rates and different time mechanisms—but they get to keep the interest. All you get is the quote-unquote dollar that you bought in."

The system is designed so that "the stable coin people get a real good deal. They're letting you buy their stable coin, giving you a dollar bill for it, and keeping the interest on it. And that's a big demand. And the system is going to be geared toward the stable coins."
