## The Four-Year Cycle Remains Intact

In a recent interview at the "Into Investing Through the Cycles" conference, Ben Cowen, host of *Into the Cryptoverse*, shared his perspective on the current market downturn and the timing of future bottoms for Bitcoin, gold, and tech stocks. A central theme of Cowen’s analysis is his continued faith in Bitcoin’s four-year cycle, driven by halving events. While many argue that institutional adoption via ETFs has broken this pattern, Cowen contends that the cycle has not yet failed.

"The top occurred when it always occurs in Q4 of the post-halving year," Cowen noted, referencing the late 2025 top. "And it seems like the low is potentially occurring when it usually occurs in Q4 of the midterm year." He argues that betting against the cycle has historically been less profitable than trading with it, even if the cycle eventually breaks due to market maturation.

## Defining Bitcoin’s Bottom: Balance Price vs. Realized Price

Cowen provided specific technical metrics to gauge where Bitcoin might find its floor. He distinguished between two key on-chain indicators:

1.  **Realized Price:** The average price at which all bitcoins last transacted on-chain.
2.  **Balance Price:** A metric incorporating the "transferred price" and "coin days destroyed." Historically, this level has marked Bitcoin’s cycle bottoms.

Currently, Cowen identifies the balance price in the "high 30s" (referring to $30,000+ levels). He views the current market phase as a window of weakness lasting four to seven weeks. If Bitcoin does not drop further during this period, he expects bears to turn into bulls by early 2027, potentially leading to a rally in the March-to-June timeframe.

## The Role of Liquidity and Interest Rates

A critical component of Cowen’s macro analysis is the relationship between global liquidity and risk assets. He tracks "global net liquidity," noting it has fallen from approximately $30 trillion in 2021–2022 to around $25 trillion today. This tightening liquidity environment disproportionately affects long-duration assets like Bitcoin, which are highly sensitive to monetary policy shifts.

Cowen emphasized that Bitcoin acts as a **leading indicator** for the broader business cycle. Just as it topped before rate cuts were fully priced in during 2025, he believes it will bottom at "peak hawkishness." He drew parallels to late 2018, when the Federal Reserve projected two rate hikes for 2019 that never materialized. Bitcoin’s sell-off then was reversed once peak hawkishness passed. Similarly, Cowen anticipates a bottom soon as the market absorbs current tight monetary conditions, even if actual rate cuts do not occur immediately.

## Gold: The Likely First Mover

When asked which asset would bottom first—Bitcoin, gold, or tech stocks—Cowen firmly stated that **gold** typically leads. Commodities generally bottom before risk assets because they are more sensitive to real interest rates and dollar strength.

Historical precedents support this view:
*   **2018:** Gold bottomed in August; Bitcoin and stocks bottomed in December.
*   **2022:** Gold bottomed in September/October; Bitcoin bottomed in November.
*   **2008:** Gold bottomed in October; stocks didn’t bottom until March 2009.

Cowen expects the US Dollar Index (DXY) to rise toward 105–106 levels, which would pressure gold initially but ultimately signal a macro low. Once the dollar peaks and yields stabilize, gold is likely to show strength first, followed by Bitcoin and equities.

## Tech Stocks and the AI Bubble Debate

The conversation also touched on the "AI bubble" narrative. Cowen suggested that we are not in a post-bubble collapse but rather an adjustment phase similar to March 1997, when markets repriced after Fed rate hikes during the dot-com era build-out.

He noted that while valuations look high relative to earnings, measuring the stock market against gold shows it is nowhere near the excesses of 2000. The current correction, marked by a ~10% drop in the NASDAQ and significant yield curve movements, reflects a repricing of rate expectations rather than a fundamental collapse of AI’s potential.

## Structural Higher Rates and Investment Strategy

Cowen warned that we may be entering an era of **structurally higher rates**. He argued that the Fed follows the 2-year yield, not the other way around, and that recent bond market revolts (rising long-end yields) indicate markets are demanding higher risk premiums. He does not expect a return to near-zero interest rates in the next five years.

For investors, this means preparing for deeper drawdowns. Cowen cautioned against the "buy the dip" mentality that has worked since 2008, noting that such patterns eventually fail (as seen in the Great Depression). He advised:
*   Expect 30–50% drawdowns.
*   Do not try to time these events perfectly.
*   Understand that dollar-cost averaging can help portfolios recover even if indices take years to regain all-time highs.

## Conclusion

Ben Cowen’s outlook suggests a cautious but ultimately bullish path for Bitcoin in 2027 and beyond. By respecting the four-year cycle, monitoring global liquidity, and recognizing gold as the first mover in bottoming out, investors can better navigate the current window of weakness. The key takeaway is that while volatility and structural rate changes pose risks, Bitcoin remains a leading indicator of economic shifts and a compelling long-term store of value.
