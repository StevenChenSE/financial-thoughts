**Insights from the Royal Symposium on precious metals, energy markets, AI risks, and mining due diligence.**

Lobo Tra, editor of *The Independent Speculator*, joins Rick Rule at the Royal Symposium to discuss his current market outlook, highlighting a base case of gold consolidation, a compelling oil versus copper pair trade, and strategic opportunities arising from a potential AI trade unwind. Tra also shares his views on uranium, macroeconomic shifts in the second half of 2026, and how to identify high-quality mining stocks in bearish sentiment.

## Gold and Silver: Base Case Consolidation and "Plan C"

Tra outlines a measured stance on precious metals, emphasizing that his base case involves a correction and consolidation phase before the next major upward move in gold and silver. He clarifies that he is not a bear but rather lacks the urgency to buy immediately, noting the current market feels like "catching a falling knife."

Tra distinguishes between bullion and equities, noting he has not sold an ounce of physical gold or silver. However, for trading and leverage, he focuses on stocks. He describes a "Plan C" scenario: if gold were to drop 50%, falling below $3,000 into the high $2,000s, he would buy stocks aggressively regardless of technical charts or macro factors. In such a scenario, he expects mining stocks to crater even if the underlying metals remain profitable, creating a "stupid cheap" buying opportunity.

## Oil Stocks: Sale Conditions and Narrative Risks

Outside of metals, Tra identifies oil stocks as a sector nearing a major buying opportunity. He notes that many oil equities have retreated to pre-war levels, which were already elevated due to prior saber-rattling. While analysts like Jeff Curry and Nutte are calling a bottom, Tra remains cautious.

He highlights a conflicting narrative of an "oil glut," driven by ships fleeing the Strait of Hormuz and the continued draining of the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR). Tra suggests that even if oil prices bottom, stocks could decline further on this narrative. He is waiting for clear capitulation before deploying capital aggressively. However, he maintains a near-term bullish view on an oil rebound for the second half of 2026, provided the war does not reignite.

## The Oil vs. Copper Pair Trade

Tra proposes a pair trade based on geopolitical risk: long oil versus copper. He explains that the immediate course of geopolitics affects these two commodities inversely.

*   **War Reignition:** A flare-up in conflict would be bearish for copper due to economic destruction but bullish for oil due to supply concerns and inflationary shocks.
*   **War De-escalation:** If the war fades, the war premium evaporates, hurting oil prices while boosting copper as economic activity stabilizes and demand returns.

Given the current context where the war premium is fading, Tra suggests this dynamic offers a strategic pair trade opportunity for the year.

## AI Trade Unwind and the Copper Opportunity

Tra warns of a potential unwind in the AI trade later in 2026, driven by concerns over capital expenditure (capex) returns and earnings deterioration. He points to signs such as Meta discussing renting out excess compute and skepticism surrounding whether large language models (LLMs) truly qualify as AI.

While an AI unwind would remove a tailwind for commodities like copper, silver, and uranium, Tra views this as a bullish scenario for copper specifically. He believes "Mr. Market always overreacts." If the market wrongly assumes that a pullback in AI spending eliminates the need for copper, the metal could be sold off irrationally. Tra, who maintains a long-term bullish outlook on copper, would welcome such an overreaction as a chance to accumulate at discounted levels.

## Uranium: Solid Thesis Near Incentive Prices

When asked about uranium, Tra reiterates his strong long-term conviction but notes that oil currently offers a more compelling near-term rebound. Uranium is approaching its incentive price, which Tra estimates at $60 per pound, or $80+ when adjusted for inflation.

He observes that spot prices have consistently traded below long-term contract prices, suggesting a "rubber band" effect where the spot price will eventually snap back upward. Tra believes the uranium thesis is robust and would require a "Chernobyl-scale event" to derail. While stocks are not particularly cheap due to widespread bullishness, he advises investors without a position to buy, as the fundamentals remain exceptional.

## Macro Outlook: The Fed, Inflation, and Fiscal Dominance

Looking toward the second half of 2026, Tra expects markets to shift focus from war headlines to Federal Reserve policy, assuming the conflict does not reignite. He discusses the Fed's reaction function under "Worsh," noting that while the tone has shifted, there is no hard evidence of hawkish action yet.

Tra emphasizes that economic data indicates hotter inflation, and fiscal dominance is returning to the markets. He warns that a reignition of war would be highly inflationary, not just through oil prices but via government money printing to fund the conflict. He suggests that interest rate hikes may be ineffective against supply-driven oil inflation, as rate increases rarely reduce essential oil consumption without causing severe demand destruction.

## Gold Myths and Market Psychology

Tra addresses common misconceptions about gold, noting that the "pet rock" narrative has faded, partly thanks to the crypto community educating younger generations on the dangers of fiat currency. He dismisses the argument that gold pays no interest as a fallacy, comparing gold in a safe to cash in a safe, both of which suffer from inflation without yield mechanisms.

He highlights a paradigm shift where Wall Street now embraces the "debasement trade," recognizing gold's role as a hedge against fiat-versus-fiat battles. Tra advises that gold is best viewed as money for long-term preservation rather than a short-term trading vehicle. For trading, he prefers stocks for leverage. He also analyzes gold cycles, comparing the current market to the steady bull run of 2001–2011 versus the flat period of 2012–2019. His base case remains that the current move is a shoulder before higher prices, though he acknowledges the risk of a peak in January 2025 leading to a long bear market.

## Mining Stock Due Diligence in a Bearish Sentiment

Tra notes the irony of bearish sentiment in mining stocks at gold prices near $4,000, a level that previously triggered euphoria. He invokes the lesson from Doug Casey that "there is a difference between price and value," and Mr. Market's irrationality creates opportunities.

For investors evaluating miners, Tra offers specific due diligence criteria:
*   **Red Flags:** Companies whining about the inability to raise capital are suspect; capital exists for quality projects. Firms with no cash reserves or those that failed to raise money when gold was higher are also red flags.
*   **Green Flags:** Fiscal discipline, strong free cash flow, and a focus on exploration and development over general and administrative (G&A) costs.
*   **Performance:** There is no excuse for a gold company not to generate profit at $4,000 gold. Investors should demand deliverables and cash generation.

Tra concludes by emphasizing the value of the Royal Symposium for connecting with disciplined investors and verifying project quality firsthand. He invites readers to explore his work via *The Independent Speculator* for ongoing macro analysis and market insights.
