## Executive Summary
BlackBerry is executing a successful transition from turnaround to profitable growth by focusing on mission-critical secure communications and QNX software, driving strong cash flow, margin expansion, and expanding into physical AI and robotics markets.

## Introduction
At the Canaccord Genuity 46th Annual Growth Conference, BlackBerry CEO John G. Matteo and CFO Tim Foote discussed the company's strategic evolution and financial performance. Following a strong first quarter with 26% growth and the first cash-positive fiscal quarter in nine years, the leadership team outlined how BlackBerry is leveraging its core strengths to capture new market opportunities in automotive, secure communications, and physical AI.

## Strategic Focus Driving Sustainable Execution
John G. Matteo emphasized that BlackBerry's recent success stems from a laser-like focus on two key areas: mission-critical secure communications and QNX software. After transitioning from devices to software and services a decade ago, the company dabbled in various segments, including endpoint security through the Cylance acquisition. However, divesting non-core assets and narrowing its focus to QNX and secure communications allowed BlackBerry to allocate resources more effectively, improve execution, and achieve sustainable profitability.

Matteo highlighted that the company is focused on building long-term business fundamentals rather than reacting to stock market fluctuations. With consistent revenue growth, margin expansion, and cash generation, BlackBerry is delivering on its promise of stable, profitable growth.

## QNX Backlog and Revenue Visibility
BlackBerry's QNX business benefits from a robust royalty backlog of nearly $1 billion, providing long-term revenue visibility uncommon in the software industry. In the past year, BlackBerry added twice as much to its backlog as it recognized in revenue, signaling strong future growth. The backlog has low churn due to diversification across OEMs and powertrain technologies, including electric vehicles (EVs), internal combustion engines (ICE), and hybrids.

The backlog is expected to convert into revenue sequentially over the first four years, with more than half recognized during that period. This steady conversion, combined with ongoing design wins, reinforces QNX's position as a stable growth engine.

## Development Licenses as Leading Indicators
Development license revenue reached record levels in the most recent quarter, serving as a key leading indicator of future growth. OEMs purchase software development kits (SDKs) to build applications on QNX before products enter production. These early-stage sales precede royalty revenue, which is recognized at 100% margin once devices reach the market.

The strong performance in development licenses, particularly for the latest QNX version, SDP 8, signals increasing adoption for future designs. SDP 8 supports high-performance computing at the edge, scales efficiently across multiple cores, and maintains real-time determinism and safety certifications—key advantages over competitors like Linux.

## Alloy Core: A New Growth Platform
Alloy Core represents a significant expansion opportunity for BlackBerry, transitioning the company from an OS provider to a comprehensive platform provider. By partnering with Vector, a leading middleware provider, BlackBerry is offering customers a high-performance, integrated platform that simplifies software development and reduces costs.

While Alloy Core is not yet reflected in the current backlog, the company expects it to drive substantial future growth as the backlog surpasses the $1 billion mark. Early traction and pipeline momentum suggest Alloy Core will play a pivotal role in BlackBerry's next growth phase.

## Physical AI and Robotics Opportunities
BlackBerry is well-positioned to capitalize on the growing physical AI and robotics market, driven by its automotive expertise and partnerships with leading silicon vendors like Nvidia, Qualcomm, ARM, and Texas Instruments. Nvidia's endorsement, with its Halo safety stack built on QNX, underscores BlackBerry's competitive advantage in safety-critical applications.

The company views physical AI as a near-term growth opportunity, with momentum building across robotics, medical devices, industrial automation, and autonomous mobile robots. While automotive adoption timelines span 3–5 years, the rapid investment in physical AI suggests accelerated growth potential.

## Secure Communications: Stabilization and Growth
BlackBerry's secure communications segment has shown strong improvement, with 24% revenue growth and sequential ARR growth over the past four quarters. While dollar-based net retention remains below 100% due to churn in commoditized UEM products, the company is offsetting losses with new wins, particularly in government and defense sectors.

Sovereign demand, driven by rising defense budgets and geopolitical dynamics, provides a structural tailwind for secure communications. Government contracts, though subject to certification delays, are highly sticky and recurring. BlackBerry's certifications, including FedRAMP and Germany's BSI, position it as a trusted vendor in global government markets.

## Outlook and Employee Engagement
Looking ahead, BlackBerry is on a solid foundation with clear visibility into top-line growth and margin expansion. The company's lean cost structure enables rapid translation of revenue growth into profitability, supporting Rule of 40 and occasionally Rule of 50 performance.

Beyond financial metrics, Matteo highlighted record employee engagement globally, emphasizing that a motivated workforce is a key driver of BlackBerry's turnaround and future success.

## Conclusion
BlackBerry has successfully navigated from turnaround to profitable growth by focusing on its core strengths in QNX and secure communications. With a strong backlog, leading indicators of future growth, and expanding opportunities in physical AI and robotics, the company is well-positioned for sustained long-term success.
