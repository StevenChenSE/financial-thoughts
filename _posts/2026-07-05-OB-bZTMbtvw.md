## Ed Steer breaks down massive options activity, market manipulation, and why precious metals are poised for a historic rally in 2026.

As of July 1, 2026, the precious metals complex is exhibiting clear signs of a structural inflection point. In a detailed discussion on Capital Calls, host Danny sat down with veteran market analyst Ed Steer to examine the underlying mechanics driving gold and silver pricing. Despite recent price softness across equities, bonds, and commodities, Steer’s analysis points to exhausted selling pressure, unprecedented institutional positioning, and the terminal phase of a managed fiat system. The conversation reveals that what appears to be market weakness may actually be the final stage of a long-term price suppression cycle.

Steer’s thesis centers on the intersection of derivatives activity, commercial positioning, and macroeconomic reality. With commercial traders having covered the vast majority of their short positions and long-position holders refusing to capitulate further, the market has reached a point of diminishing returns for sellers. Backing this technical view is extraordinary options volume, where tens of millions of dollars are quietly backing a five-digit gold price by year-end. This positioning, combined with structural supply deficits and central bank accumulation, suggests that the current environment represents a rare accumulation zone for long-term investors.

### The $20,000 Gold Options Gamble

One of the most striking developments in the current market is the surge in December call options for gold at the $10,000, $15,000, and $20,000 strike prices. Steer notes that this activity began gaining traction in February and has since escalated, with volume and interest now exceeding $30,000 in notional positioning. These are not retail speculations; they represent serious capital deployment by institutional investors and sophisticated funds betting on a fantastically higher gold price before the end of the year. This derivatives activity stands in stark contrast to the moribund retail bullion sector, where US Mint sales have been weak, dealer inventories are full, and buyback premiums sit roughly 10% below spot. The divergence between paper market positioning and physical retail demand highlights where the smart money is placing its chips.

### Spotting the Market Bottom

Historically, July and August have served as seasonal lows for precious metals, but Steer argues that the current bottom is being driven by structural market dynamics rather than calendar seasonality. Commercial traders have spent the last month attempting to cover short positions, but they have hit a wall. Long-position holders, managed money, and other reportable traders have reached a threshold where they refuse to sell further or go short, particularly with prices trading significantly below the 200-day moving averages. Once this psychological and technical floor is established, commercial traders can no longer drive prices lower regardless of their efforts. Steer emphasizes that the bottom is effectively in, and the next rally will begin the moment large traders allow it to unfold.

### Mining Stocks and the Silver Anomaly

The equity performance of precious metals miners tells a divergent story from spot prices. Year-to-date through June, gold has declined 7.35%, while the HUI gold mining index has fallen 8.15%. Silver, however, has dropped 18% year-to-date, yet silver equities are only down 2.39%. This massive decoupling indicates substantial accumulation in silver stocks by institutional players, positioning them for a sharp re-rating once spot fundamentals catch up. While lower WTI crude and diesel prices provide a marginal tailwind for mining margins, Steer cautions that fuel costs represent a small fraction of overall extraction expenses. Furthermore, the heavy management of precious metals markets masks what would otherwise be clear fundamental correlations, making mining stocks a highly asymmetric play on the upcoming reset.

### Managed Markets and Fiat Currency Decline

Steer’s macro framework rests on the premise that modern financial markets are heavily managed, a reality that has persisted since President Nixon closed the gold window in 1971. From the 10-year Treasury yield to the dollar index and precious metals futures, price discovery has been largely replaced by active yield curve control and interventionist policies. The Fed’s recent steps to cap yields and the broader "wash, rinse, and spin cycles" of commercial trading illustrate a system struggling to maintain artificial price levels. Central banks, particularly in China and Russia, have been steadily accumulating physical reserves, exacerbating structural deficits in silver, gold, copper, platinum, and palladium. With exploration failing to replace annual consumption and new mine development taking over a decade to come online, the physical market is edging closer to a supply crunch that paper markets cannot indefinitely suppress.

### Interest Rates, Debt, and the Gold Revaluation Debate

Rising interest rates and hawkish rhetoric from figures like Fed Chair Kevin Warsh are often cited as headwinds for precious metals, but Steer argues they signal desperation rather than strength. Capital flight and a "print and die" monetary policy have forced central banks to raise rates to attract capital, yet this will not halt the erosion of fiat purchasing power. Official inflation data from the BLS is widely regarded as suppressed, and the real cost of living continues to outpace reported metrics. Against a US debt burden exceeding $40 trillion, discussions of Treasury gold revaluation have gained traction. Steer notes that revaluing gold from its book value of $42 to current market levels would only offset roughly $2 trillion of debt. Meaningful fiscal relief would require a revaluation to $30,000 or $40,000 per ounce, a move that could trigger margin call cascades, force COMEX and LBMA closures, and catalyze a broader financial reset.

### Summary

Ed Steer’s analysis paints a clear picture of a precious metals market at a historic crossroads. Tens of millions in December call options, exhausted commercial shorts, and massive accumulation in silver equities all point to a imminent reversal. The broader macro environment, characterized by managed yields, structural commodity deficits, and unsustainable sovereign debt, leaves little room for traditional fiat assets to maintain their dominance. While short-term volatility and rate hikes may create noise, the long-term trajectory favors hard assets that cannot be printed or diluted. For investors, the current environment mirrors classic contrarian principles: buy when others are fearful, focus on physical reserves and high-quality mining equities, and prepare for a market structure that is fundamentally shifting away from paper-based valuation.
