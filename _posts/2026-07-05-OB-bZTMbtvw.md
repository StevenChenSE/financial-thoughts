## Introduction

In a striking display of confidence in the gold market, a major investor has accumulated over 30,000 call options contracts targeting $20,000 gold by December—a position that represents tens of millions of dollars in bets on significantly higher prices. Ed Steer, editor of the Ed Steer Gold and Silver newsletter, joined Danny from Capital Calls to discuss this extraordinary wager, the state of the precious metals market, and what these developments mean for investors navigating a heavily managed financial system.

Speaking from his column dated July 1st, 2026, Steer revealed that the $20,000 call options position has grown dramatically from approximately 20 contracts back in February to over 30,000 today. "There's some serious money being bet that we're going to see fantastically higher gold price before the end of the year," Steer noted during the interview. This accumulation of bullish positions signals that sophisticated investors are positioning for a major move in the yellow metal.

The discussion went beyond mere price predictions, delving into the structural forces shaping commodity markets. From seasonal patterns in gold and silver to the unprecedented capital accumulation in silver equities despite a declining silver price, Steer offered insights into how the "big eight" commercial traders manipulate prices—and why the current downturn may represent a generational buying opportunity.

## The Call Options Bet: What It Means

The surge in December $20,000 call options for gold represents one of the most significant bullish wagers visible in the futures market. Steer explained that these options strike prices—ranging from $10,000 to $20,000—indicate the holder's expectation of a five-digit gold price within months.

"They've been investors just snapping those things up," Steer observed. "I remember back in February when I first heard about it, there was the $20,000 call options in December for December gold, and now it's up to over 30,000. So, there's some serious money being bet."

The scale of this position is notable because it requires substantial capital commitment and conviction. Unlike simple directional bets, call options strategies often indicate sophisticated investors who anticipate volatility or significant moves that may not be immediately reflected in current prices.

## Has the Bottom Been Reached?

One of the most pressing questions facing precious metals investors is whether the recent decline—gold down 7.35% year-to-date as of late June—represents a bottom or further downside ahead. Steer offered a definitive assessment: the bottom appears to be in.

According to Steer, commercial traders have been "pounding away" attempting to cover short positions since gold's highs at the end of January. However, these manipulators have reached a critical limitation. "They've reached that point about a month ago, and they've been pounding away, and pounding, but they just can't get any more," Steer explained.

The key dynamic involves what Steer calls the "law of diminishing returns." Once long positions and managed money traders reach their threshold—typically when prices fall significantly below 200-day moving averages—they refuse to sell additional contracts or add new shorts. When this occurs, commercial traders can no longer drive prices lower regardless of their efforts.

"Once that point is reached, the commercial traders can do whatever they want, but as long as the other traders aren't prepared to sell long or go short, the price can go no further," Steer stated. "And once that happens, the bottom is in."

Historically, July and August represent the weakest seasonal period for gold and silver. Yet Steer suggested that 2026 represents an exception, as the commercial sector has already exhausted its capacity to suppress prices. "They're only a very small portion of the overall cost of mining," he noted regarding lower oil prices as a tailwind. "This particular year where the commercial traders have covered all the short positions they can—when the next rally will start, when they allow it, should be anytime, and hopefully it was today."

## The Silver Stock Anomaly: A Signal of Accumulation

Perhaps the most compelling evidence of institutional accumulation emerges from a comparison of silver prices versus silver equities. While silver itself has declined 18% year-to-date, the silver mining stocks are down only 2.39%—a stark divergence that suggests significant buying pressure.

Steer highlighted this discrepancy as evidence of large-scale accumulation. "Silver is down 18% so far this year, up—the first half of the year, but the silver stocks are only down 2.39%," he noted. "So, there's a huge difference between how the silver stocks are performing versus the gold stocks versus the gold price."

In contrast, gold stocks (tracked by the HUI index) have declined 8.15%, closely mirroring gold's 7.35% drop. This differential performance in silver equities cannot occur without substantial buying. "There's massive, massive accumulation going on in the silver stocks in order for them to be performing as well as they are considering what the silver price has done year-to-date," Steer explained.

This dynamic carries important implications for future price movements. Historically, when silver prices rise, they tend to outpace gold—creating the gold-silver ratio compression that favors silver's percentage gains. The accumulation occurring in silver equities suggests sophisticated investors anticipate a reversal in the metal's fortunes.

## Market Manipulation and the Managed Market Reality

Throughout the interview, Steer emphasized that precious metals markets, like most financial markets, operate under significant manipulation. This isn't speculation but rather an observation about how prices are established across bonds, currencies, and commodities.

"The markets are so managed now, okay? Everybody knows that the precious metal prices have been managed, and they've been managed for the last 50 years," Steer stated flatly. "You can take—you can look at any market out there. I don't care. You look at the bond market this morning. If you take a look at the 10-year Treasury, I mean, it was going to blow sky-high, but the Fed came in and then smacked it lower."

Steer characterized current market conditions as a "total hallucination"—a term describing how price discovery has been replaced by managed outcomes. "Whatever you see on your screen right now when you're looking at any financial product is a total hallucination," he continued. "There's just no price discovery being allowed out there."

This management extends to suppressing yields—the 10-year Treasury briefly touched 4.501% before the Federal Reserve intervened with purchases to cap the advance. "The reason it didn't go any higher than that is the Fed came in and bought up the 10-year," Steer explained. "And they've been in active yield curve control now for the last two or three years."

Despite this manipulation, Steer remains confident in precious metals as the ultimate store of value. "Precious metals should be sky-high right now, and the 10-year yield should be sky-high, and the stock market should be in the toilet, but that's just not being allowed to happen."

## Supply Deficits Across the Metals Complex

Beyond manipulation, fundamental supply-demand dynamics are creating structural deficits across multiple metals. Silver has experienced a documented supply deficit for approximately six years. However, Steer suggests gold is approaching similar conditions.

Central banks worldwide have been accumulating gold at rates exceeding 1,000 tons annually—absorbing a significant portion of global supply. Meanwhile, mining discoveries have failed to keep pace with consumption.

"There hasn't been a monster deposit of gold found in the last 10 or 15 years," Steer noted. "And even if they do find it, it's going to take them another 10 years to bring it online. So, you know, they can invest all the money that they want in bringing a mine into production, but the fact of the matter is how much gold is being consumed every year—125 million ounces?"

This consumption significantly exceeds discovery rates. "In the last five or ten years, they haven't come close to discovering that amount of gold to replace what's being consumed every year," Steer warned. "So, sooner or later it's going to come up and bite them."

The same pattern exists in platinum, palladium, and copper. Steer specifically identified copper as one of six commodities that "the boys in the COMEX are shorting and actively suppressing the price." Despite supply deficits requiring three to four times current production, prices remain suppressed through market intervention.

China's role in this dynamic remains opaque but potentially significant. Steer cited analyst Alister Macleod's estimate that China holds "north of 20,000 tons of gold" in unreported reserves. "When they do decide they're going to announce how much gold they got, it's going to change a lot of things in a hurry," Steer predicted.

## Investing Strategy in a Managed Market

Navigating manipulated markets requires understanding their cyclical nature. Steer described the precious metals market as operating through "wash, rinse, and spin cycles"—periods where commercial traders alternately push prices to highs (allowing them to cover shorts) and then drive prices lower (covering additional shorts at reduced levels).

"You look for the bottoms of the market," Steer advised. "Buy when blood is running in the streets—that quote is from hundreds of years ago. I think Warren Buffett said you buy where others are afraid to buy, and you sell when others won't sell anything."

Steer practices what he preaches, having been "fully invested in the precious metals for 20 years" and continuing to buy during the current decline. "I bought something last week, and I bought something on Monday," he revealed during the interview. "Because I just said this is ridiculous, and the prices are so low."

He characterized the current environment as representing the bottom of the market. "This is the bottom of the market as far as I'm concerned, and how high we go from here is entirely up to what the big traders allow the price to go."

## Interest Rates and the Fiat Currency Crossroads

The interview addressed the relationship between interest rate policy and precious metals. With the 10-year Treasury yield approaching 4.5% and Japanese yields reaching 40-year highs, the question arises: how do metals perform in a rate-hiking environment?

Steer's assessment: rising rates won't suppress metals as conventional wisdom suggests. "When you're looking at currency debasement the way we are now and the death of fiat currencies—you know, we're well along in that process right now," he explained. "Raising interest rates won't make any difference because people are going to recognize the fact that the governments are raising rates is because they just can't attract enough capital."

The mechanism is straightforward: governments raise rates because they're losing the confidence battle. Higher rates signal fiscal stress rather than monetary strength. As investors recognize this dynamic, they increasingly seek hard assets.

"The smart money out there can see that all governments of the world are printing themselves into oblivion, and raising rates is just trying to prevent the inevitable," Steer observed. "So, I think this time around, we're going to see if interest rates rise, not going to have any effect on the price of gold and silver because they're going to rise as well."

## Gold Revaluation and the Path Forward

The conversation addressed gold revaluation—a concept where the U.S. Treasury revalues its official gold holdings from the current $42 per ounce (reflected on balance sheets) to market prices. If gold reached $4,000, this revaluation could theoretically eliminate approximately $2 trillion in national debt.

Steer dismissed this as insufficient. "Two trillion dollars is coffee money when you're talking 40 trillion," he noted with characteristic bluntness. "They would have to revalue gold to $30 or $40,000 an ounce to make a really different big difference in the debt."

More importantly, such a revaluation would signal the end of the current system. "And of course, if that happened, you know, the price—we'd see sky-high inflation."

This connects to Steer's broader thesis: the 50-year-old paper monetary system established when Nixon closed the gold window in 1971 is approaching its terminus. "We're living in the biggest financial and paper bubble the world has ever known," Steer declared. "It far exceeds 1929, the tulip mania, and the South Sea bubble."

Central banks from China to Russia to India have rebuilt their economies from "financial and economic basket cases" into serious competitors. "When they dug up this price management scheme back 50 plus years ago, they never even gave any thought to saying, 'Okay, how are we going to get out of this thing?'" Steer observed. "Well, we're living through the process of getting out of it now."

When asked about the "Great Reset" concept, Steer confirmed that such an event appears inevitable. The mechanism remains uncertain, but its characteristics are becoming clearer.

And then when the markets open tonight too, gold will be repriced at whatever."

The implications would be severe for existing paper markets. "The COMEX will close, the LBMA will close because those sharks just can't won't be able to meet the margin calls, and it'll blow up the entire market," Steer warned. "It's going to be a momentous event when it happens."
