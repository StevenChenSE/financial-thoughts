## Dan Wilton discusses the Spring Pole project's regulatory milestones, First Nations term sheets, financing options, and the macro drivers supporting gold's long-term trajectory.

Dan Wilton, CEO of First Mining Gold Corp, provided a comprehensive update on the company's progress, highlighting the recently approved federal environmental assessment for the Spring Pole project in Northern Ontario. The discussion covered the company's 255% stock performance over the last 12 months, term sheet agreements with key First Nations, financing strategies for the $1.1 billion development, and a detailed analysis of the gold market's macroeconomic backdrop.

### Stock Performance and Market Context

First Mining Gold has delivered exceptional returns, with the stock surging 255% over the past year. Wilton noted that the share price experienced significant volatility, dropping 55% in the first half of the year before recovering to near all-time highs. The recent momentum was driven largely by company-specific catalysts rather than gold price movements.

In the last month alone, the stock jumped approximately 70%, even as gold prices declined. Wilton attributed this outperformance to the convergence of major milestones, including regulatory approvals and community agreements. He emphasized that the stock is currently trading at a significant discount to peers, valuing the project at roughly $50 to $60 per ounce in the ground, compared to peer acquisitions occurring at $400 to $500 per ounce.

### Spring Pole Project and Regulatory Milestones

The Spring Pole project has reached a pivotal stage following the federal environmental assessment (EA) approval. The process, which began in February 2018, concluded with a ruling by the Impact Assessment Agency of Canada, which determined the proposed open pit is not likely to cause significant adverse environmental effects. The approval was signed off by the Federal Environment Minister just before Canada Day.

Wilton explained that the EA approval is the single largest gating item for regulatory permission to advance. The company now targets the completion of the provincial environmental assessment process by the end of the summer. With these approvals, First Mining Gold is positioned to finalize the feasibility study, detailed engineering, and permitting, with a construction decision targeted for the beginning of 2028.

### First Nations Relationships and Term Sheets

A critical component of the project's advancement is the company's relationship with Indigenous communities. Wilton highlighted that term sheet agreements have been secured with the Cat Lake First Nation, Laxual First Nation, and Slate Falls Nation. These agreements, years in the making, represent a significant step toward full project agreements.

Wilton stressed that building trust and relationships cannot be rushed. He noted that the company has navigated a complex process involving over 3,000 comments and questions from regulators and communities during the EA process. The team, led by permitting and community relations experts Steve Lines and Megan Burton, has been instrumental in managing these engagements since late 2020. The goal is to have long-term project agreements in place with all communities by the end of the summer.

### Project Economics and Valuation

First Mining Gold operates two of the largest undeveloped gold projects in Canada: Spring Pole and the DUP project. The Spring Pole project demonstrates robust economics. Based on a prefeasibility study conducted at a $3,100 gold price, the project offers a 40% after-tax internal rate of return (IRR) and a sub-two-year payback period. At current spot prices, the after-tax net present value (NPV) is estimated at $3.8 billion.

Wilton addressed the valuation disconnect in the market. While producers are often valued at $800 to $1,000 per ounce, First Mining Gold trades at a fraction of that multiple. He argued that the fundamental value is clear, with the project benchmarked against top-tier Canadian mines. The scarcity of large-scale projects with EA approvals in Canada adds further value, potentially attracting institutional investors and strategic partners.

### Financing Strategy

With a capital expenditure (capex) of $1.1 billion US, financing is a key focus. Wilton outlined several avenues to fund the project while minimizing shareholder dilution. Options include traditional project debt financing, which could cover 50% to 60% of construction costs, as well as royalty or stream agreements.

The company is also open to partnering with a financial party or mining company for an earn-in arrangement. A partner acquiring a 50% to 60% stake could provide sufficient capital to build the project, allowing the equity component to be managed at the project level rather than diluting the entire company. This approach preserves the value of the DUP project, which holds 6 million ounces in the Abitibi region and has a lower infrastructure burden due to its proximity to a smelter.

### Gold Market Outlook and Macro Factors

Wilton shared a bullish long-term view on gold, drawing parallels to 2011 while noting that current macroeconomic conditions are even more supportive. He pointed to massive fiscal deficits, geopolitical instability, and a lack of trust in global systems as drivers for gold's consolidation and potential breakout.

Unlike 2011, where the industry learned to be prudent after a bubble, Wilton believes the current environment is structurally stronger for gold. He noted that producers are generating record free cash flow, with margins expanding as gold trades well above the $3,100 long-term consensus used in many studies. Today, the consensus price has shifted higher, with banks using $2,500 as a downside case and consensus estimates ranging between $3,600 and $3,700.

Wilton concluded that the gold trade remains intact, with no signs of a long-term retracement. The combination of robust project fundamentals, regulatory progress, and a favorable macro backdrop positions First Mining Gold for continued value creation.
