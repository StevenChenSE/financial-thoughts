## A Compelling Case for Chinese Equities Amidst Global Uncertainty

Investor Clive Thompson is currently increasing his exposure to the Hong Kong stock market, viewing it as one of the most attractive investment opportunities globally at this moment. Speaking from Deyang, China, Thompson outlines his rationale for shifting capital from overvalued US technology sectors into Hong Kong-listed companies, emphasizing high growth potential, robust dividend yields, and significantly lower price-to-earnings ratios.

## The Macro View: Hong Kong vs. The US Market

Thompson begins by comparing the performance of the Hang Seng Index in Hong Kong with the S&P 500 in the United States over the last five years (July 2021 to July 2026). While the S&P 500 rose by approximately 73%, the Hang Seng Index dropped by roughly 14%. However, this price divergence does not reflect underlying corporate earnings.

*   **Earnings Growth:** Despite the index decline, earnings for Hang Seng stocks rose by 13.23%, nearly matching the 18.67% earnings growth seen in the S&P 500.
*   **Valuation Gap:** Five years ago, the Hang Seng traded at a P/E ratio of 17.71, while the S&P 500 was at 22.31. Today, the Hang Seng P/E has collapsed to 13.44, whereas the S&P 500 has expanded to 32.6.

Thompson argues that Hong Kong stocks are significantly undervalued relative to their earnings power, whereas US stocks are trading at historically high multiples that may not be sustainable.

## Dividend Yields and Tax Efficiency

Another key factor in Thompson's bullish outlook is the dividend yield. The Hang Seng Index offers a yield of approximately 3.27%, more than three times the gross yield of the S&P 500 (1.03%). Furthermore, Hong Kong does not impose dividend withholding taxes on local stocks, although Chinese mainland companies may apply a 10% withholding tax. In contrast, US investors often face withholding taxes ranging from 15% to 30% on dividends, depending on their residence.

## Portfolio Strategy and Risk Management

Thompson emphasizes that while he is increasing his Hong Kong exposure, it remains a small portion of his total portfolio, capped between 5% and 8%. He cites political risk as a primary concern, noting that Hong Kong-listed companies are heavily dependent on the Chinese economy and political landscape. He warns against "all-in" bets, advocating for gradual position building and diversification.

To fund these purchases, he has been reducing his exposure to US technology stocks, particularly the semiconductor sector. He views semiconductors as a cyclical industry that is currently overpriced, anticipating a potential 50% correction when supply outpaces demand.

## Market Headwinds and Recovery Potential

Thompson acknowledges the significant challenges facing the Chinese market, including regulatory crackdowns on technology and education sectors, and the severe property price crash exemplified by the Evergrande crisis. These events have severely damaged investor confidence.

However, he believes the worst is behind us. As the property crisis stabilizes and political fears subside, he expects the Hang Seng Index to revert to a more normal P/E ratio of 16-17x. Combined with forecasted earnings growth of 16-25% over the next two years, Thompson sees potential for a 50% upside in the Hong Kong market.

## Conclusion

For investors willing to accept the political and economic risks associated with China, Thompson argues that Hong Kong stocks offer a compelling value proposition.
