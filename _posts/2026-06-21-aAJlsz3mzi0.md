## The critical commodity investors are overlooking in favor of precious metals

Twenty years ago, the investment thesis for steel, copper, and base metals was simple: the rise of China. Construction of housing, commercial property, and buildings erupted into what many believed would become the world's largest economy. But as the global landscape shifts, a new resource crisis is brewing—one that may dwarf what we've witnessed with oil.

Chris Patala, CEO of Oceanic Iron Ore (FEO on TSXV), argues that iron ore represents one of the most compelling opportunities in the commodities space. "Iron ore value is always about what the world needs, not about what investors think," Patala explains. "You haven't found a replacement for steel yet. Everybody needs steel, and it's quite believable that there are infrastructure demands in the short, medium, and long term as the world grows."

The Strait of Hormuz closure taught economies a crucial lesson: critical minerals and reliable supply chains are not guaranteed. Countries worldwide are now rushing to secure their own sources, recognizing that choke points can be cut off without warning.

### From Beijing to Beyond: The Changing Demand Landscape

While China remains the dominant driver of construction demand—and iron ore prices still respond immediately to news about Chinese infrastructure needs—the narrative is evolving. India is emerging with increasingly aggressive infrastructure demands. The Middle East, particularly Saudi Arabia and the UAE, represents moderate but growing demand. Japan continues its steady consumption.

More significantly, the composition of demand is shifting. Defense infrastructure, power grids, industrial restoration, and AI data centers are becoming meaningful contributors to steel consumption. "I don't know that it's primary," Patala notes, "but it's having a significant impact."

### Understanding the Iron Ore Market Dynamics

Iron ore prices have climbed approximately 50% since 2016, but the journey has been volatile. The 2021 spike was driven by infrastructure outlook news from China, which subsequently normalized. Before that cycle, prices had settled in the $40-50 per ton range following a super cycle that peaked around $150 per ton—when miners expanded production just as Chinese demand began slowing.

The crucial distinction investors must understand is the difference between benchmark products and high-purity iron ore. The standard benchmark is 62% purity, but Oceanic's product achieves 66.6% purity—targeting both traditional blast furnace and emerging direct reduction iron (DRI) applications.

### The Green Steel Revolution and Its Implications

Steel production accounts for 8 to 9% of the world's carbon emissions. As nations push toward lower carbon footprints, the industry is migrating from traditional blast furnaces to DRI processed through shaft furnaces and electric arc furnaces. This transition requires significantly higher purity iron ore—typically 67% or above with extremely low impurities, particularly silica.

"The world is pushing towards lower carbon emissions with steel production," Patala explains. "There's a specific focus on steel being created with green iron ore. And when you get to high purity iron ore, which is what you need for green steel, there is a more finite supply of product in the world."

The problem? High-grade deposits capable of producing this critical mineral are being depleted. Much of the high-purity iron in Australia and India has already been mined out. "When you've got something that's not as high-grade, you have to beneficiate that product and process it to get to that critical mineral level," Patala notes. "Some of those deposits don't have any more headgrade to actually get to that 67%."

### The Supply Challenge: Why Scarcity Is Inevitable

The two main geographical areas with significant high-purity iron ore resources at volume are Brazil and Quebec. Other regions like Guinea—with the Simandou project producing around 120 million tons annually—exist, but Simandou's deposit is primarily geared toward traditional blast furnace products, though some processing into direct reduction iron remains possible.

Patala addresses concerns about market flooding: "You get these words on the street about 'they'll glut the market because they're going to produce 120 million tons per annum.' But it doesn't cover what the demand is. Simandou is simply replacing the current depletion in the iron ore production market. Other producers aren't replacing what they're depleting because they mined out all the good stuff, particularly in Western Australia."

As green steel mandates continue and countries seek secured sources of high-purity supply, the disparity between demand and available supply for this specific product will only widen.

### Quebec's Strategic Advantage

Oceanic Iron Ore operates in northern Quebec within the Labrador Trough—a geological trend of voluminous iron ore formation spanning hundreds of kilometers. Their projects include Unava Bay, Hope's Advance, Robert's Lake, and Morgan Lake.

The geology presents significant advantages. Head grades run 30-32% before processing, with deposits near surface and low strip ratios enabling economic extraction. But the true differentiator lies in geography: Oceanic's project is located just 25 kilometers from deep water.

"In the iron ore industry, all-in costs are about 50% transportation and logistics, and the rest is actual mining and processing," Patala explains. "When you cut out the rail component—because we just need to truck it 25 kilometers to the water—you're left with extra margin and the ability to reduce operating risk."

This logistical advantage eliminates the need for rail infrastructure, which competitors in the Labrador Trough typically require. Combined with high-purity ore capable of meeting green steel specifications, the company's position becomes strategically compelling.

### The Processing Pathway

Once extracted, iron ore undergoes crushing to suitable levels depending on mineralization. Oceanic's operation is notable: 84% of their product can be recovered through gravity circuits using spiral concentrators—the simplest and most cost-effective method. The remaining 16% requires grinding finer and processing through low-intensity magnetic separation.

For high-purity DRI applications, additional processing may involve more intense magnetic separation or reverse flotation circuits to reduce silica content below 3%—compared to the 4.5-6% acceptable for traditional blast furnace products.

The concentrate then travels by capsize vessels to steel producers, who process it through their furnaces to create the end product. Steel production remains heavily concentrated in China, which still accounts for approximately two-thirds of global output, though this share is gradually decreasing as other nations develop domestic capabilities.

### The Investment Thesis

For investors seeking exposure to the next resource crisis, iron ore—particularly high-purity iron ore—presents a unique proposition. Unlike precious metals driven by sentiment, iron ore value derives from fundamental global needs that show no signs of abating.

As Patala emphasizes, the combination of depleting high-grade deposits, increasing green steel mandates, and concentrated geographic sources creates a supply-demand dynamic that hasn't existed since China's initial infrastructure boom.

The question is no longer whether demand will grow. It's whether supply can keep pace—and for investors who recognize this reality early, the opportunity may prove substantial.
