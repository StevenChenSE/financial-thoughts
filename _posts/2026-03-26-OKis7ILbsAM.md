In a recent video, Andy from the *Finding Value* channel cut through the noise of daily market chatter to share the core philosophy behind his investment success. The secret isn’t a complex algorithm or a hot stock tip—it’s a mindset shift: **prioritize patience over panic, and focus relentlessly on the long term.**

## The Cookie Test: A Lesson in Discipline

Andy frames the investor’s dilemma with a simple analogy: the classic “cookie test” given to children. If a child can wait patiently for a few minutes without eating the cookie in front of them, they get a second one. Most fail, gobbling the treat immediately.

“That’s basically all I’m doing,” Andy says. “And most everyone gobbles up that cookie as fast as they possibly can because they want to get rich faster than everyone else. And you know what actually ends up happening? They don’t get rich at all.”

The frantic short-term trading, the chasing of trends, and the reaction to every news headline lead to “underperforming the S&P 500 massively,” he argues. The alternative is a disciplined, value-based approach: buy when assets are cheap, then do the hardest thing of all—sit and wait.

## Embracing the Pain: The Path to 10-Baggers

A cornerstone of Andy’s strategy is the willingness to endure significant volatility. He quotes Charlie Munger: “If you can’t handle a 50% drop, you deserve a mediocre result.”

“I’ve gone through a whole bunch of 50% pullbacks. I have been kicked in the nuts multiple times… in each stock as I hold it,” he admits. But this pain is “part of the game” for those seeking extraordinary returns.

He makes a compelling probabilistic case: “There is… a very low probability to go straight up… Most of the time you get a big move… and then a pullback. If you're going for maximum return, you're going to have to endure that.” For Andy, the goal isn’t just making money, but building **relative purchasing power**, especially during market declines.

## The Chart Doesn't Lie: Psychology in Patterns

Andy uses technical analysis not as a crystal ball, but as a map of human psychology. He points to patterns like cup-and-handle formations and “dream within a dream” fractal bottoms (falling wedges within larger falling wedges) as evidence that “humans are behaving the same exact way in history.”

“The patterns are a reflection of the psychology of humans,” he notes, whether they’re trading silver or tech stocks. His current chart focus highlights a major regime change: the breakout of commodities relative to financial assets. “The commodities bear market ended in 2020… You know what that looks like? Pure gold.”

## The Macro Backdrop: A "Permanent Shock" to the System

Andy weaves his long-term thesis with a stark macro outlook, particularly in energy. Reviewing social media posts, he highlights several alarming data points:

*   **Oil Supply Crisis:** “We need to find 8 to 10 million new barrels per day of production every single year… We need to find 80 to 100 million barrels of fresh production to stay flat.” He believes the current Middle East conflict represents a “permanent shock,” with the potential for “high oil prices for a very long time… an entire decade.”
*   **Critical Inventories:** European gas storage in the Netherlands has fallen to a seasonal low of **6.1%** of capacity, a precarious position heading into next winter.
*   **Inflationary Pressures:** Citing Peter Schiff, he notes that monthly import/export price spikes in February were annualizing to **16.8% to 19.6%** inflation—*before* the recent 50% surge in oil prices. “Are we going to get skyrocketing inflation? I think so.”
*   **Geopolitical Corruption:** He highlights a report of a **$580 million oil trade** placed 15 minutes before a major geopolitical tweet, calling it evidence of “blatant” corruption.

## The Antidote: A Boring, Focused Plan

In this chaotic environment, Andy’s advice is to stay boring and focused.

“It's really boring. Supposed to be boring. That's what investing is,” he says. The process is simple: identify long-term value using technical and fundamental analysis, buy during fear, and hold through volatility. He warns against the twin enemies cited by Warren Buffett: **excitement and expenses**—where excitement leads to chasing highs, and expenses come from short-term trading and taxes.

His final reminder is to block out the noise: “Never ever ever let the long-term view slip your mind… Act in the short term, focus and think in the long term.”

For Andy, the ultimate edge isn’t information speed; it’s emotional stamina. The real wealth is built not by those who trade the most, but by those who can wait the longest.
