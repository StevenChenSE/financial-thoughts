### Mike McGlone Analyzes the "Endgame" for Commodities and Equities

## Introduction

In a recent interview, Bloomberg Intelligence Senior Commodity Strategist Mike McGlone outlined a stark outlook for global markets, describing the current market environment as an "endgame" that may resolve by the end of the year. With both WTI and Brent crude oil prices surpassing the $100 mark, McGlone argues that the surge in energy costs is a "dear in the headlights" scenario that threatens to break the global economy. He warns that the U.S. stock market is the only major asset class that has not yet corrected, and its potential collapse could serve as the trigger for a broader deflationary event.

## The "Stock Puppet" Phenomenon

A central theme in McGlone's analysis is the concept of "stock puppets"—assets that have become highly correlated with the S&P 500, losing their traditional safe-haven or independent commodity characteristics.

*   **Gold:** McGlone classifies gold as a stock puppet, noting that its 100-day correlation with the S&P 500 recently reached a multi-decade high of approximately 0.52. Historically, gold has a negative correlation with equities. He points out that gold's volatility is currently two times that of the S&P 500, the highest in 20 years, and 40-year peak relative to Treasury bonds.
*   **Copper:** Similarly, copper is described as a severe "accident waiting to happen." Its 100-day correlation with the S&P 500 is the highest in the history of the contract (since 1988). McGlone notes that copper has been trading at a 40% premium to its 200-week moving average, a level historically associated with market tops.
*   **Metals Basket:** The Bloomberg All Metals Index is currently at its highest 100-day correlation with the S&P 500 in its 30-year history.

McGlone argues that when these correlations reach these extremes, the assets are vulnerable to a sharp reversion. If the stock market corrects, these "puppets" will likely fall with it, rather than providing a hedge.

## Oil and Energy: The Primary Driver

The surge in crude oil is the primary catalyst for the current market stress. McGlone attributes the spike to geopolitical tensions, specifically the ongoing conflict involving Iran, which has disrupted supply expectations.

*   **Price Levels:** With crude oil above $100, diesel prices in the U.S. have reached record highs of approximately $6 per gallon. McGlone draws a parallel to 2008, when gasoline prices hit $4 per gallon, a level that preceded the global financial crisis.
*   **Natural Gas Signal:** A key leading indicator for McGlone is the price of U.S. natural gas. The January contract is currently trading at $3.80 per MMBtu, the lowest since the end of 2021 (pre-Russia invasion of Ukraine). This disparity between the high price of crude and the low price of natural gas suggests that the energy complex is overvalued and that prices are due for a significant reversion.
*   **Supply Surpluses:** McGlone points to record production in the Western Hemisphere, including record production in the U.S., Canada, Canada, Argentina, Brazil, Guyana, and Venezuela. These surpluses, combined with the potential for the U.S. to suppress Iran's offensive capabilities, suggest that the oil price spike is temporary.

## The Deflationary Scenario

McGlone maintains a long-term deflationary thesis, arguing that the current inflationary pressure from oil is a short-term shock that will eventually reverse. He compares the current situation to the European Central Bank's (ECB) actions in 2011, where rate hikes in response to oil inflation were followed by a severe deflationary correction when the stock market crashed.

*   **Fed Policy:** The Federal Reserve is currently priced for a 60 basis point hike over the next year, the most on a one-year future since Q4 2021. McGlone believes the Fed is forced to tighten due to inflation, but this tightening will likely accelerate the stock market correction.
*   **Bonds as a Hedge:** In this scenario, U.S. Treasury bonds (specifically the 10-year yield at 4.92% and the 30-year yield) become attractive. McGlone describes bonds as a "put on the S&P 500 with positive carry and no time decay."
*   **The Trigger:** The trigger for this deflationary event is a correction in the stock market. McGlone notes that the S&P 500 is currently trading at 2.1 times total U.S. debt, a historically high valuation. A 10% correction in the stock market would represent a 25% reduction in the U.S. economy's asset value, a severe shock that would likely lead to a deflationary spiral.

## Conclusion

Mike McGlone's analysis paints a picture of a market system that is highly leveraged, overvalued, and vulnerable to a sharp correction. The "stock puppet" phenomenon in gold and copper, combined with the temporary nature of the oil price spike, suggests that the current rally is unsustainable.
