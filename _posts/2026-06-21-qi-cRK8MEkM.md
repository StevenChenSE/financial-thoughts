## Michael Pento Reveals What the Fed Knows About the Coming Economic Depression

---

### Introduction

In a recent episode of Capital Cosm, host Danny spoke with renowned economist and market strategist Michael Pento about the critical indicators that institutional investors are monitoring as the global economy teeters on the edge of a historic collapse. The conversation, recorded on June 18, 2026, covered everything from the Iran nuclear deal to the Federal Reserve's impossible position—and what it means for every investor holding stocks, bonds, or real estate today.

Pento's core thesis is straightforward yet alarming: the Federal Reserve understands that the next economic downturn will not be a ordinary recession. It will be a depression. And because of this knowledge, the Fed will continue printing money until the bond market finally collapses under the weight of its own debt.

"The Federal Reserve knows that the next recession is not going to be a recession," Pento stated during the interview. "It's going to be a depression. That's why they're willing to pump in trillions and trillions of preemptive dollars credit to make sure we don't ever have a drop in asset prices."

---

### Reading the Footprints of Oligarchs

One of Pento's most compelling arguments centers on his proprietary "inflation deflation and economic cycle model," which he developed to track what he calls "the trades of the handful of oligarchs that run this planet."

According to Pento, the market is not an enigmatic force operating on rational expectations. Instead, it trades heavily on insider information. His model monitors various financial indicators—credit spreads, five-year forward inflation expectations, CRB index, energy prices, and overall financial conditions—to detect when those with advance knowledge are positioning their portfolios.

A perfect example, Pento argues, can be found in the energy markets leading up to the Russia-Ukraine conflict. "Look at the run-up of oil stocks leading up to this war and then when the bomb started flying," he said. "I mean, why would that be a massive move higher in energy prices when no one knew what the hell was going on? But somebody knew."

The energy sector began breaking out in January, even as headlines screamed about imminent conflict. The actual invasion didn't occur until late February—yet those with insider knowledge had already accumulated positions in energy assets weeks earlier.

"The model's reading that, and saying somebody knows something, and ignore the headlines and just stick with your model," Pento explained. This counterintuitive approach allowed him to remain long equities during what most considered a perilous moment in global affairs.

---

### The Iran Deal and What Markets Are Actually Trading On

The interview touched on the significant development of a new Iran nuclear agreement, signed on a Friday with markets reacting positively—especially tech-heavy indices like the NASDAQ, S&P, and Dow Jones. However, commodity markets told a different story.

Pento's assessment is that the Iran situation has been largely priced in by markets, which have moved beyond the conflict. Both sides, he argues, are seeking an exit strategy. President Trump himself reportedly admitted that without finding a way to open the Strait of Hormuz, oil prices would have reached $200 per barrel, causing the global economy to crash.

The agreement represents "Trump realizing he made a mistake, in my opinion, and doing the right thing," Pento said. While the threat of $200 or $150 oil is temporarily over, Pento cautions that the underlying issues in the Strait haven't been fully resolved.

---

### The Federal Reserve's Impossible Dilemma

Perhaps the most alarming portion of the interview focused on Federal Reserve policy and its inevitable consequences. Pento, citing Austrian economist Ludwig von Mises, argues that inflation is not caused by economic growth or low unemployment—it's a choice made by policymakers at the Fed.

"Mr. Powell missed the 2% inflation target for five, well over five years and didn't care," Pento said. "He was lowering interest rates into that, which I think is disgusting. He also oversaw the increase of the balance sheet by $5 trillion."

When Kevin Warsh—a fellow Austrian economist—assumed a prominent Fed role, Pento had hoped for a change in direction. Instead, Warsh announced plans to expand the Fed's balance sheet by $10 billion per month. What's more, in just one week after taking the helm, the balance sheet had already expanded by $14 billion—more than the promised monthly amount.

Pento is unambiguous about his disappointment: "I'm very disappointed the Fed will not shrink it. At least not now."

---

### Why the Fed Can't Stop Printing Money

The reason for the Fed's continued money printing, according to Pento, is both simple and terrifying: the alternative is worse.

The financialization of the American economy has reached historic levels. Asset prices—particularly in real estate and equities—have expanded to valuations that make traditional recessions potentially catastrophic. "We're talking about real estate and equities primarily, is so off the charts that they understand that we're not going to have a normal run-of-the-mill contraction in the economy," Pento explained. "It is going to make home prices drop by 25-30% and the stock market drop by 50 to 80%."

Consider the Buffett Indicator—the total market cap of equities relative to GDP:

- During the NASDAQ bubble: 140% of GDP
- Before the 2008 financial crisis: 104% of GDP
- Today: 233% of GDP

"That's 100 percentage points more than it was at the prior peak in the NASDAQ bubble," Pento emphasized. Home price to income ratios are similarly elevated, even surpassing the levels seen during the 2008 housing bubble.

Additionally, the federal government's balance sheet is in a vastly different position than during previous crises. In 2008, national debt stood at just 63% of GDP with deficits around $200-300 billion. Today, debt-to-GDP has reached 123%, with deficits already at $2 trillion annually. Worse, $25 trillion in debt was added just since COVID.

"At 120% is when you start seeing economic collapse," Pento warned. "We are past that."

---

### The Coming Depression: Why This Time Is Different

Pento makes a crucial distinction that differentiates the coming downturn from all previous recessions. When the next contraction arrives—and he believes it will—three factors will combine to create what he calls a "trenchant, cutting, acute, protracted depression."

**First**, asset prices are falling from unprecedented heights, meaning the percentage decline will be historically severe.

**Second**, the government's ability to respond is severely limited. While the Fed bailed out the system in 2008, it cannot do so effectively this time without triggering hyperinflation and a sovereign debt crisis.

**Third**, the response itself will likely cause the dollar to crash and bond yields to spike—the opposite of what happened in every previous recession when central banks could cut rates and buy bonds with impunity.

"So, that's the reason why the Fed is panicking already and why no one's going to preemptively hike interest rates a lot or really shrink the balance sheet a lot," Pento explained. "Because they'll engender such a collapse like we've never seen before."

When the next crisis hits, Pento predicts the Fed will launch universal basic income and helicopter money programs, pushing deficits from $2 trillion to $5-6 trillion per year. "Inflation will go bonkers. The dollar is going to crash, and the sovereign treasury market is going to crash. So, bond yields are going to rise most likely the next time."

---

### Pento's Investment Strategy: Timing the Collapse

Despite these grim predictions, Pento remains tactical in his approach. His current strategy is to stay long the stock market—until the bond market signals its imminent collapse.

"What they're going to do is going to keep printing money until the bond market collapses," he said. "And until that happens, I'm going to stay long the stock market."

However, when the insiders know the bond market is about to break—which his model is designed to detect—Pento will make dramatic changes to his portfolio:

- Short the stock market
- Go long the dollar
- Go long the short end of the bond market

The rationale is clear: when the crash comes, the Fed will respond by dropping interest rates to zero, which will cause a short-term rally in shorter-duration bonds. "Then sit back and relax and watch," Pento concluded.

### Summary

Michael Pento's analysis presents a sobering picture of where the global economy is heading. The key takeaways from this interview are:

1. **Insider tracking matters**: Markets move on insider information, and tracking commodity prices, credit spreads, and inflation expectations can reveal what those in power already know.

2. **The Iran deal is priced in**: Markets have largely moved beyond the conflict, with both sides seeking an off-ramp from hostilities.

3. **Inflation is a choice**: The Fed's decision to expand its balance sheet by trillions is deliberate, not accidental.

4. **The Fed is trapped**: With debt at 123% of GDP and asset valuations at historic highs, the Federal Reserve cannot raise rates or shrink its balance sheet without triggering a catastrophic collapse.

5. **The next recession will be a depression**: Because of inflated asset prices and limited fiscal capacity, any economic contraction will be far more severe than normal recessions.

6. **Bond market collapse is the trigger**: When the bond market finally breaks—and Pento believes it will—that's the signal to rotate from stocks to defensive positions.

The question is not whether these events will unfold, but when. And according to Pento, those who understand these dynamics will be positioned to profit while others are caught flat-footed when the music finally stops.
