## Soar Financial CEO analyzes commodity divergences, AI risks, and mining investment strategies at the Rule Symposium

**Introduction**

Kai Hoffmann, CEO of Soar Financial and portfolio manager of a long-only mining equity fund, recently joined the Rule Symposium to discuss the evolving commodity landscape. Hoffmann highlighted a sharp divergence between copper and gold, attributing copper's resilience to fundamental supply constraints while viewing gold's recent volatility as sentiment-driven. He also addressed risks surrounding the AI trade, geopolitical impacts on oil and agriculture, and the strategic positioning of his fund within the resource sector.

### Copper vs. Gold: Fundamentals Versus Sentiment

Hoffmann emphasized that copper and gold are currently driven by different forces. Copper remains fundamentally supported, trading above $6 per pound despite recent pressure on tech stocks. The metal is essential for global electrification, data center build-outs, and industrial applications, creating a massive, inelastic demand. Hoffmann noted that copper is fundamentally driven, which explains its strength relative to sentiment-heavy assets.

In contrast, gold has been more reactive to headlines, inflation fears, and geopolitical developments in the Middle East. Hoffmann observed that gold's pullback was partly due to investors liquidating positions to cover losses elsewhere, using gold as a source of liquidity. While gold remains a critical store of value, its recent price action reflects sentiment shifts rather than the structural supply-demand imbalances seen in copper.

### AI Risks, Liquidity, and Recession Fears

The conversation turned to the artificial intelligence sector, with Hoffmann acknowledging that AI is revolutionary but currently in "bubble territory." He pointed to Samsung's recent capex warnings as a sign of caution, which triggered market sell-offs. Hoffmann warned that if the AI bubble bursts, a liquidity crunch could emerge, impacting risk assets first.

However, he suggested that the implementation of AI and its impact on the workforce may be slower than social media hype implies. While recession risks are real, Hoffmann believes the structural need for copper in infrastructure and technology will persist. A global recession would eventually pressure copper, but the long-term demand thesis remains intact.

### Oil, Geopolitics, and the Middle East Trade

Hoffmann analyzed the decoupling of oil and gold, noting that oil's recent surge was driven by a supply shock related to Middle East tensions, whereas gold's movement was tied to rate hike fears and liquidity. He highlighted that pre-crisis oversupply helped absorb some of the shock, though regional price discrepancies remain stark, with some markets paying premiums and others seeing negative pricing.

Regarding the Iran war trade, Hoffmann suggested the market has become numb to geopolitical noise, shrugging off events like the attack on a Qatari ship. He expects the situation to normalize over the summer, provided no major escalation occurs. He also discussed ripple effects from the Strait of Hormuz, noting potential lag effects on fertilizer supplies (sulfur and phosphates) that could impact agricultural markets during the fall harvest.

### Soar Financial Fund Strategy and Junior Mining

Hoffmann detailed the structure of his mining equity fund, which follows a pyramid approach. The base consists of large producers and streamers for stability, the mid-layer focuses on developers (his preferred segment for leverage to commodity prices), and a small allocation is reserved for explorers. The fund focuses on gold, silver, and copper, with holdings in companies like Cameco and Ivanhoe Mines.

He emphasized active management, rebalancing daily based on flows and sentiment. Hoffmann recently added positions on dips but remains cautious about short-term volatility. He highlighted the importance of the Orning Index, which tracks financing for junior miners. The index shows $4.5 billion raised by July 1st, ahead of last year's total, indicating that capital is still flowing into the sector despite a pause driven by geopolitical concerns. Hoffmann does not view this as a market top but rather a consolidation phase.

### Gold Margins, Flat Scenarios, and Management Alpha

Addressing a scenario where gold remains flat at $4,000 per ounce for five years, Hoffmann argued that mining equities could still generate significant returns through production growth, M&A, and deposit expansion. He noted that $4,000 gold provides healthy margins, as normal margins typically range from $1,000 to $1,700 per ounce.

In such an environment, alpha comes from management quality. Hoffmann pointed out that underperformers often suffer from poor storytelling, inability to raise capital, or slow catalysts. He described some junior companies as "cockroaches" that survive on small raises without achieving meaningful progress, stressing the need for investors to focus on teams that can execute and communicate value effectively.

### USD Strength, Euro Irrelevance, and Safe Haven Dynamics

Hoffmann challenged the narrative of dollar collapse, asserting that the US remains a global powerhouse, particularly in AI innovation and financial infrastructure. He argued that the petrodollar is stronger than ever, citing Turkey's recent gold sales to buy dollars as evidence of the currency's resilience. In contrast, he viewed the eurozone as increasingly irrelevant on the global stage, lacking the resources and technological edge of the US.

Regarding gold as a safe haven, Hoffmann acknowledged its volatility but noted that gold has outperformed the S&P 500 over the last 25 years. He advised that for long-term wealth preservation, investors should hold physical gold, while short-term traders might use paper gold for leverage. The appropriate strategy depends on the investor's time horizon and risk tolerance.

### Copper Supply Crisis and Government Action

Hoffmann expressed strong conviction in copper, suggesting prices could reach $12 to $18 per pound due to an impending supply shock. He noted that attempts to substitute copper with aluminum, such as Ferrari's efforts in electric vehicles, fail due to copper's unique properties. With no significant new supply coming online, the "doo-doo will hit the fan" as demand outstrips production.

He discussed the role of governments in addressing critical mineral shortages, mentioning the EU's 10 billion fund for mineral security and the US Project Volt initiative. Hoffmann highlighted grants like the $450 million awarded to MP Materials for rare earth processing. He argued that governments should focus on funding exploration and streamlining permitting to secure supply chains, potentially creating strategic critical minerals reserves similar to the strategic petroleum reserve.

### Summary

Kai Hoffmann presents a bullish case for copper, driven by structural supply shortages and growing demand from electrification and AI infrastructure, with potential for prices to triple. He contrasts this with gold, which he views as sentiment-driven in the short term but valuable for long-term wealth preservation. Hoffmann's investment strategy focuses on high-quality mining equities, particularly developers with strong management teams, while maintaining a cautious stance on AI valuations and geopolitical risks. He emphasizes the enduring strength of the US dollar and the importance of government action in securing critical mineral supply chains.
