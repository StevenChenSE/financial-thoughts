## A secular bull market climax, extreme leverage, and the roadmap for an unprecedented global unwind

Macro strategist David Hunter, Chief Macro Strategist at Contrarian Macro Advisors, forecasts that financial markets are entering the final, vertical stage of a 44-year secular bull market. According to Hunter, major equity indices are poised for an explosive 30% to 40% melt-up over the next three to six months. However, once this parabolic climax concludes, he warns that an unprecedented global bust will trigger a broad-based market collapse of up to 80%, driving 10-year Treasury yields down toward 0%.

### The Final Melt-Up: Targets for the Equity Climax

Hunter argues that equity markets remain remarkably resilient despite recent interest rate pressures. Analyzing a mix of technicals, fundamentals, macro drivers, and persistent investor skepticism, he views the current setup as the setup for a steep, parabolic run.

Hunter’s updated targets for the top of this cycle include:

* **S&P 500:** 10,000 (~31% upside)
* **Dow Jones Industrial Average:** 70,000 (~38% upside)
* **Nasdaq Composite:** 36,000 (~34% upside)
* **Russell 2000:** 4,000 (~43% upside)

Hunter notes that secular bull market tops are often characterized by skepticism rebuilding after every minor pullback. He points out that institutional investors have largely kept "one foot out the door" since 2022. In his contrarian framework, the ultimate market peak will only arrive when broad institutional and retail sentiment turns uniformly bullish and declares the rally unstoppable.

### The Looming 80% Bust and Systemic Leverage

Once the melt-up reaches its exhaustion point, Hunter anticipates a severe deflationary bust that could wipe out up to 80% of equity values peak-to-trough, potentially taking the S&P 500 from 10,000 back down to 2,000. 

Unlike previous cyclical recessions, Hunter believes the next downturn will be exacerbated by unprecedented systemic leverage, spanning excessive global debt, derivative exposure, and private credit. 

While the 2008 Great Financial Crisis was primarily centered around U.S. subprime mortgages, Hunter suggests the coming bust could be ignited abroad. Potential flashpoints include:
* **Japan:** Rising Japanese Government Bond (JGB) yields breaking out of decades-long central bank yield curve control.
* **Europe and China:** Lingering structural and debt imbalances.
* **Financial Sector Linkages:** Counterparty strains across opaque credit markets.

### Bonds, Deflation, and Central Bank Intervention

While consensus expectations remain focused on sticky inflation and rising bond yields, Hunter takes a strongly bullish view on U.S. Treasuries. He believes the 10-year Treasury yield is near a cyclical top comparable to 2007 levels and will soon reverse lower.

* **Near-Term:** Yields could fall toward 3% to 4% as economic growth slows and energy prices decline.
* **During the Bust:** As systemic stress accelerates, Hunter projects the 10-year yield could reach 0%.
* **Monetary Response:** To combat a deflationary crisis of this scale, Hunter expects central banks to inject liquidity far exceeding past crises. He estimates the Federal Reserve could expand its balance sheet by up to $20 trillion through Treasury purchases, with total global central bank money printing reaching roughly $50 trillion.

While this massive liquidity wave would likely trigger a major inflationary cycle later in the decade, the immediate aftermath of the bust will be sharply deflationary.

### Commodities, Currencies, and Precious Metals

Hunter’s outlook outlines divergent paths across major commodity and currency markets:

* **Oil (WTI):** Bearish near-term and throughout the bust. Hunter expects crude oil to roll over in the coming months as global supply normalizes, eventually declining into the $30s during the bust.
* **U.S. Dollar (DXY):** Hunter expects the dollar index to break lower from its multi-month consolidation, targeting the 82–84 range over the next nine months. Concurrently, he sees the Euro reaching 1.30–1.35 and the British Pound moving toward 1.50. Later in the crisis, foreign demand for U.S. Treasuries as a safe haven could spark a secondary dollar rebound.
* **Gold and Silver:** Hunter remains structurally bullish on precious metals. He sets cycle targets of **$7,000/oz for gold** and **$200/oz for silver**. Over a longer horizon into the early 2030s (around 2032–2033), Hunter projects gold could reach $20,000/oz and silver $1,000/oz amid the post-bust inflationary aftermath.
* **Real Estate:** Hunter views the broad real estate market as having already peaked, pressured by high mortgage rates, growing inventory in select regional markets, and impending economic weakness.
