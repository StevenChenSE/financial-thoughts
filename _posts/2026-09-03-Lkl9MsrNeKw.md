**Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh's hawkish stance at the Jackson Hole Symposium has sent shockwaves through global markets, with investors now pricing in a high probability of a September interest rate hike.**

## The Hawkish Signal at Jackson Hole

Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh delivered a decisive message at his first Jackson Hole Symposium as Fed Chair, asserting that responsibility for 65 months of sustained elevated inflation sits squarely with the central bank. His standard for policy action is clear: the Fed must be confident that underlying inflation is moving to its 2% objective "clearly and at sufficient speed."

Markets interpreted these comments as a strong hawkish signal. On the day of the speech, the S&P 500 sold off sharply, gold gapped down 3%, Bitcoin dropped 3%, and Treasury yields climbed. According to the CME Fed Watch Tool, the probability of a 25 basis point rate hike at the next FOMC meeting on September 16th jumped to 66%.

## Inflation Trends and Policy Focus

Warsh emphasized that the Fed's predominant focus must be on prices, noting that inflation is currently running above the 2% target. He stressed the importance of capturing "underlying trend inflation" rather than reacting to individual data points. While broad inflation measures have fallen from their highs, progress over the last couple of years has been modest.

Warsh reiterated that the Fed's price stability objective is a "firm fixed target" and that price stability is not self-executing. He stated, "It's the Fed's job to deliver stable prices. No excuses." This stance prioritizes inflation control over labor market concerns, a shift that has unsettled investors who had hoped for rate cuts.

## The AI Boom and Economic Resilience

A key component of Warsh's speech was his assessment of the current economic environment, which he described as remarkably resilient. He noted that real consumer spending has increased by more than 2% over the past four quarters, and private domestic financial purchases have risen at a pace of nearly 3%.

Warsh highlighted a "hinge point in history" driven by AI. He observed that business CapEx is rising rapidly, with the four-quarter change in investment in equipment and intangibles reaching around 9%, the highest growth rate since 2021. More than half of this CapEx growth is ascribed to the AI build-out. He noted that annualized token sales for the two leading AI labs alone exceed $100 billion, a 500% increase from 12 months ago. This technological burst is contributing to a period of "secular growth" rather than the "secular stagnation" feared by some economists.

## Labor Market Stability

Warsh provided a positive assessment of the labor market, stating that it is "quite stable." The jobless rate remains low at 4.1%, and unemployment claims are near their lowest levels in decades. He attributed the relatively low turnover to significant rematching in the post-pandemic environment and noted that when labor supply is barely growing, monthly job gains naturally run low. This stability allows the Fed to focus more aggressively on price stability without immediate concern for employment deterioration.

## Rejection of Forward Guidance

In a significant policy shift, Warsh criticized the Fed's reliance on forward guidance, describing it as a practice that has "outstayed its welcome." He argued that oversharing policy deliberations and overcommitting to future decisions can leave markets, businesses, and households astray.

Warsh went as far as to suggest that forward guidance in 2021 might have slowed the policy response to high inflation. He warned against a "hall of mirrors" problem where markets rely on Fed guidance and the Fed relies on market prices, leading to policy errors. He emphasized that the Fed should be "humble and never naive," and that market participants should track real information and draw their own conclusions.

## Global Context and G20 Remarks

At the G20 summit, Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Fed Chair Kevin Warsh addressed global economic risks. Bessent discussed the impact of sanctions on Iran, noting that the Iranian economy is in shock with gas lines forming, and that Iran is "lashing out kinetically because they are losing economically." He described the US economy as a patient that has been stabilized and is now in the healing phase, with real incomes increasing.

Warsh echoed the theme of a "hinge point in history," stating that the previous era of secular stagnation is over. He emphasized the need for change and recognized that the current period is one of secular growth, driven by technological innovation and investment.

## Conclusion

The market's reaction to Warsh's speech underscores a significant shift in Federal Reserve policy communication. With inflation remaining above target and the economy showing strength, particularly in AI-driven sectors, the Fed appears prepared to maintain a restrictive stance. The September 16 FOMC meeting is now a critical event, with a 66% probability of a rate hike. Investors are advised to monitor upcoming inflation data, such as the August CPI report, which could further solidify the case for a hike. The rejection of forward guidance adds an element of uncertainty, requiring markets to react to real-time economic data rather than Fed hints.
