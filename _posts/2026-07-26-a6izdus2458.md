## Copper Deficits and Energy Constraints

Copper is identified as another critical asset facing severe supply constraints. Friedland notes that humanity needs to mine as much copper in the next 18 years as it did in the last 10,000 years. The energy required to produce copper has increased 16 times since 1900, and mine grades are declining. Andy emphasizes that these deficits in copper, silver, and other metals are ultimately deficits in energy. Without sufficient energy production, the mining sector cannot meet demand, creating a feedback loop that supports higher commodity prices. He suggests that the lack of investment in supply capacity means prices must rise significantly to incentivize new production.
