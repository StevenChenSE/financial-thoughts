## Why the 2025-2026 Bull Market Differs from 2011-2012 — And What It Means for Investors

The precious metals space is experiencing a dramatic correction that has left many investors questioning whether the bull market is over. Gold has dropped approximately 25% from its highs and silver has fallen nearly 40%, yet these losses come on the heels of triple-digit gains over the past year. At press time, gold was trading just above $4,200 an ounce, down about 1% on the day, while mining stocks like GDX have seen declines of 35-40% — well beyond the typical 20% correction threshold.

Despite the painful short-term losses, one prominent analyst believes the fundamental case for gold and silver remains stronger than ever, with institutional backing and central bank buying reaching unprecedented levels.

## Understanding the Current Correction

The first quarter of the year delivered two significant "rug pulls" that shook investor confidence in precious metals. On January 30th, silver experienced an extraordinary single-day decline of 34% — a move that John Feneck, CEO of Feneck Consulting, described as an orchestrated situation. "I watched that whole day on my computer like in disbelief," Feneck said during a recent interview. "This is why we talk about institutions being so powerful. Whether you like them or not, you have to respect them."

JP Morgan later acknowledged leading the silver shorting activity. Then on March 3rd, just one day after markets reopened following geopolitical developments, another sharp selloff occurred as margin calls cascaded through the system, compounding losses through March and April.

## Why 2025-2026 Is Not 2011-2012

Critics have drawn parallels between the current correction and the 2011-2012 period, when gold fell more than 50% from its highs. However, Feneck argues the fundamental dynamics have fundamentally changed. "There's actual billionaires buying this stuff," he emphasized. "There's central banks buying it hand over fist."

The paradigm shift is particularly evident in institutional positioning. Bank of America has reiterated a gold price target of $6,000 by year-end, Goldman Sachs projects $5,400, and JP Morgan forecasts $5,050. These same institutions have not typically been friendly to gold over the years — many had shorted precious metals since the 2008 financial crisis. Their current positioning represents a significant vote of confidence.

## The Sentiment Reversal and What It Signals

Perhaps most telling is the reversal in retail sentiment. Just months ago, Feneck noted that friends and colleagues outside the finance industry — including tech workers and medical professionals — were asking whether it was a good time to buy silver at Costco. "They're asking me, 'Is this a good time to buy silver at Costco? It's run out. Should I buy some more?'" Those questions have now disappeared, which historically marks capitulation among retail investors.

The weak sentiment has triggered concerns about a potential feedback loop — if sentiment remains depressed, could it trigger another wave of selling? Feneck doesn't believe the bull market is over, pointing to the structural support from institutional buyers and central banks as a fundamental floor beneath prices.

## The Gold-Copper Divergence

One notable development is the growing divergence between gold and copper prices. While gold has trended downward since mid-April, copper has surged to new all-time highs above $6.30 per pound, closely tracking the S&P 500. Copper — often viewed as a barometer for economic health — has been moving alongside equity markets rather than traditional safe-haven assets.

Feneck attributes this to copper's industrial nature. "Copper does better in times of economic strength, which clearly we're in right now," he explained. The metal's correlation with the AI trade and technology sector has intensified, as evidenced by the fact that 12 of 15 lead articles on CNBC during one recent weekend focused on artificial intelligence.

This divergence also highlights the critical difference between precious and industrial metals. While gold and silver are finding their footing, copper benefits from infrastructure projects and technological demand — but Feneck cautions that copper will "suffer if the Nasdaq, the S&P, AI, global infrastructure type projects are going to suffer."

## Investment Strategy During Volatility

For investors facing significant losses, Feneck's advice centers on cost basis. "It all depends on your cost basis," he said. His own cost basis in silver remains under $20 per ounce, allowing him to weather extreme volatility without panic selling.

The analyst previously sold silver at prices above $100 and is now watching for opportunities to reload if prices touch the high 40s or low 50s. He believes that level will hold based on historical chart patterns. The January 30th crash, while severe, proved temporary — silver made a V-shaped recovery back to over $100 by March 2nd.

## Looking Ahead: What to Watch

Several factors will determine the near-term direction of precious metals. The Federal Reserve's policy trajectory remains crucial, as does geopolitical uncertainty. The dynamic has changed, Feneck noted, with "Trump being a wild card" who could impact markets at any moment in ways no one anticipated — Venezuela, Cuba, and Oman being recent examples.

The silver market faces additional pressure from its dual nature. While over 60% of silver now goes toward industrial uses — a critical mineral designation that emerged only recently — this industrial demand makes it more sensitive to economic cycles than gold.

Despite the exhaustion many investors feel from monitoring daily developments, the underlying case for precious metals remains intact. Institutional price targets suggest significant upside from current levels, and central bank buying continues at historic rates. The question for investors is whether they can maintain conviction during what may be the final phase of distribution before the next leg higher.
