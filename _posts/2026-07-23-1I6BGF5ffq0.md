## The Everything Bubble and the Looming Depression

In a stark warning to investors, renowned economist and trend forecaster Harry Dent predicts that the current economic expansion is an unsustainable "everything bubble" poised for a catastrophic burst. During a July 2026 interview with *Capital*, Dent outlined a scenario where the S&P 500 and NASDAQ could face declines of 80% to 95%, echoing the severity of the 1929 Great Depression. Dent argues that decades of artificial stimulus, demographic shifts, and suppressed recessions have created a fragile economic structure that is now ready to unwind violently.

### The Role of Money Velocity

Central to Dent’s analysis is the metric of M2 money velocity, which he describes as the "truth meter" for the economy. While the M2 money supply has expanded significantly due to government deficits and central bank printing, money velocity—the rate at which money changes hands—has been in a steady decline since 1997. Dent explains that this divergence indicates that new investments are not generating sufficient returns to sustain themselves, forcing the economy to rely on ever-increasing amounts of artificial stimulus.

According to Dent, the current velocity levels are the worst seen since the period leading up to the 1930s Great Depression. He notes that when money velocity drops this precipitously, it signals that the underlying economy is effectively in a recession, despite nominal GDP growth. The recent inflection in M2 growth is merely a desperate attempt to cover up the lack of organic economic vitality.

### Demographics and the Baby Boom Cycle

Dent’s forecasting model relies heavily on demographic spending waves. He points out that the Baby Boom generation drove a massive spending boom from 1982 to 2007, peaking in late 2007. This peak spending naturally transitioned into a 12- to 14-year downturn, similar to the 1930-1942 period. However, instead of allowing the market to correct naturally after the 2008 financial crisis, policymakers intervened with trillions of dollars in stimulus.

This intervention prevented the necessary "shakeout" of zombie companies and failed investments. Dent argues that recessions are a healthy and necessary part of free-market capitalism, akin to sleep for the human body. By declaring "war on recessions," governments have kept the economy in an artificial state of wakefulness, leading to a buildup of debt and overvalued assets that can no longer be sustained. The current downturn is now colliding with the Millennial generation's spending peak, exacerbating the potential for a severe correction.

### The AI and Real Estate Bubbles

The recent surge in Artificial Intelligence (AI) stocks has been cited by many as a new growth engine, but Dent views it as the latest phase of the broader bubble. He compares the current AI hype to the automobile boom of the 1920s, where massive speculation preceded a brutal shakeout that left only a few dominant players. Similarly, companies like SpaceX are being valued at trillions of dollars despite being in the early "0 to 10%" innovation phase, creating extreme overvaluation.

Real estate is another critical component of this "everything bubble." Dent highlights that housing markets, particularly in Florida and California, have reached unprecedented levels of overvaluation. Unlike the 1920s, where strict financing requirements limited leverage, modern 30-year mortgages with low down payments have fueled a housing bubble that dwarfs historical precedents. Dent warns that when this bubble bursts, it will not only devastate household wealth but also "smash the banks" due to the high leverage involved.

### Investment Strategy: Cash and Treasuries

Given the impending crash, Dent advises investors to move away from equities and traditional safe havens like gold and silver, which he notes have also become part of the bubble. He suggests that the first wave of the crash could see a 50-60% drop in the S&P 500 and a 60% drop in the NASDAQ 100 within just a few months. This initial crash will likely be triggered by rising bond yields or a sudden loss of confidence in the stimulus-driven economy.

Dent recommends holding cash and short-term instruments initially, but identifies U.S. Treasury bonds as the ultimate safe haven. He argues that as financial assets collapse, capital will flee to the safety of 10- and 30-year Treasury bonds, causing their values to appreciate significantly. However, he cautions that if even Treasury bonds fail to respond positively, it would signal a complete loss of confidence in the U.S. government, necessitating a retreat into pure cash.

### Conclusion

Harry Dent’s message is clear: the current economic prosperity is an illusion maintained by excessive stimulus and suppressed market corrections. With money velocity at historic lows and asset valuations detached from reality, the market is due for a severe reset. Dent urges investors to prepare for a depression-level crash, emphasizing that waiting for confirmation will result in catastrophic losses. By positioning portfolios in cash and Treasuries, investors can protect themselves from the impending storm and potentially capitalize on the eventual recovery.
