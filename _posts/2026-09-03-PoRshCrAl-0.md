**Subtitle:** Why rising yields signal a broken bond market and the path forward for precious metals and mining stocks.

## Introduction

In a recent interview with Capital Cause, Adrian Day, a prominent investor in precious metals, addressed the current state of the global bond market. With the 10-year US Treasury yield at its highest level since January 2025 and Japanese bond yields hitting new highs, Day argues that the market is not simply repricing for higher interest rates, but is reacting to a structural failure in the bond market. He contends that traditional long-term buyers are exiting the market, forcing yields higher to entice buyers, a dynamic that ultimately benefits gold and silver investors.

## The Broken Bond Market

Day’s central thesis is that the current rise in yields is a sign of a broken market rather than a healthy opportunity to lock in returns. He points to the behavior of traditional long-term buyers—insurance companies, pension funds, and foreign governments—who are increasingly reluctant to lend money to highly indebted governments for 20 to 30 years.

Specifically, Day highlights the shift in Japanese investment behavior. With the Japanese 10-year yield reaching 3% and the yen strengthening, Japanese institutions are finding it more attractive to invest domestically rather than in US Treasuries. The narrowing rate differential, combined with the strength of the yen, has made the currency risk of holding US debt unappealing for Japanese entities. This exodus of foreign buyers, particularly from allies like Japan, signals a lack of sufficient long-term demand for US government debt.

## The Impact of Intervention and Geopolitics

The conversation also touched on the transient nature of currency interventions. Day noted that the recent Japanese intervention to support the yen was effective for only three days before the market reversed 50% of the move. Similarly, US Treasury buybacks provided only a brief reprieve from rising yields. Day emphasizes that one-off interventions rarely succeed against the tide of fundamental supply and demand.

Geopolitical tensions in the Middle East, specifically the ongoing conflict involving Iran and Iraq, have further complicated the market. The escalation in rhetoric and bombing has sent oil prices above $90 a barrel and the US dollar higher as a safe haven. However, Day observes that the market’s reaction to these escalations is diminishing. After six months of conflict, each new headline has a smaller impact on oil prices, suggesting the market is adapting to a "new normal."

## Gold, Silver, and the Real Rate Argument

Despite the recent pullback in gold prices, Day remains bullish. He distinguishes between rising yields due to a strong economy (which is bad for gold) and rising yields due to a broken bond market (which is good for gold). He argues that the latter is the current reality.

Day notes that gold had an "astonishing" month in August, with prices up significantly and gold stocks up over 50%. This sharp rise set the stage for a pullback, which was triggered by a hawkish interpretation of the Fed Chair's speech at Jackson Hole and subsequent geopolitical tensions. However, Day believes the narrative will shift back to the reality of broken bonds, driving gold and silver prices higher.

He also highlighted the relationship between gold and the CPI (Consumer Price Index). Since 2000, gold has significantly outperformed the CPI, suggesting that the official inflation measure underestimates the true cost of living. Gold, as a constant of purchasing power over 2,000 years, provides a more accurate gauge of inflation than fiat currency measures.

## Mining Stocks: A Resilient Sector

A key takeaway from the interview is the resilience of gold mining stocks (represented by the GDX ETF) during the recent correction. While gold prices pulled back, mining stocks did not decline as sharply, with some even breaking out to new highs. Day attributes this to investors recognizing the massive margins and cash flow growth in the mining sector.

The sector has seen nine consecutive quarters of increased free cash flow, the best record of any sector in the S&P 500. Investors are increasingly viewing gold stocks as undervalued relative to their cash flow generation, making them less inclined to sell during short-term price dips. Day suggests that while he is already well-invested, new investors should consider the sector, as the fundamentals remain strong.

## Summary

Adrian Day’s analysis suggests that the current bond market volatility is a symptom of a deeper structural issue: the inability of governments to sell long-term debt without offering high yields. This environment is favorable for gold and silver, which serve as a hedge against currency debasement and sovereign risk. While short-term factors like the Iran conflict and Fed policy create volatility, the long-term trend remains bullish for precious metals. Investors are advised to focus on the underlying break in the bond market and the strong cash flow fundamentals of the mining sector when making investment decisions.
