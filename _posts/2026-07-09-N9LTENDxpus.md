## Veteran Analyst Outlines Path to Major Targets and Warns of Impending Seasonal Correction

Veteran precious metals analyst Don Durrett asserts that the bull market in gold and silver remains robust despite a recent severe correction. In a detailed market breakdown, Durrett outlines a trajectory for gold to reach $8,000 over the next 36 months and silver to surge past $200, driven by structural deficits and a shift toward monetary metal status. However, he warns investors to brace for a seasonal Wall Street correction between mid-August and mid-October, which could push gold back below $4,000 before the next major leg up.

### Bull Market Health and Historical Context

The precious metals market has endured a brutal five-month correction, with gold dropping 29% from its January peak of $5,600 to a bottom of $3,959. Silver suffered an even sharper decline, falling 54% from $121 to $56, though it has since recovered slightly to around $62. Durrett emphasizes that this drawdown is consistent with historical patterns in bull markets.

"This is the third gold bull market," Durrett notes, referencing the runs from 1971 to 1980 and 2002 to 2011. He explains that previous bull cycles featured significant corrections averaging 25% to 30% over a period of five to seven months. The current 29% drop in gold fits squarely within this norm.

Technical indicators support the continuation of the bull trend. The 200-week moving average for gold sits at $2,800, a level Durrett believes gold will not approach again. Despite the price action dipping below the 200-day moving average twice in June, Durrett characterizes these moves as false breakouts that provided buying opportunities. He describes the gold chart as "unbelievably healthy," confirming that the long-term uptrend is intact.

### Short-Term Outlook and Seasonal Risks

While the long-term trend is positive, Durrett cautions that gold will not rally straight back to its all-time highs. He anticipates a moderate run in July and early August, offering a window for gains. However, he warns of significant headwinds approaching.

"The bad news is we're not going to go straight up," Durrett states. He highlights the risks associated with market seasonality, specifically the period from mid-August to mid-October. This window is historically "ugly" for mining stocks and the broader S&P 500. Durrett expects a Wall Street correction during this timeframe, ranging from 5% to potentially 10% or 20%.

When this correction occurs, gold and silver will likely be sold off again. Durrett does not believe the $4,000 level will hold as a bottom for the year and expects gold to trade back below that threshold. Rather than fearing this decline, he views it as a strategic entry point. "I've already created my list for the next correction," he reveals, noting that he is prepared to buy the dip as soon as the seasonal weakness emerges.

### Long-Term Targets: Gold to $8,000 and Beyond

Looking beyond the immediate volatility, Durrett presents a bullish long-term forecast. He expects gold to reach $8,000 over the next 36 months, a target he considers conservative. For valuation purposes, he is currently using a $7,000 price point for gold miners.

Durrett also suggests that higher prices are plausible as the bull market matures. "10, 12, 15 is not crazy talk," he asserts, indicating that gold could eventually test $10,000, $12,000, or even $15,000. This trajectory is supported by continued central bank accumulation, which remains a primary driver of gold's strength as institutions add the metal to their balance sheets.

### Silver Dynamics: From Base Metal to Monetary Asset

While gold leads the charge, Durrett identifies significant opportunities in silver, which he describes as a "tiny, illiquid market" that will eventually follow gold's ascent. The narrative for silver shifts as gold prices rise; when gold reaches $4,000 or $5,000 per ounce, retail investors will be priced out of the gold market and turn to silver as an affordable proxy.

This transition marks silver's evolution from a base metal to a monetary metal. Durrett points out that silver has already entered a period of massive structural deficits, averaging over 100 million ounces annually for the past five years. These deficits are driven almost entirely by investor demand, which has grown from 20% to 30% of total silver supply, while fabricator demand remains steady between 700 and 900 million ounces.

"We're going to run out of silver," Durrett warns. As the bull market intensifies and mania sets in, the physical shortage will constrain supply, potentially causing prices to skyrocket. He projects the Gold-Silver Ratio (GSR) could drop into the 30s, while the silver-to-gold ratio may rise to 2% or 3%.

Based on these metrics, Durrett sets a conservative silver target of $140, with a probable range of $150 to $175. If gold hits $7,000 and the ratio expands to 3%, silver could reach $210. He concludes that $200 is a realistic destination for silver as the supply squeeze intensifies.

### Strategy for Investors

Durrett's advice for investors centers on discipline and patience. The current market environment rewards those who accumulate during dips rather than chasing tops. He emphasizes that the window to position portfolios before the next leg up is closing fast, particularly as physical supply windows begin to shut.

Investors should focus on quality miners and physical metal holdings. Durrett suggests using the expected seasonal correction between August and October as a prime opportunity to add to positions. By buying when Wall Street corrects and gold potentially dips below $4,000, investors can secure assets ahead of what Durrett believes will be one of the strongest precious metals runs in decades.

### Summary

Don Durrett confirms that the precious metals bull market is healthy and far from over, despite a recent 29% correction in gold. He forecasts gold reaching $8,000 within 36 months, with potential for much higher prices, while silver is poised to explode past $200 due to structural deficits and a shift to monetary metal status. Investors are urged to prepare for a seasonal correction in late summer, viewing any drop below $4,000 in gold as a critical buying opportunity to capture the next major leg up.
