In a recent interview, defense analyst Michael O’Hanlon pulled back the curtain on Iran’s long-term military planning, revealing a strategic pivot that has largely flown under the radar. According to O’Hanlon, Iran recognized decades ago that it could not match the United States in conventional air or naval power. Instead, the country funneled its resources into building a massive, survivable missile force.

“The Iranians knew they could never stand up to our air force,” O’Hanlon explained. “They could probably shoot down some of us if they were lucky, and they had practically no air force to begin with.” He addressed recent claims of destroying Iran’s air capabilities with a dose of reality: “A lot of people are listening to President Trump say, ‘Well, I destroyed Iran’s air force.’ Well, there wasn’t much to destroy.”

The same logic applied to Iran’s naval assets. “And then he says, ‘Well, we’ve destroyed their navy.’ Well, seven frigates is about all the Iranians had. Everything else were small little patrol boats or motorboats.” O’Hanlon did note one exception: “They do have some diesel submarines. I don’t know whether or not those have been accounted for because the Navy doesn’t like diesel subs because they’re very, very hard to track, especially in shallow water.”

With the understanding that its traditional forces were vulnerable, Iran made a calculated decision. “My point is Iran knew all of these things were going to happen,” O’Hanlon stated. “So where did they put all of their investment over the last 20 years?”

The answer lies underground. “They put it into huge underground facilities that are very, very safe,” he said, “and they have thousands upon thousands upon thousands of missiles.”

The scale of this arsenal is staggering. O’Hanlon shared the most reliable pre-war estimate available to him: “Before the war began, the best estimate that I could get from people I trust was 45,000 missiles.”

This revelation underscores a central pillar of Iran’s defense doctrine: forgo competing in domains where the U.S. dominates, and invest heavily in asymmetric capabilities designed to deter and retaliate. While the world often focuses on Iran’s conventional military displays, its real strategic weight may be hidden deep beneath the earth, in the form of one of the largest missile inventories in the Middle East.
