## A Final Parabolic Surge Before the Bust

Contrarian macro strategist David Hunter outlines a bold thesis for the current financial landscape: we are witnessing the final stages of a 44-year secular bull market that began in August 1982. Hunter predicts a "melt-up" scenario where equity markets will experience a vertical, parabolic rise before topping out in the fall of 2024 or early 2025. Following this peak, he anticipates a severe global financial bust, worse than the 2008 crisis, which will ultimately pivot the economic cycle toward an inflation-driven, industrial era led by commodities.

## The Melt-Up Thesis and Market Targets

Despite recent consolidation in the Nasdaq and a broader rally in the SPY, Hunter remains convinced that the momentum is intact. He argues that the market does not need to rise every day to be parabolic; monthly charts still indicate the beginning of a vertical move. The catalyst for this final surge is not just fundamental strength, but the psychology of the "momentum of the tape." As prices rise, narratives shift from caution to optimism, forcing institutional investors to participate to avoid being left behind.

Hunter provides specific targets for this melt-up phase:
*   **S&P 500 (SPY):** 10,000 (approximately 33% upside from 7,500).
*   **Nasdaq:** 36,000.
*   **Russell 2000:** 4,000.
*   **Dow Jones:** 70,000.

He believes liquidity already exists in the system to support this move, which could occur over three to six months. However, this liquidity is finite. Once these targets are reached, the Federal Reserve’s reluctance to engage in "QE infinity" will leave no room for further expansion, triggering the cycle's end.

## The Role of the Fed, Oil, and Interest Rates

A central pillar of Hunter’s thesis involves the interplay between oil prices, inflation, and interest rates. He views the recent spike in oil prices due to tensions with Iran as a countertrend move. While oil may briefly test $90–$95 per barrel, Hunter expects it to roll over and return to the $60s or lower in the second half of the year.

This decline in oil will cause inflation to roll over, allowing interest rates to fall. Hunter predicts the 10-year Treasury yield, currently around 4.65%, will top out and then drop below 4%, potentially heading toward 3%. He argues that the Fed, including Governor Kevin Warsh, is focused on shrinking the balance sheet and fighting inflation, making them slow to respond to economic weakness. This delay will exacerbate the coming downturn, forcing central banks to eventually flood the system with liquidity only after a "free-falling financial system" emerges.

## Precious Metals: Gold and Silver Targets

Hunter sees precious metals participating strongly in the melt-up alongside equities. He believes gold and silver have recently found support after a prolonged consolidation and are poised for a steep rally.

*   **Gold Target:** $7,000 (from current levels near $4,000).
*   **Silver Target:** $200 (from current levels near $55–$60).

He suggests that silver could see a steeper percentage gain than its previous run from November to January. However, these gains will be followed by a significant correction during the bust phase. Gold may retrace 50% to $3,500, while silver, being more industrial and volatile, could drop 75% to $50. Despite this, Hunter views these corrections as buying opportunities for the next cycle.

## The Coming Global Bust and Housing Correction

Hunter predicts a severe recession, likely beginning by the end of this year, driven by consumer deleveraging, rising delinquencies, and Fed tightening. This will evolve into a global bust next year. The housing market is expected to correct by 30–40% from its highs. While low mortgage rates (potentially 2–2.5%) may provide a short-term bounce, Hunter argues housing is not a long-term inflation hedge. He forecasts that by the early 2030s, inflation and interest rates will surge, pushing mortgage rates above 15%, which will severely limit affordability and demand.

## The Next Cycle: A Commodity Supercycle

The aftermath of the bust will define the next economic era. Hunter anticipates that policymakers will be forced to print money aggressively to stabilize the system, leading to currency debasement and a weak dollar. The DXY is expected to drop to 82–83 in the near term, potentially falling to 50 or lower in the long term.

This monetary expansion will jump-start an industrial and commodity-driven recovery. Hunter predicts:
*   **Oil:** Could rise from a low of $30 to $500 per barrel over the next decade.
*   **Gold:** Long-term target of $20,000 by 2032–2033.
*   **Silver:** Long-term target of $1,000.

The next cycle will favor commodities, including copper, agricultural products, and precious metals, rather than the tech and growth stocks that dominated the last two decades. Hunter advises investors to sell at the melt-up top and rotate into oil and metals for the ensuing supercycle.

## Conclusion

David Hunter’s outlook presents a high-conviction view of a short-term equity melt-up followed by a deep global bust. The key to navigating this transition lies in recognizing the end of the liquidity-driven bull market and positioning for the subsequent inflationary, commodity-led era. With targets set for major indices and metals, Hunter emphasizes that the coming volatility will create historic opportunities for those prepared to pivot from financial assets to real assets.
