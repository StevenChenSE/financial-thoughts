Azura Capital CEO Tavi Costa argues that the US cannot afford tighter monetary policy or prolonged conflict, necessitating an inflationary exit strategy that favors hard assets over cash.

The current macroeconomic landscape presents a precarious situation for global markets, according to Tavi Costa, co-founder and CEO of Azura Capital. In a candid discussion regarding market rotations, inflation, and the future of commodities, Costa outlines a bearish view on the US dollar and traditional equities, while highlighting significant opportunities in precious metals, copper, and Latin American emerging markets. He warns that the "path of least resistance" for policymakers is to inflate their way out of the debt crisis, a scenario where holding cash becomes increasingly dangerous.

## The Illusion of Cooling Inflation

Recent data has shown softer inflation prints, with CPI and PPI figures suggesting a disinflationary trend. However, Costa remains skeptical of these government metrics. He asserts that "real inflation" as experienced by consumers in grocery stores and service sectors remains high and unsustainable.

Costa argues that long-term inflation is a monetary phenomenon, yet fiscal discipline has evaporated. Despite rhetoric from Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Walsh about controlling inflation through monetary policy, Costa believes the Fed’s hands are tied. The US government cannot afford the interest rates required to truly crush inflation without causing a systemic collapse.

"If we tighten conditions and we're really going to be serious about inflation... we need 15% interest rates, 10% interest rates. That would be insane," Costa explains. "The system cannot afford this."

He suggests that the Fed is likely to manipulate data or accept higher inflation to avoid the economic devastation of aggressive tightening. Consequently, the "path of least resistance" is inflating the debt away, which devalues cash and favors hard assets.

## Debt, War, and the Dollar

The US is facing a "twin deficit" problem, with both fiscal and trade deficits running at double-digit levels. Costa notes that the US is growing its economy at 4-5% just to service existing debt, making further rate hikes mathematically untenable. Approximately one-third of the entire US debt needs to be rolled over in the next 12 months, and increasing the cost of capital would inflict severe pain on the Treasury market.

This fiscal reality extends to geopolitical conflicts. Costa argues that the US "cannot afford a war" financially, citing the strain on the Treasury market. The volatility in US Treasuries is now comparable to that of emerging markets, signaling a loss of confidence in the dollar's stability as a global reserve currency.

"The treasury market is what holds the key for the dollar," Costa states. "If we start seeing this level of volatility persist, it's going to be a problem very quickly."

He predicts a weaker US dollar in the medium term, driven by the need to improve the trade balance and the compounding effect of national debt. This depreciation will likely benefit commodities priced in dollars.

## The Case for Gold and Silver

Despite gold rallying to unprecedented highs above $5,000 per ounce earlier this year before a 25% correction, Costa views the pullback as a buying opportunity rather than a topping signal. He argues that the fundamental thesis for gold remains intact, driven by sovereign accumulation and the need for a monetary anchor amid rising global debt.

Costa points out that on an inflation-adjusted basis, gold has not yet retraced its 1980 top, indicating significant room for growth. He believes central banks are in a structural accumulation phase, recognizing gold's utility and historical role as currency.

"Gold is a wonderful asset to own for the next five to 10 years," he says. "It's going to be volatile... but I want to make sure I have my arms around the best ways to express that view."

Silver is also viewed as extremely attractive, potentially offering higher upside due to its oversold status relative to gold. Costa sees the current correction as an "accumulation phase" for investors willing to step in during volatility.

## Copper: The New Safe Haven?

Copper has recently diverged from gold, trading more closely with equities. Costa attributes this to a shift in copper's market dynamics from a cyclical economic barometer to an asset driven by severe supply-demand imbalances. Structural demand from data centers, onshoring, and industrial capacity, combined with constrained supply, is fueling this divergence.

He agrees with mining financier Robert Friedland’s assertion that "copper is the new safe haven." Unlike previous cycles where copper led the downside in metals markets, its resilience suggests a broader bull market for commodities. Costa believes copper is in a price discovery phase and expects an explosive move to the upside once it breaks through key resistance levels.

## Latin America: The Next Frontier for Capital

Costa is increasingly bullish on Latin America, citing strong currency performance, political shifts toward capitalism, and abundant natural resources. He notes that Latin American currencies, such as those of Peru, Mexico, and Brazil, have outperformed many developed market currencies over the last five years.

The region is becoming a strategic partner for the US in securing critical minerals. Costa highlights the diversity within Latin America, advising investors to pick and choose based on positive directional changes rather than perfection. He suggests that the region will attract institutional capital as the gap between US and global valuations narrows, and as leadership in markets rotates.

"Latin America is going to benefit tremendously from the trend that would move back into the commodity space," Costa explains. He points to indices like Brazil’s Ibovespa, which delivered massive returns during the previous commodity supercycle in the early 2000s.

## Mining Stocks: A Generational Opportunity

Gold and silver mining stocks have suffered significant declines, with the GDX ETF down roughly 35% year-to-date and major producers like Agnico Eagle down over 40%. Costa views this sell-off as a massive opportunity to acquire high-quality businesses at discounted valuations.

He notes that the price-to-net-asset-value ratios for senior miners are at levels not seen when gold was trading below $3,000. Despite the drop in share prices, the free cash flow and reserves of these companies remain robust. Costa is actively accumulating positions in senior miners and high-quality developers, believing that the market is overreacting to short-term fears about rate hikes and war.

"The lower it goes, the more I add to my position," he says, emphasizing the generational value of these assets.

## Conclusion

Tavi Costa’s outlook for the end of 2026 is defined by a skepticism of fiat stability and a conviction in hard assets. With the US unable to afford tighter monetary policy or prolonged conflict, inflation remains the likely tool for debt management. Investors should rotate out of concentrated tech positions and cash, and instead focus on gold, silver, copper, and select emerging market opportunities in Latin America. As Costa prepares to launch a new mining-focused fund, his strategy is clear: buy quality during volatility and position for a world where debt drives the value of real assets higher.
