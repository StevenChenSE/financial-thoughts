The geopolitical landscape has shifted dramatically following the recent military strikes on Iran, sending shockwaves through global financial markets. In the wake of this volatility, **Clem Chambers**, a seasoned market analyst and founder of the new FN, returned to discuss the unfolding crisis, his recent exit from the market, and his predictions for the next 30 days.

Chambers, who recently was appointed by the British government as the next **Chancellor of the Exchequer** (a role he humorously noted ruined his April 1st joke), provides a stark assessment: the world is entering a period of prolonged uncertainty.

> "So, we're going to have a mess for up to a year or longer."

This sentiment follows his accurate prediction made on **February 27th**—a day before the US and Israel struck Iran—that the conflict would escalate rapidly.

### The Signal in the Noise: Why Chambers Exited

On February 27th, Chambers had outlined his logic for an imminent attack. He noted that while money wasn't the primary concern at that stage, the real question was whether Iran would sensibly agree to limit its nuclear program to re-enter the global community.

> "So it's not a question of money. Certainly not at this level of of um date. Yeah. It's about whether they're going to actually do it and whether Iran's sensible enough to say we won't build a nuclear weapon. We'll make sure that you can check that we're not and we're all reenter the the the global community. And if they had any sense, they do that. But you notice the problem with that statement if they've got any sense. But, you know, it can't it can't happen because obviously, you know, that would be too sensible and too straightforward and too easy and everybody would be, you know, so much better off. So, probably the bombs will drop any minute."

His prediction proved correct. When President Trump announced a 10-day ultimatum for Iran to surrender, Chambers saw a classic sign of market manipulation. He recalled his previous analysis:

> "So, anyway, no, I I am out. I got out on the Friday because I was just when when President Trump says, "Oh, no. We'll give you another 10 days to surrender and I just went, "No, no. Brain crash. What? Don't know. Ah, help me. I think I need to be in cash. I just let this one pass.""

Chambers explained his methodology, which relied heavily on price action rather than just headlines. He highlighted the adage: **"Gold is for war. Bitcoin is for flight."**

He observed that as nervousness mounted, Bitcoin surged vertically days before the news broke.

> "And when people are nervous or something bad is about to happen, something wicked this way come, it goes up. And a couple of days before, out of nowhere, Bitcoin went vertical. And that and for me that was a signal that people in the no were in the no knew."

He reasoned that if Bitcoin moved up *before* the news, it indicated insider trading or near-siders anticipating the event. Conversely, when the news broke, the initial reaction was a price drop, suggesting the market was already pricing in the worst-case scenario.

> "And as we know now there's so many people inside trading this stuff or near nearsider trading or three doors down trading this stuff that the signals are in the price before. So actually what happened today is after the news. So that's probably a good signal that it's not real. So if things start to move and there's no news, then you know something's about to happen."

### Market Dynamics: A "Mess" or a Rebound?

With the dust settling after the initial shock, markets have shown mixed signals. Over the last 30 days, **gold, stocks, and Bitcoin have all fallen**, despite the outbreak of war. However, as of **March 31st**, there was a notable rebound:
*   **S&P 500:** Up 2.5%
*   **Gold:** Up 3%
*   **Bitcoin:** Up 2%

This movement coincided with reports of Iran talking about negotiations, creating a "good news" scenario. Yet, Chambers remains cautious about jumping back in immediately.

> "Well, okay, So, there's there's a very very important technical point that you won't read about in books. It's from Clim's Book of Mystery Stock Market Analysis. Okay. So, trends, we all like trends. Yeah. And trends that go up, go float, float, float, float, float. Correct. Float float float float float float. Correct. Bare markets go float float float downwards. Float float float downwards. Spike, float, float, float, float, spike. It's the opposite waveform. So in a bear, in a crash, you expect violent rises. So I won't feel comfortable until it's floating up. If it's spiking up, you know, a bull trap or whatever you want to call it. Maybe, maybe. I I just feel it will take a miracle to sort out this mess in short."

Chambers emphasizes that in a bear market, violent rises are often "bull traps." He believes a true resolution requires a "miracle" or a clear de-escalation, which hasn't materialized yet.

> "And then what? I mean, what a mess that will be. Yeah. Or they stick around and and make a further mess. Yeah. And And what's the mic going to do with boots on the ground? And it it's just it's and it's interesting because my Substack and my YouTube channel, I've got a lot of people when I said I'm out, which I said on on Friday, as soon as I was out, I posted the video and and they all went, "Yeah, yeah, we've been out for days. We've been out for weeks." A lot of my my people that follow me have are long gone. So, I think a lot of private investors, a lot of retailers, it's called in America, have gone."

### The Strait of Hormuz and the 51% Probability

A central issue is the **Strait of Hormuz**. Iran has demanded a toll for ships passing through, citing sovereignty as a condition for ending the war. This has created a standoff:
*   **Saudi Arabia and Oman** border the strait and are protesting.
*   **Global Impact:** No nation can afford to pay the ransom, which starts at **$2 million per ship** and could rise to **$5 million or $10 million**.

A prediction market, **Koshi**, offers a 51% chance that the strait will reopen by **July** (less than 3 months from now).

Chambers agrees the situation is volatile, comparing it to the Ukraine conflict but noting the fragility of the region.

> "And I do see and I I I do think and I have thought all along that America will just go right, you know, we've we've shown them and we're going to go away now and if they don't behave, I'll come back and do it again. And that's a pretty pretty interesting strategy. It's a new one and quite quite clever. But what it does to um you know everything else is another matter because you are in this totally volatile um situation where nobody knows who's coming and going and and what happens next? Are we going to get Cuba next week? Yeah. And are we going to get um Taiwan next year? I mean it's all the the entropy is high. The randomness is high. Therefore the volatility is high. Therefore the range of outcomes is massively expanded. So, you go from an investing situation into a trading situation."

He estimates the cleanup time:
*   **9 months** if the situation starts sorting out immediately upon disengagement.
*   **18 months** if all the destroyed infrastructure needs to be rebuilt.

> "So we're going to have a mess for up to a year or longer. Well, it's Yeah, I think that's fair because if America disengages, it's a year and if America goes boots in, then it's going to be a year. So, it's it's just Well, what does that what's the clever guy said? The the um the difficult um part of a 4-day war is the first two years."
