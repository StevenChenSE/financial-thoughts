## Navigating a Shifting Global Order with Peter Grandich

In a candid discussion with David Linn, Peter Grandich, founder of Grandich & Co., outlines a bearish outlook for US equities and the dollar while highlighting significant opportunities in metals, particularly copper. Grandich argues that geopolitical missteps, a draining strategic petroleum reserve, and a growing wealth divide are accelerating the end of the petrodollar era and US global dominance.

## The Geopolitical Landscape: Iran and the Petrodollar

Grandich assesses the current US-Iran situation as a "net negative" for the United States. He contends that the conflict has not been resolved in a manner conducive to US interests, leading Gulf nations to lose faith in US protection. This shift marks "the beginning of the end of the petrodollar," as these nations look elsewhere for security and trade partners. Grandich notes that China is poised to emerge significantly ahead in this new world order, while the US strategic petroleum reserve is dangerously depleted, leaving the country vulnerable to future supply shocks.

## Consumer Stress and the Wealth Divide

On the domestic front, Grandich observes severe consumer stress. He highlights a stark "K-shaped" economy where the top 1% own more assets than the entire middle class. While the elite remain insulated from rising costs, the average American faces wages that fail to keep pace with inflation. This growing disparity, combined with the alienation of key allies through aggressive trade policies, poses a significant challenge for the current administration and contributes to a loss of confidence in US economic leadership.

## Federal Reserve, Japan, and Liquidity Risks

Regarding monetary policy, Grandich believes the Federal Reserve faces a difficult choice. While a rate hike is possible, he warns that failing to act could damage the bond market more than raising rates. Crucially, he points to Japan as a major risk factor. The weakening yen and the Bank of Japan's potential rate hikes could trigger a global liquidity crisis as the "yen carry trade" unwinds. Grandich argues that rising US interest rates alone cannot offset this global capital flight, especially given the US debt burden and the ongoing de-dollarization efforts by BRICS nations.

## The AI Bubble and Market Outlook

Grandich expresses concern over the AI sector, comparing current dynamics to the dot-com bubble, particularly noting Nvidia's lending practices to buyers. He does not predict an immediate 50% market crash but warns of a prolonged period of underperformance for US equities. He advises avoiding US stocks, noting that a sudden crash would trigger a liquidity crisis that harms all assets, including gold. Instead, he anticipates a "rolling over" of the market as political pressures mount post-Labor Day.

## The Case for Metals: Gold and Copper

Despite fears that higher interest rates hurt gold, Grandich points to historical precedents from the 1970s and early 2000s where gold rallied alongside rising rates. He attributes gold's strength to global re-monization efforts led by China rather than just inflation. His primary focus, however, is copper. Grandich describes a massive supply-demand imbalance, noting that six tier-one copper deposits need to come online annually to meet demand from electrification and AI, yet few are emerging. He cites political instability in major producing regions like Chile and the DRC as further constraints.

## Conclusion: A Changing World Empire

Grandich concludes that the US is losing its status as the sole superpower, a transition that will continue regardless of election outcomes. He advises diversifying away from US-centric assets and embracing the shift toward a multipolar world. With Canada and other nations seeking new trade partners, including China, the era of US economic hegemony is waning. For investors, Grandich recommends focusing on tangible assets like copper and gold, which offer better capital appreciation potential than technology stocks in this evolving landscape.
