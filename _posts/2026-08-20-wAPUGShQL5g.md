## Introduction: A Global Yield Shock

On August 18, 2026, the financial landscape is defined by a synchronized surge in bond yields across major economies. The US 30-year Treasury yield has hit a 19-year high, Japanese yields have soared to a 30-year peak, and German yields are at an 11-year high. This "global bond rout" has triggered a broad correction in risk assets, including the S&P 500, gold, and silver. However, veteran commodity investor Lobo Tiggre, appearing on *Capital Cosm* with host Danny, offers a nuanced perspective on how these macroeconomic shifts impact precious metals and mining stocks.

## The Impact of Rising Yields on Risk Assets

Tiggre notes that rising yields represent a "direct frontal assault on risk assets." When the risk-free rate approaches the returns available in the equity market, passive investors may withdraw capital, creating a self-fulfilling prophecy of selling. This dynamic is particularly relevant for junior mining companies, which rely on equity markets to raise capital. However, Tiggre argues that the direct impact of higher borrowing costs is less significant than the operational costs, such as rising oil and diesel prices for mining equipment.

## The "Bond Vigilantes" and Monetary Debasement

The resurgence of "bond vigilantes" signals that governments can no longer easily "grow their way out" of debt problems. This dynamic points toward continued economic weakness, inflation, and monetary debasement. For investors, this environment is historically bullish for assets that governments cannot print, such as gold, silver, real estate, and other commodities. Tiggre emphasizes that while the near-term price action may be volatile due to war rhetoric and market psychology, the long-term fundamentals—driven by multi-trillion dollar deficits and central bank buying—remain extremely strong.

## Debunking the "Gold Doesn't Pay Interest" Myth

A common argument against holding gold during high-interest periods is that it does not yield interest. Tiggre dismisses this as a flawed comparison. Gold is money and should be compared to other forms of money, not to bonds. Cash in a bank account loses purchasing power to inflation, whereas gold retains its value. Furthermore, historical data from the 1940s through the 1980s shows that rising interest rates often coincided with rising gold prices, as investors moved out of bonds into hard assets.

## Gold as a Leading Indicator of Inflation

Tiggre highlights that gold often "front-runs" inflation and volatility. In the post-COVID environment, gold surged before the full impact of inflation was felt, signaling to savvy investors that monetary policy was becoming highly inflationary. The recent surge of gold to over $5,000 earlier in the year is viewed not as a peak to be mourned, but as a warning sign of the economic conditions to come. The correlation between gold and CPI is not always immediate, but over time, gold serves as a reliable hedge against currency debasement.

## The Current Market: Corrections and Opportunities

While Tiggre remains fundamentally bullish on gold and silver, he has taken profits on mining stocks, a move that has drawn criticism. He clarifies that he remains a "hardcore stacker" of bullion but is cautious as a speculator. He is currently looking for opportunities to "buy low" rather than chasing vertical price movements. Among commodities, he is particularly interested in uranium. The spot price of uranium has fallen below the long-term contract price, a historical setup that often precedes a significant upward move.

## Uranium: The Picks and Shovels of the Energy Transition

Tiggre distinguishes between the AI-driven hype and the fundamental need for nuclear energy. The global nuclear buildout, particularly in China which plans to overtake the US in nuclear power generation by 2040, creates a sustained demand for uranium. If the AI bubble bursts, uranium stocks may experience a short-term dip, but this would present an opportunity to buy fundamentally strong assets at a discount.

## Conclusion: Patience and Optionality

For investors holding cash while waiting for the next opportunity, Tiggre suggests using options strategies, such as selling puts on stocks they already want to own, to offset the "optionality fee" of inflation. The takeaway is clear: while the bond market is creating near-term turbulence, the long-term trajectory of monetary debasement supports a bullish case for hard assets. Patience, discipline, and a focus on fundamental value remain the keys to navigating this volatile market environment.
