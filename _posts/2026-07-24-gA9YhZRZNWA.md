Veteran trader and founder of the Crowded Market Report, Jason Shapiro, returns to discuss the current state of global markets. In this detailed analysis, Shapiro debunks persistent "bubble" narratives surrounding technology stocks, examines the shifting sentiment in Bitcoin and gold, and identifies the most overcrowded trades in currency and commodities.

## The Semiconductor "Bubble" Myth

The conversation begins with the recent sell-off in semiconductor stocks, particularly in South Korea, where retail investors faced significant margin calls. Shapiro argues that while the Korean market was indeed overcrowded and speculative, the broader narrative that the entire AI and tech sector is a bubble is flawed.

Shapiro emphasizes that calling these assets a bubble is often a coping mechanism for those who missed out on what he considers the greatest technological investment opportunity in modern history. He points out that despite recent drops of 30-40% in some stocks, these assets remain up thousands of percent from their lows. While he acknowledges that the market is currently in a trading range and not ideal for aggressive buying, he firmly rejects the bearish "bubble" thesis, labeling it as recency bias and a desire for popularity among commentators.

## Bitcoin: The Power of Indifference

Shapiro draws a parallel between the current sentiment in Bitcoin and the market conditions that often precede a turn. He notes that interest in Bitcoin among retail investors and content creators has waned significantly compared to previous years. This lack of enthusiasm, he argues, is a positive sign.

"When everybody gets too bearish, it's going to go up," Shapiro explains. He views the current apathy as a sign that the asset is underappreciated. While he does not trade based on fundamental arguments about fiat currency, he trades based on market action. The fact that Bitcoin is no longer the center of attention suggests that the risk-reward ratio is improving, even if it is not an immediate buy signal.

## Gold and the Breakdown of Narrative

Gold has faced headwinds due to rising interest rates and narratives suggesting central banks, particularly China, are shifting focus to oil reserves. However, Shapiro highlights a critical shift: gold has stopped reacting negatively to higher interest rates.

He identifies this divergence as a potential turning point. When the market stops reacting poorly to the prevailing negative narrative, it signals a change in positioning. Shapiro advises traders to look for these moments where the tape contradicts the news. He does not rely on consolidation patterns alone but waits for the market to confirm that the bearish narrative has lost its power.

## Overcrowded Trades: Currencies and Crude Oil

Shapiro identifies the long dollar trade, as well as short positions in the Canadian and New Zealand dollars, as currently overcrowded. In the commodity space, he points to crude oil as a market heavily influenced by sentiment and positioning rather than just fundamentals.

He recounts how crude oil previously failed to rise during geopolitical tensions because positioning was too crowded on the long side. However, recent data shows traders are currently underweight on crude due to "recency bias" from previous losses. Shapiro suggests this lack of participation could allow oil prices to rise further, potentially impacting interest rates and market dynamics. He advises traders to focus on how the market reacts to news rather than predicting the news itself.

## Trading Philosophy: Risk and Reward

Throughout the discussion, Shapiro emphasizes a disciplined approach to trading. He does not predict market moves but waits for confirmation. His strategy involves identifying overcrowded positions and waiting for the market action to contradict the prevailing narrative.

Key elements of his risk management include:
*   **Confirmation:** Entering trades only when the market confirms the thesis by breaking the prevailing narrative's influence.
*   **Stop Losses:** Defining clear exit points if the market makes new lows, invalidating the turn.
*   **Position Sizing:** Risking a small percentage (70 basis points) of the portfolio per trade to maintain lower volatility.

Shapiro concludes by warning against the allure of "get rich quick" schemes and copy trading. He advocates for education and discipline, urging traders to understand market mechanics and focus on favorable risk-reward scenarios rather than chasing excitement or validation from popular bearish narratives.
