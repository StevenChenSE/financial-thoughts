## Matthew Tuttle Reveals Where Smart Money Is Flowing in 2026

The artificial intelligence revolution continues to reshape markets, but investors need to be more selective than ever. In a recent interview, Matthew Tuttle, founder and CIO of Tuttle Capital Management, shared his contrarian take on navigating the AI trade—arguing that while concerns about a tech bubble are valid, the real opportunity lies in identifying the next wave of bottlenecks rather than chasing last year's winners.

"We're not in a March 2000 scenario," Tuttle stated confidently. "There are meaningful differences between then and now. Are we in a bubble? We might be. Good luck calling the top. But you have to assume that all bubbles eventually come to an end, and the question is how you stay ahead."

## The Memory Trade: AI's Hottest Bottleneck

According to Tuttle, memory has become the most powerful trade in the current market environment. DRAM contract prices have surged approximately 60% in a single quarter, with companies like Micron seeing extraordinary gains—rising from $320 to $1,200, a staggering 275% increase.

"Memory right now is the most powerful bottleneck," Tuttle explained. "You could look at a chart of SanDisk, Kioxia, Samsung—every memory name looks identical because memory prices are skyrocketing. AI needs so much of it."

This performance came despite Google announcing TurboQuant, which promised to reduce memory footprint by up to six to eight times. Rather than tanking the memory trade, this news created a buying opportunity, demonstrating that AI's insatiable demand for memory far outweighs efficiency gains.

However, Tuttle cautions investors to watch position sizing carefully. "We've got memory ETFs in our portfolios at small sizes—one and 2%," he noted. "If we do have a 50% correction in these names, it's not that big a deal."

## The Three AI Bottlenecks Investors Must Understand

Tuttle identifies three critical infrastructure bottlenecks driving AI growth:

**Memory** – The most powerful bottleneck today, essential for AI compute operations

**Photonics** – A data transmission bottleneck that enables faster information flow

**Space** – The physical constraint of where to place data centers

"Until memory ceases to be a bottleneck, that's an area I'm going to want to be in," Tuttle said. His firm currently offers three different ways to play memory through ETFs, plus a 2x leverage option on SanDisk.

## Software Stocks: Proceed with Caution

Not all AI-adjacent sectors merit enthusiasm. Tuttle specifically warned against broad software exposure through indexes like IGV (the software index).

"Before AI, you could make an argument for owning everything," Tuttle explained. "Now, in the age of AI, I want to strip out the companies that are going to get crushed. I'd stay away from IGV. It's not a smart index—it's going to include everything."

The fund manager highlighted companies he believes will survive the AI transition: Microsoft, Palantir, cybersecurity firms, ServiceNow, and Salesforce. He previously traded CrowdStrike successfully, taking profits after substantial gains, but now remains selective.

"You can trade names like ServiceNow or CRM when they get beaten down," Tuttle noted. "Cybersecurity names—we're going to need more of them, not less. But beyond that, I'd be really careful with software."

## Broadcom's Warning: The Entry Problem

Broadcom's recent 14% single-day selloff despite solid earnings highlights what Tuttle calls the "entry problem" in AI stocks.

"Revenue wasn't collapsing. AI demand wasn't disappointing," Tuttle analyzed. "The stock fell because expectations have moved beyond strong and into perfect. The business was fine—the multiple was not."

This signals an end to some of the market froth, Tuttle believes, but emphasizes that Broadcom isn't a bottleneck trade. "It's a massive company that could easily trade at an inflated multiple. There are better opportunities in those bottleneck areas."

## Looking Ahead: The 2027 AI Thesis

Tuttle's strategy for 2027 focuses on "peeling the onion" of AI infrastructure:

- **Substrates** – Essential components underlying AI hardware
- **Inference** – The cost of tokens and processing
- **Infrastructure and energy** – The backbone supporting AI operations

He also advocates for "Halo names"—traditional value companies that are additive to AI rather than threatened by it. "Oil companies, utilities, copper—heavy asset, low obsolescence businesses that AI isn't going to put out of business."

## SpaceX IPO Lessons: Don't Be Exit Liquidity

The SpaceX IPO offered a masterclass in investment discipline. Despite media consensus that the stock was overvalued, early buyers saw double-digit gains in the first two days. However, the stock has since slid from its $220 peak.

"I've been investing since 1981," Tuttle said. "I've never seen anything like SpaceX. But the rules still apply—you don't want to be exit liquidity for the big investors getting in at the IPO price."

Tuttle waited before adding SpaceX exposure to his ETFs (SPCI, UFO, and HALX). "I told people over and over: stay away day one. SpaceX is a must-own stock, but you don't want to be exit liquidity for all the people getting in at the IPO price."

## Tuttle's Top IPO Pick: Anduril Over OpenAI

With major AI IPOs anticipated—including OpenAI and Anthropic—Tuttle revealed his favorite opportunity.

"I'd buy all of them, but my favorite is probably Anduril," he stated. "There's a 6% chance on prediction markets they'll announce an IPO this year, but I'm very excited about this company."

His enthusiasm stems from what he calls the "convergence trade" in defense: traditional defense spending is shifting from big ships, tanks, and planes toward drones, drone killers, lasers, robots, and AI. Anduril sits perfectly at this intersection.

"Anduril is my favorite company there," Tuttle explained. "It's perfect for my UFO ETF, where I own Lockheed, Northrop, and Kratos. I would love Anduril on the name."

## Key Takeaways for Investors

Tuttle's framework centers on several principles:

1. **Focus on bottlenecks** rather than broad AI exposure
2. **Position sizing matters**—keep single positions small
3. **Avoid day-one IPO purchases**—be patient with major listings
4. **Separate software winners from losers**—AI will devastate some legacy companies
5. **Look beyond AI names**—traditional industries providing AI infrastructure may offer better risk-reward

"Everything related to AI was the trade from 2023 to 2025," Tuttle concluded. "Now you want to look at the bottlenecks—memory, photonics, space—and keep peeling the onion. The next bottleneck is always where you want to be."
