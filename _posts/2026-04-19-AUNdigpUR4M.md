**By Capital Kosa | April 17, 2026**

On a day when markets posted new highs and oil collapsed over 11%, Danny sat down with Patrick Kareem of Northstar Bad Charts for a deep dive into the technical landscape. The conversation covered everything from the S&P 500's relentless uptrend to the potential for a historic capital rotation event—and why corn might be the next big thing nobody's talking about.

## The S&P 500: A "Well-Oiled Fine Machine"

The discussion began with the broad market, which continues to grind higher despite geopolitical uncertainty and economic headwinds. Patrick pulled up the quarterly chart, highlighting the 12-quarter and 36-quarter moving averages that have guided price action for years.

"Look at that beautiful bounce," Patrick noted, counting four consecutive quarters of recovery. "Whatever is happening on the shorter time frames, we had a small correction here a few quarters ago and now it's bouncing up. Well, you got to respect the trend, right? It's going up. Whatever the reasons, that trend is just going to keep going up until we lose this line here."

The critical support level sits around 5,300. Patrick outlined his three-stage approach to becoming bearish: first, lose the rising support line (becoming "less bullish"), then lose the red line (becoming "agnostic"), and finally, break below the cloud before getting outright bearish.

"You don't get bearish at tops," he emphasized. "You get bearish after price has gone sideways, broken rising support lines, and then eventually you're going to create some type of neckline. And after once it starts breaking down, then you're bearish, right?"

The S&P's structure—with 11 sectors that rotate leadership—makes shorting it consistently a losing proposition over time. "The S&P is a well-oiled fine machine, right? When a sector doesn't do so well, well, some of the other ones could pick up the slack."

## Capital Rotation: Process, Not Event

The conversation shifted to what many investors are watching: the rotation of capital out of equities and into commodities. Patrick clarified that capital rotation isn't a single event but an ongoing process.

He presented a silver-versus-SPX ratio chart dating back decades, showing how each major rotation event was triggered by stock market weakness—specifically, when the S&P lost a rising support line.

"Capital rotation events are instigated by stock market weakness," Patrick explained. "So until you have that, you don't get the big move in commodities. But the stock market weakness is what the Fed's going to react to, the government's going to react to—money printing, stimulus programs, lowering rates."

Historical examples abound: the late 1960s, the dot-com bubble in 2000, the 2007-2008 financial crisis. Each time, silver and gold eventually outperformed dramatically. Patrick believes we're in the early stages of another cycle.

"We probably had a ninja capital rotation event back in January 2022 when the stock market did a good correction. That kept a floor under the silver price and it also kept the floor under silver's performance versus the stock market. And here we are again right now. We have this rising support line. We bounced on it."

The main capital rotation event everyone anticipates? When the S&P finally loses its current rising support line—potentially triggering a super-CR that could also see silver break out above a 40-45 year descending trend line. "That will be the main one, Danny—the one that everybody's looking for. And it's probably going to be a super CR. It'll be because at the same time, we're probably going to break out above this descending trend line."

## Gold: Historic Stretch but Still Uptrending

Moving to gold, Patrick acknowledged the metal has gotten extended. "We've never been that stretched from the 36-month moving average since the 1970s—and the peak at the end of all this craziness in 1980. Look at that. That's how stretched we were back in the days."

Despite the stretch, gold hasn't lost its 12-month moving average or its rising support line. Patrick called the $5,300 area "the play zone"—a region where price has consolidated historically before resuming higher.

"Often in corrections, the price will drop hard at the beginning. Most of the price correction, a lot of the times happens in the candles immediate to the last green candle going up. But then after that you have the second part of the correction which is the consolidation. And how long will that be?"

His advice for sideline cash: wait for more evidence. "There's still some room here for this to remain uber bullish right above the 12-period moving average."

## The Gold-Silver Ratio: A Stacker's Compass

Patrick uses the gold-silver ratio as a practical allocation tool. When it's declining (silver outperforming), he holds more silver. When it bottoms and moves sideways, he shifts toward a 50/50 split, reserving the option to go 100% gold if the ratio starts climbing again.

"It's telling me as a stacker which one should I hold," he said. "So if that's going down, if that's breaking down, I'm going to hold silver more. If that thing's starting to bottom and goes sideways, then I'm probably going to go in a 50/50 allocation just in case that thing goes back up and then I could reallocate all my silver and be 100% in gold."

Currently, the ratio sits at historically low levels—a function of silver's strong recent performance. Patrick noted a "crazy capitulation wick" in the ratio that most traders have already forgotten, a sign of just how dramatically sentiment can shift.

## Silver: Corrections, Consolidations, and Patience

Silver's monthly chart shows the metal still "uber stretch" from its moving averages, but Patrick isn't calling a top. Instead, he's watching for a resolution above key resistance.

"Look at all these corrections in silver," he said, walking through historical examples. "Run up, huge correction in price, and then look the rally. Everybody got excited here, right? Everybody must have went crazy here. But guess what happened after? Sideways explosion in price."

He pointed to 1979-1980 as an example: a huge rally that failed to break out, followed by a "rug pull" correction. The 2000s saw similar dynamics during the dot-com era and the 2007-2008 financial crisis.

"What you want is that to happen and this—anybody on the sidelines, we're going to be buying like crazy. That's called a continuation breakout once we correct here. So you want the distance from that red line to reset even more, and then eventually we go crazy upwards."

His $93-94 price zone represents the "play zone" on the monthly chart—historically, areas where silver has found buyers and consolidated before higher moves.

## Oil: The Exciting Setup Nobody's Watching

If silver is stretched and fully recognized, oil represents the contrarian play. Patrick laid out his "tree amigos" thesis: gold, silver, and oil track each other over time. While all three are in bull markets, oil remains the least extended.

"What's beautiful about right now is we've discovered—or confirmed—the next breakout line," Patrick noted, pointing to a multi-month consolidation on oil's monthly chart. "I love it because that morphs into existence this breakout line. And now, once you get the breakout, whatever it consolidates, remember again—most of the damage is often done at the beginning."

The yearly chart reveals why Patrick is excited. A massive breakout line has formed over decades, and oil is coiling tightly near it. "If oil breaks out here, it's just going to be bonkers. I don't know. If by the end of the year this could be all the way back up here. It could be above that breakout line."

Currently bouncing from support around $110, Patrick wants a monthly close above that level. "The longer oil takes to break out there, I would be so happy there because I'll show you the yearly chart—the distance from the 36-year moving average is coiled and tied there. My goodness. It's like we're not stretched. Tops in gold and oil happen when you're super stretched. And we are nowhere near that."

## Corn: The Unpopular Opportunity

Perhaps the most contrarian call of the interview: corn futures might be setting up for a significant move, and almost nobody is watching.

"You know what's fun about correction? People don't like corrections," Patrick said with a laugh. "I kind of like the start of a correction because I know that corrections are the building blocks for the next continuation breakout."

He showed a weekly chart of corn futures that had formed a tight coil, trading above key moving averages and the Ichimoku cloud, which is beginning to turn green. "That's the move that I have on the table. We're not quite there yet. Never front-run a possible breakout. But we're close."

The setup is textbook: higher lows forming, with resistance overhead that represents a measured move target potentially hundreds of points higher. Compare this to silver, which is historically over-extended on the same timeframe.

"Corn is like somewhere here," Patrick said, gesturing to a compressed zone on the chart. "If we get a legit breakout, you know..."

He acknowledged the challenge: discussing corn doesn't generate clicks or social media engagement. "If you want to lose viewers or followers, trust me, talk about corn. I tried it on Twitter. I got crickets. If I don't put out an $800 target for gold, nobody cares."

## Fertilizers: Missed the Window

For those interested in the agricultural sector, Patrick suggested fertilizer stocks have already had their run. "They're not doing very well. They're in correction mode. They've already run up everything there. They're tracking oil a little bit. They're already in correction mode there. So it's too late for that."

The entry point came months ago. Now, investors must wait for the next consolidation phase before the risk-reward becomes attractive again.

## Newcastle Coal: Wedge vs. Horizontal Lines

Patrick briefly touched on Newcastle coal futures (NCF1), which has formed what appears to be a falling wedge from 2022 highs. While technically bullish, he emphasized the limitations of diagonal trend lines.

"People see these big patterns and they think it's like that there. No, it's not," he cautioned. "The ultimate target is probably that, but that's what I call the wake-up line. Because of the steepness of it, you're going to lose many months and years. This thing could go sideways, right?"

Horizontal lines matter more because they require price to make new highs—eliminating the ambiguity of diagonal resistance. "These crazy breakouts, they often—the crazy moves in the shortest amount of time—they happen after a horizontal line breakout, and it's not there yet."

## The Contrarian's Philosophy

Throughout the interview, Patrick emphasized patience over momentum-chasing. His process centers on identifying low-risk, high-reward entries at key support levels, then waiting for confirmed breakouts before committing capital.

"My job is to spot the low-risk, high-reward entries. After that, I'm not here to pump it. I'm just moving on," he said. "We spotted the breakout in silver back in the days. It hadn't broken out here back in January 2025. I think it broke out like a few weeks after this, a few months. And Danny, man, I was super excited. You get the breakout, I'm okay, breakout's done. I'm happy with that. But then as the price gets away from my breakout, I'm just naturally less interested. My job is done, guys."

The most actionable opportunities exist where nobody is looking: coiled patterns, oversold conditions, breakout lines approaching resolution. Once an asset has already run, it becomes less interesting from a risk-reward standpoint—even if the trend remains intact.

"That's why corn is exciting right now. Silver is popular but stretched. Corn is unpopular but coiled."
