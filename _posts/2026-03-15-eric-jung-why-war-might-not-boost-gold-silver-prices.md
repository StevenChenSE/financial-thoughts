*March 13, 2026* — In a world where geopolitical conflict often sends investors rushing toward gold and silver, analyst Eric Jung presents a counterintuitive case: the ongoing war in the Middle East may not trigger the precious metals rally many expect. In a recent interview on Capital with host Danny, Jung outlined a multi-phase framework explaining why metals could face headwinds before any sustained rally materializes.

## The Current Reality: A Grinding War

Jung began by grounding the discussion in observable facts. “The war is dragging on,” he noted, suggesting that initial hopes for a quick resolution have faded. Despite U.S. and Israeli successes in degrading Iranian air and naval capabilities, retaliatory strikes continue. At the same time, political rhetoric around withdrawal contrasts with ongoing military action, creating uncertainty.

A critical flaw in mainstream analysis, according to Jung, is the assumption that the Strait of Hormuz would be completely closed, halting oil exports. “In reality, we’re seeing that Iran is actually shipping a lot of oil to China,” he said, and possibly to Russia and India. Satellite imagery and Chinese confirmations support this. Instead of a total shutdown, Jung estimates oil flow may be reduced to 25–50% of pre-war levels.

## Phase One: The Dash for Dollars

This partial oil flow sets in motion Jung’s first phase. Countries dependent on importing that remaining oil must scramble for U.S. dollars to pay for it, as oil is still priced in dollars. “The bigger participants… might try to sell a lot of their assets for U.S. dollars,” Jung explained. This includes selling gold, silver, and bonds. This theory aligns with recent market data: the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) hitting 100 and rising bond yields indicate a sell-off to raise dollars.

“So, everything including bonds, right? Including bonds to get US dollars to buy the marginal oil denominated in US dollars,” Jung said. “We’re in the middle of that what I call phase one.”

## Phase Two: A Shift to Gold

If the conflict settles into a grinding stalemate with continued partial oil flow, phase two begins. Here, oil-exporting Gulf states—having witnessed limited U.S. military protection—may start diversifying their dollar proceeds into physical gold.

“The petro dollar deal is that… when the poo poo hits the fan, you got to protect us,” Jung stated. “Apparently… the US tried but like it’s not really successful.” This erosion of confidence could lead these nations to convert a portion of their dollar inflows into gold, potentially driving its price higher. Silver and base metals like copper could also see demand here due to a separate, critical supply-chain issue.

## The Sulfuric Acid Wildcard

Jung highlighted a less-discussed consequence: a looming sulfuric acid shortage. Most of the world’s sulfuric acid is a byproduct of refining sour crude oil from the Middle East. Reduced oil flow means less sulfuric acid, which is essential for extracting base metals like copper, zinc, and uranium from ore.

“If that… yield is cut in half… the silver production by non-primary silver producers… also reduce,” Jung explained. This could benefit primary silver miners. In this phase, with enough acid and existing copper stockpiles, industrial demand for silver in solar panels, EVs, and weapons could actually ramp up as nations race to build critical infrastructure.

## Phase Three: The Industrial Crash

The scenario turns bearish for silver if the conflict worsens severely, slashing oil exports to below 20%. A “severe supply crunch of the sulfuric acid” would cripple base metals refinement. After depleting global copper stockpiles—which might last only months—industrial production would crash.

“Then it might it might crash the price of silver because if you can’t make solar panels or like EVs because you don’t have the copper then you don’t need the silver even if the silver is available,” Jung cautioned. He argued many analysts jump from phase one directly to this severe outcome, missing the intermediate demand ramp-up.

## Phase Four: A New Equilibrium

Eventually, supply chains would reconfigure, perhaps bypassing the Strait via rail. Russia might increase production, and a new equilibrium would emerge. In this final phase, Jung believes gold and silver would “accelerate their rise in prices” as the world fully grasps the diminished role of the U.S. dollar.

“US dollars may simply become a trade token like a hot potato. Once they get the US dollar, they convert it into physical gold, physical silver, and other precious commodities.”

## Base Case vs. Bull Case

When asked for his base case, Jung pointed to the stalemate continuing. “What I just told the audience is probably the base case of what’s going to happen.” The bull case would be a swift end to the war, but even then, “the US loses credibility… and then countries just diversify more into gold and silver.”

For now, the data fits his thesis: gold around $5,080, silver near $82 (down 3%), and oil (Brent) capped around $100. “It’s happening right now as we speak,” he observed.

## Personal Strategy: Hold, Don’t Trade

Given the extreme uncertainty, where rumors—like those of Benjamin Netanyahu’s status—can swing markets, Jung’s personal strategy is straightforward. “I’m holding on to all my gold and silver… I think it’s extremely difficult to trade this stuff… It’s pure gambling at this point.”

He isn’t buying more himself but suggests that investors with minimal exposure could consider dollar-cost averaging. “Who’s to say this is not the bottom? Nobody knows.”
