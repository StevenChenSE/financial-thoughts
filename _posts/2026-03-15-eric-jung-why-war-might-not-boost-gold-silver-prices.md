*March 13, 2026 – Capital Cosm Interview*

In a recent interview on *Capital Cosm*, metals analyst Eric Jung presented a counterintuitive and layered thesis: the ongoing war in Iran and the Middle East may not lead to the surge in gold and silver prices that many investors expect. Instead, Jung argues, the conflict’s complex second- and third-order effects on global supply chains could initially suppress precious metals before potentially driving them higher in later stages.

## The Current Phase: A Rush for Dollars, Not Gold

Jung observes that the conflict has settled into a grinding stalemate, contrary to initial U.S. objectives for a quick resolution. Despite daily exchanges of fire, a critical assumption held by many analysts is proving false: the Strait of Hormuz is **not closed**.

“Iran is actually shipping a lot of oil to China,” Jung notes, “and apparently they are now shipping stuff to Russia and also to India.” Satellite imagery from Chinese sources appears to confirm that oil flow continues, albeit at a reduced capacity—perhaps 25-50% of pre-war levels.

This reality triggers what Jung calls **Phase One**. Major oil-importing nations are compelled to sell dollar-denominated assets—including gold, silver, and bonds—to raise U.S. dollars. Why? To purchase whatever marginal oil is still exiting the strait, which remains priced in dollars.

“Sell US dollar assets… get US dollars, and buy as much of the marginal oil denominated in US dollars,” Jung explains. This dynamic, he suggests, is evidenced by a strong U.S. Dollar Index (DXY near 100) and rising bond yields, as these assets are liquidated. Consequently, gold and silver face selling pressure despite the geopolitical turmoil.

## Phase Two: A Shift to Physical Gold

If the war continues as a protracted stalemate with sustained, reduced oil flow, Jung envisions a **Phase Two**. Here, petrodollar dynamics begin to fracture.

“The petro dollar deal is that… when the poo poo hits the fan, you got to protect us,” Jung states. With the U.S. military seemingly struggling to protect even Israel—having reportedly moved missile defenses from Gulf states—the security guarantee underpinning the dollar’s dominance weakens.

Gulf states receiving dollar payments for their oil may then begin diversifying a portion of those proceeds into **physical gold**. This could create a new source of demand, driving gold prices higher. Silver and base metals like copper could also see a ramp-up in this phase due to a related, critical supply chain issue.

## The Sulfuric Acid Squeeze: A Hidden Catalyst

Jung highlights a seldom-discussed vulnerability: **sulfuric acid**. Most of the world’s sulfuric acid is a byproduct of refining sour crude oil from the Middle East. With oil flows halved, sulfuric acid production is also slashed.

This matters immensely for industrial metals. “Sulfuric acid is a very important agent that… metals refiners, mostly base metals… use to extract the metals out of the ore,” Jung says. Copper, zinc, and uranium refining all depend on it.

In Phase Two, with sulfuric acid still available but scarce, and with large above-ground copper stockpiles to draw from, Jung foresees a “race” to produce critical goods like solar panels, EVs, and weapons. This would support silver prices, as silver is primarily a byproduct of copper and zinc mining. Primary silver miners could particularly benefit.

## Phase Three: The Industrial Collapse Scenario

The analysis takes a darker turn in **Phase Three**, a scenario Jung admits is “five or six degrees of separation” out. If the conflict worsens and oil flow drops below 20%, a “severe supply crunch of the sulfuric acid” would occur.

Base metal extraction and refinement would then be in “serious trouble.” Once global copper stockpiles are depleted in a matter of months, industrial production could crash. Paradoxically, this could **crash the price of silver**.

“If you can't make solar panels or like EVs because you don't have the copper,” Jung reasons, “then you don't need the silver even if the silver is available.”

## The Long-Term Equilibrium: Phase Four

Eventually, Jung believes supply chains will reconfigure, bypassing the Strait of Hormuz via rail or other routes. Russia might increase production to gain market share. In this new equilibrium (**Phase Four**), the loss of U.S. credibility becomes cemented.

“The world realizes that Uncle Sam can't really protect them. So why would they hold US dollars?” Jung posits. The dollar may become a mere “trade token,” instantly converted into physical gold, silver, and commodities, accelerating their rise.

## Investment Implications: Hold, Don’t Trade

Given this unpredictable, multi-phase outlook, Jung’s personal strategy is straightforward: **hold**.

“I'm holding on to all my gold and silver… I think it's extremely difficult to trade this stuff,” he says. The news flow is too chaotic, with rumors—like those of Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu’s status—swinging sentiment wildly. For those under-allocated, he suggests dollar-cost averaging, as identifying a precise bottom is impossible.

His base case? The stalemate continues, and his outlined phased scenario plays out. A quick end to the war (a bull case) would still damage U.S. credibility and support metals, but he views this as less likely.

“My thesis… is happening in real time. It's happening right now as we speak,” Jung concludes, pointing to gold trading near $5,080 and silver at $82, both down amid oil prices (Brent ~$100) that have been capped but not collapsed.
