*March 13, 2026 –* In a recent interview on Capital, market analyst Eric Jung presented a counterintuitive and layered thesis on why the ongoing war in Iran and the Middle East may not lead to the surge in gold and silver prices that many investors expect. Instead, Jung argues, the metals complex is caught in a web of second- and third-order effects stemming from the conflict, with prices potentially moving in unexpected and non-linear phases.

### The Current Reality: A Grinding War & Oil Flows

Jung immediately challenged a common assumption. "A lot of experts out there… assume that the oil coming out from the Straits of Hormuz… is going to be zero," he stated. "But that's not what's happening in reality."

Satellite imagery and reports confirm that Iran continues shipping oil to China, Russia, and India. While volumes may be reduced—perhaps 25-50% of pre-war levels—the vital passage is not closed. This reality underpins Jung’s entire multi-phase model.

**Phase One: The Dollar Scramble**
With oil still flowing but constrained, Jung posits that major oil-importing nations are currently in "phase one": a scramble for U.S. dollars.
> "The bigger participants… might try to sell a lot of their assets for US dollars… get the US dollars and you buy whatever marginal oil that is still out there… that is denominated in US dollars."

This explains the recent strength in the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY), which hit 100, and selling pressure on dollar-denominated assets—including gold, silver, and bonds—as entities liquidate to secure hard currency for essential oil purchases.

**Phase Two: The Gold Diversification**
If the war settles into a grinding stalemate with a reduced but persistent oil flow, "phase two" begins. Here, the petrodollar system itself comes under pressure.
> "The petro dollar deal is that… when the poo poo hits the fan, you got to protect us. Uncle Sam has to protect us… Apparently that's… not really successful."

With U.S. military credibility questioned, Jung believes oil-producing Gulf states will begin diversifying their dollar proceeds into physical gold. This could drive a "physical gold price ramp up," alongside silver and base metals like copper.

This phase also introduces a critical industrial variable: **sulfuric acid**. Most of the world’s sulfuric acid is a byproduct of refining sour crude oil from the Middle East. Reduced oil flow means reduced sulfuric acid supply—a crucial agent for extracting base metals like copper and zinc from ore.

With sulfuric acid still available but declining, Jung foresees a "rush" for copper, zinc, and uranium. This could benefit silver, as it is largely a byproduct of base metal mining. Reduced base metal output could constrain silver supply, potentially favoring primary silver producers.

**Phase Three: The Industrial Collapse**
The scenario turns bearish for industrial metals if the conflict worsens drastically, cutting oil flow to perhaps 10-20%.
> "Then you have a severe supply crunch of the sulfuric acid and then your… base metals refinement is in serious trouble… Extraction and refinement in serious trouble."

Nations would burn through above-ground copper stockpiles for months. Once exhausted, industrial production could crash.
> "Then it might… crash the price of silver because if you can't make solar panels or like EVs because you don't have the copper, then you don't need the silver even if the silver is available."

Jung criticizes analysts who jump from "stage one right away to stage three," missing this potential intermediate collapse in industrial demand.

**Phase Four: The New Equilibrium**
Eventually, supply chains would reconfigure—bypassing the Strait via rail, increasing Russian production, and localizing within new spheres of influence. Industrial demand would return as sulfuric acid supply chains are rebuilt.

In this final stage, Jung believes the loss of faith in U.S. security guarantees would be cemented.
> "Gold and silver would… accelerate their rise in prices… the world realizes that Uncle Sam can't really protect them. So why would they hold US dollars?… US dollars may simply become a trade token… Once they get the US dollar, they convert it into physical gold, physical silver."

### Base Case vs. Bull Case

For Jung, the "bull case" is a swift end to the war, but this still damages U.S. credibility and fuels diversification into metals. His base case, however, is the prolonged stalemate he outlined.
> "It's already happening. I mean, look at gold is at… 5,080 right now. Silver… 82 bucks down 3%... They managed to cap [oil]… I think my thesis… is happening in real time."

Given the extreme uncertainty—where rumors, like those of Benjamin Netanyahu's status, can swing markets—Jung’s personal strategy is to hold.
> "I'm holding on to all my gold and silver… I think it's extremely difficult to trade this stuff… It's pure gambling at this point."

For those under-allocated, he suggests dollar-cost averaging, as pinpointing a bottom is impossible.

### Conclusion: A Complex Web of Effects

Eric Jung’s analysis moves beyond simplistic "war = inflation = higher gold" narratives. He paints a picture where immediate dollar strength pressures metals, a potential intermediate industrial collapse could crush silver demand, and only in a later reordering of global trust would precious metals find their lasting bid. For investors, the key takeaway is the profound complexity of modern conflict's impact on interconnected commodity markets, where a disruption in oil refining can ultimately dictate the price of a solar panel—and the silver inside it.
