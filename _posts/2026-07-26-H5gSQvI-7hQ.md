## A Catastrophic Shift in Global Energy Markets

As of July 24, 2026, the global energy landscape has shifted from concern to catastrophe. In a recent interview on *Capital Cause*, host Danny spoke with Bob Morardi, publisher of 321Gold.com, about the rapid deterioration of oil and natural gas supplies. Morardi argues that the world has crossed a "red line," with oil prices surging 35% in just two weeks due to the collapse of key supply chains.

## The Breakdown of Oil Supply Chains

Previously, several factors kept oil prices suppressed, including exports from Russia, Iran, and Saudi Arabia, as well as high storage levels in the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) and Cushing, Oklahoma. However, these buffers have evaporated. Morardi highlights that Cushing and the SPR are now dry. Furthermore, Kazakhstan has eliminated exports, and Russia has halted its daily export of one million barrels of diesel.

Geopolitical tensions have further crippled supply routes. The Red Sea is effectively closed to Saudi Arabian oil following Houthi actions, and the Strait of Hormuz is totally closed. With these critical arteries severed, shortages are manifesting rapidly. Morardi notes that the market has transitioned from having ten factors keeping prices low to none, leading to immediate and critical shortages.

## The Looming Natural Gas Disaster in Europe

While oil dominates headlines, Morardi warns that natural gas presents an even more immediate disaster, particularly for Europe. Unlike oil, there were no significant headwinds to keep natural gas prices down. European storage levels, which are typically filled from March to November for winter use, are at historic lows. Morardi predicts an absolute catastrophe for Europe this fall, noting that it will take years for production to return to pre-crisis levels.

## Broader Resource Shortages and Food Security

The crisis extends beyond fossil fuels. Morardi points to severe supply decreases in sulfur (down 35%) and helium (down 50%). Urea, a critical fertilizer ingredient, is in short supply. In the United States, a combination of severe drought and skyrocketing fertilizer costs threatens to dramatically reduce food production by August or September.

Morardi draws a historical parallel to the French Revolution of 1789, which was sparked by wheat shortages. He warns that if gasoline reaches $8–$10 per gallon, or if food stamps are cut, the United States could face a revolution. He emphasizes that population growth is directly linked to energy production; lowering energy production inevitably lowers population capacity.
