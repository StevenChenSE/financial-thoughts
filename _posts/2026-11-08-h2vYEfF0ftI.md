The financial markets are experiencing a period of extreme volatility, marked by a sharp correction in technology stocks and a corresponding shift in capital toward safer assets. In a recent interview on *Capital Coffee*, market analyst Gareth Soloway provided a critical perspective on the current state of the global economy, emphasizing that gold buyers should pay close attention to emerging trends. The discussion centered on the potential popping of the AI bubble, the implications for bond yields, and why precious metals are beginning to behave normally again after a period of correlation with risk assets.

## The AI Trade Wobble and Market Volatility

Following the U.S. midterm elections in November 2026, the markets witnessed significant turbulence. After an initial surge, the S&P 500 suffered a remarkable one-day decline of over 4%, while the Nasdaq dropped by 6%. Soloway noted that such dramatic single-day losses are rare, occurring perhaps once a decade, and bear resemblance to the fear seen during the March 2020 COVID-19 collapse.

This sell-off was driven by simmering concerns over the AI trade. Major tech companies like Meta and Google have become cash flow negative due to massive debt accumulation and capital expenditure (capex) on data centers. The "straw that broke the camel's back" appeared to be growing skepticism about the ease of building future infrastructure, leading to a collapse in semiconductor names such as Micron, SanDisk, and Seagate Technologies. This panic caused the VIX (fear index) to spike, signaling retail investor capitulation—a phenomenon Soloway often associates with short-term bottoms.

## Capital Rotation: From Tech to Safety

A key theme in Soloway’s analysis is the rotation of capital. For an extended period, money was overwhelmingly funneled into AI and technology stocks. However, as fears mount over debt levels and the sustainability of the AI narrative, investors are moving funds into "old-school" safe havens like Nike, which has seen a resurgence despite poor earnings reports.

Crucially, this rotation is extending into precious metals. For months, gold behaved more like a risk asset than a safe haven, falling when the Nasdaq fell. This correlation made Soloway uncomfortable, as gold should theoretically rise or hold steady during market stress due to concerns about debt, money supply printing, and de-dollarization. Recently, however, gold began to decouple from tech. When tech bounced recently, gold dipped slightly, indicating that weak hands who treated gold like a meme stock have been flushed out. This normalization of behavior is a bullish signal for long-term gold holders.

## The Bond Market and Interest Rate Outlook

The bond market has also seen shifts, with the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield pulling back to around 4.859%. Soloway suggests that while there may be one final rate hike by the Federal Reserve in December, the era of aggressive tightening is likely over. He predicts that the Fed will begin cutting rates in 2027, contingent on oil prices remaining below $90 per barrel and the economy slowing down.

However, Soloway warns of a new reality: higher structural rates during recessions compared to previous cycles. With U.S. debt surging past $40 trillion (up from $20 trillion in 2017), the long end of the yield curve is gaining control over monetary policy. Even if the Fed cuts short-term rates, long-term yields may remain elevated due to a lack of demand for U.S. Treasuries and inflationary pressures. This dynamic poses a significant risk for investors heavily allocated to bonds in their IRAs, particularly those aged 40 and older who traditionally hold 20-60% in fixed income.

## Commodities: The Next Opportunity

Beyond gold and silver, Soloway highlighted the potential in broader commodities, specifically grains like wheat and corn. After significant pullbacks from recent breakouts, these assets present attractive entry points. He pointed to ETFs such as Teucrium’s CORN (for corn) and WEAT (for wheat) as accessible vehicles for retail investors who wish to avoid futures contracts.

The logic is rooted in recessionary behavior: during economic hardship, consumers shift toward cheaper food staples like pasta and cornmeal. This creates a durable demand undertone that supports grain prices even in downturns. Additionally, silver remains a focal point, though Soloway expressed caution due to its industrial sensitivity, noting it could test lower levels around $45 before establishing a definitive bottom.

## Bitcoin and Liquidity Proxies

Bitcoin continues to serve as a proxy for market liquidity. Despite recent pullbacks, the cryptocurrency has held key support at $75,500 and formed a higher high on the daily chart, suggesting a potential cycle bottom. Soloway remains bullish on Bitcoin as a store of digital capital against fiat debasement, with a willingness to buy if prices dip to $67,000. He views the current phase as the start of a new period in the four-year cycle, albeit one that may have started earlier than historical norms.

## Conclusion: Navigating Fear with Data

Soloway’s overarching message is one of caution mixed with opportunity. The AI trade, while not dead, has entered a corrective phase where quality names are becoming attractive for technical bounces. However, investors must recognize the emotional pitfalls of fear and greed. By relying on data rather than hype, and by diversifying into assets like gold, commodities, and potentially Bitcoin, individuals can protect their portfolios against the impending risks of recession and debt-driven inflation. As the U.S. debt continues to spiral, the role of hard assets as insurance becomes increasingly vital.
