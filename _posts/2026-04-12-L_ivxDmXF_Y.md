- $552,000 at 2% inflation

**"I think we have inflation greater than 5%,"** I say. **"So, $1 million over 30 years at an inflation rate greater than 5%—say 6, 7, 8, 9, 10%—you are losing a ton of purchasing power."**

## The Mortgage Paradox: Why You Might Want to Keep That Low Rate

Here's a perspective most financial advisors won't share: if you locked in a 2.75% mortgage when rates were low, you might actually benefit from *not* paying it off early.

**"Think of it this way. I bought a house and I have a 2.75% mortgage. If $1 million in 30 years, think of that as like a mortgage, at 5% is only worth $231,000, then hypothetically, I don't want to pay off my mortgage as soon as possible if you have a low interest rate."**

The logic is elegant: **"You want to throw it out as far as you possibly can into the future because you'll be paying it back with less valuable dollars."**

Andy explains the mechanism: **"As long as your mortgage rate is lower than the inflation rate, you're paying the mortgage back in more depreciated currency."**

This is where most people fail to do the math. **"And that's what a lot of people—they don't take that into consideration. They only look at the today: 'Do I pay more today to buy or do I pay less if I rent?' But if you're paying a house back with depreciating currency over a long period of time, your actual cost may be lower than what you think."**

The hypothetical example: **"Let's just say you take out a $100,000 mortgage, and if it's over a 30-year time frame and you pay it back, you may pay it back with 60 or 50 or 40,000 dollars in purchasing power currency."**

**"That's what people need to understand—is that net present value of the mortgage. And when you take that mortgage out, do you think inflation is going to increase or decrease?"**

The uncomfortable conclusion: **"They made us all into speculators. That's what they did."**

### The Distinction That Matters: Money Printing vs. Supply Shocks

Andy interjects with what he considers a critical distinction: **"What drove this back here in 2022 was a bunch of money printing. Money printing coming into the system, aka inflation, puts everything into a shortage. That's what inflation is. It's money being printed faster than the supply of goods and services."**

A supply shock from oil is fundamentally different: **"So, the money is in the system, but you get a shock because there's a gap in oil. So, it's a weird situation because in order for that to be in balance, you have to take currency out of the system somehow. If you want to match up GDP and money in the system."**

The reality, he argues, is less comforting: **"But they're not going to do that. So, you're going to get increasing prices of things that are in a shortage and then other things, whatever's in the system, will have to go down in price. Money's going to have to flow to where the problems are."**

## Fundamentals Over Forecasts: Warren Buffett's Wisdom Applied

Andy reveals his investment philosophy with a Warren Buffett quote: **"Fundamentals over forecasts. I make no attempt to forecast the general market. My efforts are devoted to finding undervalued securities."**

This mirrors his own approach: **"That's exactly what I do on the website. I don't try to make forecasts. And people ask me all the time, 'Well, where do you think... what is the price objective? What's... where's this going to go in the next month?' I don't know, guys."**

The harder truth: **"What you do is you buy undervalued securities, and then you wait for them to get repriced. What you are asking is, 'When are other people going to buy my stock?' That's what you're ultimately asking me. 'What day are they going to buy my stock?' Nobody knows that. Come on, get serious."**

The better approach: **"The better thing is, what is undervalued in relationship to history? That's what the ratios are. You find what sectors are down, and you buy the undervalued securities from a fundamentally fundamental perspective. Then you wait for the market conditions to change and people to come in and revalue those assets."**

**"The market prediction stuff is dumb. It doesn't work that well, if at all. Doing what I'm doing, it's worked out quite well."**
