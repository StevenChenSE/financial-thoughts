The current volatility in the stock market is not the end of the AI boom, but rather a necessary "shakeout" within an overheated sector. According to Felix Zulauf, founder of Zulof Consulting and a respected macro strategist, investors are currently experiencing a transition from complacency to fear. While a medium-term bottom may be forming now, Zulauf warns that the true danger lies ahead: a potential 50% correction in the NASDAQ in 2027, triggered by extreme market concentration and a black swan event.

## The Current Tech Correction: A Normal Shakeout

Recent data from Bank of America’s Global Fund Manager Survey indicates the largest one-month drop in tech overweight positioning since 2006. This liquidation reflects growing concerns over capital expenditures (capex) and slowing free cash flow among hyperscalers. However, Zulauf argues that this is a standard correction within a long-term technological boom.

"We have gone from complacency to fear... What we are experiencing with the stocks in the stock market is just a major shakeout," Zulauf explains. He views the AI technology and data center investment boom as here to stay, but notes that such booms always involve overinvestment followed by price corrections.

Zulauf’s near-term targets for the current correction phase include:
*   **S&P 500:** A bottom in the range of 6,900 to 7,200 (ballpark 7,100).
*   **NASDAQ 100:** A level below 26,000.

He believes sentiment has improved as fear sets in, which is often a precursor to a market bottom. "I think we are going to see over the next few weeks a medium-term bottom put in place for a rally again in Q1 and perhaps even into Q2," he states.

## The 2027 Scenario: A Higher High Before the Crash

While the current decline is manageable, Zulauf’s primary warning concerns the setup for 2027. He anticipates a new high later in 2026 or early 2027, likely unconfirmed by technical indicators, which will create the perfect conditions for a severe panic sell-off.

The core vulnerability lies in market concentration. "Because we are so much concentrated in the market cap... that 10 stocks make 40% of the market capitalization," Zulauf notes. In such an environment, a shock can lead to disproportionate declines:
*   **S&P 500:** Potential 30% correction.
*   **NASDAQ:** Potential 50% correction.

Zulauf does not predict the specific trigger—a "black swan"—but suggests it could be an AI accident, a systemic cyber failure, or a major corporate collapse. He draws parallels to the 1998 Long-Term Capital Management crisis, where a sudden event triggered a rapid, painful unwind. "I think we will have a short-term panic type of slump," he says.

## Economic and Geopolitical Headwinds

The potential crash in 2027 is supported by broader structural weaknesses in the global economy. Zulauf highlights several key factors:

### Demographic Decline
For the first time in centuries, the working-age population (under 64) in major economies like the OECD, China, Brazil, and Russia is shrinking. With population growth negative, economic growth relies heavily on productivity gains, which are currently insufficient to offset demographic headwinds. "You cannot grow much because population growth plus productivity growth is economic growth," Zulauf argues.

### Global Fragmentation
The world is transitioning from a unipolar order to a "multipolar disorder." This shift brings increased tariffs, protectionism, and geopolitical conflicts. Zulauf points to recent cyber incidents affecting banks, Apple Pay, and Microsoft as potential components of a "dirty war" linked to this power struggle. The U.S. faces challenges in maintaining hegemony against China and Russia, leading to instability that markets are not yet fully pricing in.

### Corporate Overreach
Hyperscalers are running out of free cash flow to fund AI infrastructure, forcing them into debt and equity issuance. This has alarmed shareholders, who are demanding a slowdown in capex. While earnings may remain strong in the first half of next year, Zulauf expects problems to emerge in late 2027 and 2028 as new semiconductor supply comes online and prices fall, squeezing margins that are currently unsustainably high (60-70%).

## Bonds and Commodities: A Short-Term Trade

After calling for higher bond yields in early 2025, Zulauf is now reversing his stance for the short term. He expects a temporary decline in bond yields (a rally in bond prices) over the next 12 months due to several factors:
*   **Slowing Credit Demand:** As the investment boom cools, demand for credit will decrease.
*   **Lower Oil Prices:** Zulauf forecasts oil dropping to around $60/barrel as geopolitical tensions stabilize and China’s commodity demand weakens.
*   **Central Bank Intervention:** A stock market crash would likely force central banks into quantitative easing (QE), buying government bonds and steepening the yield curve.

However, Zulauf emphasizes this is a "trade," not a long-term investment. He views the secular trend for inflation and bond yields as still upward due to fiscal mismanagement and debt accumulation. "This is the first counter-trend move in a secular uptrend... But it's just a trade for 12 months."

## Portfolio Strategy: Gold, Shorts, and Bonds

For investors preparing for the potential 2027 downturn, Zulauf recommends a defensive but opportunistic approach:

1.  **Gold:** He remains bullish on gold long-term. After a correction phase targeting prices below $4,000 (specifically $3,800–$4,000), he sees buying opportunities in physical gold and gold mining stocks as real yields turn down.
2.  **Short Positions:** For experienced traders, Zulauf suggests preparing for short positions in equities once the market rallies to new highs and momentum fades. "The short side will be very good... it pays back very fast."
3.  **Bonds:** He plans to go long on U.S. Treasuries (10-year), German Bunds, and Japanese Government Bonds (JGBs) in the coming weeks to capitalize on the anticipated yield decline.

Zulauf’s outlook suggests a volatile rollercoaster: a rally to new highs in 2026/early 2027, followed by a sharp panic decline, and then a recovery driven by massive stimulus in 2028-2029. Investors should watch for signs of exhaustion in tech stocks—when bullish news no longer drives prices higher—as the signal to exit long positions and prepare for the downturn.
