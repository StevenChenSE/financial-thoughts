## Why the recent gold surge above $4,300 may be a bear market bounce, not a bull trend confirmation.

Gold recently posted one of its largest daily gains in months, climbing above $4,300 an ounce as Treasury yields eased, the US dollar weakened, and optimism grew around a potential Middle East solution. While the rally has sparked excitement among precious metals investors, technical expert Chris Vermeulen of The Technical Traders warns that this move may be a trap. Speaking with Darrell Thomas at the Vancouver Resource Investment Conference, Vermeulen explained that despite the strong price action, the technical structure of gold, silver, and miners remains bearish.

### Market Rotation Away from Big Tech

Before examining the metals, Vermeulen provided context on the broader stock market. He noted that while the S&P 500 has been trading sideways, big tech and AI stocks have pulled back significantly, dropping around 11-12% over the past couple of months. However, money has not been leaving the equity market; instead, it has rotated into equal-weighted indices like the RSP.

Vermeulen highlighted the divergence between the cap-weighted S&P 500, the tech-heavy QQQ, and the equal-weighted S&P 500 (RSP). While big tech’s decline has dragged down the broader index, the equal-weighted market has been moving up. This rotation suggests that investors are growing nervous about the volatility and uncertainty surrounding AI stocks and are seeking opportunities elsewhere.

### Intermarket Analysis and Cycle Lows

Vermeulen uses a three-dimensional intermarket analysis framework that combines price, time, and sentiment to identify market health. He pointed to recent "standout lows" in the stock market indices, where multiple time cycles, panic selling, and defensive money flows converged. These clusters typically signal short-term bottoms.

Following a recent standout low, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq staged a powerful reversal, with the S&P 500 hitting a profit target for his strategy. Vermeulen believes the market is now in a full-on bull mode and may be entering a euphoric blow-off phase, similar to what was seen in 2021. He expects another significant leg higher, with the Nasdaq showing more potential due to its volatility.

### Gold and Silver: Still in a Downtrend

Turning to precious metals, Vermeulen cautioned that today’s big rally in gold, silver, and miners is likely just a bear market bounce. He emphasized that the long-term moving averages are sloping down, and shorter-term moving averages are below them. Price remains below the key 150-day moving average, which he considers a critical barometer for trend direction.

Gold miners surged nearly 8% in a single day, fueling FOMO buying and short covering. However, Vermeulen described the move as emotional and warned that it could fizzle out quickly. He noted that a series of lower highs and lower lows defines a downtrend, and until that pattern is broken, investors should view this rally as a countertrend move.

### What Investors Should Watch For

For Vermeulen, confirmation of a new uptrend in gold and silver requires more than a single day of gains. He looks for an "impulse move" where price breaks through at least two significant resistance levels. In the case of gold miners (GDX), a break above previous highs would be a bullish signal, potentially allowing the first pullback to be bought.

He also noted that moving averages take time to realign. Until the 20-day moving average crosses above the 50-day, and the broader trend shows higher highs and higher lows, the market remains mixed. In such conditions, Vermeulen advises investors to watch, plan, and avoid jumping in too early.

### Trading vs. Holding: A Clear Distinction

When discussing position management, Vermeulen stressed the importance of distinguishing between trading and long-term holding. He separates his physical gold holdings, which serve as a long-term insurance policy, from his trading positions in precious metals ETFs and stocks. This approach allows him to take profits during rallies without jeopardizing his core position.

For investors already holding positions like Wheaton Precious Metals (WPM), Vermeulen suggested considering partial profit-taking during strong gaps up, especially when the broader trend remains down. He warned against letting short-term trading decisions conflict with long-term investment goals.

### Copper and Bitcoin: Different Stories

Copper, according to Vermeulen, is bucking the trend and showing genuine strength. Supported by AI-driven demand and favorable fundamentals, copper has broken out to new highs. The copper miners chart is forming a massive bull flag, suggesting the potential for a euphoric move. Vermeulen sees copper as primed to pop, with technical targets pointing to a 9-10% upside.

Bitcoin, on the other hand, mirrors the pattern seen in gold and silver. It is currently flagging higher within a broader downtrend, creating a series of bear flags that can lure investors in before rolling over. Vermeulen advises waiting for confirmation of a trend reversal before committing to new positions.

### Treasury Yields and Bond Market Risks

Vermeulen also highlighted a concerning trend in the bond market. The 30-year Treasury yield has remained above 5% for a significant portion of the year, a level last seen before the 2007 financial crisis. Long-term yield charts are pointing toward dramatically higher rates, potentially reaching 8%. If this occurs, it could trigger a financial crisis, with bond prices collapsing and the TLT ETF potentially losing half its value.

He described the current bond market environment as unhealthy, with governments injecting liquidity to avoid short-term pain. While he does not predict an immediate crash, he emphasizes the importance of being aware of these risks.
