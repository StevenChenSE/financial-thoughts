## Kevin Stewart on Deflation Signals, Oil Peaks, and Why He's Staying in Cash

The market dynamics have shifted dramatically from 2025, when everything was reaching new all-time highs. Now in 2025, a trader who describes himself as staying mostly in cash is warning about the next drop for stocks, Bitcoin, and gold. Kevin Stewart, managing partner at stockta.com, brings a data-driven perspective that challenges the prevailing inflation narrative and offers specific levels traders should watch.

### A Market Inflection Has Already Happened

According to Stewart, the S&P 500 topped out earlier in June, while gold and Bitcoin prices both peaked earlier in the year—with Bitcoin topping out late last year. Both assets have fallen dramatically from their highs. This creates a very different tone compared to last year's uptrend environment where everything was reaching new all-time highs.

"This year, not only do we not have that, we have the question of whether or not to buy in now and buy the dip or ride this downward trend even more," Stewart explains. "Perhaps it's not a good idea to catch a falling knife."

The technical picture shows significant damage to momentum structures. Stewart notes that gaps—both to the upside and downside—have been creating unusual volatility patterns. "Some of the momentum was broken," he says. "I think some of the technicals need a little bit of time to reset."

### Deflation Signals Despite Inflation Narratives

Perhaps the most contrarian observation Stewart makes is that his algorithm is picking up deflation signals when the entire world is talking about higher inflation expectations and the Fed discussing a more hawkish stance.

"Our algorithm picked up right before I think it was on Friday when Trump announced the peace deal with Iran a couple weeks ago," Stewart explains. "The strongest signals in our algorithm were UUP—which is the US dollar—and then a deflation ETF that goes by the ticker BNDD."

When his algorithm generates those signals, it typically indicates a shift in market conditions. On the flip side, the weakest signals were crypto—which has been "getting crushed"—oil starting to show weakness, and agricultural commodities. "Anything that was veggie, DBA, hard, COM, all those commodities were doing really poorly from a signal standpoint, and they've continued to trade lower since then," Stewart notes.

This is significant because energy and food are key inputs for inflation calculations. "If you look at a lot of the inputs for inflation, which is energy, food, all those different things, seems like a lot of that's been taken care of already," Stewart observes. The question now becomes whether Fed Chairman Jerome Powell will actually need to raise rates or if he's "doing it to prove he's tough or going to follow the data."

### Oil Has Peaked—Unless Iran Escalates

Oil is trading at just over $70 per barrel WTI and Stewart believes it has peaked for this cycle unless there's a dramatic escalation with Iran. "I think unless we get boots on the ground in Iran, I think oil's peaked for this cycle at least," he says. "I think that'll help with the ten-year. I think the 10-year's peaked."

The trading range for oil is approximately $63 to $90, with the trend level sitting just under $94. "Having the trend above the trading range is really not a great setup as a trader," Stewart notes. His charts show oil making lower lows, and when it does bounce, it's making lower highs.

The Iran peace deal changes the supply dynamics significantly. Stewart explains that Iran will likely want to push as much oil through to capture as much revenue as possible now that sanctions are being loosened. Trump himself reportedly stated during a press conference that the world was four weeks away from running out of oil and he didn't want a global depression on his watch.

### Key Levels to Watch for Stocks

Stewart identifies a critical support level for the S&P 500. While the SPY is currently around 734, he's watching 693 as the key level. "If it breaks below that, I think we could have a bigger sell-off," he warns. That represents approximately 6% downside from current levels.

What makes this particularly interesting is what's happening under the surface. Stewart's algorithm shows Amazon, AVGO, Netflix, and MSTR as four of the five weakest signals in the entire stock market. These stocks make up a significant component of both the QQQ and SPY.

"If you take out the tech stocks, the MAG7 and the AI-related stocks, the S&P is actually flat on the year," the host notes. Stewart agrees and sees the S&P pausing ahead of potential Fed rate hikes.

### The Midterm Election Factor

The 2026 midterm elections create additional market uncertainty. Prediction markets on platforms like Kalshi show a nearly even split—around 39-40% chance Democrats clean sweep both the House and Senate versus approximately 38% chance Democrats take the House while Republicans retain the Senate.

For Republicans to hold the House, Stewart believes they need to keep oil under $85, maintain strong job numbers, and get AI-related layoff headlines out of the news. He also notes Trump's postponement of the Housing Affordability Act vote as significant.

The political calculus extends to Trump's behavior if Democrats win. Stewart offers a provocative prediction: "I could see Trump quitting. Like, I feel like—what do you mean quitting? Just stepping down and being like, 'Hey, JD, take over. I'm done.'"

He elaborates that if Trump loses political support and can't get his proposals through Congress, he may not want to "bash his head against the wall for two more years." Conversely, Stewart theorizes that a split government—with Democrats taking one chamber and Republicans another—could actually slow deficit growth and put less pressure on long-term bond yields.

### Investment Strategy: Staying in Cash

For those wondering whether to buy at current levels, Stewart's answer is clear: not right now. He's still mostly in cash and has been for some time.

"I really want to see if this—I don't want to be pessimistic, but I worry about a possible ramp up of aggression with Iran again or something else derailing the peace deal or the Strait of Hormuz closes," he explains.

His risk management approach is strict. "If I lose my downside is limited and I use my stops, right? So I don't want to lose more than 35% on a trade. Like, I'm not holding something 30, 40, 50% down like some people do."

### Summary

The market faces several crosscurrents heading into the second half of 2025. While deflation signals are emerging in Stewart's algorithm, the Fed continues to talk tough on inflation. Oil appears to have peaked absent Middle East escalation, which could keep inflation expectations contained. The key level to watch for the SPY is 693—a break below that could trigger a more significant sell-off. With midterm elections approaching, political factors will weigh on markets, particularly if a divided government emerges. For now, Stewart is content to wait on the sidelines, viewing cash as the prudent position until more clarity emerges on whether this is a momentary pause or the beginning of a larger correction.
