**By Duneberg, as interviewed on Capital Cosm**

In the high-stakes arena of global energy markets, one truth is often treated as a conspiracy: governments actively manipulate oil prices. As geopolitical tensions flare, this isn't a shadowy operation—it’s explicit policy.

"Of course, they're manipulating the price of oil," states analyst Duneberg. "The US government does not want you to make money going long oil in a time of war. That's like shorting a bank during a financial crisis."

The mechanism is straightforward. During crises, instead of shorting futures, governments release physical barrels from strategic reserves with a clear intent. "They're releasing 172 million barrels a day from the strategic petroleum reserve over the next 120 days with the expressed intent to screw the longs in oil," Duneberg notes. "They're not hiding it. It's a fact."

Why the blunt intervention? Because oil is the lifeblood of modern economies and state power. "The G7 doesn't get together in an emergency meeting when there's a shortage of solar panels," he observes. "Oil is too important to governments around the world to allow a bunch of speculators on Twitter to make life-changing fortunes on the back of the despair of these economies."

### A Market Fractured by War

This manipulation occurs against a backdrop of a Middle East conflict that has shattered the illusion of a single, fungible global oil market. As of late March 2026, prices are telling a fractured story:
*   **WTI Crude:** $96 per barrel
*   **Brent Crude:** ~$110 per barrel (a ~$14 spread)
*   **Omani Crude:** North of $150 per barrel

Duneberg argues these disparities are logical, not evidence of hidden manipulation. "What you're actually buying is a specific quality and quantity of oil for delivery at a certain place by a certain time... Of course oil for prompt delivery in the Middle East is going to be more expensive than oil for prompt delivery in Oklahoma during a period of war in the Middle East."

The war itself has followed a dangerous script. It began with a catastrophic Israeli attack on Iranian gas fields, met with a shocking Iranian counter-punch that struck deca-billion-dollar LNG facilities in Qatar. "19 days into the war... Iran's still able to call its shot and take out $20 billion of advanced oil and gas infrastructure," Duneberg says, questioning official narratives about the degradation of Iran's capabilities.

The world came "precipitously close to worst-case scenario short of nuclear war," he believes. One more major strike could have led to the "complete destruction of all... oil and gas production facilities in the Middle East and a rapid mining of the Strait of Hormuz, which would have effectively closed it for years irreversibly."

### The Strait of Hormuz: Iran's Trump Card

For nearly three weeks, Iran has successfully constrained traffic through the Strait of Hormuz—a chokepoint for roughly a third of the world's seaborne oil. This reality check has exposed a critical miscalculation.

"The fact that Iran has successfully closed the trade of Hormuz for coming on 3 weeks now is proof that Western military planners radically underestimated Iran's potential to do so," Duneberg argues. He dismisses the strategic value of proposed operations like seizing Qeshm (Car) Island. "What's the point of occupying an island on the wrong side of the strait when the strait is fully mined?"

The human and economic toll is mounting. Reports indicate ships are paying multi-million dollar "fines" to Iran for passage. Beyond the cargo, thousands of crew members are stranded. "They're stuck on the wrong side of this strait with limited supplies of water, food... It's a terrible situation."

### The Domino Effect: From Margin Calls to Famine

The disruption is triggering a dangerous chain reaction in global finance. Duneberg breaks it down with three key points from a conversation with veteran oil trader Jack Johnson:

1.  **Oil is driven by credit.**
2.  **There's no such thing as "just a banker."**
3.  **In the long run, all collateral gets sold.**

He explains: "If you're short a million barrels of oil... and Brent spikes to $120, you got to put up margin... In the long run, all collateral gets sold." This includes gold and U.S. Treasuries, as nations and companies scramble for dollars to meet margin calls. "There is no force more powerful in the world of finance than a margin call."

The ultimate consequences could be catastrophic for the most vulnerable. Duneberg sees a "risk of significant pockets of famine," not in rich, self-sufficient nations like those in North America, but in "island nations," parts of Africa, and South Asia. "The poorest nations are going to suffer the most."

### A Leader's Contradictions and an Uncertain Future

Amidst the crisis, leadership rhetoric adds to the volatility. Analyzing a live Truth Social post from former President Donald Trump, Duneberg highlights its contradictions. Trump labeled NATO allies "cowards and paper tigers" for not helping to open the Strait, calling it a "simple military maneuver."

"If it were a quote simple military maneuver," Duneberg asks, "why does Trump need the help of NATO members to do it?... It's classic Trump, which during normal times could be humorous... but in the middle of potentially World War III, it's a bit unsettling."

The market, for now, is clinging to hope. Prices have retreated from their peaks, "because I think the market is pricing in the possibility slash the hope that... we're on the other side of this war." But Duneberg remains doubtful, citing Iran's demonstrated escalation dominance and the unpredictable nature of chaotic systems.

"We have perturbed an inherently chaotic system that normally operates under a facade of linearity," he concludes. "We're operating in a chaotic system here where the facade of linearity has been finally removed."

The ultimate takeaway is that in this high-stakes game, the "invisible hand" of the market is often gripped by the very visible fist of state power. For investors and citizens alike, navigating this landscape requires looking beyond price ticks to the geopolitical forces deliberately shaping them.
