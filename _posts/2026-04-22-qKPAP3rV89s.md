The gold market is sending confusing signals. On one hand, the precious metal has surged dramatically over the past two years, drawing in retail and institutional investors alike. On the other, geopolitical tensions—from the conflict in Iran to the ongoing drama surrounding Federal Reserve leadership—are creating unprecedented volatility, with 5% single-day moves becoming almost routine. Meanwhile, a potential power transition at the Fed promises structural changes that could reshape monetary policy for years to come.

Axel Murk, CIO and President of Merc Investments, manages approximately $4.5 billion in physical gold and mining stocks. In a recent interview, he offered a comprehensive analysis of where precious metals stand, how investors should position themselves, and why the coming Fed leadership change matters far beyond a few basis points on interest rates.

---

## The Current State of Gold Investing

Murk's fund operates with a clear mandate: remain invested in precious metals. This means being fully exposed when crises emerge, rather than hiding in cash. "Investors pay us to be invested," Murk explained. "They don't pay us to be hiding under a rock when there's a crisis somewhere."

Despite gold's tremendous runup, Murk has not been reducing exposure. While he and his wife sold "a tiny, tiny bit" late last year and earlier this year—disclosures he acknowledged publicly—the overwhelming position has been accumulation. "I have been very eagerly invested," he stated. "Indeed, I actually think there's good value in that space despite the tremendous runup we have seen."

The fund can take defensive positions and hedge if necessary, but Murk's core philosophy centers on finding catalysts that extend beyond the metal's price itself—investing in management teams and operational excellence within the mining sector.

### Key Principles for Managing Precious Metals Exposure

- **Volatility is the norm, not the exception**: Investors should not expect to time these markets successfully. Daily swings of 5% have become nearly commonplace.
- **Rebalance during good times**: Murk recommends taking some chips off the table when conditions are favorable, preserving "firing power" for rougher periods.
- **Risk management is subjective**: What professionals call "managing risk," retail investors often call panic. Every investor must determine their own comfort level with elevated volatility profiles.
- **Avoid letting volatility drive decisions**: Markets adjust to shocks; the key is maintaining a disciplined investment framework rather than reacting to short-term movements.

## What Is Gold, Really?

The question seems simple but harbors significant complexity: Is gold a safe-haven asset or a risk-on investment? The answer, according to Murk, changes over time.

Since gold prices became free-floating in the early 1970s, the metal has demonstrated **uncorrelated returns to equities**—not zero correlation, but a morphing relationship that swings between positive and negative correlation depending on circumstances.

### The Cash Competition Framework

Gold's fundamental competitor is cash. This creates an important analytical framework:

- When holding cash preserves purchasing power in the long run, the incentive to hold gold diminishes
- When real interest rates rise, cash becomes relatively more valuable, pressuring gold prices
- When policy responses to economic shocks appear politically untenable, gold tends to perform well

The Iran war offers a recent case study. When hostilities began, bond yields rose but inflation expectations remained stable. The math was straightforward: if real interest rates increased, long-run cash value rose, making gold relatively less attractive. The result was a temporary gold price decline despite the geopolitical crisis.

This is precisely why gold investors "always know why they had gold in the past, never quite know why they have it in the future"—a frustration Murk describes as inherent to the asset class.

### Gold's Response to Policy Reactions

Where gold consistently performs well is during the **policy response to supply shocks**:

- Price controls in the early 1970s
- Direct payments during the pandemic

The pattern is consistent: economically sound responses to supply shocks tend to be political suicide, resulting in policy choices that erode currency purchasing power and drive investors toward gold.

## The Oil-Gold Relationship: Separating Correlation from Causation

Oil and gold operate in fundamentally different markets, though both attract significant investor attention during geopolitical stress.

### Structural Differences

| Asset | Primary Driver | Key Characteristics |
|-------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Gold** | Monetary policy and currency purchasing power | Pure indicator of monetary policy "madness" |
| **Oil** | Supply and demand fundamentals | Immediate urgent use; globally priced; heavily influenced by logistics (Strait of Hormuz) |

Australia's recent efforts illustrate oil's critical nature. The nation secured two ships carrying 100 million liters of diesel—though Murk notes Australia consumes 100 million liters **per day**, highlighting the commodity's indispensable role in modern economies.

### Historical Oil Spikes and Recessions

The data presents a troubling pattern: oil price spikes have historically preceded or coincided with recessions.

- **2007-2008**: Oil peaked in June 2008 before the financial crisis and Lehman collapse
- **1990**: Gulf War oil spike preceded economic disruption
- **1986**: Similar pattern of spike leading to recession

The notable exception was 2020, when pandemic-related demand collapse sent oil to near-zero briefly.

### Why a Recession Hasn't Arrived (Yet)

Murk acknowledges numerous recession "candidates" have failed to materialize. Several factors explain this anomaly:

1. **Federal Reserve micromanagement**: Banks that mismanaged interest rate risks received Fed programs allowing them to "push that can down the road." Repeated bailouts prevent natural market corrections.

2. **Deregulation and growth focus**: The current administration has cut red tape aggressively to boost economic potential.

3. **Productivity optimism**: An incoming Fed chair (Kevin Walsh) believes in a productivity boom from AI investments.

4. **Consumer support**: Tax reforms from last year provide direct checks to lower-income consumers, sustaining spending.

The market appears to price in the Iran situation as a temporary shock. Even in a worst-case scenario where Iran seized the Strait of Hormuz and charged $1-2 per barrel premium, global producers could largely absorb the additional cost given oil's global price-setting mechanism.

## The Federal Reserve Transition: Why It Matters Beyond Interest Rates

By the time this interview airs, Kevin Walsh—nominated to replace Jerome Powell as Fed Chair—will face Senate confirmation hearings. The transition involves more than routine succession; it represents a potential inflection point for monetary policy and market structure.

### The Powell Situation

President Trump has suggested Powell may need to be "found a way to fire" if he doesn't resign by May. The situation is legally murky:

- Powell's term as Fed Chair ends, but his term as a Fed Governor does not
- Congress, not the executive branch, sets currency policy—the Fed's constitutional foundation
- The Supreme Court will likely need to resolve what powers the president actually holds regarding Fed removal

### Why Walsh's Arrival Matters

Murk believes Walsh's succession is urgent, but not for rate adjustment reasons. "The reason why I think it's important for Kevin Walsh to come in sooner rather than later is not because interest rates will be a basis points higher or lower," Murk explained. "But because there is substantial reform that's urgently necessary at the Fed."

### The Fed's Mandate Creep

Murk identifies a core problem: the Federal Reserve has expanded far beyond its original mandate.

**The Fed's legitimate functions** (according to Murk's "purist" view):
- Managing the amount of credit in the economy
- Setting interest rates
- Regulating currency value

**What the Fed should not do**:
- Allocate credit to specific sectors
- Choose "winners and losers"
- Write checks to businesses directly

Yet these boundaries have eroded. When the Fed began purchasing mortgage-backed securities, it effectively validated the mortgage sector as favored. During the pandemic, direct payments to businesses crossed lines that "are absolutely not acceptable," in Murk's assessment.

### Walsh's Reform Credentials

Walsh resigned from the Fed in protest during Bernanke's crisis intervention era, demanding that bailouts and emergency measures wind down immediately. When that didn't happen, he left and became a persistent critic of Fed overreach.

"The Fed has become a debating club in no insignificant part because of the steps that the Fed has undertaken," Murk observed.

Importantly, Powell appears to be positioning Walsh for success. Last December, Powell began discussing a "productivity boom"—Walsh's signature issue—suggesting the current chair recognizes where policy should head. Powell has also acknowledged his failed communication strategy reform attempts, reportedly telling Walsh "good luck with that" in private conversations.

### Market Implications

Despite the political theater, Murk sees limited practical market impact from the transition timing:

- Rate curves have not dramatically adjusted based on daily tweets about Walsh's appointment
- Fed independence remains crucial for efficient monetary policy—second-guessing increases borrowing costs
- Powell has been subtly preparing the institution for Walsh's arrival

The drama, while entertaining, is "a lot of noise." What's important is structural reform, not the precise timing of leadership change.

## Practical Guidance for Precious Metals Investors

For those managing precious metals portfolios or considering entry, Murk's framework offers several actionable principles:

- **Understand gold's dual nature**: It can function as both safe haven and risk asset depending on the macroeconomic context. Don't assume uniform behavior.
- **Focus on policy reactions, not events**: Supply shocks themselves may not move gold prices. Policy responses to those shocks—particularly when politically untenable—typically do.
- **Ignore short-term noise**: Daily volatility reflects market adjustment to new information. Sustained positioning requires patience and conviction.
- **Consider the mining sector**: Beyond physical gold, quality mining companies offer exposure with operational catalysts beyond metal prices.
- **Maintain conviction during frustration**: Gold's historical characteristic—clear rationale in hindsight, uncertainty in real-time—should be expected, not avoided.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Gold accumulation continues**: Despite substantial gains, Murk sees good value in precious metals. Only minimal profit-taking has occurred at the personal and fund level.

2. **Gold's identity shifts**: The metal's correlation with equities is variable, not fixed. It competes with cash, and real interest rate movements determine relative attractiveness.

3. **Policy responses drive gold performance**: Supply shocks don't automatically lift gold prices. Gold rises when policymakers respond in ways that erode currency purchasing power—which tends to happen because sound economic responses are politically unsustainable.

4. **Oil and gold operate independently**: While both are affected by geopolitical events, their fundamental drivers differ substantially. Oil responds to supply-demand dynamics; gold responds to monetary policy credibility.

5. **Fed reform matters more than rate levels**: The likely Kevin Walsh transition represents an opportunity to restore the Fed's original mandate and reduce its role in allocating credit and choosing economic winners and losers.

The intersection of geopolitical instability, potential Fed leadership change, and structural reform creates a complex environment for precious metals investors. Murk's position—fully invested, focused on catalysts beyond price movements, and patient through volatility—offers a template for navigating this landscape.
