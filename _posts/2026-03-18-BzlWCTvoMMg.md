In today’s mining industry, timelines for bringing a project from discovery to production have stretched to lengths that would have seemed unimaginable a few decades ago. As one seasoned expert notes, “the equivalent of a project today—you’d be lucky if that schedule could be done in 15 years.” The challenge, it seems, is not a lack of resources or knowledge. “We just don’t have the will,” they observe, pointing to regulatory, logistical, and procedural hurdles that slow modern development.

This stands in stark contrast to a landmark achievement in Canadian mining history. When the speaker was first introduced to the diamond space in the Arctic, the goal was audacious: to build Canada’s first diamond mine at Akati. The timeline? Just five years.

From **1994 to 1998**, the project moved from the geologist’s resource estimate to a fully permitted mine plan, through construction, into operation, and even to the point of paying itself off. That’s five years total—from raw data to profitable enterprise.

What made this possible? A combination of focused execution, streamlined processes, and a clear sense of urgency. In an era where projects can languish for decades, the Akati diamond mine remains a powerful case study in what can be achieved with determination and efficiency.

It’s a reminder that the resources are there, and the knowledge exists. The difference between a 15-year timeline and a 5-year one may ultimately come down to will.
