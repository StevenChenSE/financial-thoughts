In today’s mining industry, timelines have stretched to lengths that would have been unthinkable a generation ago. As one seasoned project lead reflects, “You’d be lucky if that schedule could be done in 15 years.” The challenge, it seems, is not a lack of resources or knowledge. “We just don’t have the will,” he observes, pointing to a shift in pace and execution that contrasts sharply with past achievements.

That contrast is embodied in a landmark project from the 1990s: the development of Canada’s first diamond mine in the Arctic at Akati. Introduced to the diamond space with this ambitious goal, the team embarked on a timeline that stands today as a case study in speed and efficiency.

From **1994 to 1998**—just five years in total—the project moved from raw geological data to a fully operational, profitable mine. It began when geologists delivered their resource assessment. From there, the process unfolded rapidly: mine planning, permitting, construction, and the start of operations—all capped by the mine paying itself off within that same five-year window.

This achievement highlights more than just logistical skill; it underscores what’s possible with focused determination. In an era where projects often languish for decades, the Akati story is a reminder that with clear vision and relentless drive, even the most ambitious timelines can be met.
