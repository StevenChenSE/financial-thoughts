## A Convergence of Market, Political, and Geopolitical Risks

In a recent interview, Peter Grandich, founder of Peter Grandich & Co., issued a stark warning regarding the current state of the global economy. Grandich argues that a unique convergence of political instability, social unrest, and economic fragility is setting the stage for one of the worst potential market eras since the Great Depression. With the US 10-year Treasury yield hovering near 4.83% and the VIX index indicating underlying volatility, Grandich believes the "perfect storm" is already forming, driven by a loss of global confidence in the US dollar and a potential reversal of the yen carry trade.

## The Yen Carry Trade and the Treasury's Dilemma

A central component of Grandich's thesis is the fragility of the yen carry trade. He highlights recent remarks by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who suggested that the US government possesses "asymmetric information" when intervening in currency markets. Grandich views this not as a sign of strength, but as an admission that the US is on a "lonely island," isolated on the world stage.

Grandich points out that the US has effectively "cheaped" its paper by shifting debt issuance toward short-term bonds to pay off long-term obligations—a strategy he compares to using a credit card to pay off a mortgage. As foreign entities like Japan and China begin selling US securities rather than buying them, the US faces a difficult choice: raise interest rates to attract capital or risk a dramatic sell-off if the Federal Reserve fails to stay ahead of inflation.

## Bond Market Crisis and the End of the Structural Bull Market

Grandich identifies the 5% mark on the 10-year Treasury yield as a "line in the sand." If yields stay above this level for more than a few days, he predicts a "huge bond crisis" that will eventually impact the stock market. This is particularly concerning because the current equity rally has been propelled by the AI craze and a desire for yield in a high-interest-rate environment.

He notes that since the end of 2021, anyone who purchased Treasury bonds has actually lost money, including dividend yield. This marks a historic break from the "structural bull market" for bonds that lasted for decades. Grandich warns that two-thirds of the US financial service industry has never experienced a bear market, leaving both professionals and their clients unprepared for a scenario where both stocks and bonds underperform simultaneously.

## Stagflation, Inflation, and the K-Curve Effect

The economic environment is further complicated by what Grandich describes as stagflation. He argues that 3% inflation in a slow economy is more damaging than 5% inflation in a strong one. With energy costs, particularly for diesel and gas, rising sharply, the cost of living is rising faster than wages. This creates a "K-curve" effect where the top 1% of the population, who own more assets than the entire middle class combined, continue to thrive while the working class struggles to make ends meet.

CNBC survey data cited in the interview supports this view: 63% of Americans are living paycheck to paycheck, and 90% of that group has less than $500 left over each month. This financial stress is fueling a political shift, with voters increasingly open to socialist or socialist policies as a way to address the widening wealth gap.

## The Case for Gold and Commodities

In this environment, Grandich advocates for a shift in investment strategy from capital appreciation to capital preservation. He recommends a focus on gold and metals, noting that gold has doubled the performance of the stock market since his exit from Treasury bonds in 2021. He observes that financial institutions are increasingly allocating a portion of their bond allocations to gold, and that countries in Asia, particularly China, are hoarding physical gold as a hedge against fiat currency instability.

Grandich also highlights the critical importance of critical minerals, pointing to a shortage of materials like tungsten that has left the US Army unable to purchase sufficient supplies for military use. He suggests that the mining sector, currently at its lowest ownership level relative to other sectors, is poised for a significant re-rating as the world recognizes the necessity of these resources.

## Geopolitical Tensions and the US-Canada Trade War

The "perfect storm" is exacerbated by geopolitical tensions, particularly the escalating trade war with Canada. Grandich criticizes the US administration's approach, which he views as a strategic error that has alienated the US's greatest ally. He notes that Canada, despite its own economic challenges, possesses the natural resources to be self-sufficient, and that the current hostility is damaging the long-standing relationship between the two nations.

He also touches on the broader geopolitical landscape, including the ongoing conflict in the Middle East and the potential for the US to be seen as a less reliable global protector. These factors, combined with the domestic political uncertainty surrounding the upcoming midterm elections, create a volatile environment where markets may remain volatile for the foreseeable future.

## Conclusion: Navigating the Uncertain Future

Peter Grandich’s warning serves as a wake-up call for investors and the general public. He emphasizes the importance of personal financial discipline, urging individuals to spend less than they earn and build a "moat" of savings and protective assets. While he acknowledges he is not a "doomsayer," he stresses that the scenarios once considered "crazy talk" by financial experts are now becoming reality. In a world where the US dollar's dominance is being challenged and the traditional 60/40 portfolio is failing, a focus on gold, commodities, and capital preservation is more important than ever.
