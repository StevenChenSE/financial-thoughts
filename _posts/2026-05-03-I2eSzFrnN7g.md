The S&P 500 keeps grinding to new all-time highs. The NASDAQ just pierced 25,000. Oil has climbed above $100 per barrel. By most traditional metrics, this looks like a robust bull market. But Gareth Solowway, chief market strategist and president of Verified Investing, sees something else entirely — and he's not alone in his concern.

"This is a late-stage bull market," Soloway told audiences in a recent interview. "We see certain sectors driving the upside, just like dot-com in 2000. If you looked at all of the stocks universally in that 2000 high, it really started to see a lot of laggards. We had the dot names, the Amazon.coms — those were the ones propelling the market to new all-time highs."

His concern isn't just theoretical. He's actively short the S&P 500 and has laid out specific technical levels that could confirm — or invalidate — his bearish thesis.

## The Illusion of a Universal Bull Market

Behind the headline-grabbing index gains lies a market that's far more divided than it appears.

Consider these divergent realities:

- The S&P 500 and NASDAQ are at all-time highs
- The software sector (IGF expanded tech software ETF) is down approximately 20% year-to-date
- Mega-caps like Meta and Microsoft have struggled while a narrow group of stocks — particularly AI-related names — propels the broader indices

"When we talk about a bull market for the entire market, it doesn't really apply to the entire market," Soloway explained. "It really just applies to the index."

This phenomenon — where a handful of mega-cap names mask weakness elsewhere — is precisely what happened in the dot-com era. The NASDAQ pierced 5,000 in 2000, then topped out shortly after. Today, we're watching the NASDAQ approach 25,000.

## The Real Market Driver: Capex Spending

The catalyst keeping this market afloat isn't economic fundamentals in the traditional sense. It's capital expenditure — specifically, the roughly $700 billion annually being spent by AI and tech mega-caps on data centers, infrastructure, and the AI buildout.

"This is massive stimulus," Soloway noted. "We're seeing that and that's keeping these companies running and it's also keeping up the stock market. But again, just like 2000, I would just caution people that when things topped, those stocks finally came in and it took the whole thing down."

Jerome Powell himself acknowledged at a recent FOMC meeting that data centers and AI capex have been major drivers of the economy. This is a view shared by the Federal Reserve itself.

## The K-Shaped Consumer Reality

The economic picture isn't uniform either. Soloway describes a stark division:

- **70% of Americans** feel like they're in a tough spot — many believe they're already in a recession
- **Upper-income households** with significant market investments are "excited and in loving it and continuing to spend"

This dynamic mirrors the late dot-com era, where market participants were "buying Ferraris" while the broader economy showed cracks. "Once that high-end consumer pulls back a little bit, there's no one else to fill the void and everything comes tumbling down," Soloway warned.

## Federal Reserve Independence in Focus

The recent FOMC meeting revealed the most hawkish dissent in recent memory — four dissenting votes, the highest since 1992. Three of those dissents came from governors who believe the Fed shouldn't be hinting at easing, even while holding rates steady.

For Soloway, this signals something important: pushback against potential political pressure on the Fed, particularly as Kevin Worsh prepares to take the helm.

"The chairman does not get to decide," Soloway emphasized. "It's a voting member that gets to decide. The governors being as hawkish as they were is also sending that signal to Kevin Worsh that, hey, you can't come in here and just say, hey, we're going to cut rates."

Powell himself will remain as a governor, which Soloway views as a market-positive: "The market is craving stability with the Federal Reserve and that independence."

## Inflation: The Long-Term Bug That Won't Die

The spike in oil prices — now entering its third month above $100 — is creating follow-through inflation concerns that Soloway takes seriously.

"Producers are not going to eat this cost constantly," he noted. "Even after all the hikes from 2022, inflation came back to about 2.7%. We didn't get back to 2%. And the issue remains is that as long as the US government is spending like a drunken sailor — with a trillion in additional debt being spent basically every quarter — it's going to put more and more emphasis on printing money to eventually deal with that debt."

His forecast:

- **Short-term spike** in inflation due to oil prices (potentially transitory)
- **Long-term sustainable inflation** between 3-4% even after oil normalizes
- **Midterm pressure** likely to bring oil back to $70 or lower as politicians seek to address energy costs before elections

## Recession Odds: What the Data Shows

Prediction market traders currently place recession odds at just 18% for the year — a number that has actually decreased since geopolitical tensions escalated.

Soloway partially agrees, suggesting a recession is likely pushed to late 2026 or 2027 — but only as long as mega-cap capex spending remains elevated.

"The real kicker is going to be when do these institutions — Meta, Amazon, Google, Microsoft — when do they start pulling back on their spend?" he asked. "Once they go from $200 billion each to $100 billion, that's a massive amount of sucking out of the economy. That's when you'll get that recession."

## The Technical Picture: S&P 500 Parallel Channel Analysis

Soloway uses a technical approach anchored in parallel channel analysis, tracking the S&P 500 from COVID-era lows.

"The low of the market going to every major pullback bounced right along this lower period," he explained while reviewing charts. "The high of 2021 was right up against it. We hit this high here in 2025 and that kind of put in the top for a pullback to the midpoint of the channel."

His framework for determining whether the current breakout is real:

- **Need to stay above current levels for at least one week** to confirm the breakout
- Previous instances show the market dipped below channel support temporarily before eventually breaking out — but also had false breakouts (two closes below, gap down, then close above, then close back below)
- If the market fails to hold and falls back below, "there could be a major top in the making in the S&P and NASDAQ"

The NASDAQ's recent 22% rally in one month — from March lows — has pushed it directly into resistance at the highs of the channel. This mirrors what happened in 2000 right before the peak.

## Trading Strategy: Legging In and Out

Rather than entering positions all at once, Soloway employs a methodical approach:

- **Scale into positions** — typically quarter positions at a time
- **Never go full in with leverage** — "The second you do that, you give control to the markets, and the markets will make you pay"
- **Take partial profits** as trades move in his favor rather than holding everything to the target
- **Leg out of positions** on the way down, keeping "some skin in the game but also being smart about taking some profits"

On hedging with options: "If I'm right 80% of the time, which is about what I'm right, hedging on every one of those 80% takes away from my gain. It doesn't equal out what I would have made on the winners."

His S&P 500 short targets:

- **First target:** Pullback to former resistance (now support) around 5,800-6,000
- **Second target:** Midpoint of the parallel channel
- **Ultimate downside:** Filling the gap down from the recent highs

## Gold: Why It's Behaving Like a Risk Asset

Gold presents a complex picture. Despite record highs earlier in the year, it's now significantly off those levels.

Key technical levels for gold:

- **Resistance:** Upper parallel around 5,000 — a breakout above would signal a return to all-time highs
- **Support:** 3,900 zone — first major support level
- **Deep support:** 3,500 — would require approximately 20% drawdown in NASDAQ to reach

"I'm still bearish on gold near-term," Soloway stated. "But I still think five years from now, gold is much, much higher. Until I see some fiscal responsibility in the government, I'm going to continue to want to buy gold on big pullbacks."

The gold-as-risk-asset dynamic is highlighted by its current correlation with the 10-year Treasury yield — when yields rise, gold falls. This inverse relationship suggests that if bond vigilantes push yields higher (currently hovering near 4.5%), gold faces continued headwinds.

"The bond market is telling us there needs to be a caution flag on this move," Soloway observed. "Most investors are ignoring it."

## Bitcoin: Bear Flag in Play, $50,000 Target

Soloway was previously bullish on Bitcoin but has shifted to neutral — and increasingly bearish.

"Bitcoin is a tricky one, man," he admitted. "Last we talked, I was bullish. I'm now neutral on Bitcoin."

The technical setup shows a classic bear flag pattern:

- Bitcoin has trended higher and held a consolidation pattern
- Now approaching 80,000-85,000 resistance zone where "upside is somewhat limited"
- The broader pattern suggests downside eventually — "down moves followed by sideways chop like this, which slightly inclined, eventually goes to the downside more often than not"

His Bitcoin targets:

- **Consolidation resistance:** 80,000-85,000
- **Breakout level:** Above 85,000 would signal potential move toward 100,000
- **Downside target:** Approximately 50,000 — roughly 37-38% below current levels

The case for further weakness includes:

- Divergence from gold during gold's rally (Bitcoin fell while gold rose)
- Presidential crypto actions that "weren't great for crypto" — multiple rug-pull-like token launches
- No clarity act passing
- Rotation of capital from "boring Bitcoin" into more exciting AI-related plays

On the contrarian argument that low sentiment could signal a bottom: "When the NASDAQ drops 10% or 15%, technically we almost always see Bitcoin go lower. I would love to see a rotation of capital, but until I start to see that, I can't assume it."

## Natural Gas: The One Asset Worth Buying

Despite the generally bearish outlook, Soloway identified one asset he's willing to back with new capital: natural gas.

"It's one of the few assets that I'm willing to step up and put some money behind," he stated.

The thesis:

- Potential breakout above 2.88 level
- Catch-up trade to oil's recent strength
- Data centers need massive energy, nuclear isn't ready yet, and oil is expensive relative to natural gas
- Historically, when oil pulls back, natural gas attracts capital flows

## What Falls First: Stocks or Bitcoin?

When pressed on which asset falls first in a correction:

"I would say the stock market will drop more initially than Bitcoin because Bitcoin already has a lot of money that's exited," Soloway explained. "But I think if the stock market keeps falling, Bitcoin suddenly starts to panic and it'll play catch-up. The stock market is more due."

His honest assessment: he'd rather hold Bitcoin at current levels than broad stock market exposure, given Bitcoin has already discounted significant weakness.

## The NASDAQ Valuation Reality

One chart that crystallizes the bubble concern: the NASDAQ divided by M2 money supply.

"When we do that, you actually have the NASDAQ slightly above the dot-com high when you divide it by M2 money supply," Soloway revealed. "I think that's telling. I think people don't recognize that we've gotten to a place where it is massive in terms of the valuation of the NASDAQ relative to money supply."

## Key Takeaways

1. **This is a late-stage bull market** — narrow leadership masking broad weakness, with the S&P driven by a handful of mega-cap names while sectors like software are down 20% year-to-date.

2. **Capex spending is the glue** — The $700 billion annual AI/data center buildout by mega-caps is functioning as economic stimulus. When this spending slows, expect the economy — and markets — to follow.

3. **Technical levels matter** — The S&P is testing the upper end of a multi-year parallel channel. A failure to hold current levels for a week signals potential major pullback toward the channel midpoint.

4. **Bitcoin and gold are risk assets now** — Both are trading inversely with bonds and lack the safe-haven characteristics investors expect. Bitcoin targets 50,000; gold has support at 3,900.

5. **Natural gas stands out** — It's the one asset where Soloway sees a favorable risk-reward, with potential energy demand from data centers and oil-to-gas rotation providing catalysts.
