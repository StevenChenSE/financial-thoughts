## Ed Yardeni on the resilience of the economy, the role of bond markets, and the future of the AI trade

### Introduction
In a recent interview, economic strategist Ed Yardeni, founder and president of Yardeni Research, discussed the current state of the global economy, bond markets, and the technology sector. Despite fears of an impending recession, Yardeni remains bullish on the "Roaring 2020s" thesis, driven by AI-induced productivity gains, strong capital spending, and resilient consumers. He also provided insights into the return of "bond vigilantes," Federal Reserve policy under Chair Jerome Powell, and the sustainability of current market valuations.

### The Return of Bond Vigilantes
Yardeni, who coined the term "bond vigilantes" in 1983, explained that the bond market is once again signaling its displeasure with fiscal and monetary authorities. Currently, the two-year Treasury yield sits approximately 75 basis points above the Fed funds rate, implying that markets expect the Fed to reverse the rate cuts enacted late last year. 

According to Yardeni, this dynamic mirrors the 1980s when bond investors forced yields higher to counteract inflationary pressures. However, he notes that while yields in the UK and Japan have spiked due to fiscal missteps, the US market is not yet in a crisis. Yardeni argues that bond yields between 4% and 5% are "back to normal," comparable to pre-Great Financial Crisis levels. He believes the economy can sustain these rates, which reflect appropriate capital allocation rather than a looming debt catastrophe.

### Fed Policy and Inflation Expectations
Addressing Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell’s recent decision not to raise rates, Yardeni suggested that the Fed is observing market prices rather than dictating them. Powell emphasized that monetary policy impacts the real economy through these market signals. Yardeni believes inflation is closer to 3% across most components, rather than the Fed’s 2% target, and notes that expected inflation remains anchored between 2.5% and 3% based on market measures like TIPS spreads.

While Powell faces political pressure ahead of midterm elections, Yardeni predicts that the Fed may eventually need to tighten policy slightly to maintain credibility against inflation. He highlights that Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent is actively managing risks, including recent coordinated interventions to support the Japanese yen to prevent a disruptive sell-off of US Treasuries by Japanese investors.

### AI, Capex, and the "Roaring 2020s"
A central pillar of Yardeni’s bullish outlook is the artificial intelligence revolution. He asserts that the massive capital expenditure (capex) by hyperscalers and tech firms is a significant boost to economic growth. Despite concerns about overspending—citing examples like SpaceX investing heavily in AI outside its core rocket business—Yardeni argues that managers currently face demand that outstrips their capacity. 

He compares the current tech landscape to Amazon’s early years: companies are investing heavily now, accepting negative free cash flow in anticipation of future explosive earnings once capacity is built. This productivity boom, combined with labor shortages exacerbated by the retirement of baby boomers, forms the basis of his "Roaring 2020s" scenario. Consequently, he maintains his S&P 500 targets of 8,250 by the end of the year and 10,000 by the end of the decade.

### Earnings Momentum and Market Valuations
Yardeni coined the term "FIMO" (Fabulous Earnings Momentum) to describe the current earnings environment, distinguishing it from "FOMO" (Fear Of Missing Out). He explains that stock prices are a function of Price-to-Earnings (P/E) multiples and Earnings (E). While earnings have been phenomenal, investors initially sold off, compressing P/E multiples and making the market cheaper. 

Recently, investors have returned, recognizing the sustainability of this earnings strength. Yardeni notes that analysts do not lower earnings expectations unless a recession is imminent, and since corporate reports remain strong, the market’s upward trajectory is supported. He advises investors to focus on companies with understandable business models and a history of dividends, while maintaining exposure to sectors likely to benefit from AI, such as financials, industrials, and healthcare.

### Real Wages and Future Outlook
On the topic of inflation and wages, Yardeni points out that real wages are currently stagnant because nominal wage growth is roughly equal to core inflation (~3.3%). However, he argues that historically, real wages increase by over 1% annually, a trend expected to resume once inflationary pressures from tariffs and geopolitical conflicts subside and AI begins driving productivity gains rather than just costs.

In conclusion, Yardeni views the current economic environment as fundamentally resilient. While acknowledging risks such as geopolitical instability and the potential for AI-related volatility, he remains confident that supply and demand dynamics, rather than debt fears, will continue to drive markets. He suggests that while the bond vigilantes are watching, the current yield environment is healthy and sustainable for a growing economy.
