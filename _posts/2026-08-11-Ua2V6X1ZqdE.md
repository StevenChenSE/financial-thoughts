## Frank Giustra and Ian Harris discuss the impending global copper deficit, geopolitical shifts, and the strategic value of Colombia’s Makoa project.

### Introduction

The era of cheap, abundant copper is over. According to two of the mining industry's most respected voices—Frank Giustra, CEO of Fury Gold Mines, and Ian Harris, CEO of Copper Giant—the copper "supercycle" is not a future event, but a present reality. In a wide-ranging discussion, the executives outlined a perfect storm of supply deficits, geopolitical decoupling, and surging demand driven by electrification, AI data centers, and defense spending.

At the center of this macroeconomic shift is Copper Giant’s Makoa project in Colombia. With a newly elected pro-mining government in Bogotá and a recent $31 million strategic financing deal, Makoa is positioning itself as a cornerstone asset in the race for critical minerals.

### The Anatomy of a Supply Cliff

Frank Giustra, traditionally known as a gold expert, has turned his attention to copper, describing it as a commodity standing on the brink of a supply cliff. He highlights several alarming statistics:

*   **Projected Deficits:** Giustra predicts a 35% supply deficit by 2035, translating to approximately 8 million tons. By 2040, this deficit could swell to 10 million tons.
*   **Declining Grades:** Existing mines are aging, with some over a century old. As ore grades decline, extracting copper requires moving more dirt and consuming more energy, raising costs and environmental footprints.
*   **Lack of New Supply:** Despite the urgent need, only about six major copper mines have been built in the last decade. With a typical timeline of 17 to 20 years from discovery to production, the industry is woefully unprepared.
*   **Supply Disruptions:** Political instability and operational issues in key producing regions like the Democratic Republic of Congo (DRC), Peru, and Chile are exacerbating the shortage.

Ian Harris reinforces this view, noting that exploration budgets have been cut drastically for years, leading to a scarcity of new discoveries. "There’s only Kazakhstan and Colombia left" as major jurisdictions with untapped, near-surface potential, Harris notes, citing insights from renowned geophysicist Mark Gibson.

### Geopolitics and the End of Codependence

The global landscape is shifting away from the era of globalization and codependence. China’s dominance in critical mineral processing and its recent restrictions on copper exports have alarmed Western governments. Giustra points out that the era of relying on global supply chains without friction is over.

In response, the United States is taking decisive action. Harris highlights the US government's "Project Vault," a $12 billion initiative to stockpile critical minerals, including copper, with $10 billion in financing from the US Export-Import Bank. This mirrors the Strategic Petroleum Reserve established after the 1973 oil embargo.

"Buy what the government is buying, not what they're telling you to buy," Giustra advises, emphasizing that both governments and corporations are panicking and stockpiling copper, driving a "toilet paper moment" for the metal.

### Colombia: The New Mining Frontier

Amidst this global shortage, Colombia is emerging as a critical player. Giustra predicts that "Colombia is going to become the hottest new mining region in the world" in the next few years. This optimism is fueled by:

1.  **Geological Potential:** Colombia shares similar geology to the prolific copper belts of Peru and Chile but has been significantly underexplored due to historical political issues.
2.  **Infrastructure Advantage:** Unlike remote Andean projects in Argentina or Chile, Colombia has robust infrastructure, including power lines and roads, and a strong local construction industry.
3.  **Political Shift:** The inauguration of a new president in Colombia marks a turning point. The new administration is laser-focused on economic growth and security, adopting a pro-business stance and streamlining regulatory processes.

Ian Harris, who has operated in South America since 1999, emphasizes that the new government is moving quickly to remove bureaucratic obstacles. "They are going to be coming out of the cannon like a rocket," Harris says, noting that mining is now a top priority for the Colombian state.

### The Makoa Project: A Strategic Asset

Copper Giant’s Makoa project is perfectly positioned to capitalize on these tailwinds. Key attributes of the project include:

*   **Scale:** Makoa has surpassed one billion tons in resources, a threshold that attracts major mining companies.
*   **Quality:** It is a near-surface deposit with high-grade breccias, making it easier and cheaper to develop than deep underground mines.
*   **Community Relations:** Learning from past industry mistakes, Giustra and Harris have prioritized building strong relationships with local communities, a critical factor for project success.

### Strategic Financing and Industry Validation

Recently, Copper Giant announced a $31 million strategic financing deal with Daenerius Metals Corp, along with an offtake agreement with global commodity trader Trafigura. This offtake agreement covers 20% of Makoa's future copper and molybdenum concentrate production for 10 years.

These deals serve as powerful validation. Giustra notes that Trafigura’s involvement signals that major players are desperate to secure physical metal. "Trafigura is trying to get their hands on physical metal," Giustra explains. Additionally, Giustra and the new investors have agreed to a two-year lockup on their shares, signaling long-term confidence in the project’s potential.

### Why Investors Should Care

The discussion concludes with a clear message: copper is becoming a "safe haven" asset due to its essential role in modern infrastructure. Whether it’s upgrading the aging US electrical grid, building AI data centers, or manufacturing electric vehicles, the world cannot function without copper.

Giustra and Harris argue that the Makoa project offers a unique opportunity. It combines a world-class asset in a strategically important jurisdiction with a capable management team and strong industry sponsorship. As the copper deficit tightens and geopolitical tensions rise, Makoa represents a tangible solution to a critical global shortage.

Frank Giustra and Ian Harris present a compelling case for a structural copper deficit driven by aging mines, lack of exploration, and surging demand.
