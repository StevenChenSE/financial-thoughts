## A Shift in Market Sentiment
In this August 4, 2026, episode of Capital Cosm, host Danny sits down with guest J. Adrian to dissect the shifting tides in global markets. The conversation centers on signs of a bottom in precious metals, the resilience of oil stocks despite geopolitical conflicts, and a broader rotation away from overvalued US tech giants toward undervalued sectors and international markets.

## Precious Metals: Speculative Money Returns
Adrian and Danny observe increasing momentum in the precious metals complex. Recent data shows platinum up nearly 7.5% and palladium up 7.4%, outperforming gold and silver. More importantly, the junior gold mining index (GDXJ) has been outperforming the major gold index (GDX), while silver miners (SILJ) are outpacing their major counterparts (SIL).

This risk-on behavior suggests individual investors are seeking alpha, moving money back into the sector. Adrian notes that while short-term volatility exists, the "worst news" regarding wars, inflation, and potential rate hikes by the Federal Reserve appears priced in. Inflows into ETFs like GLD and GDX have been increasing over the last month, reinforcing the belief that the market has bottomed.

## Oil and Gas: A Multi-Year Opportunity
Despite ongoing disruptions in the Middle East, oil prices have dropped significantly to $75. Adrian argues this creates a compelling entry point for long-term investors. He explains that while spot prices are driven by immediate supply and demand, the broader market has suffered from extreme underinvestment by producers due to negative sentiment and energy transition narratives.

Adrian recommends a multi-year investment horizon for oil and gas stocks, favoring integrated giants like Exxon and Chevron. He also highlights US E&P companies like Enron, noting that while bargains are scarce, the sector remains fundamentally strong as fossil fuels remain essential for modern industry.

## The S&P 500 and the AI Bubble
The S&P 500 recently hit an all-time high of $7,736, driven by increased liquidity (M2 money supply) and a pause in rate hikes by the Fed. However, Adrian views the current AI rally as a bubble. He points out a significant market rotation: money is moving from expensive big tech growth stocks into value, small caps (Russell 2000), and foreign markets.

This rotation is fueled by steady passive inflows from 401(k) plans, ensuring capital continues to enter the market even as investors rebalance away from overvalued tech names. Adrian suggests this could allow the market to deflate the bubble in an "orderly" fashion.

## Undervalued Gold Miners: A Value Play
A major focus of the discussion is the extreme undervaluation of gold mining stocks. Citing a report by Scotiabank, Adrian notes that the largest 20 mining companies are trading in the lowest quartile of their valuations over the last 50 years.

He uses Agnico Eagle (AIGO) as a prime example:
*   **Price Drop:** The stock fell from a high of ~$260 in February to ~$136 in four months.
*   **Cost Advantage:** With an all-in sustaining cost of $1,459 and gold trading near $4,100, the margin cushion is massive.
*   **Valuation:** The price-to-free-cash-flow ratio is at a historic low of 16, significantly below its 12-month average.

Adrian argues that despite fears of a gold price collapse, these high-quality miners are generating record cash flows, making them excellent value investments.

## Debt Markets and Currency Movements
The bond market is signaling a divergence from equities, with yields on the 10-year and 30-year Treasuries breaking out to new highs. This reflects investor expectations of at least one rate hike by December and concerns about the US dollar's future.

Adrian points out that the US Treasury is actively intervening to support the dollar against the Japanese yen, following BOJ actions. However, the effectiveness of these interventions is waning. As the dollar weakens, foreign investors demand higher yields to compensate for currency risk, further pressuring long-end bond prices.

## Emerging Markets: The New Growth Frontier
Finally, Adrian highlights significant opportunities in non-US markets. Emerging markets are currently breaking out of a multi-decade downtrend against the US. While the broad emerging market index appears weak due to a severe correction in South Korean stocks, excluding Korea reveals that emerging markets are dramatically outperforming developed markets.

Adrian specifically recommends smaller markets like Hong Kong, Singapore, and Brazil. He believes a macro environment of strong commodities, a weak dollar, and higher inflation favors these commodity-exporting nations, offering asymmetric upside compared to the expensive US market.
