## The Illusion of Disinflation and the Reality of Debt

In a recent interview on *Capital Cosm*, economist and author Peter Schiff addressed the current economic landscape, challenging the narrative that inflation is under control. While official Consumer Price Index (CPI) and Producer Price Index (PPI) numbers suggest inflation is running below expectations, Schiff argues these figures are a "fantasy" created by the government to provide cover for its policies. He points to "demand destruction," where consumers are forced to alter their purchasing habits—buying fewer items or switching to cheaper alternatives—not because prices are falling, but because they can no longer afford them.

Schiff highlights several contradictory signals in the market. Despite reports of cooling inflation, oil prices remain volatile, recently spiking 20% in July after a temporary dip caused by a fleeting peace deal. Furthermore, bond yields are rising, with the 30-year Treasury yield hitting 5.12% and the 10-year hovering near 4.6%. Schiff contends that Federal Reserve Chair Jerome Powell’s rhetoric about bringing inflation down to 2% is merely talk without action. The Fed continues to expand its balance sheet and increase the money supply (M2), which Schiff identifies as the true driver of inflation. He notes that M2 growth is accelerating, creating a scenario where asset prices are inflated while consumer purchasing power erodes.

## The AI Bubble and the Collapse of Tech Valuations

A significant portion of the discussion focused on the artificial intelligence (AI) boom. Schiff compares the current AI investment frenzy to the dot-com bubble of the late 1990s. He argues that major tech companies, or "hyperscalers" like Meta and Alphabet, are burning through cash reserves and taking on massive debt to build data centers and purchase GPUs. Schiff challenges the classification of these expenditures as capital investments, noting that the chips have a short lifespan of two to three years, making them more akin to operating costs. He warns that much of this infrastructure will become obsolete quickly, leaving these companies with unsustainable debt burdens and no guarantee of profitability.

Schiff also points to the divergence between the NASDAQ and Bitcoin as a warning sign. While both assets were in a bubble, Bitcoin, which he describes as having no intrinsic value, has crashed 50% from its highs. He predicts that the NASDAQ bubble will eventually pop as well, exacerbated by rising interest rates and the narrowing breadth of the market rally. He cites recent declines in stocks like IBM and SpaceX as early indicators that the tech sector is losing momentum. With national debt at $40 trillion and mortgage debt significantly higher than in 2007, Schiff warns that rising interest rates could trigger a crisis far worse than the 2008 financial collapse.

## Gold and Silver: The True Store of Value

Schiff advocates for gold and silver as the only reliable hedges against government debasement and economic instability. He illustrates this by pricing the S&P 500 and real estate in terms of gold. When adjusted for gold, the stock market is down 66% from its 2000 peak, and single-family home prices are at historic lows. This perspective reveals that while nominal prices in dollars are high, the real value of these assets has plummeted due to the devaluation of the fiat currency.

Regarding silver, Schiff dismisses recent concerns over its drop from $120 to the $50s as a healthy pullback after an explosive, unsustainable rally. He views the long-term chart as bullish, noting that silver remains far from the generational highs seen in 1980. He contrasts the behavior of gold and silver investors with Bitcoin holders, arguing that most gold and silver buyers are still profitable, whereas many recent Bitcoin entrants are facing significant losses. Schiff also criticizes the Trump administration’s promotion of crypto, labeling it a grift that misallocates capital away from productive industries like manufacturing and toward speculative assets like meme coins.

## Housing Market Distortions and Government Failure

The housing market is another area where Schiff identifies government failure. He argues that subsidies such as FHA guarantees, Fannie Mae, Freddie Mac, and mortgage interest deductions have distorted the market, encouraging the construction of oversized "McMansions" rather than affordable entry-level homes. This has led to a shortage of affordable housing and inflated prices that benefit sellers rather than buyers. Schiff warns that when the market corrects, home prices will fall to their true value, causing significant wealth destruction for those who believe their homes are worth millions but cannot sell them at that price.

## Conclusion: Preparing for the Inevitable

Peter Schiff concludes by urging listeners to prepare for a severe economic crisis driven by government and central bank policies. He emphasizes that the root cause of the problem is not capitalism or greedy businessmen, but the expansion of government power and the Federal Reserve’s monetary policies. To mitigate risk, he recommends holding tangible assets like gold and silver, which preserve value regardless of currency debasement. He encourages his audience to spread awareness of these issues to counter mainstream misinformation and to advocate for market-based solutions that reduce government interference in the economy.
