## The Elasticity Paradox: Why Commodity Prices Never Stay Up

The bull market in commodities isn't about prices—it's about elasticity.

That's the central thesis from Mike McLean, senior commodity strategist at Bloomberg Intelligence, who joined the show to deliver a sobering outlook on inflation, oil, gold, and the broader market. His core argument is straightforward yet counterintuitive: when commodities spike, they don't sustain. They collapse.

"Markets are bullish crude oil for a good reason right now, but it's going to accentuate those themes and trends that really kicked in when Russia invaded Ukraine," McLean explains. "Technology replacing crude oil and surplus of supply versus demand in the US and Canada and a whole Western hemisphere to continually just balloon."

The most compelling recent evidence is natural gas. It surged 100% year-over-year, reaching nearly $7 per MMBtu. As of late April, it's back down to $2.50—the same level it traded at at the end of 1999.

"It's the same thing it did in 2022 to '23," McLean notes. "It led everything down in terms of crude oil, corn, and soybeans, and wheat. Everything that pumped went down, and natural gas led the way. And I think it's going to do that again this year."

## The $50 Crude Oil Prediction: A Politically Motivated Collapse

Front-month crude oil was trading around $91-$93 per barrel when McLean recorded the interview. His target? **$50 per barrel** in the December futures contract by midterm elections.

This isn't just a technical call—it's a political one.

"We're going to have the leader of the world's largest energy producer, net exporter of crude oil and the largest exporter of LNG natural gas, who wants prices to be lower, wants energy prices lower, wants inflation lower by the time we get to midterms," McLean says. "I think he has a good ability to do that. Trends are in his favor."

The supply picture supports this thesis. The US and Canada are running approximately 8 million barrels per day of surplus—versus a peak deficit of 12 million barrels per day just before 2008. Every price spike accelerates production, and the administration is highly motivated to bring on more supply.

"The average cost of production in the US is around $55 a barrel," McLean notes. "On the screen, you see 93. You go out to December, you see 77. That's why everything is backwardated."

The backwardation—the phenomenon where futures prices are lower than spot prices—isn't limited to crude. Corn, soybeans, and natural gas all show similar patterns because producers can bring supply online daily and lock in forward prices.

"Crude oil, if it continues higher, it'll break everything, including itself," McLean warns. "It's its own worst enemy."

## The Deflation Thesis: Post-Inflation Deflation Is Coming

McLean has been calling for deflation for years, and he sees 2025 as a critical inflection point.

"Following what's happening in China—1.76 is their 10-year note yield—that's pretty severe deflation," he says. "But what happens typically is when you get inflated markets like this, you get the Fed, we see demand destruction."

The most significant catalyst will be wealth reversion. The stock market is trading at 2.4 times GDP—the highest since 1929 in the US and 1989 in Japan. When this unwinds, it will trigger a cascade across assets.

"We're nowhere near the deflation we're going to get eventually," McLean says. "But this year's a key trigger. And remember, this really much very much happened in 2008."

In July 2008, CPI peaked around 5.6% with WTI near $147. One month's crude oil spike and then everything collapsed. McLean sees parallels this year.

"When you have things like gasoline jump over 50%, unleaded gas, diesel rally almost 75%, that cuts off people's capital budgeting decisions—not just consumers, but their investment decisions—and it makes people pull back."

He points to Europe and much of Asia, where refined product prices are spiking and curbing demand. The US hasn't seen these effects yet in equities, but the signal is clear.

## Gold and Silver: Overdone, Overextended, and Now Risky

Perhaps the most controversial call in the interview is McLean's view on gold and silver.

"Gold is just way stretched, overdone, and now it's a highly volatile spectate risk asset," he says. "If stock market goes down, it's going to go down."

Gold volatility has spiked to its highest since 2009 relative to the S&P 500, and its highest since 2006 relative to a basket of Treasuries. It's trading at 40-year highs versus the Bloomberg Commodity Index.

"That's the key thing I want to remind people," McLean notes. "I was on the sell side of that trade of gold being bullish in 2011. And remember how great it looked in 2013 after it rallied and then hovered around 1500 for like 2 years and then collapsed. We could do that."

His outlook? Gold will likely trade between $3,000 and $6,000 for a decade after this explosive move. The best of the performance is over.

"Most notably silver. Remember what happens? Prices go exponentially. It shifts the whole metric, and everybody will point out the fundamentals. But that's always what happens in peaks."

## The Stock Market Correlation: Everything Flows From Here

McLean's framework is ruthless in its simplicity: **if you're bullish metals, you have to be bullish the stock market.**

"Copper is on my potentially prudent short list because the supply-demand situation has somewhat been alleviated. It wasn't in a deficit. Now it's somewhat surplus," he explains. "The key thing for copper to go up is you need to see demand pull increasing demand out of China. So I put it between the declining bond yield in China, which shows deflationary forces, and the absolute prerequisite of copper goes up—US stock market has to go up."

The same logic applies to Bitcoin. The crypto glory days are over.

"Bitcoin's down 11% on the year. S&P 500 up 32%. On a two-year basis, it's up 21% with the S&P 500 up 43%. Its performance absolutely sucks," McLean says bluntly. "The advent of ETFs and Mr. Trump getting elected—poof. That was the end of the glory days for Bitcoin."

Crypto needs the stock market to go up to stabilize. Without that, it falls harder than equities.

"The whole crypto space is trading like it has unlimited supply," McLean observes. "The Bloomberg Galaxy Crypto Index is basically unchanged since 2021. S&P 500 is up almost 100% since that time period. It has very high volatility—it only goes up when stock market goes up, and when stock market goes down, it goes down a lot more."

His near-term target for Bitcoin is around $50,000, with potential for even lower in a sustained bear market.

## The EV Paradigm Shift: Oil Becomes Whale Oil

When crude hit $90+, McLean's response was clear: "Rest of the world's doing it."

He drives a Chevy Volt he purchased in 2012-2014. Chinese EVs offering three times the range at one-fifth the cost are now available globally. The 100% US tariffs on Chinese EVs are, in his view, a confession of vulnerability.

"It just shows what's happening with the paradigm shift in the world," McLean says. "Look what's happening with automakers in Germany and Japan and the economy in Germany. They are just getting crushed. The whole rest of the world is saying, 'Thank you very much. I'll take some of those BYDs.'"

Crude oil is becoming like whale oil—a commodity technology made obsolete by innovation.

## The End of the Bull Cycle: What Comes Next

"We're in this phase that I think towards the end of a bull cycle," McLean says. "The signals are quite clear, but it's not going to end till it ends."

The parallels to 1929 are instructive: markets can run far longer than logic suggests before collapsing in the fourth quarter.

"Being in Florida, I see what happens—it sucks everybody in. It makes everybody think they're smart. It keeps going up until finally it stops."

His prescription? Overweight Treasuries, with the 5% yield on long bonds being "wonderful." Pick spots for responsive selling and maybe responsive buying—but stay away from risk assets.

"The only thing I really found attractive to buy so far is when long bond gets near 5%."

## Speed Round Predictions

In closing, McLean offered his probabilistic outlook:

- **$10,000 Bitcoin** more likely than $10,000 gold
- **Oil at $60** more likely than $120
- **10-year Treasury at 3.9%** more likely than 4% or 5%
- **Fed funds rate** will move with stock market—down if stocks fall, unchanged if stocks rally
- **Inflation at 2%** by year-end (versus 3.5%)
- **Underweight tech and virtually every risk asset** relative to overweight Treasuries

## Key Takeaways

1. **Oil is heading to $50**: Political motivation, structural surplus, and backwardation in the futures curve all point to a significant decline by midterm elections.

2. **Commodity elasticity is the dominant theme**: When prices spike, supply responds and demand collapses. Natural gas's 65% crash from $7 back to $2.50 is the template, not the exception.

3. **Post-inflation deflation is coming**: China is already in severe deflation. US wealth reversion—when stock market valuations mean-revert from 2.4x GDP—will be the triggering event.

4. **Gold and silver are now risky assets**: At 40-year highs relative to various benchmarks, precious metals are overextended and will fall with equities, not shine as safe havens.

5. **The stock market is everything**: All commodity correlations have converged to the S&P 500. Being bullish copper, gold, or Bitcoin requires being bullish stocks—and the setup suggests caution, not enthusiasm.

The bottom line from Mike McLean: stay in long-duration Treasuries, pick your spots carefully, and remember that in markets, elasticity always wins. Prices spike, supply responds, demand销毁, and the cycle repeats.
