## The Biggest Risk and Opportunity Investors Face Right Now

In what may be one of the most complex market environments in decades, investors are navigating a landscape where extraordinary earnings strength coexists with unprecedented geopolitical instability and mounting credit risks. Jay St., founder of Special Situations Research, recently joined a financial show to dissect the confluence of forces shaping markets today—and his analysis paints a picture of a market simultaneously resilient and fragile.

"We are entering a period that will require the biggest money printing in the history of human existence," St. said, framing the central thesis of what he sees as a generational investing challenge. "I think global debt to GDP is going to go well above 120% for developed nations and the world."

The Iran war, now in its second month, has become what St. calls "the pivotal geopolitical risk event of the year." But the implications extend far beyond the battlefield. The conflict has disrupted supply chains that underpin global agriculture, semiconductor manufacturing, and energy markets in ways that haven't been fully priced by markets yet.

## The Best Earnings Backdrop in Years—With Caveats

Despite the surrounding uncertainty, corporate earnings have proven remarkably resilient.

### Why the S&P 500 Margins Are Historically Strong

The operating margin for the S&P 500 now exceeds 14%—a level many analysts would have deemed impossible a decade ago. The reason is straightforward: big tech comprises roughly one-third of index earnings, and these companies operate with exceptionally high margins.

According to FactSet data, with approximately 20% of S&P 500 companies having reported:

- **87% have beaten earnings expectations**
- **Material beats are occurring across five sectors**
- **Analyst estimates have risen 4% from January to April**
- **Full-year earnings are expected to be up 15%**

This stands in stark contrast to the typical pattern, where analysts usually revise numbers downward by 2-3% heading into first-quarter prints. The majority of this growth is concentrated in memory companies (SanDisk, Micron), semiconductors (Nvidia), and cloud infrastructure (Eli Lilly)—a narrow group of names carrying outsized influence.

### The Tale of Two Tech Sectors

Yet within technology, St. identifies a critical divergence:

**Hardware names have performed exceptionally well.** Micron shattered earnings estimates because memory prices have reached parabolic levels. The AI bottleneck has shifted from GPUs to memory and power availability.

**Software names have collapsed.** The best companies in the space—names that would seem like obvious holdings—are down dramatically year-to-date:

- ServiceNow: Down 45%
- Intuit: Down 43%
- Salesforce: Down 35%
- Adobe: Down 32%
- Autodesk: Down 22%
- Shopify: Down 20%

"Even companies announcing $25 billion share buyback plans like Adobe are still down 32%," St. noted. "What this means is that the fear is real."

The concern isn't just multiple compression from higher interest rates—it's the questioning of terminal value. With AI products like Claude capable of building frontends, backends, and even systems of record, software companies that were valued at 20-40x EBITDA face a fundamental reassessment of their long-term viability.

## The Iran War: Supply Chain Catastrophe in Slow Motion

The conflict has created cascading disruptions across critical global supply chains.

### What's at Stake in the Strait of Hormuz

This narrow waterway isn't just about oil—it's about the raw materials of modern civilization:

- **20% of the world's oil** passes through annually
- **20% of global LNG** flows through these waters
- **One-third of the world's nitrogenous fertilizer** (critical for growing corn and grain) originates from the region
- **50% of the world's helium** for semiconductor manufacturing
- **One-third of global sulfur** used in copper mining operations

### The Price Impact Is Already Severe

The war has caused:

| Commodity | Price Impact |
|-----------|--------------|
| Plastics | +100% |
| Fertilizer | +70% |
| Jet Fuel | +100%+ |
| Canceled Flights | 12,000 (across Europe, including Lufthansa to budget carriers) |

### The Energy Timeline: Two Months to Crisis

"If this lasts another two months, there will be shortages all over the world," St. warned.

Current reserves held by the US and Japan are providing a temporary cap on oil prices, but this is a short-term band-aid. Japan, which imports the majority of its oil from the Middle East, is already using reserves to support Australia as an ally—but at some point, these nations will need to prioritize domestic needs.

Regions particularly vulnerable include:

- **Southeast Asia**: Vietnam has moved to a 3-day work week; the Philippines declared a national emergency
- **Australia**: 500 petrol stations have fully run out of fuel
- **India**: Already experiencing LPG shortages; facing fertilizer crisis as spring planting season begins with 30% of US farmers hasn't purchased fertilizer yet

The math is brutal for farmers. A Midwestern farmer recently noted his average tractor now costs $800 extra per day in diesel. When combined with fertilizer cost increases, margins that were already thin become deeply negative.

## Private Credit: The $1.8 Trillion Time Bomb

While geopolitics dominate headlines, St. identifies private credit as perhaps the more insidious systemic risk.

### The Redemption Cliff

The private credit industry has grown from nothing to $1.8 trillion over 15 years. Within this market:

- **$50 billion in redemptions** expected from interval funds
- **$300 billion in bank lending** at risk as revolvers come due
- Banks will raise borrowing rates, creating a credit crunch

### The Software Valuation Problem

Perhaps most concerning is that 25% of private credit is software exposure. When terminal values are being questioned due to AI disruption:

- Companies that were lent at 8x leverage with 40% LTV
- Valued at 20-40x EBITDA
- Could now be levered at 80-100%+ LTV if valuations compress to 10-15x

"The 37% of EBITDA in the leveraged credit markets is fake," St. explained. "It's one-time fees, transaction costs, management compensation, synergies. Once you take out all the addbacks to EBITDA, you're looking at companies levered over 100% LTV."

### Distress Signals Are Already Visible

- **Finch** has raised default expectations to above 13%
- **Lincoln Financial** (4,000 private credit transactions) reports bad pick ratios at 13%—with half being "bad pick," meaning companies selling assets to pay cash
- **Medallia**: Blackstone, TPG, and a consortium of lenders took massive write-downs
- **TPG**: Taking a zero on a $5 billion deal
- **Blackstone**: Marked Medallia down to 70 cents on the dollar

"The names are often valued between 70 and par based on mark-to-model unless you have a Chapter 7 liquidation where they have to mark to zero," St. noted.

### The Maturity Wall

In 2028, there's a $900 billion maturity wall across US and European high-yield, private credit, and leveraged loans. This could be the catalyst for a significant credit event.

## Oil Markets: The Tanker Trade and $150 Scenarios

Despite the chaos, certain energy plays have become counterintuitive opportunities.

### Why Tanker Stocks Have Risen

St. has been long tanker companies like Frontline (FRO) for a specific reason: ton miles have extended dramatically.

"Ships now have to travel all the way around Africa instead of through the Red Sea and Suez Canal," St. explained. "VLCCs are able to charge higher rates for longer distances. A number of these companies are going to see the highest margins they've seen since the Russia-Ukraine war."

Even with a peace treaty, there's a backlog that will keep day rates elevated.

### The $150 Barrel Scenario

St. models several outcomes:

- **Base case**: Peace within two months; temporary oil spike
- **Bear case**: War extends; WTI approaches $150/barrel

At $150, the entire curve shifts higher:

- Plastics prices would rise another 50% from already-doubled levels
- Food shortages would accelerate as fertilizer costs become untenable
- Airlines and chemical companies face existential margin pressure
- Energy companies could actually benefit by hedging forward at higher prices

"Whether it's airlines or plastics, you're going to see commodity plastic prices continue to rise," St. said. "We'll have food shortages because we've already entered the spring planting season and 30% of US farmers haven't even bought their fertilizer yet."

## The Biggest Money Print in Human History

St.'s most provocative claim centers on monetary policy.

### Why Money Printing Becomes Inevitable

Consider the fiscal arithmetic:

- US tax revenue: ~$5 trillion annually
- New budget with war spending: ~$7 trillion
- Deficits already at 7% before the conflict started (historically only seen during pandemics or wars)
- Expected to run 5-7% deficits in perpetuity

"Making fun of Greece during the PIGS crisis for being at 120% debt-to-GDP," St. noted, "we're already above that in the United States 13 years later—and it's going higher. We'll be at $50 trillion of debt by the time Trump leaves office."

The math of who buys this debt is equally challenging:

- **Banks**: Can absorb maybe $1 trillion annually but cannot absorb all the supply
- **Europe and Japan**: Likely to keep treasury holdings flat as they lack capacity to continue growing US reserves
- **The rest of the world**: Questionable appetite given tariff tensions

### Implications for Asset Classes

If the Fed does increase its balance sheet and M2 expands:

- **Equities**: Valuations will be supported
- **Gold and silver**: Rally significantly
- **Real estate**: Valuations extended but supported
- **Cryptocurrencies**: Bitcoin and Ethereum should perform well, especially if the Clarity Act passes
- **Long-term bonds**: More complex—dependent on whether slowdown offsets inflation concerns

"I'm not worried about runaway rates," St. concluded. "I'm more worried about growth coming down dramatically. This energy shock is much worse for emerging markets than it is for the US. I'm worried about emerging market contagion."

## Special Situations: Navigating the Noise

Given the challenging environment, St. advocates for a special situations approach.

### Why Traditional Beta Trades Fail Here

The market has presented several confounding characteristics:

- **Sharpest bear market rally since 1982** since late March
- **$85 billion in CTA short covering** last week alone
- **Estimated $50 billion more** short covering this week
- Major funds were massively long oil and short everything else, getting whipsawed

"All the factors, the trades that they thought would do well actually did not do nearly as well as they thought they would do," St. noted.

### Current Portfolio Positioning

At Special Situations Research, the approach emphasizes:

- **Gold and gold miners**: Major positions benefiting from central bank diversification away from treasuries
- **Baby bonds and preferred stocks**: 7-15% yields, many retail-oriented and even secured
- **Cash allocation**: 10-20% to deploy during volatility
- **Event-driven opportunities**: Spin-offs, split-offs, accelerated buybacks, insider purchases, activism

### The SpaceX Angle

One specific opportunity St. highlighted is **EchoStar (SATS)**, which owns approximately 35 billion in SpaceX equity at a $1.75 trillion valuation indication:

- Legacy satellite business provides floor

- Six-month IPO unlock provides timing discount
- Covered calls can create synthetic dividends

"If SpaceX did IPO, there'd be so much demand that it could trade well above $1.75 trillion for a short period of time," St. said.

## The War's Uncertain Resolution

On the geopolitical front, St. is skeptical of quick resolution.

### Why "Victory" Is Elusive

The current peace negotiations reportedly involve a 10-point treaty with Iran that would effectively establish a toll system in the Strait of Hormuz. The math is sobering:

- ~2 million barrels per ship through the strait annually
- A $1/barrel toll would generate $50 billion annually for Iran
- More likely $30 billion after ally discounts
- Would effectively double Iran's oil profit, funding military rebuilding

"Any peace deal we have now would be worse than the deal we had under the Obama administration," St. stated.

### The Midterm Calculus

St. views November midterm elections as the real deadline:

- Republicans likely to lose the House
- Small probability they lose the Senate
- Trump will do "whatever he can" to declare victory before the election
- Base case: Some peace framework within two months

### The Hidden Taiwan Risk

St. raised an underappreciated concern: The Iran energy conflict has effectively handed China leverage over Taiwan.

"If Taiwan has an energy crisis, they will have to submit to China because China has 1.44 billion barrels of oil reserves and effectively has unlimited power given how big the renewable coal sector is."

This represents a significant black swan risk the market isn't pricing.

## Defense Stocks: Counterintuitive Underperformance

One puzzling market phenomenon: Defense stocks have declined despite an actual shooting war.

"Lockheed Martin is down 24% since the beginning of March," St. noted. "Boeing is down 23%."

The explanation involves multiple factors:

- **Multiple compression**: Valuations were already extended heading into the conflict
- **Sell the news**: Military-industrial complex rallied heavily in late 2023 anticipating multiple excursions
- **Margin pressure**: Administration demanding cost efficiency; $85,000 F-35 screws are becoming untenable
- **Drone transition**: Pentagon pivoting to lower-cost unmanned systems, disrupting traditional defense economics

"Long-term defense companies are probably buys, but there is so much to do in the market right now when it comes to special situations that I am not that excited about loading up on defense at the moment," St. concluded.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Earnings strength is real but narrow**: The S&P 500 has exceptional earnings backing, but concentration in mega-cap tech and memory creates vulnerability if that leadership falters.

2. **The Iran war's true cost hasn't materialized yet**: With potential oil spikes to $150, fertilizer-driven food shortages, and Taiwan energy risk, the supply chain crisis is in its early stages—not the end.

3. **Private credit represents a $1.8 trillion risk that markets are ignoring**: Software terminal values, 80%+ LTV scenarios on fallen angels, and a $900 billion maturity wall in 2028 could create systemic stress.

4. **Money printing becomes inevitable**: With 5-7% deficits in perpetuity and insufficient global demand for treasuries, the Fed's balance sheet will expand—supporting real assets, gold, and potentially crypto.

5. **Special situations offer alpha in a confusing market**: Rather than betting on beta rotations, focusing on event-driven opportunities—spinoffs, credit situations, insider buying—provides a way to generate returns while managing the unprecedented uncertainty.

The market may have returned to all-time highs, but the foundation supporting those prices contains more fragility than the S&P 500's earnings statement would suggest.
