## Why physical stacking may not be the best move right now

The precious metals market is sending some confusing signals. While spot prices have pulled back significantly from their 2025 highs, anyone trying to buy physical gold or silver coins from dealers is encountering something unexpected: enormous premiums. We're talking $8 to $10 above spot on silver eagle coins alone. That's a massive gap that can seriously eat into your investment returns.

On the July 1st, 2026 episode of Capital Gains, host Danny sat down with financial analyst John Rubino to discuss why these premiums have spiked and what it means for investors looking to add precious metals to their portfolios. The conversation touched on everything from dealer mechanics to mining stocks to the broader macro environment.

## Why Are Physical Premiums So High?

The answer lies in dealer inventory dynamics. When gold and silver prices surged in 2025, many investors cashed in. They brought family silverware, jewelry, and stray coins to dealers, who bought aggressively at those high prices. Then came the correction.

"Now gold and silver kept on going down, which stranded a lot of high-priced inventory," Rubino explained. "And destabilized a lot of them. So what they did was they widened their spreads. They underpay you when you're trying to sell, and they overcharge you when you're trying to buy."

This is basic market mechanics at work. Dealers need to protect themselves from holding inventory purchased at higher prices. By widening spreads, they either need more price movement to profit or they're trying to discourage transactions until the market stabilizes.

## Dollar Cost Averaging: Still Works, But Adjust Your Approach

For long-term precious metals investors, dollar cost averaging is typically the recommended strategy. Buy a set dollar amount every month regardless of price. This removes emotion from the equation and smooths out your entry points over time.

However, Rubino suggests now might not be the ideal time to blindly continue this strategy with physical metals. "Normally I say 'Okay, dollar cost average, buy the same amount every month.' And now might not be the best time for that because the dealers, their business models aren't quite working right now, and they're having to overcharge you."

So what's an investor to do? Rubino recommends considering alternatives that don't carry these inflated premiums:

- **Physical ETFs**: Vehicles like Sprott Physical Gold and Silver Trusts don't have the same built-in premiums as coins
- **Mining stock ETFs**: Options like GDX (major gold miners) or SIL (silver miners) track the underlying metals without storage and dealer markup concerns

"Mining stocks are leveraged plays on the price of gold and silver," Rubino noted. "When the price of the metal goes up, gold and silver miners get an even bigger boost in cash flow. And that translates into higher stock prices."

## The Mining Opportunity: Correction Creates Entry Points

Speaking of mining stocks, both hosts highlighted the significant corrections these names have experienced. The major gold mining ETF (GDX) is down roughly 35% from its highs earlier this year. Silver miners (SIL) have dropped about 33%, while junior miners (GDXJ and SILJ) are down 35-40%.

Rubino sees this as potentially the best entry point in years for the sector. "A lot of them are down 50% from their one-year high. I think there's a lot of pent-up potential in the miners that could play out over the coming year."

The fundamental case remains strong. Mining companies are generating record free cash flow at current metal prices. They're using this cash to raise dividends, buy back stock, and make accretive acquisitions. As Rubino put it, "they become better companies as this cash piles up and they do useful things with it."

For more aggressive investors, junior explorers and developers offer even greater upside potential. These companies are defining profitable-looking resources that major miners will eventually need to acquire to replace depleting reserves. Rubino suggests some of these names could be "five to 10 baggers in a lot of cases."

## Seasonality: Historical Patterns Worth Knowing

The discussion also touched on seasonal trends in precious metals pricing. Historically, July tends to mark the seasonal bottom for gold and silver prices. The pattern follows Asian wedding seasons—when Asian consumers buy gold and silver jewelry as gifts, jewelers must purchase raw materials in the fall, creating demand pressure that lifts prices through winter and early spring.

"So you tend to get a lot more demand in August, September, October, November, and those days, which lifts the price up," Rubino explained. "And then the jewelers are done buying at that point, so demand goes down, and you get summer doldrums for gold and silver in May and June and July."

We're currently in that summer doldrums period. Whether seasonality will play out as expected given current geopolitical uncertainties remains to be seen, but the historical pattern is worth noting for timing purposes.

## The Yen Carry Trade: A Hidden Risk

The conversation took an unexpected turn when Danny raised an interesting observation: CNBC appears to have removed the Japanese 10-year bond yield ticker from their bond market section. The hosts discussed what's happening in Japan and why it matters for precious metals investors.

The yen carry trade was one of the most profitable strategies in global finance for years. Investors borrowed yen at artificially low interest rates—sometimes literally zero—and converted those funds into higher-yielding assets elsewhere. They might buy US Treasuries at 4% or Brazilian bonds at 6-7%, pocketing the difference.

That arbitrage is now collapsing. Japanese yields are rising dramatically—the 10-year hit 2.7% recently while the 30-year approaches 4%. Meanwhile, the yen has weakened significantly against the dollar. The spread between US and Japanese 10-year yields has compressed from around 4% in 2023 to just 1.76% today.

"As these spreads compress, the original yen carry trade deals aren't profitable anymore," Rubino said. "A lot of them will just have to be closed down, maybe at a loss, and a lot of the other ones can't be rolled over anymore because these spreads are too narrow."

This matters for precious metals because the yen carry trade is a multi-trillion-dollar phenomenon. When it unwinds, it could trigger broader market disruptions. "Trouble there means trouble everywhere," Rubino noted.

## Fed Policy and the Inflation Question

The conversation also addressed the Federal Reserve's dilemma. New Fed Chairman Kevin Warsh recently noted that inflation remains "too elevated," with the latest reading above 4%—double the Fed's target.

However, much of this inflation is energy-driven due to the Iran war situation. Oil has spiked but has since retreated back toward pre-war levels. If oil continues to fall, it could pull commodity inflation back down into the 3% range.

The Fed faces a difficult situation regardless. The US, like Japan, is dealing with a mountain of debt. Rising interest rates mean parabolically growing interest expenses. "If we push interest rates up to, let's say, 4 and 1/2% for the 10-year Treasury, then we're in the danger zone," Rubino warned. "We're risking a stock market crash, which leads to a recession, which forces the Fed to go back to cutting interest rates really dramatically."

## The Long-Term Case for Precious Metals

Despite near-term uncertainties, the long-term fundamentals for gold and silver remain compelling. Central banks around the world continue buying gold and are "not really price sensitive," according to Rubino. This creates a persistent demand tailwind.

More importantly, government debt levels globally are unsustainable. At some point, Rubino argues, governments will have "no choice but to inflate away" their debts through currency debasement. "Low interest rates and QE are inevitable out there. So if you're a precious metals investor, that's probably what you want to be focusing on—this thing that might be a few years out but that is phenomenally good for precious metals."

## Bottom Line

The current environment presents both challenges and opportunities for precious metals investors:

- **Avoid**: Paying excessive premiums on physical coins right now. Dealers are charging $8-10 above spot on silver eagles, which significantly increases your cost basis.

- **Consider alternatives**: Physical ETFs or mining stock ETFs offer exposure to precious metals without the premium markup.

- **Mining stocks look attractive**: After 35-40% corrections, quality miners are generating record cash flows and could offer substantial upside.

- **Stay the course**: The long-term fundamental case for precious metals remains strong. Governments will continue debasing currencies, and central banks will keep buying gold.

- **Monitor risks**: Watch the yen carry trade unwinding and geopolitical developments that could impact oil prices and inflation.

As always, don't try to time the market. Build positions gradually, consider dollar cost averaging into quality assets, and maintain a long-term perspective. The near-term noise around premiums, spreads, and geopolitical headlines can obscure the bigger picture—but for patient investors, precious metals remain a valuable portfolio hedge against currency debasement and fiscal excess.
