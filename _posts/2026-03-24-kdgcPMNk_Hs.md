**An Interview with Eric Young**

In a revealing discussion, Vince Lanci sits down with analyst Eric Young to dissect two seismic shifts in global markets: the dramatic sell-off in precious metals and the accelerating, tangible reality of the yuan-for-oil trade—a development both have tracked for years.

## The Gold Sell-Off: A Dollar Panic, Not a Bear Market

The conversation opens on the shellacking of gold and silver. Lanci references analysis from Nomura, highlighting that the recent plunge stems from a "dollar panic," not a loss of faith in gold.

"The de-dollarization trade had become a crowded trade," Lanci explains, with Middle Eastern funds, Asian investors, and global speculators all positioned long on gold and short dollars. The backdrop was an expectation of global rate cuts and fiscal stimulus.

"Then the war starts," Lanci says. This triggered a liquidity crisis. "You have all these companies that are frozen and having a hard time getting cash flow. Well, you have a dollar panic." Entities leveraged long on gold were forced to sell their positions to raise urgent dollar liquidity.

Eric Young adds crucial context, noting that reports indicate North American investor allocation to gold is below 1.6%. "Does it apply to the Middle East and whales? Maybe not," he suggests. The real selling pressure, he argues, is coming from wealthy individuals and businesses within the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC—Saudi Arabia, Qatar, UAE, etc.), not central banks.

"Central banks don't dump at the COMEX," Young states. His theory? "The whales in the Middle East are selling." He paints a vivid picture: individuals with significant physical gold holdings in Dubai, facing regional instability, choosing to sell locally at a discount and rebuy in Hong Kong or Singapore rather than move the metal. "If you want to get the heck out of Dubai... You're not going to try to move the gold. You just sell it," he says, referencing the reported $30 discount for gold in Dubai. These individuals likely also sold leveraged paper positions.

Lanci concurs and expands: "Anyone who's selling gold right now is not selling gold because they're bearish gold. They're selling gold because they need dollars... It's a dollar squeeze. That's all it really is." He firmly dismisses rumors of central bank selling: "Central banks are buying gold. They're buying it as it drops right now."

## The Open Secret: Yuan for Oil is Now Operational Fact

The discussion then pivots to the topic Eric Young brought definitive evidence for: the yuan-for-oil trade, moving from theory to admitted state policy.

**Background:** Since 2017, Russia and China piloted a system where China paid for oil in yuan, convertible to gold. This model has since expanded. "The Saudi Arabians have these vaults... they sell the oil for yuan and the yuan gets converted into gold if they want it to," Lanci summarizes.

**The Iranian Admission:** Young presents the breakthrough. After years of speculation, Iran has publicly admitted it sells oil to China for yuan via its "shadow fleet" of uninsured tankers. Furthermore, Iran now demands yuan payment for any oil transiting the Strait of Hormuz. "They're saying that if you want your oil to get out of the Strait of Hormuz, you have to pay in RMB," Young states.

This brazen announcement confirms years of under-the-radar trade. "They were doing it under the radar. Why would anybody have any proof if it's sanctioned oil?" Young notes, expressing frustration at past skepticism. "Now the cat is off the bag... Let's face reality, pick it as fact."

**The Mechanics:** Young details the mechanism. Iran's yuan payments are held at the **Bank of Kunlun** (also known as the Quanong Bank), a Chinese bank with a headquarters in the Xinjiang Uyghur Autonomous Region—a Muslim-majority area culturally closer to Iran. "80% of the oil that Iran trades with China... they keep it in China at the Kunlun Bank," he says. The money is used to buy Chinese goods (drones, electronics), and the trade surplus is used to purchase **physical gold on the Shanghai Gold Exchange (domestic market)**, keeping the entire cycle within China and off Western radar.

"Why would the gold leave?" Lanci interjects, emphasizing the logic. "The money's not leaving. Why would the gold leave?"

**The Saudi Angle:** Young drops another significant piece of information: his sources indicate that since 2022, Saudi Arabia has been conducting **50% of its oil trade with China in yuan**, using a similar gold-convertible mechanism. This aligns with the long-stated BRICS de-dollarization plan. Lanci reframes it as a trust exercise: China told the Saudis, "Fine, then you could convert it into gold." The Saudis replied, "Great. Where do I sign up?"

## Conclusion: A New Financial Reality

The interview concludes by highlighting the paradigm shift. The West's "shock" at these arrangements stems from a naive belief that all nations play by the same rules. "Everyone cheats," Lanci states. "It's just ridiculous."

For Iran and China, U.S. sanctions are not legitimate international law but an imposition. "From the Chinese perspective, who the heck are you to tell us how to conduct our bilateral trade with a third-party country?" Young notes.

The evidence is now incontrovertible. A major, gold-backed, non-dollar oil trading system is not a future theory—it is a present-day operational reality involving some of the world's largest energy producers. The recent gold volatility is a short-term dislocation driven by dollar liquidity needs, overshadowing this profound and accelerating long-term trend toward a multipolar monetary system.
