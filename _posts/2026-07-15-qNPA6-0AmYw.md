**CIO Peter Bookvar analyzes the shift away from MAG 7 stocks, the global rise in real yields driven by debt, and opportunities in energy and precious metals.**

Peter Bookvar, CIO at One Point BFG Wealth Partners and author of *The Book Report*, joins the show to discuss the evolving macro landscape. With the NASDAQ down 1.5% at the start of the week, Bookvar highlights a significant rotation away from technology, a global bond bear market fueled by debt and supply, and a persistent inflationary environment. He also outlines his bullish stance on commodities and warns of potential risks to the AI trade and the U.S. dollar.

## Tech Selloff and the MAG 7 Transition

The recent market action has been dominated by a selloff in technology, with chip stocks like **SK Hynix** leading declines. Bookvar emphasizes that the tech sector must now be viewed through two lenses: the "spenders" and the "recipients" of AI capital expenditure.

*   **Spenders vs. Recipients:** Hyperscalers (the spenders) are facing rerating pressure as investors notice deteriorating free cash flow and massive capital outlays. Conversely, semiconductors (the recipients) have been rewarded, but Bookvar notes "gravity exists," particularly for DRAM and storage producers like **Western Digital** and **SanDisk**.
*   **Rotation:** Investors are rotating out of the **MAG 7** and into other sectors, such as financials, ahead of a flood of bank earnings. Bookvar points out that bank stocks are outperforming globally, including in Europe, Canada, Japan, and China.
*   **Leadership Shift:** This marks a major transition after 15 years of MAG 7 dominance. Bookvar notes that three of the MAG 7 stocks he monitors are already down for the year, including **Microsoft**.

## Fed Policy, Inflation, and the New Normal

Ahead of the FOMC meeting and **Kevin Warsh's** congressional testimony, Bookvar discusses the Federal Reserve's trajectory and the inflation outlook.

*   **Fed Dynamics:** The committee is differentiated. While Warsh avoids forward guidance, Fed Governor **Chris Waller** recently indicated a willingness to raise rates if CPI data comes in hot. This creates uncertainty, making the Fed's path a "coin toss."
*   **Inflation Normal:** Bookvar maintains that **3% to 4%** is the new inflation normal, replacing the previous 1% to 2% target. With the effective Fed funds rate at **3.62%**, the Fed remains accommodative relative to this higher inflation baseline. He expects the Fed to stay on hold.
*   **Sticky Prices:** While crude oil has declined, product prices like gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel remain elevated due to refining bottlenecks. Consumers are not seeing significant relief. Bookvar also highlights the importance of the **Producer Price Index (PPI)**, noting that inflation is being absorbed at the business level rather than passed on.

## Global Bond Bear Market and Rising Yields

Bookvar reiterates his long-held view that we are in a global bond bear market, which he describes as the flip side of the historic bubble involving $18 trillion in negative-yielding bonds.

*   **Real Rates and Debt:** The rise in long-term interest rates, with the U.S. 10-year yield approaching **5%**, is driven by real rates, not inflation expectations. Investors are reacting to excessive debt, deficits, and Treasury supply. This trend is visible globally, affecting the UK, France, Germany, and Japan.
*   **Japan Repatriation Risk:** The **Bank of Japan** has raised rates to 1%, the highest since 1995. Japanese officials are urging pension funds to diversify back into Japanese assets. Bookvar warns that as JGB yields become more attractive, Japan could repatriate its **$1.2 trillion** holding of U.S. Treasuries, potentially pressuring U.S. rates and tech stocks.
*   **Yield Forecasts:** Bookvar expects the 10-year yield to retest 5%. He believes Warsh will not institute a "soft cap" or yield curve control, as expanding the balance sheet is not on his agenda.

## AI Overbuilding and Economic Risks

The U.S. economy and stock market are heavily dependent on the **Gen AI trade** and data center buildout. Bookvar warns that this concentration poses significant risks.

*   **Signs of Excess:** There are emerging signals of overbuilding. **SpaceX** is leasing capacity to **Anthropic** and **Google**, and **Meta** has announced excess capacity available for lease.
*   **Users vs. Builders:** Historically, the benefits of infrastructure buildouts (like railroads or the internet) accrue to the users, not the builders. Bookvar expects the benefits of AI to shift toward users and consumers, while the "receivers" of the capex trade tire out.
*   **Dollar Risk:** Foreign investment managers are heavily exposed to the U.S. tech trade. Bookvar argues that if the AI trade falters, it could drag down the **U.S. dollar**. He also draws a parallel to the **2002** tech bust, where a collapse in the tech trade culminated in a broader market decline, though he remains uncertain if other sectors can offset a tech downturn.
*   **Economic Sensitivity:** Despite subscription models, MAG 7 companies remain sensitive to the economy. **Meta** and **Google** rely on advertising, **Amazon** on retail, and **Microsoft** on employee seats. A labor market deterioration would impact their revenues.

## Commodities, Gold, and Strategic Stockpiling

Bookvar remains bullish on commodities, viewing them as a hedge against inflation and geopolitical risk.

*   **Sector Picks:** He favors **energy stocks**, **agriculture**, **uranium**, and beaten-down **consumer staples**.
*   **Precious Metals:** Gold and silver have been digesting their parabolic runs. Bookvar expects them to resume their bull market in the back half of the year as real rates stabilize and inflation catches up. The dollar's rally has been modest, providing less headwind for metals.
*   **Strategic Stockpiling:** A global trend of strategic stockpiling is creating an underlying bid for commodities. Governments are building reserves for **lithium**, **natural gas**, **oil**, **fertilizer**, and **copper** to avoid supply shocks similar to those seen in the **Strait of Hormuz**.
*   **Industrial Metals:** Industrial metals benefit from the data center buildout, which requires significant copper, aluminum, and steel.

## Summary

Peter Bookvar outlines a market at a turning point. The dominance of the MAG 7 and AI trade is waning as cash flows deteriorate and signs of overbuilding emerge. Meanwhile, a global bond bear market is intensifying as investors price in debt, deficits, and supply, with yields likely to retest 5%. The Fed faces a new inflation normal of 3-4%, keeping policy accommodative but persistent. In this environment, Bookvar favors commodities, energy, and precious metals, while cautioning against the risks concentrated in the tech sector and the potential impact of Japanese capital repatriation on U.S. assets.
