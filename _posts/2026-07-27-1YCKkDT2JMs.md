## Navigating the End of the Current Financial System

Legendary investor and author Doug Casey joins BullionStar to discuss the evolution of the precious metals markets, the shifting global political landscape, and strategies for preserving wealth in an era of unprecedented uncertainty. With gold trading near $4,100 an ounce, Casey offers his perspective on whether gold is still a good speculative asset, the critical importance of political diversification, and why he remains bullish on physical gold and mining stocks.

### The Evolution of Gold Markets

Reflecting on BullionStar’s 14th anniversary, Casey notes that while gold has risen significantly from below $2,000 an ounce to its current level, he views it as reasonably priced relative to other assets like housing and consumer goods. He argues that gold is no longer a cheap speculative asset but remains a crucial store of value.

Casey emphasizes that gold is unique because it is not anyone else’s liability, meaning it carries no counterparty risk. He contrasts this with fiat currencies, which are credit units issued by governments he believes are increasingly untrustworthy. While he acknowledges Bitcoin as a potential competitor sharing some characteristics of good money defined by Aristotle, he remains steadfast in his preference for gold as "money in its most basic form."

### Political Diversification and the Decline of the US

Casey argues that we are witnessing a fundamental change in the financial, political, and monetary landscape. He describes the US as having transformed from a "human republic" into a "multicultural domestic empire," likening it to a "Colossus with feet of clay." He believes the US is nearing a precipice, making political diversification more important than ever.

Drawing historical parallels to Russia in 1917, Germany in 1933, and China in 1948, Casey stresses that politics runs the world. He advises investors not to keep all their assets in one country and to establish a "crib" outside their home jurisdiction. He identifies Singapore, Switzerland, and the Cayman Islands as the top three jurisdictions for storing gold, noting that Singapore has become a premier location for wealth preservation due to its rule of law and stability.

### Central Banks and the Return of Gold Money

Central banks are aggressively buying gold, with nearly 90% expecting reserves to grow. Casey attributes this to central banks recognizing the danger of holding the fiat currency of an adversary, particularly the US dollar, which he estimates loses 5% to 10% of its value annually. He believes gold will eventually be reinstituted as global money again, as no one trusts fiat currencies.

Casey performs a rough calculation: with approximately 7 billion ounces of above-ground gold and trillions of dollars in existence, the price of gold would need to rise significantly to redeem foreign-held dollars. He suggests that if gold were to become money again, its price would have to reflect the massive disparity between the supply of gold and the supply of fiat currency.

### Tether, Mining Stocks, and Silver

The conversation turns to Tether, a private company that has become one of the world’s largest private holders of gold, accumulating 154 tons. Casey praises this move, noting that Tether is also buying mining stocks and royalty companies. He criticizes gold mining companies for selling their product for fiat dollars instead of holding it as assets, arguing they are undervalued and represent a great speculative opportunity.

Regarding silver, Casey highlights its dual role as a monetary and industrial metal. With demand rising for electronics and supply constrained by its status as a byproduct of other mining, he is bullish on silver. He prefers silver coins, such as Canadian Maple Leafs and US Double Eagles, for their fungibility and recognizability.

### Cash, Inflation, and Independent Thinking

Despite his bearish view on fiat currency, Casey admits to holding more cash than usual. He views this as a strategic move to maintain liquidity and buy bargains, such as depressed gold mining stocks. He acknowledges that holding cash results in a loss of purchasing power but argues that flexibility is necessary in the current market.

Casey warns against complacency regarding hyperinflation, using the Zimbabwe $100 trillion note as a cautionary tale. He notes that even the head of the Zimbabwe Central Bank at the time advocated for a gold-backed currency. He believes the US government has painted itself into a corner with unsustainable spending on welfare, interest, and military, leading to inevitable currency debasement.

Finally, Casey discusses the importance of independent thinking, criticizing the modern education system for promoting bad ideas. He promotes his new book, *The Preparation*, which advocates for practical, experiential learning over traditional college education. He encourages investors to stay informed through his resources, including *internationalman.com*, *crisisinvesting.com*, and *expertsroundtable.com*.

### Summary

Doug Casey views gold as essential savings rather than speculation in today’s market. He urges investors to prioritize political diversification, store assets in stable jurisdictions like Singapore, and recognize the long-term decline of fiat currencies. While cautious about the stock market, he remains bullish on gold mining stocks and silver, advocating for independent thinking and practical preparation in an uncertain world.
