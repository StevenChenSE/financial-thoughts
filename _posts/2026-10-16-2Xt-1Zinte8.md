For decades, the financial world operated under the assumption that stability was the default state. However, historian and portfolio manager Neil Howe argues that we have entered the "Fourth Turning," a cyclical period of crisis that fundamentally reshapes society, politics, and economics. In a recent interview, Howe detailed the warning signs of this era, from the rise of authoritarianism to the vulnerability of infrastructure, and offered blunt advice for investors: get out of bonds.

## Understanding the Fourth Turning

Howe, who co-created the Strauss-Howe generational theory with the late William Strauss, explains that history moves in long cycles lasting approximately 80 to 90 years. These cycles are driven by generational change. A "Fourth Turning" occurs when the generation born into stability reaches its elder leadership years, creating a crisis that reshapes institutions.

According to Howe, the current cycle began around the 2008 Global Financial Crisis (GFC). We are now in the final season of this cycle, which he predicts will climax in the next decade. This period is characterized by:
*   **Civic Upheaval:** Unlike the previous "High" (post-WWII) or "Awakening" (1960s cultural revolution), this era involves deep institutional decay.
*   **Global Conflict:** Just as the Great Depression and WWII were global events, the current crisis is worldwide. Howe points to the rise of autocrats and ethnocentric populism, citing leaders like Vladimir Putin, Xi Jinping, Narendra Modi, and Donald Trump as part of a global trend toward authoritarianism.
*   **Loss of Trust in Democracy:** Polls indicate that younger generations are increasingly skeptical of democracy, viewing it as ineffective. This contrasts with previous eras where youth were the primary defenders of democratic ideals.

## The Myth of "This Time Is Different" with AI

A central theme of Howe’s analysis is the intersection of technology and the economic cycle. Many investors believe that Artificial Intelligence (AI) is a unique technological breakthrough that will sustain extreme market valuations indefinitely. Howe rejects this notion.

He compares the current AI boom to previous technological revolutions, such as the radio in the 1920s or the internet in the 1990s. In each case, the technology was transformative and long-term positive, but it was preceded by a massive investment bubble that eventually collapsed.

**Key Market Indicators:**
*   **Shiller’s CAPE Ratio:** Howe highlights that the Cyclically Adjusted Price-to-Earnings (CAPE) ratio is over 40, specifically looking at the "real return CAPE." Historically, starting a 10-year investment period at a CAPE above 40 has resulted in negative real returns.
*   **Tech Sector Fragility:** He notes that chip stocks (like Micron and SK Hynix) are often the first to fall when momentum shifts. These sectors are highly sensitive to changes in data center growth rates.
*   **The Minsky Moment:** Howe invokes Hyman Minsky’s theory that stability breeds risk-taking. The perceived stability of the last few decades has encouraged excessive leverage, such as 3x inverse ETFs and high levels of margin debt, setting the stage for a larger crash when the system inevitably breaks.

## Why Bonds Are Not Safe

Perhaps Howe’s most controversial financial advice is his stance on fixed income. He argues that during a Fourth Turning, governments inevitably resort to inflation to manage debt and fund crises.

"Inflation is the almost certain end-point of the crisis," Howe states. Governments need to mobilize resources quickly for war or social upheaval. They do this by devaluing currency, which transfers wealth from creditors (bondholders) to the state and productive sectors.

*   **Nominal Debt is Dangerous:** Holding long-term nominal bonds (like the 10-year or 20-year Treasury) is risky because their real value is eroded by inflation. Howe cites the example of holding a 10-year Treasury bond before Pearl Harbor; it would have been wiped out by decades of inflation and financial repression.
*   **TIPS Are Not a Perfect Hedge:** Even Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS) suffer because real interest rates and term premiums often rise alongside inflation expectations, causing price declines in these assets.
*   **Short Duration is Key:** Howe’s fund, the Hedgei Fourth Turning ETF, is currently short long-duration zero-coupon bonds (such as the 20+ year Treasuries). He advises avoiding any form of duration, whether in bonds or long-duration growth stocks (like pharma startups), as rising real rates crush their valuations.

## Geopolitical Risks and Infrastructure Vulnerability

Howe warns that the next great conflict will not resemble World War II, where the U.S. homeland was largely safe. Instead, the battlefield will include domestic infrastructure.

*   **Cyber and Physical Attacks:** He points to Chinese malware (such as "Salt Typhoon") embedded in U.S. telecommunications and power grid systems. Similarly, Iran has attempted to disrupt dam flows. The threat is not just nuclear missiles, but targeted attacks on transformers and power stations.
*   **China’s Missile Capability:** Howe notes that China’s DF-27 hypersonic missiles can reach the U.S. West Coast with incredible accuracy, posing a direct threat to infrastructure.
*   **Lack of Civil Defense:** Unlike Europe, which is rebuilding shelters and civil defense capabilities, the U.S. disbanded its civil defense programs 30 years ago. Howe warns that Americans are unprepared for homeland insecurity.

## How to Prepare: Financial and Personal

For those seeking to navigate the Fourth Turning, Howe suggests a shift in both portfolio allocation and lifestyle.

**Financial Preparation:**
*   **Avoid Nominal Bonds:** Do not lock in yields assuming they are safe.
*   **Real Assets:** Hold precious metals and commodities, which tend to perform well during inflationary crises.
*   **Non-Market Positions:** Consider assets that are not correlated with traditional market crashes, such as real estate (though even this is vulnerable in wartime) or physical commodities.
*   **Multistrategy Approach:** Howe’s fund combines long positions in selected equities (like tech leaders) with short positions in vulnerable sectors (private equity, weak chip stocks) and an anti-inflation component.

**Personal Preparation:**
*   **Geographic Flexibility:** Howe strongly advises having an option to live outside major urban areas. Cities are vulnerable to grid failure, supply chain disruptions, and social unrest.
*   **Community and Resilience:** The "hero generation" (Millennials, in Howe’s framework) will be tasked with rebuilding society. This requires a shift from individualism to community investment.

## Conclusion

Neil Howe’s thesis is not one of doom, but of cyclical reality. The Fourth Turning is a period of necessary destruction followed by reconstruction. While the transition will be painful—marked by inflation, conflict, and financial repression—it also offers the potential for a new social contract and renewed civic purpose. For investors and citizens alike, the key is to recognize that the rules of the previous 40 years (low inflation, low volatility, globalization) are no longer applicable. The safe harbor of bonds has vanished, and preparation for a more volatile, conflict-prone world is now essential.
