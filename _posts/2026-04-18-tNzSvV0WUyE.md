## The Market sniper warns of energy collapse while gold enters a structural bull market

Francis Hunt, the founder of Market Sniper and a well-known technical analyst, recently appeared on a financial YouTube channel to share his outlook on the markets. His thesis is clear and provocative: stagflation will become official policy, enriching the billionaire class while impoverishing everyone else. As part of this outlook, Hunt sees oil crashing below $68 while gold and silver grind higher over time.

"The markets seem to have rebounded to where they were pre-war," the host began. "Is peace on the horizon?"

Hunt's response was characteristically direct. "Too many people suddenly worked out that stagflationary was going to be a policy. I started to see way more people accepting our long-term theory of stagflation will enrich the billionaire class and impoverish everybody else."

---

## The Stagflation Thesis

According to Hunt, the stagflation thesis has gained mainstream acceptance recently. This means interest rates are rising while economic growth stagnates. "The rates were going up, the debt was going down. They still have to roll debt and function. And even on the short end, people were highlighting that the rates are going up. So they now need to dial that back a little bit. And we're actually seeing oil break to the downside in our opinion."

The stagflation dynamic creates a painful squeeze for ordinary consumers. "What you end up getting is they have far less disposable income. They actually become negative cash flow and they are net selling assets at the bottom of the market. Their consumption, their travel, their packages, their goods, all the things that drive the oil price—their consumption of petroleum-based products—goes substantially down."

This creates a paradox for oil prices. "The problem oil has is that whenever it runs too high, it forces inflation that then becomes too expensive for the cost of living that everyone has and you get a deflationary result."

Hunt draws parallels to previous oil peaks. "Look here when we hit 150 smash when you had a sustained period in and around 100 and at the highs for a short spell here between 110 and 115 smash post-recovery 2018 and 2019 Shanghai Accord slap in the face. First inverted HVF smash."

## Oil Technical Analysis: The Ascending Inverted HVF Pattern

Hunt is particularly bearish on oil from a technical standpoint. He identifies what he calls an "ascending inverted HVF" pattern, which he describes as one of the most difficult technical structures to call—but ultimately bearish.

"So Trump made an announcement of a 14-day peace agreement and that got a chronic reaction that led to this gap. This is very rare. You're going to gap from somewhere around $103 in a quarter and go all the way down to $92. That's an exceptional $11 gap. Only the exhaustion gap into the blowoff high here recently is anything like it. That was at about $90 and through to around $100. You fell from $103 back down. So this is an exhaustion gap."

The key level Hunt identifies is $112 on Brent crude. "You couldn't hold it. You were rejected. That's a slap in the face. It's a violent rejection. We all bought into the theory about 'yes it's hormuz and we're not going to get all these goods' and everybody became so stagflationary focused that everyone came in and was buying oil at any price. Any dip they were buying oil, buying oil, buying oil. So we ended up with this very steep ascending line under that. So there's a lot of people that are buying and paying any price and silly money entries to get in for rightful reasons at the time. There was peak mania about hormuz and nothing's going to move and 60% of this is there."

But here's the critical observation: "The key part no one's noticing is that all that buying at no point was able to beat the slap in the face high. You got rejected there and sold off. You got rejected sooner here."

Hunt describes the longer-term structure: "This piece of price behavior is a broadening structure. That's a megaphone for simple speak. And that's your bear pole. So in actual terms for us, you while you might rally inside this megaphone, you can come up to here. You could even go to the other side to a degree. I don't think you're shutting the gap. A longer term expectation is you're breaking down."

His target is clear: "We're talking sub $68 on Brent with overperformance to the downside below that."

The recent gap-down move triggered Hunt's bearish thesis. "This was a triggering event for downside that occurred roughly at our midpoint at around $102.68 and next thing you know you'd close down here at $91. That made our first interim target and that's typical when you've made the first interim target you get some form of a rally but the structure of the rally is not strong. You even had a small gap up and the minute you gapped up you closed it 100% back down in here. So this is downside continuation and that's your $68 target that should be run and overperformed."

## Why Oil Cannot Keep Up With Gold

Hunt makes a crucial distinction between gold and oil. "Gold will show the full inflation of the chicainery and fraud of the financial system. Oil won't do that because as your assets are collapsing in value and people have less money in their house, less money in their stock portfolios, everything is debasing, their life and the cost of living is climbing, food is going up a lot."

The host pushed back with the common argument that oil is cheap in real terms—particularly when measured against gold or adjusted for inflation. Hunt acknowledged this but maintained his view.

"In nominal terms it is relatively high. But you know over the decades oil has pretty much been rangebound to you know $50 to $120. So when you price it in gold, for example, it's doesn't look that expensive."

However, Hunt emphasized that oil will never behave like gold because it's too critical to the economy. "When everyone says that the economy can't handle these high oil prices, in reality oil prices aren't that high when you measure them in terms of gold or in real terms. If that is the case down in real terms, then can't the economy—even though it may sound absurd nominally—in real terms, isn't it still sustainable to a degree?"

Hunt's response was definitive: "Only if we have no more efficiencies in drilling for oil. You've got all of Venezuela that's bigger than Saudi on the heavy sour. It's the biggest reserves. The planet bleeds this fuel. Everybody's been trained to believe that this is lazy age dinosaur bones of which there's a finite amount and we've now, you know, all the pressurized dinosaur skeletons have bled out whatever they are. No, it's not. It's a planet. It's like the juice of the core of this planet. People found it seeping out. We don't actually have a shortage. We have a shortage of $20 oil. We have a shortage of $30 oil. We don't have a shortage of $110 consistently dollar oil at all."

The key difference, according to Hunt, is supply constraints. "When there's no more oil, then it has to go up at the same rate as gold. We don't have a shortage of oil and we're getting more efficient at extracting it. And there's now fracking and all of these elements and we reducing the demand. We've reduced the population probably by COVID-19, certainly in the white European [population], certainly in Japan, certainly in Korea. So we're not getting the replacement consumers with money either."

This leads to Hunt's longer-term view: "We will see the fullness of the proliferation of fiat and all the fraud. That's how high gold goes. Oil is not driven by those same dynamics because for all intent and purpose it's near infinite at the rates that we're burning it. You just have the extraction costs and we're getting better at extracting it and there's probably reducing demand, I would say, generally, particularly in an economic climate like this."

He projects: "So this goes higher and in due course I'm calling that it goes higher than $88 again but gold might be at $10,000 at that time and oil could still be $125 on a much depreciated oil and that $125 could be equivalent to $75 today or $80. So eventually you will get a number like $250 but we need way more dollar debasement for that to happen in real terms."

## The Gold-Oil Ratio: A Structural Breakdown

Hunt provided a detailed historical analysis of the gold-oil ratio, which he sees as entering a structural bull market for gold.

"When you have a look at this chart and you just take out the extremity of the spike, let's go monthly and we ignore the spike which was again event driven. What you're actually having happen is you have these excessive ridiculous low valuations of gold to oil and what you're now facing is this subsequent very strong correction upward to reflect the lack of respect put on gold for the debasement of financial standards. So oil is underperforming for a sustained period."

Hunt describes a "watermelon smile" pattern on the gold-oil ratio chart. "You actually have a rounded smile that's heading up like this. You have what I call the watermelon smile with the toothy grin. You had a very static fixed period when gold was fixed. So you can almost ignore that. The chart starts there. That was your relative highs. These were your relative lows. That was your relative highs. Financialization of everything period into dot-com and 2008. Excessively disproportionately valuable oil to gold. No one talks about that. In 1999 through to 2008, for the amount of financial fiat creation, the monetary metal should have gone up and the consumption commodity should have gone down because people's ability to continue to consume in this ponzianomics gets reduced."

The current period represents a fundamental re-rating. "Oil is 100% going down relative to gold, far further, because the one thing everybody's going to do is they're going to say, 'I want to preserve my money, buy gold.' And the other thing they're going to do is they're going to have less money for wasting on consumption—all consumption oil. And you've already begun to see that since 2008, starting at '99."

## Gold Analysis: The Broadening Structure

Turning to gold specifically, Hunt sees the precious metal recovering from recent liquidity-driven selling. "Gold is doing exactly like you said what it should do. But the more interesting one recently was actually silver."

Hunt identifies a "broadening structure" for gold, also known as a megaphone pattern. "Gold is in a broadening structure to go higher. That's typically known as a megaphone and that's your bull pole which had a blowoff moment. That's the more vertical bit. Everyone was coming in along the lows already here and was bleeding up, bleeding up, bleeding up. Didn't have many long-lasting downs. That was its last moderately significant down."

The structure has three components, including a descending grind line. "So you're in the middle. You can drift a little bit. It's not going to be necessarily that strong. You can go up and you can come down, but in the end, you're getting to this double line and you're breaking it. And that will get you in the gold bull sooner than if you wait for a new high. The longer it takes, the lower this line comes as well."

## Silver Analysis: The Falling Wedge

Hunt is more constructive on silver from a technical perspective. "Silver was better set up. Silver had the following. Instead of a broadening structure, it tightened."

He explains the volatility patterns: "Silver is always going to have a higher beta downside. Gold corrected 21.37%. Silver corrected 47%. Nearly half. You're at $121, then you're at $61. So that's 21. The higher beta silver was 47. However, the second selloff for gold was 24. Silver was 36. So it was less than the first time. It's still higher than gold's, but gold had a deeper selloff. That's what broadened the structure. What happened with silver is that because of fundamentals, it didn't get as low. And because it had a really harsh first selloff, there was less downside that they could plumb."

Hunt identifies a falling wedge on silver: "So here you go. You walked out over here. You bumped into the capping descending line, bumped into it again, spinning top, bumped in, sold off, came back, and you actually broke out. You had a return move in that candle and you even made a lower low there. And now you're moving out. So you've got beautiful three impulse—big first, very spiky impulse, a slightly slower, broader, sleepier second one, and then you've crossed."

His outlook for silver: "I don't rule out the possibility of another dip down at some point. But I think if you're patient and you're investing, you're ending up in the north of $100, $120 range again. And then you could pause. I don't think you've got the momentum to just go straight into breaking new highs."

## The Gold-Silver Ratio: Critical Juncture

Hunt analyzes the gold-silver ratio at around 60, describing it as a pivotal decision point. "Is it going to surrender to the downside the $60 level or will it go higher?"

The ratio previously found significance at the 50 level. "50 is going to be the most significant ratio long before during the bull market. We mentioned 50 is going to be one of the most significant ratios you can expect to react. What happened? You came down met 50. You created a broadening continuation pattern on 50. You had another here. You traded down, traded down, but it was the bottom. You came up, up, up, up and then you smashed back above 50. That was a technical run of the 50 and rejection of staying below that level."

Hunt sees two possibilities: "Either you'll form another descending triangle or something more like this—so could it go up? We've got a decision tree coming. What happens? Does this bounce before? Does it come flat? What's it going to do?"

If the ratio breaks down further, it would be bullish for both metals—particularly silver. "Breaking down will mean gold and silver going up, silver more so."

## Miners: A Margin Expansion Story

For miners, Hunt sees a favorable setup if oil falls while precious metals grind higher. "The miners got stopped in their tracks. Remember, energy, apart from salaries, I would imagine energy is either their biggest or second biggest cost. Digging holes in ground and moving people up and down. A lot of work, a lot of energy in that. If the oil price starts to go down for them and they are getting an upside on their revenue number, the margin increases on both ends. You'll start getting interest in the earnings again of miners and buying on this fact."

He highlights Wheaton Precious Metals as relatively strong. "Look at that. It's actually done better than Hecla and a number of others. And it's also in a technical pattern. So we can't say this is it high. It's only 11% off its high when you have gold and silver well off 11% of their highs. So that's a big tell."

Other miners like Hecla are further from their highs. "If I put one of the others in there, let's just put Hecla because I know it's gone a lot further down on that chart. You can see a far steeper flag descent here. You're not 10% off the highs, but again, it's still continuation territory. You've had your one selloff, you've had your two selloff. I continue to ask, might you get that third one more that's the weakest and you can just get another double dip before you break. But this is the line you want to see broken. These are all typically continuation patterns to the upside."

The potential upside is significant. "If I measure how far south it is of the high, it's going to be more than 10%. You can almost see it naked eye to there. And you can see you're at about 43-44% off. So a lot more potential upside on that given how hard it's re-rated, assuming management are not doing anything stupid."

## The Wealth Preservation Imperative

Hunt concluded with his core message for viewers: build wealth and maintain optionality.

"You need to build wealth not because you desire to be rich because you want to entrench your future lifestyle and some of the freedoms you now take for granted. Further to more point number two—you need to protect that wealth because they are coming for it. The hidden hand wants it and they have pre-ordained agendas that are absolutely disclosed at the very highest levels. And to avoid it, you need optionality, which means multiple possible plans and jurisdictions."

He emphasized practical preparation: "There is no workman that has one tool in his toolbox. I live in one country, bank with one nation, bank with one state. It's always going to work just fine for me. No, they're not your friends. Governments aren't your friends. You need a toolkit. You need optionality for all eventualities and most of them are damaged. That's why you're doing tools and that's why you got a medical aid kit. All of those things. So why are you not applying that thinking to your financial balance sheet, to your existence, your freedom, and the life of yourself, your sons, and even your daughters that are left behind in miserable situations?"

Hunt's personal experience underpins his advice. "I had to do it. I had to move. I had to go where I was treated better. And I did it. I took wives and children with and dogs and everything. You can do it."

His final message: "If you really value your existence, you need to build wealth to entrench your right to those other three things. It's a noble gesture and fund the machine less."
