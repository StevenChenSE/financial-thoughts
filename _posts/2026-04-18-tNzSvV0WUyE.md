*April 15, 2026 — Capital Interview*

---

"My name is Danny. It is April 15th, 2026 and my guest today is the market sniper himself. It is Francis Hunt."

With those words, the host of Capital launched into a wide-ranging conversation with trader and analyst Francis Hunt that touched on everything from crude oil technicals to geopolitical alarm bells. What followed was a masterclass in reading market structures, understanding the true nature of commodity relationships, and a sobering assessment of where Western nations are heading.

---

## The Stagflation Consensus Arrives—Finally

The markets have rebounded nearly to pre-war levels. Peace seems possible. But Hunt isn't buying the optimism.

"Too many people suddenly worked out that stagflation was going to be a policy," Hunt explained. "I started to see way more people accepting our long-term theory: stagflation will enrich the billionaire class and impoverish everybody else."

The shift in narrative matters because it changes expectations. When stagflation became the consensus view, rates started climbing and debt instruments started falling. Even the short end of the curve reflected this reality. Now, Hunt observes, there's an attempt to dial that back—but the underlying structural problems haven't been resolved.

---

## Oil's Critical Paradox

The most technically detailed portion of the interview focused on crude oil, where Hunt sees a significant downside setup developing.

"We're seeing oil break to the downside in our opinion. And everyone will go, 'Oh, that's great. That's such good news. It'll be peace and love and kisses.' I don't see that."

### The Technical Picture

Hunt walked through a detailed technical analysis of Brent crude, identifying what he calls an "ascending inverted HVF" pattern—a downside continuation structure that he describes as one of the most difficult technical formations to call.

The setup began when oil was rejected at the 112 level. "That's a slap in the face," Hunt said. "It's a violent rejection. We all bought into the theory about the Hormuz situation—that we're not going to get all these goods and everybody became so stagflationary-focused that everyone came in and was buying oil at any price. Any dip they were buying oil, buying oil, buying oil."

The result was a steep ascending line of support beneath the price. But here's the key observation: "All that buying at no point was able to beat the slap in the face high. You got rejected there and sold off. You got rejected sooner here."

Trump's announcement of a 14-day peace agreement created an extraordinary gap—oil fell roughly $11 in a single move, from around 103 down to 92. "That's an exceptional $11 gap," Hunt noted. "Only the exhaustion gap into the blowoff high here recently is anything like it."

The target: sub-68 on Brent, with overperformance to the downside.

### Why Oil Can't Sustain $250

Hunt pushed back hard against the calls for oil to reach 250 or even 300 dollars. "The problem you have is that the economy can't survive that price, and it's already far weaker than it was in the subprime era 2007 when it traded at 150. And that was on a stronger dollar than it is today—a less proliferated dollar."

He's right to emphasize the dollar's deterioration. Since 2007, we've had the subprime proliferation of dollars, followed by the COVID proliferation. The dollar is significantly weaker. That $150 oil in 2007 isn't comparable to a nominally similar price today.

But the deeper issue is oil's unique position in the economy. Unlike gold, oil cannot sustainably rise at the pace of monetary debasement because it's too critical to everything.

"Gold will show the full inflation of the chicanery and fraud of the financial system," Hunt explained. "Oil won't do that because as your assets are collapsing in value and people have less money in their house, less money in their stock portfolios, everything is debasing, their life and the cost of living is climbing, food is going up a lot. What you end up getting is they have far less disposable income. They actually become negative cash flow and they are net selling assets at the bottom of the market."

The result: consumption of petroleum-based products—fuel for engines, planes, boats, cars, and motorbikes—"goes substantially down."

**"The problem oil has is that whenever it runs too high, it forces inflation that then becomes too expensive for the cost of living that everyone has, and you get a deflationary result."**

This is the oil paradox. Push it too high, and you destroy the demand that sustains it.

### The Gold-to-Oil Relationship

Hunt introduced a long-term framework that challenges conventional wisdom about oil as an inflation hedge. He showed a gold-versus-oil ratio chart going back decades and identified what he calls a "watermelon smile"—an asymmetric smile pattern heading upward.

"People will always choose stock market—it always beats gold, plus it pays dividends," Hunt said. "They'll choose a certain year. They never choose 1968 to 1982. That's not the decade and a half they're choosing. They'll choose 1980, or they'll choose 1900, you know, to catch the roaring '20s."

During that 1968-1982 period, gold wasn't a great investment because "we were getting away with the fraud and we were paying a higher consumption tax. Rockefeller tax is oil during this period."

The relative valuations were absurd. In 1999 through 2008, "for the amount of financial fiat creation, the monetary metal should have gone up and the consumption commodity should have gone down because people's ability to continue to consume in this ponzinomics gets reduced—the retail world, not the billionaire class."

Now that dynamic is reversing. "The one thing everybody's going to do is they're going to say, 'I want to preserve my money, buy gold.' And the other thing they're going to do is they're gonna have less money for wasting on consumption—all consumption, oil."

Hunt's long-term forecast: "If we're at 20,000 gold in, let's say, two and a half years' time or three years or whatever, and we're at 20,000 gold, that's four times on gold. The oil price won't be four times what it is today. It won't be 400 if it's 100 today. It just won't, because it's not going to be proportionately spread across all the assets because there'll be less people with money to drive lots of cars, do lots of trips, fly lots of planes."

Oil at 125 could be equivalent to 75 dollars today once gold reaches 10,000.

## Gold's Path Forward

Turning to gold in isolation, Hunt identified the current structure as a broadening pattern—a megaphone that represents the "bull pole."

"You're in the middle. You can drift a little bit. It's not going to be necessarily that strong. You can go up and you can come down, but in the end, you're getting to this double line and you're breaking it. And that will get you in the gold bull sooner than if you wait for a new high."

The longer the consolidation, the lower that capping line comes—making eventual breakout more achievable.

---

## Silver: The Better Setup

Hunt made a compelling case that silver is technically superior to gold at this juncture.

"Gold corrected 21.37%. Silver corrected 47%. Nearly half. You're at 121, then you're at 61." But the second selloff told a different story: "Silver was 36%. So it was less than the first time."

This dynamic—harsher initial drop but shallower subsequent correction—created a falling wedge pattern in silver that Hunt views as more bullish than gold's broadening structure.

"Silver is always going to have a higher beta downside," he acknowledged, but the current setup shows "the volatility was dissipating sooner."

Silver has broken out of that falling wedge. Hunt expects it to reach "the north of 100 to 120 range again" over time. He doesn't rule out another dip, but the longer-term trajectory is clear.

---

## The Gold-Silver Ratio: A Decision Point

The ratio currently sits around 60, testing a crucial decision point. Hunt had previously identified 50 as a major level, and when price reached it, "You came down, made a broadening continuation pattern on 50. You had another here. You traded down, traded down, but it was the bottom. You came up, up, up, up, and then you smashed back above 50."

Now the question becomes: does the ratio surrender 60 to the downside, or push back higher? A descending triangle in the ratio—meaning the ratio falls—would be bullish for both metals, with silver outperforming.

---

## Miners: The Beneficiary

There's an elegant logic to what Hunt sees unfolding: if oil trades down while gold and silver grind higher, miners benefit on both ends.

"Energy, apart from salaries, I would imagine energy is either their biggest or second biggest cost—digging holes in the ground and moving people up and down. A lot of work, a lot of energy in that."

Wheaton Precious Metals has already demonstrated this dynamic, trading only 11% off its highs while gold and silver are down significantly more. "That's a big tell."

The broader mining complex (GDX) remains further from its highs—Hunt estimated roughly 43-44% off—but these patterns are typically continuation patterns to the upside.

## May Crash and the Geopolitical Picture

Hunt sees the whole energy dynamic turning "more into a financial economic crisis coming in May. The typical analogy of 'sell in May, come back in St. Leger's Day.' I can see that happening. So I can see the tech market coming down."

On the geopolitical front, his assessment was stark. The Russia-Ukraine conflict has devolved into "a messy stalemate." The Russians aren't marching on Kyiv, and the Ukrainians aren't taking Moscow. But this isn't necessarily reassuring.

"It's like an open vein for fraud, organ trade, money laundering, and bungs for politicians."

His concern centers on European intentions. "European leaders had no issue with Zelensky and his team press-ganging Orthodox Christian Ukrainian males off the street in a kidnap environment and then dragging them off to a meat grinder where they would be dead in 3 days after a day's training. They had no issue with that."

Hunt sees this as preparation. "The reason I think they have no issue with that is they're intending to do similar, hence my recent YouTubes on the German 17 to 45 years registration. Europe is hellbent on a war with Russia."

The recent Hungarian election—where Orban, who had maintained dialogue with Russia and resisted Western pressure, was replaced—deepens his concern. "If I were Poland, I'd be worried."

## The Uncomfortable Advice

The interview ended with Hunt delivering an unusually direct message to Western viewers:

"Dear Western European individual watching this in Western nation, Canada, America, Europe, UK, Australia, New Zealand—you need to leave your country because you will be treated as future fodder for this war machine. And part of the goal is to hollow you out."

He recommended looking at nations "that you might have looked down on. You should never look down on a nation, but that may not have been the first world, Western nations where you can have a better quality of life, live more cheaply and be happy and have health and good food and supply chain and less urbanized existence."

Hunt emphasized that this isn't about desire but necessity: "I had to do it. I didn't do it when I had lots of money. I had to do it. I had to move. I had to go where I was treated better."

The financial advice flows from this worldview. Build wealth, Hunt argued, not because of desire for riches, but "because you want to entrench your future lifestyle and some of the freedoms you now take for granted. Further to that, you need to protect that wealth because they are coming for it. The hidden hand wants it and they have pre-ordained agendas that are absolutely disclosed at the very highest levels."

The solution is optionality: "multiple possible plans and jurisdictions. There is no workman that has one tool in his toolbox. I live in one country, bank with one nation, bank with one state. It's always going to work just fine for me. No, they're not your friends. Government aren't your friends. You need a toolkit."

## Conclusion

Francis Hunt's April 2026 assessment presents a coherent thesis: the global financial system is engaged in sustained monetary debasement that benefits those with hard assets and punishes those dependent on wages and nominal savings. Gold will capture this debasement most fully. Oil cannot, due to its critical role in economic function. The dollar weakens, but oil's nominal rise won't match gold's—meaning oil falls in real terms even as its dollar price inches higher.

The immediate technical picture favors downside in oil and continued grinding upside in precious metals. Miners may offer the most leveraged exposure to this divergence. And for those seeking to preserve freedom and wealth, the path forward requires geographic and financial optionality that most Westerners currently lack.

As Hunt put it: "If the city's burning down, get out. You know, given the choice—if it's now your life, would you leave? Yes. Is it your children's life? Would you leave? Yes. If it's your entire future and your financial—would you leave? You'll find a way."
