## Market strategist sees US recession following AI unwind, with oil set to rise amid Middle East tensions

David Woo, CEO and founder of David Woo Unbound, recently appeared on a financial show to discuss his market outlook, warning that the unwinding of the AI trade could send the NASDAQ down 20% and push the US economy into recession. The discussion covered everything from semiconductor stocks to geopolitical tensions in the Middle East.

---

### The AI Bubble: A Question of Timing

Woo has maintained a bearish stance on AI for months, and his conviction appears to be strengthening. "My problem is that Mythos has actually dramatically increased regulatory risk for frontier models," he explained, referring to Anthropic's latest AI model. The concern stems from the model's capabilities being so advanced that national security concerns have led to restricted access, with only about 150 users currently able to access it.

The strategist pointed to several warning signs for the AI sector. Meta's recent push into cloud computing, for instance, signals that even major tech companies are struggling to monetize their AI investments. "He's basically saying that we bought too many chips, we overbuilt our AI infrastructure and now we cannot pay for it because our models are no good," Woo noted.

The market dynamics are shifting from training chip demand to inference chip demand, which is far less differentiated and can be manufactured by more players, including Chinese companies. "Anybody can make inference chips because inference chips, unlike learning chips, are relatively less complicated," Woo explained.

---

### Economic Data: Soft Jobs Numbers and Real Yields

The recent jobs report showed only 57,000 non-farm payroll additions against a Wall Street consensus of 115,000—a significantly weaker-than-expected figure. However, Woo urged caution about reading too much into a single data point.

"The whisper number probably was lower than the consensus number published on Bloomberg," he suggested, noting that the bond market's modest rally indicated investors are recognizing the economy isn't performing as poorly as some had feared.

Gold has struggled this year primarily due to rising real yields, which Woo identifies as gold's biggest obstacle. "Real yields have been going higher in the US primarily because the US economy has been doing better than expected, and I think largely because of the AI bubble," he said. The real yield environment has pushed gold's traditional safe-haven appeal to the sidelines.

---

### The Path Forward: Capex Unwinding and Market Impact

Woo expects the AI capex spending story to unwind in the second half of the year, with the Q2 earnings season from Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta serving as a critical inflection point.

"Even a little tiny hint that the capex story is over, I'm telling you NASDAQ will be down 20% like in no time," Woo declared. "There's no doubt in my mind."

The strategist drew parallels to the dot-com era: "It was not until the dotcom bubble burst, the dollar started to go down and then the Fed started to cut interest rates. That's when gold got this huge boost. You know, that lasted a whole decade."

High-bandwidth memory stocks like Samsung, Micron, and SK Hynix have provided market support recently, but Woo expects this rally to fizzle out. "New capacity is coming online by the end of this year," he warned, noting that Chinese competition in this space is intensifying.

---

### Geopolitical Tensions: Oil and the Middle East Ceasefire

Woo sees oil heading higher in the near term, particularly after political events in the Middle East settle. The ceasefire deal between Israel and Lebanon, brokered by Secretary of State Marco Rubio, represents a significant development that could reshape regional dynamics.

"This was the first time the Lebanese government essentially accepted and acknowledged Israel's right to exist as a sovereign state," Woo explained. The deal signals a fracture between Iran hawks and dovish elements within the Trump administration, potentially complicating nuclear negotiations with Iran.

More concerning, according to Woo, are revelations about the recent Iran conflict. Satellite imagery from Planet Labs has revealed the extent of damage to Iranian military sites—and what remains intact.

"There's no doubt in my mind the US got its ass kicked in Iran," Woo stated bluntly. "This was actually the first proxy war between the United States and China. If Iran got this far, it had a lot to do with China." Iranian drones and missiles demonstrated unprecedented precision, largely due to integration with China's BeiDou satellite navigation system.

---

### Bitcoin and Crypto: Limited Upside

Woo sees little upside for Bitcoin in the current environment. While the cryptocurrency benefited from Trump administration support during the election cycle, the president's actual priorities lie elsewhere.

"Trump is not interested in crypto. Trump is interested in stablecoins because stablecoins are pegged to the US dollar," Woo explained. The administration's crypto legislation focus has been on requiring stablecoin issuers to purchase US Treasuries—supporting the dollar rather than Bitcoin.

With Bitcoin having already retraced 50% from its highs and no meaningful regulatory support expected before the next midterm elections, Woo remains unconvinced. "I definitely don't see very little upside for Bitcoin," he said.

---

### Current Positions and Key Trade Ideas

Woo maintains several targeted positions reflecting his macro outlook:

- **Long gold and oil**
- **Short NASDAQ 100**
- **Long the 5-year/30-year Treasury steepener** (long 5-year, short 30-year)

These trades are interconnected, Woo explained. "If you tell me where NASDAQ is going to be, where gold is going to be, where oil is going to be, I'll tell you exactly what's going to happen to all other markets. These two things are going to control all other markets."

The strategist acknowledged uncertainty but emphasized that investors should understand these relationships. "If the AI bubble has burst, the Fed is going to be cutting rates and then the curve is going to steepen."

---

### Summary

David Woo's core thesis centers on the imminent unwinding of AI-driven capex spending, which he believes will trigger a 20% decline in the NASDAQ, push the US economy into recession, and eventually create conditions favorable for gold. Meanwhile, geopolitical tensions in the Middle East—particularly surrounding Iran—suggest oil prices are headed higher. The strategist remains short Bitcoin and other cryptocurrencies, viewing stablecoins as the only digital asset with administration support.

For investors seeking asymmetric opportunities, Woo's message is clear: watch NASDAQ and oil. These two markets, he argues, will determine the trajectory of virtually every other asset class in the coming months.
