In a wide-ranging conversation on *Mining Stock Daily*, David Hunter—Chief Macro Strategist at Contrarian Macro Advisors—laid out one of the most consequential macro frameworks of his career. Building on a thesis he has refined for decades, Hunter argued that the world is approaching the end of a historic 44-year secular bull market, after which a deflationary global bust will usher in a new cycle dominated by commodities, precious metals, and industrial reshoring.

## A Lifelong Contrarian Approach

Hunter, who began his career in 1973, emphasized that true contrarianism is not about being perpetually contrary. "Contrarianism is not knee-jerk contrary," he explained. "It's at the important inflection points—whether tops or bottoms—where I tend to be most contrarian." Throughout the current cycle, institutions have "fought this market all the way up," skeptical that a bear market rally in October 2022 could sustain. That persistent skepticism, he argued, has been part of the "wall of worry" fueling the advance.

He raised his equity targets once again: S&P at 10,000, Nasdaq at 36,000 (up from 32,000), the Dow at 67,000, and the Russell 2000 at 4,000. "The bull market's still intact," he said. "I see nothing to indicate a top here."

## A Broadening Market—Not Just the Mag 7

While headlines continue to focus on AI and semiconductor stocks, Hunter pushed back on the narrative that the rally remains narrow. Financials, he noted, have broken out of a long consolidation and look "early in their move," with a target of 90 on the XLF implying potential 50% upside from current levels. Industrials and basic materials have quietly built strong trends, partly driven by reshoring, grid buildout, and data center expansion.

Although AI enthusiasm continues to dominate financial media, Hunter pointed to Micron—a stock he noted was up roughly 3.6 to 3.8 times in just four months—as an example of parabolic price action. He warned, however, that semiconductor memory cycles always end, and that double- or triple-ordering at peak demand has historically preceded sharp reversals.

## The Mechanics Behind the Melt-Up

Hunter attributed much of the secular bull market's longevity to structural forces that emerged in the mid-1980s. As baby boomers shifted from home buying to retirement savings, the financial industry built an entire ecosystem of passive funds, ETFs, and online brokerage platforms around the mantra of "time in the market, not timing the market." The pandemic accelerated retail participation further, with sports bettors migrating to stocks and platforms like Robinhood democratizing access. Combined with leveraged ETFs and a record level of retail engagement, these mechanics have added fuel to the upside.

## Warning Signs: Leverage, Private Credit, and a Coming Reckoning

Despite his near-term bullishness, Hunter is acutely aware of the risks. "Debt is the leverage on the economy, derivatives are the leverage on the markets," he said, noting that both are "far, far higher" than at the 2008-2009 peak. He flagged private credit as a potential "canary in the coal mine," pointing to recent concerns from PIMCO about cracks emerging in the credit system. Private equity, which he described as essentially "leveraged buyouts prettied up," is also loaded across endowments and pension funds and is likely to exacerbate any downturn.

Institutions and hedge funds remain heavily skeptical—Hunter noted record short positioning—which in his view is itself fuel for further upside before the eventual top.

## The Global Bust

Hunter believes the long secular bull market that began in August 1982 is nearing its end. "Markets often end in a parabolic move, and I think we'll see this thing have a final blow-off," he said. He warned of a forthcoming "global bust"—a historic 80% peak-to-trough decline in equities, the largest since the 1929 crash. The bust, he argued, will be deflationary and could involve the biggest economic downturn in the U.S. since the Great Depression.

Central banks, despite stated reluctance, will ultimately be forced to print aggressively. Hunter projected the Fed's balance sheet could expand from roughly $6.7 trillion today to as much as $30 trillion, with central banks worldwide creating an estimated $50 trillion in new money. While this will initially stabilize the system, it will eventually ignite an inflation cycle "the likes of which we have never seen in this country."

He drew parallels to the early 1980s, when T-bills hit 21% and inflation exceeded 20%. In the next cycle, he expects 25% inflation, long-bond yields potentially reaching 18-20%, and T-bills well above 20%. By the end of this decade or early next, the U.S. will likely face a debt service crisis where interest obligations exceed the federal budget. "That's the collapse of the system," he warned, with a "game-changing depression" arriving in the mid-2030s.

## Commodities, Gold, and the Next Leadership Cycle

Coming out of the bust, Hunter expects markets to be led by commodities and industrial stocks, not consumer stocks. Massive money printing combined with continued reshoring, grid expansion, and data center buildout will push industrial demand beyond available supply. The resulting inflation environment will compress P/E multiples back toward single digits, but producers of real goods—particularly commodities—will be the primary beneficiaries.

For precious metals, Hunter is targeting $7,000 gold and $200 silver before the bust. In the bust phase, he expects gold to pull back roughly 50% (to around $3,500) and silver to fall 70-75% (toward the $50 breakout level from the 2000 high). After the bust, however, his longer-term targets are striking: **$20,000 gold and $1,000 silver by the early 2030s**. He also sees copper potentially reaching $8 in this cycle and possibly $20-30 in the next, with oil at $500 by the 2030s.

## What to Watch

Hunter advised listeners to focus primarily on sentiment. Indicators like the Bank of America portfolio manager survey, as well as mainstream financial commentary, will provide clues. "When you hear strategists and portfolio managers saying 'Oh, this thing has several years to run'—that's when I want to run the other way," he said. The key signal will be when skeptics finally capitulate and everyone is "all in."

He cautioned against trying to time the exact top, noting that the current market could still deliver an additional 30-40% upside before reversing. Chasing the market down, however, is essential. "Know yourself," he advised. "Don't be greedy."

David Hunter remains bullish in the near term, raising his S&P target to 10,000, but warns that the 1982 secular bull market is nearing a parabolic top. He forecasts a historic 80% global bust, deflation, and ultimately massive money printing that will push gold to $20,000 and silver to $1,000 by the 2030s—with commodities leading the next market cycle.
