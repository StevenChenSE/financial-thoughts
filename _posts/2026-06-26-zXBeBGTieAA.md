## Key market signals suggest deflation despite global inflation concerns

The trading landscape has shifted dramatically in 2025. After a year when stocks, gold, and Bitcoin all reached new all-time highs, the tone has completely changed. Market participants are now grappling with whether to buy the dip or brace for further declines. Kevin Stewart, managing partner at stockta.com, offers a data-driven perspective on what comes next.

## The Deflation Paradox

While headlines scream about inflation and central banks signaling hawkish stances, Stewart's algorithmic analysis tells a different story. His system detected early deflation signals through two key indicators: the US dollar and BNDD, a deflation ETF.

"This is very different from last year in 2025 when everything was on the uptrend and everything was reaching new all-time highs," Stewart explains. The algorithm's strongest signals pointed to USD strength and deflationary pressure, while the weakest performers included crypto, oil, and agricultural commodities.

This divergence raises questions about whether the Federal Reserve will actually need to raise interest rates. "If he doesn't need to raise rates because inflation, according to our stuff, looks like it could be slowing, is he going to actually have to raise rates? Is he doing it to prove he's tough or is he going to kind of follow the data?" Stewart ponders.

## Oil and the Iran Factor

Oil prices hover around $70 per barrel WTI, a level many traders use as a baseline for inflation expectations. However, Stewart sees oil as having already peaked for this cycle. "Unless we get boots on the ground in Iran, I think oil's peaked for this cycle at least."

The Trump administration's Iran peace deal appears to have fundamentally altered the energy equation. Stewart believes Iran will want to maximize oil revenue with loosened sanctions, keeping prices contained. His trading range analysis shows oil bounded between $63 and $90, with the current trend making lower lows and lower highs on bounces.

## S&P 500: The Critical Level to Watch

The S&P 500 remains near 7,300 points, but Stewart notes that excluding the Magnificent Seven tech stocks and AI-related companies, the index is essentially flat for the year. His algorithm identified Amazon, AVGO, Netflix, and MSTR among the five weakest market signals.

The key level Stewart is monitoring: 693 on the SPY. "If it breaks below that, I think we could have a bigger sell-off." That represents approximately 6% downside from current levels.

"I'm not super bearish, but I do think we're going to have a little bit of a pullback here, at least for July," Stewart adds. The Iran situation and subsequent market gaps have broken technical momentum, requiring time to reset.

## A Cautious Approach

Stewart is staying predominantly in cash, waiting for clearer signals. His risk management philosophy limits downside on any position to 35%—he avoids holding assets down 30-50% like some investors do.

"I'm not trying to be pessimistic, but I worry about a possible ramp up of aggression with Iran again or something else derailing the peace deal or the Strait of Hormuz closing," he explains.

## Political Calculus and Market Implications

The 2026 midterm elections loom as a crucial factor. Prediction markets on Koshi show nearly even odds between a Democratic clean sweep and divided government. Stewart believes Republicans need several conditions to hold power: oil under $85, strong employment numbers, and relief from AI-related layoffs dominating headlines.

Perhaps most provocatively, Stewart suggests Trump might step down if political support evaporates. "I could see Trump quitting. Like, I'm done. I tried to drain the swamp. I tried to make America great again. The system wouldn't let me."

## Key Takeaways

The confluence of deflation signals, contained oil prices, and technical weakness creates an uncertain environment. Stewart recommends patience and discipline—maintaining cash positions while waiting for the market to establish clearer direction. Whether it's Fed policy, geopolitical developments, or election outcomes, multiple forces are at play, making this a period where careful risk management may prove more valuable than aggressive positioning.
