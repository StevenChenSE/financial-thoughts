## Portfolio manager Adrien Day analyzes gold mining valuations, central bank tailwinds, and emerging risks in AI and private credit at the Rule Symposium.

At the Rule Symposium, Adrien Day, president of Adrien Day Asset Management and portfolio manager of the Europacific Gold Fund, provided a comprehensive update on the precious metals sector and broader market dynamics. Day highlighted that while macro headwinds persist, gold mining valuations have reached attractive levels, and structural demand from central banks continues to support the metal. He also warned of significant risks brewing in artificial intelligence valuations and the private credit market.

### Gold Mining Valuations Hit Historic Lows

Day described the current environment for gold equities as a "perfect setup" for a strong move, citing a convergence of low prices, depressed valuations, and weak sentiment. The GDX gold mining index has fallen approximately 35% since January. According to a recent report by Scotia, the gold mining space is now in the lowest 20th percentile of its historic valuation range across nearly every metric.

Sentiment indicators reflect extreme pessimism. The bull/bear sentiment index for gold equity miners hovers around 7%, with one day recently showing zero bullish sentiment. Day noted that despite these conditions, investors are not taking advantage of the lows; withdrawals from the GDX continue.

"For clients that opened in January, we are definitely buying more now," Day said. He also revealed that a conservative global account, originally allocated 20% to gold three years ago, has increased its allocation to 35% following recent purchases. While Day remains cautious about a potential gold price pullback to $3,600 if support levels break, he views the current equity valuations as a significant opportunity.

### Macro Pressures and the Federal Reserve

The macroeconomic landscape presents what Day calls the "worst trifecta": a higher dollar, elevated CPI, and rising bond yields. These factors have shifted Fed funds futures dramatically, with expectations swinging from rate cuts to rate hikes over the past few months. However, Day pointed out that real interest rates remain negative, which supports gold.

Recent data suggests some easing of pressure on the Federal Reserve. The latest jobs report was weak and subject to downward revisions, and declining oil prices may feed through to lower gasoline and CPI numbers in July and August. Day also emphasized the resilience of the U.S. economy, noting Q1 GDP was revised up to 2.1%. He argued that the U.S. is now oil self-sufficient, reducing the recession risk associated with oil price spikes compared to historical episodes, though a spike to $120-$150 could still cause issues.

Day discussed the potential impact of Kevin Warsh, noting Warsh's private sector experience and understanding of market mechanics. Day praised Warsh's awareness of the Fed's reliance on backward-looking data and the need for real-time signals from corporate conference calls. Current signals are mixed: consumer resilience remains strong, but suppliers are signaling price increases, and a low quits rate suggests underlying weakness in the labor market.

### Central Banks Drive Structural Gold Demand

A major tailwind for gold remains central bank buying. Day explained that central banks are reducing dollar exposure due to concerns over fiscal irresponsibility and the weaponization of the dollar, a trend that accelerated after the confiscation of Russian assets. Gold is attractive because it is an asset that is no one else's liability and cannot be easily confiscated.

"Central banks are really only returning to their traditional primary reserve asset," Day observed, noting that gold was dominant in reserves prior to the dollar's surge in the late 1990s. He also highlighted Tether as a significant recent buyer. These institutional buyers are largely price-insensitive, driven by strategic rather than speculative motives. Day added that a resolution to the Iran situation could weaken the dollar, providing another tailwind for gold.

### M&A Dynamics and the Critical Role of Management

Regarding mergers and acquisitions, Day cautioned investors against expecting immediate deals when prices are low. Senior mining companies prefer to acquire junior assets once they are de-risked, possess a social license, and have permits in place. Seniors are willing to pay a premium for these attributes and are not rushed unless competitive tension exists.

Day emphasized that management quality is paramount. "Bad management can always screw up," he warned, citing risks such as drilling in the wrong locations, poor community relations, and excessive dilution. He stated he would always prefer a company with good management and no project over one with bad management, ample capital, and a good project. Adrien Day Asset Management buys stocks based on value and fundamentals, viewing potential M&A as "icing on the cake" rather than a primary thesis.

### Emerging Risks: AI Valuations and Private Credit

Looking ahead, Day identified two significant risks: the unwinding of the AI bubble and vulnerabilities in private credit. He described AI stocks as "grossly overvalued," pointing out that many companies building data centers lack clear business models for monetization and face shortages of energy and resources like copper and silver. Major tech stocks, including Nvidia, Microsoft, and Amazon, have already seen declines of 15% to 20% recently.

In the private credit sector, Day highlighted the risk of self-marking loans. Private companies can value loans at 100 cents on the dollar until a borrower defaults, at which point values may drop to zero. This practice, combined with leverage, creates opacity and potential for sudden write-downs. Day drew a parallel to the 2007 financial crisis, where the blowup of a mortgage company preceded the collapse of Bear Stearns and Lehman by a year. He warned that the absence of recent credit events does not mean the risk has dissipated.

### Summary

Adrien Day concludes that gold mining stocks offer a compelling entry point due to depressed valuations and sentiment, supported by robust central bank demand. While the macro environment presents challenges, the U.S. economy shows resilience, and real rates remain negative. Investors should remain vigilant regarding overvalued AI equities and the latent risks in the private credit market, which could trigger broader market volatility.
