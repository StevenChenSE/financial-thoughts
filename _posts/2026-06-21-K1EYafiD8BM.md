## How AI Power Demand Is Creating an Insane Supply Shock in the World's Most Critical Metal

For decades, investors have speculated about what could replace oil as the world's most important commodity. Now, with WTI crude trading above $80 per barrel and the global economy accelerating toward a digitalized, decarbonized future, the answer is becoming clear: copper.

Ian Harris, CEO of Copper Giant, recently joined the show to discuss why he believes copper—and metals broadly—are positioned to become the world's next oil. His thesis isn't just theoretical. It's playing out in real time, driven by explosive AI demand, government stockpiling, and a structural supply deficit that the market is only beginning to price in.

"Metals are the next oil, and the biggest component of that composite is copper," Harris explained. "If it carries an electron, that electron is going over copper at some point during its path."

This simple fact—copper's unmatched electrical conductivity—makes it indispensable for everything from electric vehicles and data centers to the infrastructure powering the AI revolution. And the world is waking up to this reality faster than most investors realize.

## The AI Power Demand Explosion

The numbers behind copper's ascent are staggering. AI power demand currently sits at 4.4% of global electricity consumption and is projected to surge to 12% by 2028—a near-tripling in just a few years. This represents a dramatic shift for an industry that historically grew at only 2% annually.

"We're moving as fast as possible into a digitalized world, a digitalized economy," Harris noted. "The difference now is that it's a race. It's existential for companies and countries. I only see it accelerating."

Projections suggest AI could consume 14% of the United States' total power generation by 2030. This exponential growth in electricity demand translates directly into exponential demand for copper, which serves as the nervous system of every data center, power line, and electrical infrastructure piece supporting this digital expansion.

BHP's research underscores this reality. A study of Microsoft's $500 million data center facility in Chicago found it used 2,177 tons of copper—equivalent to 27 tons per megawatt of power capacity. When you consider the thousands of data centers either under construction or planned globally, the copper demand picture becomes almost overwhelming.

## Market Dynamics: Copper Breaking Free

On the price front, copper is trading at approximately $6.50 per pound and has been behaving remarkably differently than historical patterns suggest. While gold has pulled back from its highs since early March, copper has moved in the opposite direction since late April—a stark divergence that Harris views as structurally significant.

"This is the classic cycle that starts in the commodity, moves to the majors, moves to the mid-tiers, moves to the juniors as the cycle becomes more mature," Harris explained. "We're only in the very beginning of this rotation on the equity side."

What's particularly noteworthy is copper's correlation with technology stocks. Overlay copper prices with Nvidia, and the two charts move in tandem far more closely than copper tracks gold. This makes sense given copper's utility in AI infrastructure, data centers, and electric vehicles—all sectors where Nvidia serves as a barometer for broader market sentiment.

"Copper is no longer just trading like Dr. Copper," Harris observed. "It's trading to a structural deficit that is anticipated and an insatiable demand that won't care about price."

## Governments Are Secretly Accumulating

Perhaps the most telling signal of copper's strategic importance is how governments worldwide are positioning themselves. Contrary to the perception that copper isn't treated as a national security issue, Harris insists the opposite is true.

"The United States set up specific tariffs just on copper and revised those recently. It's been designated as a strategic metal," Harris stated. "We see stockpiling in the United States and China. At the beginning of two years ago, there were four strategic stockpiles in the world. Now there are six, and the major difference is they were kind of just there before—they didn't do anything. Now they're active."

This mirrors the old adage Harris cited from Frank Juice: "Don't buy what the government tells you to buy. Buy what the government's buying."

Governments are accumulating copper before the rest of the planet wakes up to its importance. They don't want to create a scandal or drive prices higher through public purchases, so the stockpiling happens quietly. But the evidence is unmistakable—every major economy is taking steps to secure copper supply.

Harris went further, describing China's position: "China's assassin's mace has been copper because they have such a stranglehold on it. It's such an important metal that crosses all the different areas. You might need antimony for bullets, germanium for robotics—I'm making stuff up. But you need copper for everything. You need it for electric vehicles. You need it for AI. You need it for anything that carries electricity."

## The Investment Opportunity in Miners

For investors looking to capitalize on copper's ascent, Harris sees the mining sector as historically undervalued and positioned for significant appreciation. Mining equities currently represent approximately 1% of total market capitalization—a historical low that creates substantial upside potential.

"Mining is a way to leverage metals prices," Harris explained. "If it costs you $1,000 to produce copper and copper is $2,000, you're making a lot of money. When it goes to $3,000, you're making twice as much. You're naturally leveraged to the metals price."

Yet despite gold miners posting record profit margins—reportedly double or triple the next closest sector—investor sentiment remains bearish. The GDX (gold miners ETF) has seen a rerating lower since gold's pullback, with most components still trading near all-time highs.

This sentiment disconnect creates opportunity. As money rotates from overheated tech positions into hard assets, miners could see significant inflows. Harris expects a classic rotation pattern: "It starts out in the commodity, it moves to the majors, moves to the mid-tiers, moves to the juniors."

Additionally, vertical integration trends in technology companies could drive further demand for copper producers. Harris noted that companies are already approaching Copper Giant about offtake agreements and investment opportunities—even before preliminary economic assessments are complete.

"When we see a deglobalized world, the underlying assets that start those production chains are the big winners. Even just little drips coming into the mining sector could quickly turn into a flood."

## Summary

Copper is emerging as the defining commodity of the AI era. With power demand from artificial intelligence projected to triple by 2028, governments secretly building strategic stockpiles, and the mining sector trading at historical valuation lows, the setup for copper and copper miners appears compelling.

The world is waking up to what copper means for the future—and smart money is positioning ahead of the crowd. As Harris put it: "Buy what the government's buying."

For investors willing to look beyond the current bearish sentiment in the mining sector, copper represents not just a trade, but a generational opportunity to own the infrastructure backbone of the digital economy.
