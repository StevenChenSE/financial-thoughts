## The S&P 500: A Historical Benchmark

The S&P 500 has long been the benchmark for US equity performance. Historically, its chart reflects a consistent upward trajectory dating back to the 1800s, punctuated by occasional bubbles and crashes, such as the dot-com bust and the 2008 financial crisis. While there was a notable consolidation period between 2000 and 2011—often referred to as a "dead period"—the market has generally been "all gas, no brakes" relative to global peers.

Even through recent volatility, including the drop from 4,792 to 3,436 during the 2020-2021 correction and the COVID-19 crash, the long-term trend remains bullish. To understand current market dynamics, however, one must look beyond absolute performance and analyze relative performance against other global asset classes.
