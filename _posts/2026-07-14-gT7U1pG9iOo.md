## Simon Hunt Analyzes the Coordinated Escalation Against US Energy Supplies and the Path to Global Stagflation

### Introduction
In an interview recorded on July 13, 2026, Capital Cosm host Danny speaks with guest Simon Hunt about the escalating geopolitical and economic landscape. Hunt presents a comprehensive analysis suggesting that the current war footing between the United States and Iran is part of a broader strategy orchestrated by Russia and China. He argues that this conflict targets America's critical dependency on sour crude for diesel production, creating conditions for a severe economic downturn, a potential global debt reset, and a shift toward a gold-backed monetary system led by China.

### The Iran Conflict and US Diesel Vulnerability
Hunt identifies Ahmed Vahidi, the commander-in-chief of the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC), as the true power behind Iran's political apparatus, military, and management of the Strait of Hormuz. He emphasizes that Iran's primary leverage is its control over the strait, which is vital for the flow of sour crude.

The United States faces a critical vulnerability due to its refineries' reliance on sour crude to produce diesel and other essential products. Hunt warns that US stocks of sour crude will be depleted within a month or two at current disposal rates. He notes that while the market price of a barrel of oil may reflect optimism, product prices in the physical market have already risen by over 30%. Compounding the energy crisis, Russia has cancelled all exports of diesel fuel, further straining global supplies. Recent US military actions have targeted Iranian infrastructure, including a railway bridge built by China and the port of Chabahar, which is used by India, China, and Russia.

### Orchestration by Beijing and Moscow
Hunt contends that the escalation is not accidental but coordinated. He states that when Vladimir Putin visited Beijing, he and Chinese leadership finalized the orchestration of Iran's war with America. The strategy involves simultaneous escalation in the Middle East and Ukraine to stretch Western resources thin.

In the European theater, Hunt describes the conflict as a war between NATO member countries and Russia, fueled by deep missile incursions into Russia using components from named European nations. He reports that Putin has issued strong warnings to NATO, threatening retaliation against military establishments in European countries if the supply of weapons and technicians continues. Hunt observes that while the US may be fighting with outdated equipment, adversaries like Iran, China, and Russia are preparing for a new style of warfare utilizing drones and missiles rather than traditional aircraft carriers and destroyers.

### China's Financial Strategy and the Gold Link
China is navigating significant internal economic challenges, including local government debt accounting for 42% of a total debt stock of $66.67 trillion. Hunt explains that China's strategy involves devaluing the domestic yuan to allow incomes, asset prices, and cash flows to rise relative to the debt. This devaluation is being managed by increasing the yuan-gold price.

Beijing is expanding gold trading facilities and yuan operations in Hong Kong to execute this policy. Hunt predicts that China will increase the yuan-gold price by 10% to 20% over the next three to six months. Regarding the currency's external value, he notes the yuan remains stable around the 6.5 level against the US dollar, with a projected range of 6.3 to 6.9 through next year. From 2028 onwards, Hunt expects the yuan to appreciate significantly against the dollar, alongside other currencies, as the US Dollar Index (DXY) weakens.

### US Economic Forecast: Recession and Stagflation
With total US debt at $140 trillion and global debt at $264 trillion, Hunt forecasts a recession for the US and G7 economies next year. He anticipates that the 10-year Treasury yield, currently around 4.6%, may drop temporarily to about 3% due to recessionary pressures before spiking again.

Hunt warns of a period between 2028 and 2032 that could resemble the stagflation of 1978 to 1982. Driven by high oil prices and food inflation—exacerbated by shortages of sulfuric acid and weather conditions—consumer inflation could reach double digits by 2030 to 2032. In this environment, long-dated Treasury yields could also hit double digits, potentially crashing the highly leveraged system. Hunt suggests this crisis could create the conditions for a "reset" of government debt, possibly involving stablecoins or Bitcoin, a scenario he associates with the views of Kevin Walsh. In contrast, he believes China and Russia will anchor their stability to gold, with Hong Kong serving as the central hub for this new gold-linked order.

### Regional Flashpoints and Market Dynamics
Hunt addresses several other geopolitical and market issues. Regarding Taiwan, he maintains that the island will likely fall to the mainland through domestic political developments rather than invasion, unless the US introduces offensive weaponry that forces Beijing's hand.

He also discusses reports of Turkey selling S-400 air defense systems to the UAE. Hunt suggests Russia would strongly oppose this move, as it would escalate tensions and potentially neutralize Iran. He highlights the divergent stances within the UAE, noting that Abu Dhabi leans pro-US and pro-Israel, while Dubai has quietly supported Iran through financial transactions and historical trade routes, raising questions about the unity of the emirates.

On financial markets, Hunt points to the narrowing spread between US and Japanese 10-year yields as evidence of orchestration to prevent a collapse of the yen carry trade. He expects a weakening of the US dollar and a strengthening of the yen as authorities manage these risks.

### Conclusion and Preparation Advice
Simon Hunt concludes with urgent advice for individuals. He recommends stockpiling non-perishable foods and converting garden spaces into kitchen gardens. He warns that food prices are poised to soar and shortages are likely due to the combination of high energy costs and supply chain disruptions. Hunt emphasizes that the geopolitical maneuvers and economic indicators point toward a fundamental restructuring of the global order, urging preparedness for the challenges ahead.
