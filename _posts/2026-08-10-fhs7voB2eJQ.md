### Culture, technology, and competition at a company aiming to replace the keyboard

## Introduction

Google is coming for us. Those are the opening words of Tanay, CEO and co-founder of WhisperFlow, a company valued at $2 billion. Five years ago, WhisperFlow began as a hardware startup. Today, it is replacing millions of people’s keyboards with their voice. Tanay’s journey back into voice technology started when he was 11 years old: he and a friend built one of the world’s first voice assistants, reaching 2.5 million users. Google sent a cease and desist, and the project was shut down.

Sixteen years later, Tanay is back, running a major AI company and facing the same giant. In this behind-the-scenes look, Tanay shares how WhisperFlow works, how it builds products, and why voice is becoming the primary interface for interacting with machines.

## From Silent Speech Hardware to Software-First

When WhisperFlow started, it aimed to solve a specific problem: how to use voice discreetly around other people. The team spent three years building a wearable brain-computer interface (BCI) prototype.

How the hardware worked:

- A headset with electromyography (EMG) sensors
- Sensors picked up tiny electrical impulses from speech muscles
- The system interpreted what you were thinking about saying, not just what you said out loud

Early prototypes were bulky, even taped to Tanay’s face. The goal was to miniaturize the device into an earbud form factor for use anywhere.

However, once connected to assistants like ChatGPT, Siri, and Alexa, the experience “sucked.” The team realized they needed a system that could take raw mental input (“mental RAM”) and turn it into structured, ready-to-send content.

That system became Flow—an operating system for the hardware device, now a standalone product. Flow powers the core of WhisperFlow today, enabling high-quality voice-to-text, formatting, and context-aware outputs.

## Why Voice Is the Future Interface

WhisperFlow’s thesis is straightforward: voice will become the primary interface for interacting with machines.

Reasons:

- People speak faster and more naturally than they type.
- Voice reduces friction in creating structured content.
- Context-aware AI can organize and act on spoken input at scale.

Big incumbents like Google and Apple are investing heavily because:

- They understand this is the next major consumer interface.
- Whoever owns this layer gains massive leverage over how people use devices.
