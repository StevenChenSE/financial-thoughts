## A software engineer who hasn't written a line of code since November

Boris Churny has a confession that would have been unthinkable just two years ago: he hasn't edited a single line of code by hand since November. Every day, he ships 10, 20, even 30 features—run by five autonomous AI agents working simultaneously. And he believes this isn't an anomaly; it's the future of software development itself.

"Coding is largely solved," says Churny, head of Claude Code at Anthropic, in a recent podcast appearance. "I imagine a world where everyone is able to program. Anyone can just build software anytime."

His perspective carries weight. Under his leadership, Claude Code has become a defining force in the AI coding revolution—one that is fundamentally reshaping what it means to be a software engineer.

---

## The Numbers That Shook the Industry

The scale of transformation is staggering. A recent SemiAnalysis report revealed that **4% of all GitHub commits worldwide are now authored by Claude Code**. The prediction: by the end of this year, that number will reach one-fifth of all code commits globally.

The report's stark assessment: "While we blinked, AI consumed all software development."

Spotify recently made headlines when it announced that their best developers haven't written a line of code since December—thanks to AI. The pattern is repeating across the industry: the most accomplished senior engineers, including Churny himself, are stepping away from traditional coding entirely.

For Churny, these numbers are both surprising and expected. "These numbers are just totally crazy, right? Like 4% of all commits in the world is just way more than I imagined," he admits. "But the crazy thing for me isn't even the number that we're at right now—it's the pace at which we're growing. If you look at Claude Code's growth rate across any metric, it's continuing to accelerate."

The growth metrics are particularly remarkable:

- **Daily active users doubled in just one month**
- **Private repository usage significantly exceeds public commits**
- **Growth is accelerating, not plateauing**

Anthropic recently raised at a valuation exceeding $350 billion, with Claude Code serving as a primary driver of that growth.

---

## The 200% Productivity Leap

Churny doesn't just observe these changes from a managerial distance—he lives them daily. His productivity metrics paint a picture of transformation:

- **200% increase in productivity per engineer**
- **Multiple features shipped per day** through AI agent orchestration
- **Zero manual code editing** since November
- **Five agents running concurrently** during the interview itself

Yet Churny frames this not as displacement, but as liberation. When asked if he misses writing code, his answer reveals a fundamental shift in perspective:

"I have never enjoyed coding as much as I do today because I don't have to deal with all the minutia."

The tedious aspects of software engineering—the syntax errors, the boilerplate, the context-switching between implementation and debugging—fade away. What remains is problem-solving at a higher level of abstraction.

---

## From Software Engineer to "Builder": The Coming Transformation

For product managers and aspiring developers listening to this shift with concern, Churny offers a candid prediction:

"By the end of the year, everyone's gonna be a product manager and everyone codes. The title of software engineer is gonna start to go away. It's just gonna be replaced by builder—and it's gonna be painful for a lot of people."

This isn't hyperbole. It's an observation based on watching the technology evolve in real-time. The question "should I learn to code?" is becoming increasingly irrelevant.

"In a year or two, it's not gonna matter. Coding is largely solved."

The implications extend beyond individual careers. Entire organizational structures built around traditional software development roles will need to adapt. Companies that embrace this shift early will gain significant competitive advantages in shipping products and iterating on features.

## The Philosophy Behind Claude Code

### Starting with Safety

Churny's path to this work runs through Anthropic's defining mission: AI safety. Before joining the company, he worked in Big Tech but wanted to help shape the future of AI in a meaningful way.

"The thing that drew me to Anthropic was the mission," Churny explains. "It's all about safety. When you talk to people at Anthropic, just like find someone in the hallway, if you ask them why they're here, the answer is always going to be safety."

This mission-driven culture proved essential. When Churny briefly left Anthropic for Cursor—the AI-powered code editor—he discovered that even excellent products and teams couldn't replace the sense of purpose he found in safety-focused work.

"I just know personally it's something I need in order to be happy," he reflects. "Whatever the work might be, no matter how exciting, even if it's building a really cool product, it's just not really a substitute for that."

He returned to Anthropic within two weeks.

### Understanding the Layer Underneath

A recurring theme in Churny's approach is deep technical understanding. When he joined Anthropic, he spent time not just building products but studying the research side—specifically, post-training processes.

"For me as an engineer, I find that to do good work, you really have to understand the layer under the layer at which you work," he explains. "If you're working on product, you want to understand the infrastructure, the runtime, the virtual machine, the language—whatever that is, the system that you're building on. If you're working in AI, you just really have to understand the model to some degree to do good work."

This philosophy shapes everything from Claude Code's architecture to its development priorities.

## The Evolution: From Coding to Computer Use

Claude Code's development follows a trajectory that Churny describes as foundational to Anthropic's approach:

1. **Coding** — Models become excellent at writing code
2. **Tool Use** — Models learn to use external tools and APIs
3. **Computer Use** — Models learn to act autonomously in digital environments

The first breakthrough came during Churny's initial prototyping. He gave the model a bash tool and asked it what music he was listening to—a question he wasn't even sure the system could answer.

"The model was given this tool, and it figured out how to use it to answer this question that I had," Churny recalls. "There's no instruction telling it to use this tool for this or do whatever. The model just... figured it out."

This moment crystallized something fundamental: the AI wasn't just a conversational partner or a code generator. It was learning to act in the world.

With Claude Code's newest capabilities, the transition extends beyond engineers. Non-technical users can now interact with AI that actually performs actions—checking Gmail, managing Slack, handling tasks autonomously. The "acting" capability that was previously confined to coding contexts now reaches across all functions.

---

## The Terminal Bet: Why Simple Wins

The initial design choice that seemed most controversial became one of Claude Code's greatest strengths: building for the terminal first.

"When I think about it, part of the reason Claude Code works is this idea of latent demand, where we bring the tool to where people are, and it makes existing workflows a little bit easier," Churny explains.

The terminal approach was initially questioned internally. IDEs were the expected form factor. A command-line tool seemed too primitive, too alien for sophisticated development work.

But the team decided to stick with it, and the reasoning was prescient: **the model was improving so quickly that no other form factor could keep up**. Terminal-based tools could evolve faster than integrated environments.

"That wasn't really the intention," Churny admits. "From the start, I built it in a terminal because for the first couple months, it was just me. So it was just the easiest way to build."

Today, Claude Code is available across platforms:

- iOS and Android mobile apps
- Desktop application
- Website interface
- IDE extensions
- Slack integration
- GitHub integration

The terminal was the proving ground. Now the technology meets developers wherever they work.

## The Humbling Journey of Discovery

Perhaps the most striking aspect of Churny's reflection is the humility with which he describes the past year:

"Just reflecting back, it's been such a humbling experience. We just keep learning from our users. And the most exciting thing is like, none of us really know what we're doing. And we're just trying to figure it out along with everyone else."

Early internal reception was muted. When he announced the project internally, it received "two likes"—the measure of enthusiasm at the time. The concept seemed too different, too unconventional.

But once released externally, adoption went "vertical almost immediately." From Anthropic's internal hit to a global tool used by startups and Fortune 500 companies alike, the trajectory surprised even its creator.

"The single best signal for what we're doing is just feedback from users," Churny says. "I've been surprised so many times. It's incredible how fast something can change in today's world."
