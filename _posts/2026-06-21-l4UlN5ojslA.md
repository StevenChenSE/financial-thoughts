## Wall Street veteran warns of underlying economic weakness even as stocks surge on geopolitical optimism

On Monday, June 15th, financial markets experienced what traders are calling a historic day. President Trump signed a landmark deal with Iran, triggering a sweeping market rally that pushed the Dow Jones Industrial Average up approximately 600 points intraday—a record for the session. The S&P 500 climbed 1.5%, while the NASDAQ surged 3%, fueled partly by SpaceX IPO excitement. Bitcoin added 5%, and gold gained 2.5% in a broad-based move higher.

But Todd "Bubba" Horowitz, founder of bubbatrading.com and veteran market trader, isn't joining the celebration. Speaking on the episode, Horowitz delivered a sobering counterpoint to the euphoria: "We're in trouble," he warned, urging traders to prepare for an inevitable pullback even as the Iran deal sends markets to new highs.

## The Contrarian Case: Why the Rally Won't Last

Despite remaining long on the stock market indices, Horowitz is actively hunting for opportunities to establish short positions. His concern stems from what he sees as a fragile economic foundation beneath the surface rally.

"Look at the credit card debt—over 13% of people are 90 days past due on their credit card bills," Horowitz explained. "Six percent of the population is defaulting on car loans, and 4% are defaulting on their mortgages. And we've only just begun."

The true unemployment picture, measured by the U6 gauge, sits around 8.5%, according to Horowitz, with jobs being lost at record numbers. The K-shaped economy continues to hollow out, with the top tier shrinking while economic pressures mount on everyday Americans.

"There's no real excitement out there, and we're losing jobs at record numbers," he said. "The small business guy is struggling. You've got chains closing, restaurants closing, sporting goods stores—the longer-term economy does not bode well right here."

## Trading Strategy: Where Horowitz Sees Opportunity

For Horowitz, the current rally represents a selling opportunity rather than a reason to increase long exposure. He's already short crude oil and plans to maintain that position until prices reach approximately $85 per barrel.

"I've been short since $110 and $115, and I will continue to stay short because we have an overwhelming glut of oil in this country," he stated. "Now that they have oil flowing again from Iran, that'll build an even bigger glut. I think before the year is over, we're going to see oil back in the 60s."

His analysis draws on historical precedent: out of 39 previous deals with Iran, every single time oil has made a lower high and lower low during that cycle. "I don't think that's going to change right now," Horowitz added.

Beyond commodities, Horowitz is eyeing the NASDAQ and S&P 500 for potential short positions. While he acknowledges stocks will likely trade higher ten years from now, he expects a 5% to 10% sell-off within the year.

"We saw the first look at that about 10 days ago on a Friday when the NASDAQ was down 6%," he noted. "We got this bounce, and let's see how it plays once the news is run through the market. I'm looking for a place to sell into this market and actually take a short position as a trader."

## The S&P 8,000 Question: Speed Matters More Than Level

Market observers have been speculating about the S&P 500 reaching 8,000 points—a level the index is approaching from its current position around 7,570. For Horowitz, the destination matters less than the journey.

"If it goes to 8,000 in the next five days, I'd be an automatic seller," he said. "It would be just too fast, too parabolic. Those ranges can't last."

The veteran trader distinguishes between sustainable, grinding rallies and parabolic moves driven by FOMO (fear of missing out) trading. Parabolic advances, he suggests, are unsustainable and represent prime selling opportunities.

"If it's a slow, dull grind, then I would have to be much more patient," Horowitz explained. "But if it's the Robin Hood traders chasing it up like they did with GameStop and other stocks, that would be a market I would sell into."

## Gold and Silver: The Real Bright Spots

Interestingly, Horowitz's most bullish outlook isn't on equities but on precious metals. Gold, which lagged the S&P recently, may have already found its bottom.

"Gold did exactly what I'm talking about in crude oil and exactly what I'm expecting to happen in the S&P," Horowitz observed. "It got up too far too fast, too parabolic, and now you're paying the penalty."

He identifies the lows around $4,100, then $4,065 to $4,056, as the bottom. "I think that's the low. I think gold will now go higher again, and it would not surprise me at all if gold got back to $5,500—even an outside shot at $6,000 before the year's over."

His top trading recommendations are gold and silver, with silver having taken a 50% haircut from its highs. He's also bullish on platinum.

## How to Trade the Iran Deal Rally

The Iran agreement represents a significant geopolitical shift, with markets responding enthusiastically to the prospect of reduced tensions and increased oil supply. However, Horowitz advises caution for traders.

"The volume is so light that it's hard to want to get involved," he said. "You have to be very particular from a trading aspect. Dull markets, again, they grind higher. There's not enough action to take them to the downside, and there's not enough seller participation right now."

The natural bid in markets comes from retirement accounts, IRAs, 401ks, and pension funds that continue to flow in regardless of market conditions. But Horowitz warns: "If we got a little bit of a spike in volume, I would definitely be a seller. I am not a new buyer here with new money."

## Investor Versus Trader: Different Playbooks

Horowitz draws a sharp distinction between investment and trading strategies. For long-term investors, staying invested is non-negotiable.

"If you missed the 10 biggest days over the last 40 years, you made 50% less money than you would have made if you'd have been in the entire time," he emphasized. "Investors should always stay in."

But for active traders, the current environment demands profit-taking. "If you're a trader, you're definitely taking profits here," Horowitz advised. "At some point, you have to become more robotic and say, 'I've reached my profit target. Either I'm going to put a tight stop in and make sure I lock up those profits, or I'm going to take the profit and move on to the next trade.'"

## What About Gas Prices?

With traders on prediction markets pricing a 41% chance that U.S. gas prices will exceed $4.60 per gallon by year-end, Horowitz offers a more optimistic view for consumers.

"I think we've seen the highs at the pump for buyers and consumers," he said. "We will not see those ridiculous high prices. You had a panic in the beginning, a gigantic move in oil, and we've seen subsequently every rally in crude has been sold. If the Iran deal is maintained and peace is maintained, we should get lower gas prices."

## Key Takeaways

Horowitz's core message is clear: enjoy the current rally, but prepare for turbulence ahead. The fundamental economic picture—rising defaults, weakening employment, and a contracting middle class—remains deeply concerning despite the geopolitical tailwind from Iran.

For traders, his playbook is straightforward: take profits on rallies, look for shorting opportunities in the NASDAQ and S&P, maintain a bearish stance on oil with $85 as a potential re-entry point to cover, and consider adding exposure to gold and silver which may be forming long-term bottoms.

For investors, the advice is simpler still: stay the course, but be prepared to weather the inevitable 2% to 5% pullback that Horowitz believes will come—whether markets ultimately move higher or not.
