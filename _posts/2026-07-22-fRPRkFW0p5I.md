## A Deep Dive into the Metals Market with Kevin Wadsworth and Patrick Karim

**By Danny (Capital Cosm)**

In a comprehensive technical analysis session, Kevin Wadsworth and Patrick Karim from Northstar Batcharts Service join host Danny to dissect the current state of the precious metals market. As of July 2026, gold and silver are undergoing a significant correction, leading many investors to question where the bottom lies. The experts argue that while the short-term trend is bearish, the long-term bull era remains intact, provided specific technical conditions are met.

## Gold: Basing Out or More Bleeding?

Kevin Wadsworth begins by reviewing the gold roadmap, highlighting the massive "cup and handle" pattern that previously drove prices from the $2,000 area to a peak of approximately $5,600. He notes that as gold surpassed $4,000, it became historically stretched from its three-year moving average, making a substantial correction inevitable.

Currently, gold is testing critical support levels just below the psychological $4,000 mark, with specific interest around $3,760. If this support fails, the next major support lies near the rising three-year moving average at approximately $3,128. Wadsworth emphasizes that the correction is likely to be time-consuming. Using a six-month candlestick chart, he points out that the current bearish momentum resembles patterns seen in 2021, which required six to twelve months to stabilize. Consequently, he advises that confident trading entries should only occur after a breakout is confirmed on the daily, weekly, and monthly timeframes.

## Silver: The Challenge of Catching Bottoms

Patrick Karim takes over to discuss silver, cautioning against the common investor urge to "catch bottoms." He argues that most of the price correction occurs close to the peak, but the subsequent base-building phase takes significantly longer. Using historical examples from the 2000s, Karim illustrates that after a hard correction, silver often requires double the time to form a breakout pattern that challenges all-time highs.

Currently, silver is bouncing between $55 and $57, but Karim states there is no evidence of a bottom yet. He looks for a break above $64 on the 8-hour chart as the first sign of momentum shifting. He also highlights the "distance from moving average" indicator, which shows silver is still contracting toward its mean rather than expanding, signaling that the counter-trend rally necessary to launch the next leg up has not yet begun.

## The Macro Backdrop: Yields and Capital Rotation

The conversation shifts to the broader macroeconomic environment, specifically the relationship between metals, the stock market, and yields. Karim draws a parallel between the current market structure and the late 1960s. He points to the 10-year yield chart, which has broken out of a 40-year descending expanding wedge. This breakout suggests a fundamental shift in debt dynamics and interest rates, with yields likely to rise further.

Rising yields act as a floor for the stock market in nominal terms due to the destruction of fiat purchasing power, but they create pressure on equity valuations relative to hard assets. Karim emphasizes that global yields, including those in Japan, Germany, and the UK, are converging and rising, indicating a macro force that central banks cannot easily suppress.

## Ratio Charts: The Key to Timing Entries

A significant portion of the analysis focuses on ratio charts, particularly Gold/S&P and Dow/Silver. Karim identifies a critical support line on the Dow/Silver ratio. He argues that until this ratio breaks below this support line, silver is not outperforming the stock market, and investors should avoid allocating capital to it.

He explains that the true "super capital rotation event" occurs when this ratio breaks down, a move that historically has led to massive outperformance for silver relative to equities. While the current correction is painful, Karim views it as a necessary phase. He warns that if the ratio breaks upward instead, it would signal a continuation of stock market dominance, a scenario he considers low probability but technically possible.

## Diversification Within the Commodity Bull Era

Wadsworth concludes by discussing diversification within the broader commodity complex. He notes that platinum and uranium have not yet broken out relative to gold and silver. However, as the bull era resumes, these assets, along with base metals like copper and energy stocks, will likely take turns outperforming. He advises investors to use ratio charts to identify rotation opportunities, moving capital into underperforming assets when they show signs of breaking out.

## Summary

The consensus from Wadsworth and Karim is that the precious metals market is in a corrective, base-building phase that may take months or even years to resolve. Investors are advised to avoid catching bottoms and instead wait for technical confirmation of a breakout. The rising yield environment and historical ratio patterns suggest that a massive capital rotation from equities to hard assets is likely, but patience and strict technical discipline are required to navigate the interim volatility.
