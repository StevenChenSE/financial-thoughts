*March 19, 2026* — In a world where headlines scream of conflict and markets tremble, conventional wisdom often links war with soaring gold prices. But according to two leading financial analysts, that narrative is dangerously misleading.

In a recent VRIC Media panel, **Michael Oliver**, founder of Momentum Structural Analysis, and **Alistair Mloud**, publisher of the *Mloud Finance* newsletter, argued that the current sell-off in gold and silver—amidst the escalating war in Iran—is a temporary distraction. The real driver for precious metals, they insist, is not geopolitics, but the relentless, structural degradation of fiat currencies.

## The "War Trade" Narrative: A Historical Misreading

The discussion opened with host Jesse Day noting that gold had fallen to around $4,500 and silver to $70, a move many attribute to a "sell-everything" scramble for liquidity due to the Iran conflict.

Michael Oliver was quick to dismiss the war-gold correlation. **"War is always… people like to link war with gold. They usually interpret it as bullish for gold, which is nonsense,"** he stated. **"Historically, you go back and look and the correlation isn't there."**

He challenged the idea that gold must fall simply because equities are falling. **"The notion that… gold's got to go down too because the stock market's going to go down… is hype. It's a news story that has no real lasting impact."**

Oliver pointed to the 2008 financial crisis as a prime example. After the stock market crash in October 2008, gold had a severe corrective month. **"Then they separated again and gold continued up for another three, four years… whereas gold had already… doubled from there."** These geopolitical shocks, he argues, are mere "interruption moments," not the core rationale for gold's long-term trend.

Alistair Mloud concurred, framing the current sell-off as a predictable, short-term reaction from a Keynesian-driven investment community that has lost its "lodestar." **"Investors tend to look back… within their own experience,"** he said, likely recalling gold's sharp drop in 2008 before its historic rally. In times of panic, the dollar is the "easy way out"—a liquid, understandable asset for fund managers to flee to, even if it's not the wisest long-term choice.

## The True Engine: Monetary Debasement

Both experts redirected the focus to the fundamental driver: currency debasement.

**"What ultimately… drives gold,"** Oliver emphasized, **"is continued monetary degradation."** He instructed viewers to pull up an M2 money supply chart from the Federal Reserve. **"You want to know why gold goes up? Take a look at it."** He argued that the S&P 500's gains since 2000 have largely just mirrored the decline in the dollar's purchasing power, while gold has vastly outperformed.

Mloud framed it in even starker terms. The war hasn't created a new crisis but has accelerated an existing one: the collapse of the fiat currency system. **"The key to watch,"** he advised, **"is bond yields of highly indebted G6 nations."** He warned that spiking yields signal a loss of faith, which is independent of money supply. **"You could actually see [the dollar's value] go to zero without any increase in the quantity of money… if the circumstances drove it that way."**

## The Coming Inflationary Tsunami

Mloud painted a dire picture of the war's second-order effects, which he believes will unleash a massive inflationary wave later in 2026.

*   **Food Crisis:** He highlighted fertilizers, a third of which come from Gulf oil exports now halted. With China also halting fertilizer exports to protect its own food security, a global shortfall at the start of the growing season is imminent.
*   **Supply Chain Collapse:** Noting that a professor estimated **"45,000 products are dependent on oil,"** from pharmaceuticals to plastics, Mloud foresaw widespread shortages.
*   **Monetary Response:** Central banks will be forced to create more credit to pay for these higher prices, leading to yet higher prices—a classic inflationary spiral. **"The purchasing power of the dollar is going to go down."**

This environment, he argues, is why you "stack gold and silver." All commodities, priced in dollars, are set to soar.

## Beyond Metals: The Case for Real Assets

When asked where to preserve capital outside of precious metals, both analysts pointed to tangible, "real-world" assets.

Oliver called commodities **"vastly cheap compared to even their own history… compared to the stock market… compared to gold."** He remains bullish on oil, having called a buy signal in January when it crossed $63, though he cautions the war news has created an exaggerated move that may see a sharp pullback. **"The big picture says green light… it's a pullback in a bull market."**

Mloud analyzed commodities through the lens of gold. **"Priced in gold… commodities are very, very underpriced."** He estimates that to return to historical norms, a basket of commodities would need to rise roughly **300% in gold terms**. A fiat currency debasement on top of that could send prices stratospheric.

## A System on the Edge

The conversation concluded with a warning about systemic fragility. Oliver pointed to the perilous technical position of major bank stocks, suggesting **"all it takes is one… half story about a credit problem"** to trigger panic. In response, central banks will have no choice but to print. **"They can talk tough all they want, but… their house is going to burn down if they don't."**

Mloud believes the Iran conflict is a point of no return, uniting the Iranian people and ensuring a prolonged war aimed at removing U.S. influence from the Gulf. This structural shift, combined with moves to bypass the dollar (like Iran allowing oil passage for yuan payments), accelerates the fiat system's demise.

**"Imagine at the end of this year 2026… bond yields over 15%… inflation at 25%. I can't rule it out,"** Mloud stated. **"We face… the collapse of the fiat currency system."**

### The Bottom Line

For Oliver and Mloud, the current dip in gold is noise. The signal is the decades-long expansion of money supply now hitting an inflection point, supercharged by a war that disrupts global commodity flows and shatters faith in government paper. They argue that investors waiting for a wartime spike in gold are missing the point. The real trade isn't about conflict; it's about seeking refuge from the accelerating decay of the very foundation of the modern financial system.
