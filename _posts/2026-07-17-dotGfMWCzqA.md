## John Feneck: Buy Metals Now, Correction Not Bear Market

### Expert Analysis on Market Timing, Sector Rotation, and Top Picks in Gold, Silver, Tungsten, and Copper

John Feneck, founder of Feneck Consulting, joins host Gary Bohm on *Metals and Miners* to deliver a compelling thesis for investors in the precious metals sector. Despite recent volatility and depressed prices, Feneck argues that the market is experiencing a major correction within a bull market framework, not a bear market. With sentiment at historic lows and macroeconomic risks stabilizing, Feneck asserts that this is the optimal time to accumulate high-quality equities across gold, silver, tungsten, and copper.

## Core Thesis: Bull Market Correction, Not a Bear Market

Feneck's primary message is clear: "This is the time you buy equities." He rejects the bear market narrative, emphasizing that the current price action represents a significant correction within a broader bull trend. He notes that risk levels today are substantially lower than they were at the end of February or early March. 

A key indicator for Feneck is investor sentiment, which he describes as crushed, hitting zero on sentiment indices recently. He views this abandonment of the sector as a contrarian buying signal. "You have to make money, you've got to take risk," Feneck states, noting that the risk-reward profile has shifted favorably for metals investors. He encourages a "sniper" approach to investing, advocating for selectivity rather than broad ETF purchases, as value exists in specific names that have been oversold.

## Macro Outlook: Tech Cracks and Sector Rotation

Feneck highlights emerging cracks in the technology sector as a precursor to a broader market shift. He points to weakness in major names like MicroStrategy (MSTR), Samsung, Micron (MU), and IBM as signs that the growth-oriented rally is losing steam. He warns investors to watch the Semiconductor Index (SOX) and the Russell 2000 (IWM) as leading indicators; breakdowns in these indices often preceded major market declines in 2000 and 2008.

The analyst anticipates a rotation from growth to value, a trend he sees beginning in assets like Bitcoin and MSTR. He identifies a potential window between September and November for broad market weakness, driven by historical seasonality, geopolitical tensions, and Fed uncertainty. Following such a downturn, Feneck expects a sector rotation into miners, supported by outstanding earnings and free cash flow generation. He notes that the free cash flow of large-cap gold miners in the GDX is now ten times better than it was during the COVID peak in 2020.

Regarding Federal Reserve policy, Feneck advises investors to monitor the CME FedWatch Tool and key data releases like CPI, PPI, and Non-Farm Payrolls. He emphasizes that falling real rates provide the perfect backdrop for gold. While the Fed remains data-dependent, the recent moderation in inflation prints offers relief, though he cautions that rate expectations remain dynamic.

## Gold: Supply Stagnation, M&A Wave, and Asset Class Status

The supply side of the gold market presents a structural imbalance. Global gold mine output hit a record 3,672 tons last year, yet this figure has been essentially flat since 2018, even as gold prices have nearly tripled. Feneck explains that the reserve pipeline is broken; majors are depleting reserves faster than they are replacing them. The average time from discovery to production has ballooned to 18 years, compared to six years in the 1980s, meaning new deposits discovered today may not produce until the mid-2040s.

This scarcity is driving a massive M&A wave, with over $140 billion in mining deals committed, the busiest period since 2011. Majors are acquiring developers and explorers at earlier stages to secure ounces, removing buildable projects from the market and increasing the scarcity value of existing assets.

Feneck also addresses the practice of high-grading, where producers mine the highest-grade ore first to boost margins. While this inflates current free cash flow and suppresses All-In Sustaining Costs (AISC), it is unsustainable. He expects AISC to rise in the future as grades decline, but argues that current gold prices provide a buffer. Furthermore, he stresses that gold has evolved into a recognized asset class, with major institutions like Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan now recommending allocations of 10% or more, a stark contrast to the indifference seen in 2011.

## Silver: Leverage and Mexico Improvements

Silver remains in a supply deficit for the sixth consecutive year. Feneck discusses the dynamics between primary silver producers and byproduct producers. While byproduct silver from copper, zinc, lead, and gold mines has near-zero marginal costs, primary silver producers offer greater leverage to price movements. For investors seeking exposure to silver's upside, primary producers provide higher beta, though with increased volatility.

Feneck highlights improvements in Mexico, a top silver jurisdiction. Under new presidential leadership, the permitting process is reportedly streamlining, offering a catalyst for development projects. He notes that companies like First Majestic and Silvercorp are well-positioned to benefit from these developments.

**Silver Picks Mentioned:**
*   **First Majestic Silver (AG):** High silver mix offering significant leverage.
*   **Silvercorp Metal (SICO/SICOF):** Primary silver mines in Mexico with improving fundamentals.
*   **Sonora Gold (SGO/SMOFF):** Pivoting to exploration while awaiting open-pit permits; experienced management team.

## Critical Minerals: Tungsten and Copper Opportunities

Feneck dedicates significant attention to tungsten, a critical metal with severe supply risks. Approximately 86% of global tungsten production is controlled by China, North Korea, and Russia. The U.S., which has had no domestic production since 2015, relies on tungsten for essential military applications, including body armor and armor-piercing ammunition. Feneck describes tungsten as a "conspiracy theory metal" due to lack of transparency and price suppression, but he sees equities as the best way to gain exposure. He notes increased government interest, with discussions underway regarding non-diluted funding for domestic projects.

**Tungsten Picks Mentioned:**
*   **Western Star Resources (WSR/WSRIF):** High cash balance relative to market cap; tungsten projects in the U.S. and gold in Canada.
*   **Guardian Metals (GMTL/GMTLF):** Secured non-diluted funding; preliminary feasibility study shows exceptional payback periods.
*   **Spartan Metals (SPRMF):** Projects in Nevada and New Mexico with pro-mining jurisdictions.

Copper is also highlighted, quietly making all-time highs driven by the AI trade and broader industrial demand. Feneck suggests that copper equities offer value, particularly for companies with proximity to major producers ("closology"), which increases the likelihood of M&A activity.

## Strategic Approach: Sniper Investing and Cost Basis

Feneck concludes with actionable advice for investors. He advocates for a disciplined process of buying positions multiple times to lower the average cost basis, rather than attempting to time a single perfect entry. He warns against holding stocks out of spite after a poor entry and urges investors to reassess holdings based on 3-to-6-month potential.
