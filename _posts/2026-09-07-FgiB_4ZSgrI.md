## The Unwinding of the Japanese Yen Carry Trade
A critical theme in the discussion was the potential collapse of the yen carry trade. Grummes explained that for decades, hedge funds and institutional investors have borrowed low-yielding Japanese yen to invest in higher-yielding US assets (stocks and Treasuries). However, with the Japanese 10-year yield rising to 2.905% and the 30-year yield remaining elevated, the spread between US and Japanese yields is narrowing.

"The gap between the 30-year on the Japanese and the US has been coming down," Danny observed, noting the trend peaked in October 2023. Grummes warned that if the yen continues to strengthen, these carry trades will become unprofitable, forcing investors to close positions. This could lead to a rapid reversal of capital flows, potentially spilling over into a global currency crisis and heavily impacting stock markets. He pointed out that the US Treasury has already had to intervene, bailing out the Japanese yen and bond market in early August, indicating the situation is not yet resolved.
