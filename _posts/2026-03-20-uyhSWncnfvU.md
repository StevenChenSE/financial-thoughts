*March 19, 2026 – VRIC Media*

In a recent VRIC Media panel, momentum analyst Michael Oliver and finance publisher Alistair Mloud challenged conventional wisdom linking gold prices directly to geopolitical conflict. As gold and silver sold off amid escalating war in the Gulf—with gold near $4,500 and silver around $70—the experts argued that these moves are noise, not signal, for long-term precious metals investors.

## War and Gold: A Misleading Correlation

Host Jesse Day opened the discussion by noting the sell-off in precious metals, often attributed to a “sell everything” scramble for liquidity amid the Iran conflict. Michael Oliver was quick to dismiss the popular narrative.

“War is always people like to link war with gold. They usually interpret it as bullish for gold, which is nonsense,” said Oliver. “Historically, you go back and look and the correlation isn’t there… The notion that gold’s got to go down too because the stock market's going down and everything going margin calls etc. is hype. It’s a news story that has no real lasting impact.”

Oliver emphasized that what ultimately drives gold is **continued monetary degradation**, not headlines.

“Get an M2 chart. You want to know why gold goes up? Take a look at it,” he advised, pointing to the relentless expansion of the money supply. He illustrated this by comparing the S&P 500’s performance since 2000 to the rise in M2, noting that the stock market’s gains largely reflected dollar debasement, while “gold vastly outpaced that.”

## The Real Driver: Fiat Currency Debasement

Alistair Mloud concurred, framing gold’s role as a mirror to fiat currency decay.

“Gold reflects the degradation of the fiat currency,” Mloud stated. He suggested the current sell-off might be a short-term, knee-jerk reaction similar to 2008, when gold fell sharply before tripling over the next few years. “Investors tend to look back… they're looking back towards that 2008-2009 financial crisis.”

He explained that in moments of panic, the dollar becomes the default “easy way out” for institutional managers—it’s liquid, understood by clients, and doesn’t require complex justification. However, he believes a pivot is coming.

“There does come a point where people start considering the longer-term effects. What is how is this going to affect the dollar?… At that stage they then start considering whether they should move out of the dollar into gold.”

## A Tipping Point for Inflation and Bond Markets

Both analysts warned that the Gulf conflict is accelerating an existing crisis. Mloud predicted the war could last most of the year, with severe inflationary consequences.

“The rate of inflation we're going to see later on this year is going to be massive,” he said, highlighting disruptions to global fertilizer supplies—a third of which originate from the Gulf—and China’s decision to halt exports. “That leaves the rest of us with massive shortfalls at the beginning of the growing season.”

He expanded the scope, noting that roughly 45,000 products depend on oil, including pharmaceuticals. “The purchasing power of the dollar is going to go down. Why? Because they will produce the credit in order to pay the higher prices.”

A critical signal to watch, according to Mloud, is the bond market. “The bond yields on the 10-year… in all the major currencies are shooting up and they look like breaking out on the upside. When that happens, then you can see that the equity markets are going to collapse… They are in the biggest bubble in history.”

## Why This Sell-Off May Be a Buying Opportunity

Despite the downturn, the panelists saw little fundamental weakness in the metals markets.

“If you look at the amount of loose speculative bull positions in both these markets, they're virtually none,” noted Mloud. “The open interest in the silver contract on COMEX is as low as it's been for 20 years… The speculators are not there. This isn't going to shake out anything.”

He believes the turnaround could be swift. “If you haven't got in… you’ll probably miss it.”

## Beyond Metals: The Case for Real Assets

Oliver reiterated his published warning that “cash is not a place to be.” He urged investors to look at **real-world assets**—commodities and tangible resources—which he sees as historically cheap.

“The stuff you walk on, stuff in a field, stuff under the ground, copper, base metals, grains. Those prices are vastly cheap compared to even their own history… They're vastly cheap compared to gold.”

Using oil as an example, Oliver noted his firm identified a technical buy signal in January when it crossed $63, well before the war. “It got exaggerated,” he said of the recent spike, warning that a pullback is possible on any positive news, but the long-term bull trajectory remains intact. “The big picture says green light.”

## The Final Stage: A Fiat System Under Siege

Mloud’s closing remarks were stark. He argued the attack on Iran has “brought forward the collapse of the fiat currency system,” a process China has insulated itself against. He pointed to Iran’s strategic endurance and its move to demand yuan for oil passage as game-changers.

“The Iranians are working to a plan… They want Satan out of their backyard. They're going to do it… Trump has started something he cannot stop.”

The ultimate trigger, he suggests, will be bond yields of heavily indebted G6 nations. “Imagine at the end of this year 2026… bond yields over 15%, inflation at 25%. I can't rule it out.”

## The Bottom Line

The consensus from the panel was clear: **geopolitical war is not a reliable gold trade; the monetary war is.** Short-term sell-offs driven by liquidity scrambles and headline fear are distractions from the core narrative of systemic currency debasement. For investors, the advice was to look through the noise, focus on money supply and bond yield breakouts, and consider positions in precious metals and deeply undervalued commodities before the crowded rush for real assets begins.

As Michael Oliver concluded, recalling 2008: “What did gold do? Right back to its high and then ultimately higher highs. That's your chief beneficiary of this crisis that's building… Expect it to be more chaos theory type. I think we're on the edge of that right now.”
