*March 18, 2026* – In a recent interview on Capital, market analyst Francis Hunt, alongside guest Eric Yung, unpacked the dramatic swings in gold and silver prices and issued a stark warning: the engineered crisis in the Strait of Hormuz could lead to severe inflation, liquidity squeezes, and even the return of lockdowns in the Western world.

## The Gold Correction: A Healthy Cooldown

Gold recently fell below the key psychological level of $5,000, with silver dropping under $80. However, Hunt, known as "The Market Sniper," isn’t alarmed.

> "Not for me. I think we could skim quite a bit lower. We could even get to four sixes [$4,600]," he stated, clarifying that his medium-to-long-term bullish view on gold only changes if it breaks below $4,400.

He presented a technical chart showing gold is currently consolidating within a range defined by a blow-off high of $5,600 and a subsequent low of $4,400. The recent drop below $5,000—the 50% Fibonacci retracement of that range—is seen as a natural shakeout of volatility after a violent $1,300 rally from late January.

> "This was a high volatility period... I think you need a down leg and I'm quite comfortable with it coming all the way back down here," Hunt explained, noting the market's energy is "dissipating and going to sleep." He believes this calm will set the stage for a powerful continuation pattern, similar to the low-volatility, steady run gold experienced after a consolidation phase in 2008.

## The Engineered Crisis: Strait of Hormuz & The Liquidity Squeeze

Both analysts framed the Middle East escalation as an intentional, engineered event with profound macroeconomic consequences.

Francis Hunt drew a direct parallel to the 1970s OPEC crisis, which was used to inflate away debt after Nixon abandoned the gold standard.

> "This Strait of Hormuz event is literally the OPEC event that's going to be utilized to drive costs up," Hunt said. He argued that today's debt levels are catastrophically worse—with U.S. debt-to-GDP at 135%, not including unfunded liabilities—necessitating an even more aggressive "hyper-stagflation" to debase obligations.

Eric Yung detailed the immediate mechanism: a US dollar liquidity crisis. With Iran demanding payment in yuan or renminbi (RMB) and restricting passage to aligned nations, global oil importers are scrambling for dollars.

> "They're going to sell whatever they can sell... even US Treasury bonds will get sold off," Yung said, pointing to rising yields as evidence. This forced liquidation is pressuring all assets, including gold, in the short term.

The crisis is compounded by attacks on energy infrastructure, disrupting the revenue flywheel for Gulf states and creating a physical bottleneck. Storage is filling, and if production stops, restarting it is costly and slow.

## The Chilling Prospect: Lockdowns & Restricted Movement

Hunt took the analysis a step further, warning that the resulting energy scarcity and inflation could be used to justify severe restrictions on civilian life.

> "I think this leads eventually to potential lockdowns in the Western world... The consumer gets throttled," he said. He painted a scenario where governments prioritize military, emergency services, and critical infrastructure over public mobility.
> 
> "And that can be a great leading [excuse] with other agendas like 15-minute cities, restricted movement."

This environment, he argues, becomes fertile ground for policies like Universal Basic Income (UBI) and aggressive wealth taxes, targeting those who preserved capital through assets like gold.

## Asia in the Crosshairs

The analysts agreed the pain won't be evenly distributed. While China and Japan have substantial reserves, other Asian nations are deeply vulnerable.

> "I'm predicting an Asian currency crisis," Hunt said, specifically naming South Korea, the Philippines, and Thailand as exposed due to their reliance on Strait of Hormuz energy and, in some cases, overheated economies.
> 
> Eric Yung, based in Hong Kong, concurred: "It's not looking so good for places like Taiwan, Thailand... Taiwan, I heard two weeks [of reserves]."

## The Long-Term Outlook: Patience & Physical Preparation

Despite the short-term liquidation pressure, the long-term thesis for gold and silver remains intact. The engineered inflation is ultimately designed to debase fiat currency, reinforcing gold's role as the "king anti-fiat."

Hunt's advice to investors is to be patient during the volatility squeeze.

> "People shouldn't bemoan a quiet spell. Go spend some time with your family. Get off the computer, the screens."

The discussion concluded with a practical note on acquiring physical metals, warning of high retail markups and promoting wholesale purchasing methods to avoid overpaying in a rising market.

**The Bottom Line:** The current sell-off in precious metals is viewed as a short-term liquidity event within a larger, engineered inflationary crisis. The analysts warn that the real-world consequences—soaring costs, energy scarcity, and potential lockdowns—will ultimately drive a renewed, powerful bull market in gold and silver, while testing the economic and social resilience of nations across the globe.
