### The Waller Effect: Gold Selloff and Rate Hike Fears

The primary catalyst for the gold and silver sell-off was Fed Governor Christopher Waller, a Trump appointee, issuing unexpectedly hawkish comments. These remarks completely reversed market sentiment, flipping the probability of a rate hike at the July meeting to a 50/50 chance.

Waller cited three key drivers for inflationary pressures:
*   Tariffs.
*   Rising energy prices.
*   Strong consumer demand and consumption growth.

The mention of energy prices proved particularly relevant, as oil prices surged simultaneously. This dynamic has reignited the correlation between rising oil, inflation fears, and pressure on gold, a relationship that had weakened in the spring but appears to be returning.

### Oil Rally, Technicals, and Energy Correlation

Oil experienced a massive jump, driven by technical factors and a short squeeze. The price broke above the 200-day moving average, triggering margin calls for those positioned incorrectly. While the trend has shifted, Wilhelmi advises against chasing the oil trade directly at these levels. Instead, he suggests playing the move through energy stocks, which offer attractive entry points.

Despite the rally, Wilhelmi remains skeptical of long-term bullish projections, dismissing claims of $200-a-barrel oil. He anticipates political pressure from the Trump administration to keep oil prices lower ahead of the midterms. However, the recent technical breakout confirms a trend change that offers opportunities for swing trades in the energy sector.

### Gold Technicals: The 4,000 Level and Leverage Risks

Gold faced a severe sell-off, with the price testing the critical 4,000 level. The downturn was exacerbated by leveraged players caught on the wrong side, with margin calls likely triggered when prices dipped below the 470-475 range. This leverage flush contributed to the "Bunsen burner" style burn-down of portfolios.

**Key Technical Observations:**
*   **Support:** The 4,000 level held firm. Long tails on the chart indicate a "Swiffer vacuum" effect, where buyers absorbed selling pressure below this mark.
*   **Bearish Signals:** Gold closed at its lowest level since June 2025, establishing a new three-week low on the close. This confirms the downtrend, returning the market to conditions seen at the end of June.
*   **Bull Case:** If 4,000 holds, the Relative Strength Index (RSI) remains aligned with an uptrend line. A reversal here could serve as a second confirmation of higher lows, strengthening the bullish structure despite the recent lower close.
*   **Bear Case:** A break below 4,000 risks a liquidity grab or undercut, which could trigger further downside.
