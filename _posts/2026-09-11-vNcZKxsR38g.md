In a September 9, 2026, interview on *Capital Cosm*, economist and author Peter Schiff provided a dire assessment of the current state of the US economy. Schiff argued that the United States is facing an "unimaginable" crisis driven by a $40 trillion national debt, a weakening yen that threatens to destabilize the bond market, and a Federal Reserve that is incapable of solving the inflation problem. ## The Yen Carry Trade and the Bond Market Squeeze
A central theme of the interview was the complex dynamic between the Japanese yen and US Treasury bonds. Schiff noted that the US Treasury announced a $6 billion buyback of long-term debt—triple the normal level—but the market sold off anyway, pushing the 10-year yield to 4.835%.

Schiff explained that the US is in a "damned if it does, damned if it doesn't" scenario regarding the yen:
*   **If the Yen Falls:** Japan is forced to sell US Treasuries to buy back yen to support its currency, flooding the market with supply.
*   **If the Yen Rises:** The "yen carry trade" unwinds. Investors who borrowed cheap yen to buy US assets are forced to sell their US assets to repay their debts, creating a massive sell-off.

Schiff pointed out that the US cannot afford this supply shock because the US is already dumping Treasuries on the market due to $3 trillion annual budget deficits and the Social Security trust fund selling its holdings. He cited the Norwegian sovereign wealth fund's announcement to unload $60 billion in Treasuries as a sign that foreign buyers are disappearing.

## The $40 Trillion Debt and the "Grow Your Way Out" Myth
With the national debt now exceeding $40 trillion—up from roughly $6-7 trillion pre-2008—Schiff argued that the US is a much worse credit risk than it was a decade ago. He dismissed the government's strategy of "growing our way out" of the debt, a narrative that has failed for 40 to 50 years.

*   **Debt vs. Growth:** Even if AI and AI-driven productivity boost economic growth, the debt is growing faster than the economy.
*   **No Spending Cuts:** Schiff criticized the current administration for refusing to cut government spending, noting that even "waste, fraud, and abuse" programs like DOGE were shut down.
*   **Interest Rate Reality:** Rates are currently too low to service the debt. Schiff argued that real interest rates are actually falling because inflation is accelerating faster than nominal rates.

## AI, Commodities, and the Short-Term Cost
Schiff addressed the popular investment thesis that AI is a deflationary force. While he agreed that AI is ultimately deflationary in the long run by increasing the supply of goods, he warned that the short-term dynamic is the exact opposite.

*   **Resource Intensity:** Building AI infrastructure (data centers, robots, and robots) requires a massive expenditure of raw materials like copper, nickel, and energy.
*   **Inflationary Pressure:** This massive demand for commodities drives prices up in the short term. Schiff noted that while copper hit an all-time high in dollar terms, it is still historically cheap when priced in gold, indicating that the dollar is the asset losing value.
*   **Energy Dependence:** Robots and robots run on electricity, not food. The energy sector is a key beneficiary of the AI buildout, and Schiff predicted oil prices could reach $200 a barrel in the coming years.

## The Rigged Inflation Statistic
Perhaps the most provocative part of the interview was Schiff’s critique of the Consumer Price Index (CPI). He argued that the CPI was not created to accurately measure inflation but was designed to understate it.

*   **Hedonic Adjustments:** Schiff used an example of newspaper and magazine prices. While the CPI claimed a 30% increase from 2003 to 2013, his own analysis of cover prices showed a 130% increase. The government uses "hedonic adjustments" to assume that quality improvements (like more pages) offset the price increase.
*   **Quality Decline:** Schiff countered that quality is actually declining—citing airline services, customer service, and product durability—yet the CPI only adjusts for quality *improvements*, never the reverse.
*   **The Real Measure:** Schiff stated that the only true measure of inflation is the price of gold. He argued that when you price commodities and wages in gold, the picture of economic health is revealed: wages are at rock bottom, and the cost of living is skyrocketing.

Schiff argued that the Fed will continue to choose inflation over economic stability, a choice that has been made by every Fed chair since Greenspan.
