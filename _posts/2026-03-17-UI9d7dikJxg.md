In a stark message to the small Gulf states, Iran has made it clear that hosting American military bases comes with a severe and potentially devastating price. According to analysis drawn from recent strategic assessments, Iran has demonstrated both the capability and the willingness to retaliate against neighboring countries that facilitate U.S. operations—even at the cost of absorbing significant damage to its own cities.

“The Iranians were waiting for us,” the analysis states. “They were ready. They knew they would take some losses. They knew they could not prevent their cities from being damaged to the extent that we've done it. But they were prepared to accept that damage because they had the means to strike back.”

This calculus of deterrence rests on a critical vulnerability: the current lack of effective air and missile defense systems across the Gulf region. Without robust defenses, the small states along the Arabian Peninsula are exposed to Iranian retaliatory strikes.

The historical context of these nations further complicates their strategic position. Many are “legacies of the British Empire,” born from arrangements made centuries ago. As described, “the British moved into the Gulf a couple of hundred years ago and they picked up various sheiks from tribes and said, ‘Okay, we're going to let you run this area in return for which you're going to get paid a lot of money. In the meantime, we're going to get all the oil we want.’”

Today, these oil-rich states find themselves caught in a modern geopolitical crossfire. Iran, the analysis concludes, “has gone to great lengths to demonstrate to them conclusively that if you allow Americans into your country to build bases and think that you're going to attack us with impunity, we are going to destroy you.”

The warning is unambiguous. For the Gulf monarchies, the alliance with the United States—a cornerstone of their security for decades—is now being framed by Tehran as an existential gamble. The message underscores a harsh reality: in any future conflict, these states may not be shielded spectators, but primary targets. The era of hosting foreign bases without consequence, Iran signals, is over.
