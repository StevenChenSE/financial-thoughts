## Navigating Macro Shifts, Geopolitics, and Asset Reallocation

### Introduction
In a comprehensive market outlook, David Woo, CEO and founder of David Woo Unbound, outlines a bearish thesis centered on the imminent unwinding of the artificial intelligence trade. Woo argues that the current market structure is heavily reliant on unsustainable capital expenditure (capex) narratives and that a correction is structurally inevitable. His analysis spans equity market fragility, semiconductor supply dynamics, the inverse relationship between real yields and gold, Middle East geopolitical fractures, and the evolving regulatory landscape for digital assets.

### The AI Bubble and the Capex Unwind
Woo maintains that the AI rally has been fundamentally propped up by hyperscaler capex spending rather than genuine commercial monetization. He points to Anthropic’s Mythos model as a critical inflection point. While Mythos initially re-energized market sentiment by demonstrating capabilities bridging large language models and artificial general intelligence, its extreme potency has triggered severe regulatory and national security concerns. The Five Eyes intelligence alliance recently issued unprecedented warnings about the cyber destabilization risks posed by widely accessible frontier models. Consequently, Mythos remains restricted to a limited user base, severely hampering Anthropic and OpenAI’s ability to monetize their research and development. Woo notes that with Chinese and Japanese startups already developing comparable models, the competitive moat for US frontier AI is narrowing, making the capex story increasingly untenable.

### Semiconductor Dynamics and Hyperscaler Realities
The market is beginning to price in the diminishing returns of AI infrastructure. Meta’s strategic pivot into cloud computing signals an admission of overbuilt capacity and underwhelming return on investment, particularly given that its Llama models trail significantly behind industry leaders on performance scoreboards. This shift has triggered sell-offs across Asian semiconductor indices, notably impacting South Korea’s Kospi and chipmakers like Samsung and Hynix.

Woo distinguishes between training and inference chip markets. While Nvidia dominated the training chip cycle, demand is now shifting toward inference chips, where competition from Intel, AMD, and Broadcom is intensifying. Furthermore, the recent rally in high-bandwidth memory (HBM) stocks like Micron, Samsung, and Hynix stems from a temporary capacity shortage rather than structural scarcity. With new fabrication capacity coming online by year-end and Chinese manufacturers advancing their capabilities, Woo anticipates the memory rally will fizzle, removing a key pillar supporting the NASDAQ.

### Real Yields, Gold, and the Recession Trigger
A central pillar of Woo’s macro thesis is the relationship between the AI bubble, real yields, and precious metals. He argues that the AI-driven wealth effect has artificially propped up consumer spending and corporate investment, keeping real yields elevated and suppressing gold prices. Real yields, which represent the return on inflation-indexed bonds, act as a direct competitor to non-yielding assets like gold. 

Woo predicts that once the AI bubble bursts, the US economy will abruptly contract into a recession. This scenario would force the Federal Reserve to pivot from a data-dependent, hawkish stance to aggressive rate cuts. Drawing parallels to the post-dotcom era, Woo explains that a collapsing tech bubble will weaken the dollar, depress real yields, and ultimately ignite a sustained bull market for gold. He identifies the upcoming Q2 earnings season for Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta as the potential catalyst, warning that even minor guidance cuts on capex could trigger a rapid 20% decline in the NASDAQ.

### Middle East Tensions and Oil Price Catalysts
Geopolitical instability in the Middle East remains a critical variable for energy markets. Woo remains long oil, anticipating a price resurgence following the July 4 holiday period. He highlights a fragile ceasefire agreement between Israel and Lebanon, brokered by Marco Rubio, which represents a significant fracture within the US administration between hawks and doves. The deal, which includes the Lebanese government’s unprecedented acknowledgment of Israel’s sovereignty, was largely driven by regional Christian and Sunni factions seeking to counter Iranian influence.

Woo is highly critical of the US strategic posture, arguing that the recent military engagement exposed significant vulnerabilities. Despite launching over 1,000 Tomahawk cruise missiles, US forces failed to neutralize Iran’s missile and drone capabilities, which were enhanced by real-time guidance from China’s Beidou satellite navigation system. With Vice President JD Vance pushing for the release of $6 billion in frozen Iranian assets and widespread political backlash from the MAGA base, Woo believes the nuclear negotiations are likely to collapse. This prolonged stalemate, combined with normalizing shipping flows and weak Chinese demand, will eventually give way to supply-side shocks, driving WTI crude higher.

### Bitcoin, Stablecoins, and Policy Realities
Contrary to market expectations of a crypto-friendly administration, Woo asserts that President Trump’s policy focus remains firmly on stablecoins rather than Bitcoin. Stablecoins align with US hegemony by reinforcing the dollar’s dominance and mandating large purchases of US Treasuries to back their issuance. Bitcoin, conversely, poses no such strategic benefit. With midterm elections looming and Republican enthusiasm waning, Woo sees zero probability of meaningful crypto legislation passing in the next two and a half years. He views Bitcoin as technically oversold but fundamentally unconvincing, noting that retail-driven liquidation following an AI bust would likely drag crypto prices lower alongside equities.

### Summary and Portfolio Positioning
David Woo’s macro strategy is built on a clear risk-off framework. He is currently long oil, long gold, short the NASDAQ 100, and long the 5s30s Treasury steepener, betting on a future Federal Reserve easing cycle triggered by tech sector capitulation. He emphasizes that oil and NASDAQ direction will dictate the trajectory of all other asset classes. With the market currently pricing in minimal recession probability and zero near-term rate cuts, Woo believes traders are severely underestimating the fragility of the capex narrative. His recommended asymmetric trade remains short technology equities and long commodities, positioning portfolios for the inevitable unwind of the AI bubble and the subsequent macroeconomic reset.
