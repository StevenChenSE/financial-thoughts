*March 13, 2026* — In a recent interview on Capital, metals analyst Eric Jung presented a counterintuitive and layered perspective on how the ongoing war in Iran and the Middle East could impact precious metals. Contrary to the common assumption that geopolitical conflict automatically drives gold and silver higher, Jung argues the reality is far more complex—and may initially suppress prices before any sustained rally begins.

### The Current Phase: A Rush for Dollars, Not Metals
Jung observes that the war has entered a grinding stalemate, disrupting initial U.S. plans for a quick resolution. Despite daily exchanges of fire, a critical assumption is proving false: the Strait of Hormuz is **not closed**.

“A lot of experts out there… assume that the oil coming out from there… is going to be zero or close to zero. But in reality, we [see] that Iran is actually shipping a lot of oil to China,” Jung notes. He estimates perhaps 25-50% of pre-war oil volumes are still moving.

This leads to **Phase One** of Jung’s thesis: a scramble for U.S. dollars.
> “The bigger participants, the oil importing countries, they might try to sell a lot of their assets for US dollars… get the US dollars and you buy whatever marginal oil that is still out there… denominated in US dollars.”

This explains the strength in the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY), recently hitting 100, and selling pressure on dollar-denominated assets—**including gold, silver, and bonds**. The immediate need for petrodollars overrides safe-haven demand.

### Phase Two: The Grind and a Shift to Gold
If the conflict settles into a prolonged war of attrition with the strait partially open, Jung anticipates **Phase Two**. Here, the geopolitical calculus changes for oil-producing U.S. allies.

> “The other Gulf countries… might actually start diversifying their… oil sales proceeds… into physical gold because… the US military was unable to protect the Gulf States.”

The core of the petrodollar deal—U.S. security for dollar-denominated oil sales—is seen as faltering. Jung believes a portion of new dollar revenues will flow into physical gold, potentially driving its price up. Silver and base metals like copper could also see a bid due to a looming industrial crisis.

### The Sulfuric Acid Chokepoint
Here, Jung introduces a crucial, underappreciated variable: **sulfuric acid**. Most of the world’s sulfuric acid is a byproduct of refining sour crude oil from the Middle East. Reduced oil flow means reduced acid supply.

> “Sulfuric acid is a very important agent that… metals refiners… use to extract the metals out of the ore.”

With acid supplies potentially cut in half, a rush for copper, zinc, and uranium (which all require acid for processing) could ensue. This could benefit **primary silver producers**, as silver is mainly a byproduct of base metal mining. Reduced copper/zinc output would constrain non-primary silver supply.

### Phase Three: The Industrial Collapse Scenario
Jung’s **Phase Three** is a warning. If the strait’s throughput falls below 20%, creating a “severe supply crunch of the sulfuric acid,” base metals refinement faces “serious trouble.”

The world would then burn through above-ground copper stockpiles for months. Once exhausted, industrial production could crash.
> “Then it might… crash the price of silver because if you can't make solar panels or like EVs because you don't have the copper, then you don't need the silver even if the silver is available.”

This highlights a flaw in skipping analysis steps: extreme supply disruption can destroy demand itself.

### Phase Four & The Bull Case: A New Equilibrium
Eventually, in **Phase Four**, supply chains would reconfigure—bypassing the strait via rail, or Russia increasing market share. A new, more localized equilibrium would emerge.

> “At that stage I think gold and silver would… accelerate their rise in prices because… the world realizes that Uncle Sam can't really protect them. So why would they hold US dollars?”

The bull case, Jung says, is a swift end to the war. But even then, “the US loses credibility,” likely accelerating diversification into gold and silver.

### Personal Positioning in an Uncertain World
Given the dizzying complexity and unpredictable news flow—like rumors about Netanyahu’s status—Jung advocates for holding rather than trading.
> “I'm holding on to all my gold and silver… I think it's extremely difficult to trade this stuff… It's pure gambling.”

For those under-allocated, he suggests dollar-cost averaging, as pinpointing a bottom is impossible. His base case remains the multi-phase grind he outlined, which he believes is already unfolding, with gold around $5,080 and silver near $82 as of the interview.

**Eric Jung’s analysis underscores that in modern, interconnected warfare, the path for metals is not linear. Second and third-order effects—from petrodollar mechanics to industrial chemistry—can create paradoxical outcomes, where war first fuels dollar strength before potentially unmaking its dominance.**
