As the stock market navigates its most volatile stretch in months, technical analyst and Chief Investment Officer of Vermilion Research, David Nicoski, offers a sobering outlook. Speaking on October 23rd, Nicoski highlights that the market tone shifted dramatically following recent CPI data, signaling a rotation out of technology and into defensive sectors. With AI buildout concerns mounting and credit spreads widening, investors face a complex environment where "good news has stopped being good for stocks."

## The Shift in Market Tone

The pivot point for this current volatility appears to be the release of the latest Consumer Price Index (CPI) report. According to Nicoski, yields on the 10-year Treasury saw an enormous swing—erasing basis point gains and closing dramatically lower—which served as a "gravitation for safety." This move indicated that investors were rotating funds out of equity markets, particularly tech, which had been leading the rally.

Since October 2nd, every sector outside of technology has outperformed the tech index. Sectors previously considered oversold, such as financials (XLF), consumer staples, and consumer discretionary, have seen a surge in relative strength. This rotation suggests that while dip buyers are testing the waters after the NASDAQ’s worst day since June, the broader market sentiment is cautious.

## AI Buildout: Cracks in the Foundation

A central theme of Nicoski’s analysis is the pressure on the Artificial Intelligence (AI) sector, specifically regarding the infrastructure and capital expenditure required to sustain it. While companies like Meta reported revenue growth, their free cash flow turned negative due to massive investments in AI data centers. This disconnect between earnings and spending is spooking investors.

Nicoski points to specific stress signals within the "food chain" of AI construction:

*   **CoreWeave:** The cloud computing provider has seen its bond yields rise to 15-16%, despite a projected return on investment capital of only 8.9%. Its stock is down significantly, forming a major descending triangle that points to further downside potential.
*   **Engineering & Construction (ENC):** Stocks like Sterling Infrastructure (STRL), EMCOR Group (ECG), and Comfort Systems USA (CDNS) have fallen 50-60% from recent highs. Nicoski notes that these companies are facing collapsing margins due to high labor costs (electricians, plumbers) and specialty steel prices, directly contributing to inflation.
*   **Power Constraints:** Data centers require immense power, but access is limited. Without reliable energy sources, built facilities remain empty. This bottleneck is causing a slowdown in the build side of the AI boom.

Jensen Huang, CEO of Nvidia, acknowledged that while demand for neo-clouds like CoreWeave is tremendous, capital to fund these projects is scarce. In a higher interest rate environment, raising private capital becomes difficult as investors compete against attractive bond yields.

## Private Funding and Valuation Risks

Nicoski draws parallels between the current AI boom and historical bubbles, such as the dot-com era or railroad expansion. He highlights the excessive number of funding rounds for major AI players:
*   **Anthropic:** Has reportedly undertaken 18 series offerings, far exceeding the typical five rounds for private companies. A recent reduction in market cap from $2 trillion to $1.5 trillion suggests that later investors may be underwater.
*   **OpenAI:** Has completed 14 funding rounds.

The risk, according to Nicoski, is that if these valuations compress, subsequent fundraising becomes nearly impossible, potentially stalling the very buildout that supports tech stock growth.

## Sector Rotation and Technical Analysis

The market is currently stuck in a trading range rather than a clear bull or bear trend. Nicoski advises against calling for a "V-shaped" recovery, comparing it to getting a hole-in-one on a par-4 course—unlikely and not a reliable strategy. Instead, he focuses on relative strength inflections.

### Where to Buy: Software Over Hardware
While hardware and semiconductor stocks (SMH) face pressure, Nicoski identifies specific software names as compelling bottom-fishing candidates due to breaking longer-term downtrends:
*   **HubSpot:** Showing strong relative strength against the S&P 500, with ascending reactions and higher lows.
*   **Monday.com** and **Adobe**: Also noted for technical resilience within the tech sector.

### Where to Avoid or Sell into Bounces
*   **CoreWeave:** Not a long-term investment; bounces are trading opportunities only.
*   **Tesla:** Nicoski avoids "Elon stocks" due to what he views as egregious evaluations and poor relative performance since 2021. He notes Tesla has underperformed the market by 50% over this period.
*   **Bank of America & Citigroup:** Large banks are struggling with a compressing yield curve, which hurts their net interest margins.

### Tactical Plays: Energy and Staples
*   **Energy Services:** Nicoski is bottom-fishing oil services names like Schlumberger, Halliburton, Weatherford, and Tidewater. He also favors Transocean for its breakout potential as countries seek energy security amidst geopolitical tensions.
*   **Consumer Staples:** Stocks like General Mills and Kraft Heinz offer tactical trades with high dividend yields (6-7%), though Nicoski does not expect them to lead long-term unless the market becomes "unhinged" and correlations move to one.

## Geopolitical Risks and Oil

The conflict in the Middle East remains a significant wildcard for inflation and energy prices. With Houthi attacks disrupting shipping and tensions escalating, oil prices (WTI at $103, Brent at $112) are rising. Nicoski warns that if oil continues to climb, it will put further pressure on equity markets. He views energy as a long-term overweight, noting that lagging energy stocks are beginning to move up.

## Conclusion: A Market of Two Halves

The current market environment is defined by divergence. Tech is struggling under the weight of over-concentration and capital scarcity, while defensive sectors and select industrials gain momentum. Nicoski’s strategy relies on ignoring standard ETF structures (which cap exposure to mega-caps) in favor of custom group analysis and relative strength charts.

Investors are advised to be cautious with high-beta tech names and instead seek out areas showing secular inflections, such as specific software plays and energy services. As long as the breadth of the market improves underneath the surface, indices may go sideways, but the pressure on AI remains a critical headwind.
