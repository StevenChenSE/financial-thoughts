Jim Macdonald says the fundamentals driving silver higher are stronger than ever—and he believes $300 is just the beginning.

In an exclusive interview, Jim Macdonald, CEO of Cooney Silver, shared his bullish outlook on silver with unwavering conviction. "We're in a multi-year bull market here," he stated, emphasizing that the recent rally represents only the first leg of what promises to be an extraordinary journey for the precious metal.

A year ago, silver traded around $35. Today, it hovers near $64-65—representing a staggering 77% increase. Yet Macdonald remains focused on the bigger picture, noting that silver recently touched $120 before pulling back nearly 50% from those highs. This volatility, while dramatic, is characteristic of silver's historical behavior.

## Historical Patterns Suggest Monumental Upside

Macdonald draws compelling comparisons to previous bull markets. In 2000, silver traded at approximately $5 and subsequently ran to over $50—a 10-fold increase. Following that explosive move, silver consolidated in a $20-25 channel for roughly a decade.

Now, using similar mathematics applied to the current channel, Macdonald projects potential multiples of 4-5x from the $20-25 baseline. "That could be 80, 100 or even more," he explained, citing growing industrial demand as a critical catalyst that distinguishes this cycle from previous ones.

## Three Powerful Drivers Fueling the Rally

According to Macdonald, silver's surge stems from three converging forces:

**Monetary Demand**: With massive fiat currency and debt creation over the past 25 years, investors increasingly seek stores of value. Gold leads the way, with silver following its trajectory.

**Industrial Demand**: Unlike gold, silver possesses significant industrial applications, particularly in semiconductors, solar panels, and emerging technologies. Macdonald noted that "huge growing industrial demand for silver" creates fundamental tension that previous bull markets lacked.

**Speculation**: As fundamentals strengthen and prices break out, speculative capital floods the market, amplifying moves dramatically.

## The Mining Industry Transformation

The silver price surge has fundamentally transformed mining operations. Macdonald highlighted how Cooney Silver's assets have become "highly economic" at current prices. The company's PEA on the Lasera deposit demonstrates 14 years of mine production potential, while drilling at the Cola project aims to expand resources from 54 million to 100 million ounces.

"We have access to capital now," Macdonald explained. "Where in the last 10 years, we were struggling just to get enough capital to keep advancing things." Mining companies are now flush with cash, pursuing stock buybacks, mergers, and acquisitions—activities not seen in 15 years.

## Supply Constraints Ensure Sustained Strength

Macdonald addressed common misconceptions about industrial users controlling prices. "You cannot create more than is here," he emphasized. Only one in 2,000 mineral occurrences becomes an economically viable mine, with the discovery-to-production timeline stretching beyond the historical 10-year average due to regulatory complexities and capital requirements.

## The Road Ahead: Consolidation Followed by Higher Highs

Despite the recent pullback from $120 to the $64-65 range, Macdonald sees this as healthy consolidation. "Now it's finding another bottom, and it's going to go again," he predicted. "We're now in a changed price environment."

When asked whether the fundamentals supporting $100 silver remain intact for $300 targets, Macdonald's response was unequivocal: "Nothing's changed. The fundamentals are exactly the same."

The silver market appears poised for additional legs higher, with Macdonald anticipating multiple explosive moves before the bull market concludes. Only then will silver find a new sideways band—likely at levels substantially higher than current trading.
