## Chris Macintosh on the Coming Capital Rotation and the Quiet War Against Your Savings

The next great market reset is already underway—and the rules are being rewritten before most people even realize it. Chris Macintosh, publisher of the Capitalist Exploits Insider newsletter, recently joined Danny on Capital Cause to discuss what's really happening beneath the surface of today's markets: a capital rotation cycle that historically moves between technology and commodities, unprecedented valuation levels, and laws quietly changing in Europe and Australia that could fundamentally alter how your wealth is protected.

"The apathy that I see amongst individuals is just unbelievable," Macintosh told Danny. "It's appalling." His concern isn't just about market timing—it's about what's coming for pension plans and private savings once the bubble finally pops.

---

## The Capital Rotation Cycle: Bark on a Tree

Macintosh begins with a concept he returns to repeatedly throughout the discussion: the importance of seeing the forest for the trees. "When you have a microscope and you're looking at something, all the bark in a tree looks very important," he explains. "But if you look at the forest, that piece of bark is insignificant."

This metaphor captures his approach to energy markets and the broader economy. Rather than getting caught up in daily headlines about ceasefire agreements, oil prices fluctuating around $75, or geopolitical tensions, Macintosh focuses on structural forces that have already been set in motion.

"We're in a capital rotation cycle, and that's already begun," he states. "I don't see anything significant that's changed that." The pattern isn't new—it's historical. After the NASDAQ collapsed by 75% in 2001, commodities ran strongly through 2014. Time Magazine was talking about $150 oil. Nobody wanted to touch tech. Now, Macintosh observes, we may be at the beginning of the same rotation in reverse.

---

## The Commodity Supercycle and Why Energy Isn't Done

The foundation for Macintosh's energy thesis rests on capital expenditure data that most investors overlook. Since the highs of 2014 through 2020 and beyond, capex spend in commodities and energy "collapsed. Many of the companies have gone away." This, he argues, is "the predeterminer for a bull market which has begun."

What's striking is how little sense current energy prices make when viewed through this lens. Despite approximately 30% of global oil supply being disrupted during the recent crisis, prices remain moderate. "We haven't even hit the 2012 high, which is the equivalent of $120 a barrel," Macintosh points out. The reason? Over a billion barrels of oil have been drawn from inventories during this crisis—explaining why gasoline prices haven't reflected the severity of supply disruptions.

The current ceasefire negotiations and Middle East tensions? "That's noise. It's the bark on the tree. If you sort of scale all the way back out and look at the forest, there's nothing much that's changed."

## Bitcoin as a NASDAQ Warning Signal

One of the more technically insightful portions of the conversation involves Macintosh's analysis of the Bitcoin-to-NASDAQ ratio as a predictive indicator. "Bitcoin is basically a liquidity indicator for the NASDAQ," he explains. "It often precedes it."

When this ratio declines—meaning Bitcoin underperforms the NASDAQ—it has historically presaged NASDAQ weakness. The chart shows this pattern: Bitcoin topped against the NASDAQ in April 2021, then bottomed in December 2022. During that period, the NASDAQ fell approximately 33%, from 15,800 to 10,000 points. "Now, this green line is back down today," Macintosh notes, suggesting history may be rhyming.

The broader concern is where capital will come from to support continued NASDAQ levels, especially given upcoming IPOs. "We're looking at well over $100 billion between Anthropic and OpenAI," Macintosh observes. "Where is that capital going to come from?"

## The MicroStrategy Warning

Macintosh doesn't hide his views on Michael Sailor and MicroStrategy, calling it "mind-bogglingly stupid" to buy the company's debt instruments or stock. His reasoning is straightforward: the entire premise of owning Bitcoin is self-custody—keeping your own keys rather than trusting intermediaries. "Wall Street's come along with Sailor at the front end and said, 'No, you give us your money.' And worse, they've said, 'In fact, give us your money and we'll lend us money to buy Bitcoin.' It's like, why would you just not buy Bitcoin yourself?"

With Sailor's preferred stock trading down and the debt burden becoming unsustainable, Macintosh expects those who bought into this structure "are going to get hosed." He views Sailor as "just such an obvious shill," noting that "people follow him because people like narratives and they like confidence."

## The Bigger Picture: Laws Being Changed to Take Your Wealth

Perhaps the most sobering portion of the interview concerns what's happening legislatively, not market-wise. Macintosh references something he's discussed in his newsletter for some time: pension plans are being targeted, and the laws are already changing "specifically in Europe and specifically in Australia."

"We're facing a potentially like a '29 type of event," Macintosh warns, "and then that restructures society." The parallel to 1929—a market crash followed by fundamental societal and economic restructuring—suggests the coming reset could be far more profound than a simple recession or correction.

"We need to have both lights and energy to have power," Macintosh analogizes. "Tech is the light bulbs and the infrastructure around that. Energy is the electricity. You have to have both." But beyond markets, he's concerned about what happens to individual wealth when the system resets.

## How to Position: Probability, Pricing, and Asymmetry

For Macintosh, the investment approach isn't about predicting exact tops and bottoms—it's about probability and asymmetric opportunities. The NASDAQ's valuation metrics are "problematic from a historical standpoint," whether looking at price-to-book or price-to-sales ratios. Yet the cost of hedging against a downturn is "actually very, very cheap."

"So there you have yourself an asymmetric bet," he explains. "Now that's a bet that you should take regardless of your opinion on the market. The moment you have asymmetry, if you build multiple asymmetric bets, that as a portfolio over time does exactly what you would anticipate it does."

His strategy: focus on structural trends (like the commodity cycle), avoid getting distracted by daily noise, and always evaluate the cost of hedging against the potential payoff.

## Summary

Chris Macintosh's analysis offers a framework for thinking about markets that prioritizes structural forces over headlines. Key takeaways include:

- **The capital rotation from technology to commodities is already underway**, and investors who positioned early may benefit as this cycle develops
- **Energy markets remain fundamentally supportive** despite recent price volatility, driven by years of underinvestment in capex
- **Bitcoin's underperformance versus the NASDAQ may signal coming weakness** in tech indices, based on historical patterns
- **Highly leveraged crypto instruments like MicroStrategy carry significant risks** that most buyers haven't adequately considered
- **Upward valuation pressure on the NASDAQ** from massive upcoming IPOs faces serious questions about available capital
- **Legislative changes in Europe and Australia** suggest pension plans and private wealth may face new legal frameworks after the next crash
- **The most important investment decision** may be ensuring you have asymmetric hedges in place—not because you know exactly what will happen, but because the downside risk versus hedge cost makes the bet worthwhile

The forest, not the bark. That's Macintosh's message: step back, see the structural trends, and prepare for what history suggests comes next.
