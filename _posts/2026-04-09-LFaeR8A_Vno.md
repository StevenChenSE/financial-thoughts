The global markets have entered what Daniel Bahami describes as a "Stranger Things upside-down world." With a two-week ceasefire announced between the U.S. and Iran, the typical correlations between oil, the dollar, and precious metals have been thrown into disarray. While most investors are fixated on the volatility in equity and oil markets, Bahami argues that the real story—and the real opportunity—lies in gold and silver.

### The Inflation Signal in Oil
The immediate market reaction to the ceasefire was a sharp sell-off in oil, which dropped roughly 15% to 20% from its peak. However, Bahami warns against focusing on the headline drop.

"Everybody’s focusing on the wrong thing," Bahami notes. "I think what they should focus on is that oil is still in the mid-90s even after this drop."

Even if the ceasefire holds and oil drifts into the low 80s, the energy market remains elevated. For Bahami, this is a clear signal: inflation is coming. While energy stocks may take a breather, the underlying inflationary pressure remains a bullish tailwind for precious metals.

### Gold’s "Disappointing" Move
Gold has rallied in the wake of the ceasefire, a move that seems counterintuitive given that geopolitical risk is ostensibly being removed from the market. Bahami explains that this is a result of a shift in the "war trade." 

Previously, the trade was: *Oil long, Gold short, Dollar long.* Now, the market is pivoting to an *Oil short, Dollar short, Gold long* scenario. 

Despite this, Bahami finds the current gold rally somewhat "disappointing." When comparing the current price of gold to the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY), he points out a significant discrepancy. "The Dixie right now is at around 99... the last time we were there was around March 10th," he explains. Yet, gold is currently trading significantly below its March 10th levels.

Why the lag? Bahami points to a shift in central bank demand. "We’re missing the buyers," he says. With Turkey defending the Lira and Russia liquidating assets to fund the war in Ukraine, two of the "big five" central bank buyers have shifted to the sell side. 

### Avoiding the "Binary" Trap
For retail investors, the temptation to go "all-in" or "all-out" is high, especially during volatile windows. Bahami cautions against this binary thinking, which he contrasts with the "smart money" approach.

"Never sell your core positions," he advises. "Trade around the core positions, add to the core positions, maybe take some profits if they have run up significantly. You should always pay yourself on the way up."

He emphasizes that while today’s jump in gold and mining stocks is a reminder of why one should never be completely out of the market, it is not necessarily a signal to buy "hand over fist."

### The Outlook: Volatility and Opportunity
Bahami remains skeptical that the ceasefire will lead to lasting peace, noting that the geopolitical tension in the Middle East remains at its highest level since the Six-Day War. Because the situation is "doubtful to uncertain," he expects volatility to remain high.

Technically, gold is still struggling below its 50-day moving average. Bahami anticipates a potential "fake-out" where the price tests that level before pulling back. 

"I believe that by midsummer, we’re going to see a breakout in gold to the upside, and that’ll be the start of the next big gold rally," he predicts. 

For now, his strategy is one of patience: "I personally am going to sit tight today. It’s not going to be a pleasant day when everything goes up and you didn’t buy what you wanted to buy. But that’s very normal. We need to have our emotions under control."

As the market continues to navigate these "wild days," Bahami’s message is clear: stay calm, avoid FOMO, and prepare for the long-term trend rather than chasing the daily noise.
