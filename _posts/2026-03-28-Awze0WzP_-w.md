**A Conversation with Simon Hunt and Alistair Mloud**

*March 26, 2026* – In a stark discussion on *Capital Cause*, analysts Simon Hunt and Alistair Mloud painted a grim picture of a world at a dangerous inflection point. The conversation, hosted by Danny, wove together the accelerating decline of the petrodollar, severe Iranian threats to the Gulf, and the cascading economic catastrophe triggered by a closure of the Strait of Hormuz.

### The Accelerating End of the Petrodollar

The foundation of the discussion was the shifting global order. "It's an acceleration of the end," noted Hunt, tracing the petrodollar's decline to the Saudi agreement to supply China with oil in exchange for yuan. This realignment is forcing nations to choose sides. "At the end of the day, what Taiwan will do is it will see which side its bread is buttered on," Hunt said, suggesting this calculus is also driving Turkey away from Israel and possibly NATO.

### Iran's Stark Ultimatum and Military Prowess

From his location in the UAE, Hunt described a tense environment, with missile warnings becoming a backdrop to daily life. The central geopolitical flashpoint is Iran's explicit warning to Saudi Arabia and the UAE: support a potential American land invasion, and be treated as an enemy.

The threat is credible. Hunt emphasized Iran's demonstrated capability to destroy the oil fields, installations, and pipelines that underpin Gulf wealth. He cited Iran's use of advanced missiles, like the "Fateh-3" cluster missile, which evaded both Israeli and American defenses in tests. "The rulers really have a simple choice," Hunt stated. "Do we continue to support America? And if we do, we risk having our oil wealth destroyed. Or else we do what Iran has said... expel all American military forces and financial assets out of the Middle East."

This ultimatum was underscored by a clip of Iranian state TV, where a spokesperson declared the armed forces "100% ready" to execute coastal operations to "seize the shores of Bahrain and the UAE" should the US make a "mistake" in the region.

### The Delusion of a Quick Victory

Both analysts dismissed any notion of a swift American victory in Iran. "Those who know the region extremely well... say that's delusional," Hunt asserted. Mloud agreed, calling a US attack "the worst kept secret in the world" and predicting Iran, having prepared for decades, would give America "a thick ear." The subsequent question, "Then what?" hangs ominously over the scenario.

### Economic Cataclysm: "Worse Than COVID Plus GFC Combined"

The economic implications are staggering. The closure of the Strait of Hormuz is the trigger. Mloud referenced analyst Luke Groman's warning: "If the strait stays closed for two to four weeks, we face the worst economic crisis in anyone's alive lifetime, worse than COVID plus GFC [Global Financial Crisis] combined."

The crisis is multi-faceted:
*   **Oil & Energy:** Larry Fink, CEO of BlackRock, suggested years of oil at $100-$150 if the threat persists. Hunt recalled a 2011 quote from Donald Trump predicting $300 oil if Iran shut the strait. Mloud, pricing oil in gold, argued its true value points to "at least $300," a price the economy would have to endure.
*   **Fertilizer and Food:** 35% of global fertilizer transits the strait. Combined with Chinese export restrictions, the war in Ukraine, and adverse weather, a global food crisis is brewing.
*   **Inflation and Stagflation:** With 45,000 consumer products reliant on oil, soaring costs will fuel rampant inflation. The analysts foresee central banks, led by the Fed, responding with unprecedented quantitative easing to prevent systemic collapse, ultimately destroying credit through currency devaluation. "This is why it's the end of the fiat currency system," Mloud said, predicting a plunge in the US Dollar Index (DXY).
*   **Financial Markets:** Rising bond yields against stretched equity valuations could cause a crash that "will make Wall Street 1929 look like a teddy bear's picnic." Gulf states, major capital providers, may be forced to repatriate assets to shore up their own economies, exacerbating the sell-off.

### Gold, Silver, and Physical Dislocation

Amid the turmoil, the disconnect between paper and physical markets for precious metals is glaring. Commenting on reports of Turkey selling gold, Mloud explained a complex domestic situation but emphasized overwhelming physical demand globally, particularly in China. "They're pushing around the price in the paper market as much as they like. But what's happening is they're making the shortage of bullion even worse." Both analysts see this as a springboard for a major rise in gold and silver once the current market distortion passes.

### Societal Fallout: Rationing, Unemployment, and Desperation

The downstream effects will hit everyday life. The analysts anticipate government-imposed energy rationing and lockdowns. Hunt, from Dubai, cited a taxi driver's income collapsing from 900-1,200 dirhams daily to just 200, reflecting a desperate local economy where hotels stand empty and the "fast money" crowd has fled.

Looking further ahead, the impact of Artificial Intelligence on employment looms large. Hunt predicted unemployment reaching 8-10% within two years, a figure Mloud called "very optimistic." This would crater tax revenues just as government spending on subsidies and crisis management explodes, creating a fiscal doom loop.

### A Long Crisis with No Easy Exit

The consensus is that this conflict will not end quickly. Hunt's "working assumption" is a war lasting at least until September. The resulting economic damage will be deep and lasting, accelerating the geopolitical rise of China and the decline of the US-led financial order.

The conversation closed on a sober note. For years, so-called "doomsday preppers" have been preparing for an end-of-the-world scenario. "This could be actually the time," Mloud suggested, "when they ought to consider doing it." In a world where Gulf rulers face an existential choice, where currencies face collapse, and where a miscalculation could trigger a regional inferno, the analysts' warning was clear: the crisis is here, and its consequences are only beginning to unfold.
