In a striking critique of American foreign policy, one observer argues that the United States has maintained a posture of hostility toward Iran since 1979, refusing to engage in genuine dialogue or treat the nation as an equal partner. This approach, characterized by coercion and military intimidation, has not only defined U.S.-Iran relations but has also fostered growing international sympathy for Iran as a nation standing up to a global bully.

“We have been the enemies of Iran since 1979,” the analysis begins. “We've never been willing to sit down and really talk to these people and treat them as equals.” This foundational refusal, it suggests, reflects a broader pattern in American strategy—one that offers little room for mutual respect or diplomatic parity.

The critique goes further, outlining what it describes as a binary and heavy-handed U.S. foreign policy playbook: “We don't treat anybody as an equal. Either we bully you and you get in line or they come back and bully you with military power. That's been our approach.”

According to this perspective, this consistent tactic has led to widespread international fatigue. “I think the world is sick of it,” the speaker states, pointing to a shift in global sentiment. In this view, Iran’s resistance is seen not merely as regional defiance, but as a symbolic stand against unilateral pressure—a narrative that resonates far beyond its borders.

“I think the Iranians have a lot of support around the world because they see Iran as standing up to the bully.” This perception, the analysis concludes, plays a significant role in the psychological dimensions of geopolitical conflict, influencing how nations and publics align in an increasingly multipolar world.

The remarks underscore a growing sentiment that respect, dialogue, and equality—absent for decades in this relationship—are essential not only for peace, but for restoring credibility in international affairs. As global dynamics continue to evolve, the call for a more equitable approach to diplomacy grows louder, with Iran’s stance emblematic of a broader challenge to power politics as usual.
