## Macro Headwinds: Treasury Yields, Inflation, and the Federal Reserve

Beyond technical chart patterns, the macroeconomic environment continues to exert immense pressure on non-yielding bullion. Wagner points out that the inverse relationship between yields and gold prices remains one of the most critical correlations in financial markets.

### The Impact of 10-Year and 30-Year Yields
Because gold yields no interest or dividend, rising nominal and real yields increase the opportunity cost of holding the metal. When yields on 10-year and 30-year Treasuries surge, capital flows away from defensive assets like gold into yield-bearing alternatives, shifting market sentiment from risk-off to risk-on.

### Energy Prices and the Hawkish Fed
The primary catalyst maintaining upward pressure on yields is sticky inflation, driven heavily by elevated crude oil prices. As crude sustains levels near $90 per barrel, energy costs permeate the broader economy, elevating inflation expectations. 

While persistent inflation historically acts as a tailwind for gold, it doubles as a catalyst for central bank tightening. Market expectations—tracked via tools like the CME FedWatch Tool—point toward an overwhelming likelihood of at least one additional rate hike before year-end. If persistent energy inflation forces the Federal Reserve to implement multiple rate hikes, that policy shift could overpower inflation-hedge demand and add bearish pressure to bullion.
