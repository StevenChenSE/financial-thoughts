## The Illusion of Positive Returns and Market Control

In a candid discussion on *Capital Calls*, renowned investor Rick Rule addresses the current economic landscape, focusing on the disconnect between nominal interest rates and actual purchasing power. With the 30-year US Treasury yield at 5.26% and the 10-year at 4.73%, Rule argues that these figures are misleading. He posits that the Federal Reserve controls short-term rates, but the market—akin to the "bond vigilantes" of the Clinton era—now dictates long-term rates.

Rule emphasizes that for the average investor, the true cost of living erosion is between 8% and 10% annually. Consequently, a 5.7% nominal return on a 30-year bond represents a real loss of approximately 3% per year. This negative real yield environment makes holding cash or long-term debt obligations expensive, as it steadily diminishes wealth. Rule advises investors to view liquidity maintenance not as a savings strategy, but as an "option payment" to deploy capital during potential liquidity squeezes, similar to the 2008 crisis.

## The End of the Yen Carry Trade and Currency Diversification

A significant shift in global markets is the unwinding of the yen carry trade, driven by rising interest rates in Japan. For decades, investors borrowed in low-yielding yen to invest in higher-yielding US assets. As Japan raises rates, this capital is returning home, causing a sell-off in dollar-denominated assets and a strengthening of the yen. This dynamic, along with the outperformance of commodity-centric currencies like the Australian dollar and Norwegian kroner, suggests a weakening US dollar.

Rule compares the current period to the volatility seen between 2000 and 2008, where gradual inflation perceptions led to currency choppiness. He strongly advises US citizens to diversify their currency holdings, moving away from 100% US dollar exposure. While he does not advocate for speculative trading, he recommends holding a portion of savings in other currencies and, crucially, in gold. He notes that gold’s market share in US portfolios is historically low at roughly 0.5%, suggesting significant upside potential as it did in the 1970s.

## Housing, Gold, and the Real Cost of Living

To illustrate the erosion of purchasing power, Rule examines US single-family home prices. While nominal prices appear high, when adjusted for gold or M2 money supply, housing is near historic lows, comparable to levels seen in 1980 and 2012. This indicates that homes are not necessarily overpriced; rather, the currency used to buy them has lost significant value. This perspective underscores the importance of hard assets in preserving wealth against currency devaluation.

## The Structural Oil Shortage of 2029

Looking ahead, Rule identifies a looming structural shortage in the oil market, projected for 2029 or 2030. This is not a war-related disruption but the result of deferred sustaining capital investment. Oil companies have been prioritizing dividends and stock buybacks over maintaining production infrastructure, deferring over a billion dollars a day in necessary investments. While this boosts short-term shareholder returns, it cripples long-term production capacity.

Rule views oil stocks as undervalued when considering this future supply deficit. He distinguishes between companies that are "cannibalizing" their assets for quick returns and those maintaining sustaining capital. He advises investors with a three-to-four-year horizon to favor the latter, as they will be richly rewarded when the shortage materializes. Despite near-term volatility tied to geopolitical conflicts in the Gulf, the long-term thesis remains robust due to this fundamental supply constraint.

## Investment Discipline: Saving vs. Consuming

Rule concludes by addressing the psychological barriers to wealth creation. He observes that most people are "anti-savers" who prefer to "live rich" rather than "be rich." True wealth generation requires producing more utility than one consumes, with the delta reinvested as capital. He criticizes the modern short-term holding period for stocks, which averages less than six months, arguing that rational investment requires a multi-year perspective.

For those interested in deeper analysis, Rule promotes his Natural Resources Investment Symposium and offers free portfolio rankings for natural resource stocks via *ruleinvestmentmedia.com*, excluding crypto, tech, and pot stocks. His message is clear: patience, discipline, and a focus on absolute valuation over short-term market noise are essential for long-term financial success.
