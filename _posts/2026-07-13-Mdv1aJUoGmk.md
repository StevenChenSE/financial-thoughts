## India's Policy Shift Shakes Global Silver Markets While China Advances Gold Hub Ambitions

On July 10, 2026, silver prices are trading below $60, marking a sharp decline from recent highs in the $70s and $80s per troy ounce. In a discussion on the Capital channel, market analyst Eric Yung, known as Kong Kong 9888, provided a detailed breakdown of the factors driving this correction. The primary catalyst appears to be a sudden shift in Indian import policy that has effectively halted silver flows, combined with broader geopolitical maneuvers and structural changes in China's precious metals infrastructure.

### India's Silver Import Restriction Triggers Price Collapse

The precipitous drop in silver prices correlates directly with regulatory changes in India, the world's largest marginal silver buyer. On May 16–17, 2026, the Indian government reclassified 99.9% pure silver bars from "free importatory" to "restricted" status. This policy shift increased import duties on silver and gold from 6% to 15%.

The impact was immediate and severe. Official silver imports into India plunged by approximately 87% following the mid-May announcement. With India effectively removed from the market, silver prices retreated from the high $70s and $80s to the mid-$50s. Yung noted that India's role as a massive marginal buyer has historically been a key driver of silver price momentum; its absence has created a significant demand vacuum.

### Arbitrage, Smuggling, and Potential Policy Reversal

The import restriction has created a massive arbitrage opportunity and a physical shortage within India. Current data shows the Indian spot price at approximately $78.70 per ounce, compared to the U.S. price of roughly $59.60. This represents a premium of nearly 32%. While part of this premium accounts for the 15% import duty, a residual premium of 5% to 10% persists due to the acute shortage of physical silver in the country.

Yung highlighted a secondary consequence: the rise in gold smuggling. Because silver is bulky and relatively cheap, it is logistically difficult to smuggle. With silver imports curbed, illicit traders have shifted focus to gold, which poses a significant revenue challenge for the Indian government as transactions bypass official channels. Yung anticipates that this pressure, along with the economic impact of the shortage, will likely force a policy reversal, potentially returning import duties to 6% or lower.

### COMX Inventory Reversal and Geopolitical Speculation

The cessation of Indian imports has altered the flow of physical silver between global hubs. For the past six months, COMEX silver inventories have been drawn down as physical metal flowed from COMEX to the London Bullion Market Association (LBMA) and subsequently to China. Bullion banks exploited this by shorting silver at COMEX and importing cheaper metal from the LBMA.

However, the Indian policy shift has reversed this dynamic. The market has entered a slight contango, where COMEX futures prices exceed LBMA spot prices, signaling a halt in the arbitrage flow to India.

Yung also speculated on geopolitical motivations, suggesting that the United States, potentially via administration figures like Donald Trump and Scott Bessent, may have encouraged India and China to curb commodity imports. This strategy could aim to suppress global commodity and energy prices, thereby mitigating inflation pressures in the U.S. economy.

### Liquidity Risks: The Yen Carry Trade and Mining Stocks

Beyond India, broader macroeconomic risks loom over precious metals. Yung pointed to the narrowing spread between the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield and the 10-year Japanese Government Bond yield as a critical indicator of potential liquidity stress. The spread has contracted to 1.8 percentage points, down from a peak of 4.18% in late 2023.

Historically, collapses in this spread have coincided with major market crises, including the 2008 financial crisis and the March 2020 COVID-19 crash. A sharp unwinding of the yen carry trade could trigger an acute liquidity squeeze, forcing a sell-off across all asset classes. This risk is reflected in the underperformance of mining stocks relative to physical metals. While the GDX gold mining ETF shows bullish technical formations, such as a descending triangle, Yung warned that smart money may be positioning for a liquidity event that could drag down equities regardless of metal prices.

### China Establishes Hong Kong as a Global Gold Hub

Simultaneously, China is advancing its strategy to internationalize its gold market through Hong Kong. Yung discussed the launch of the Hong Kong Precious Metals Central Clearing Company Limited (HKPMCC), established in April 2026 and commencing operations in early July 2026. This new entity is designed to clear over-the-counter (OTC) physical gold contracts for institutions, central banks, and foreign entities.

This development contrasts with the Shanghai Gold Exchange International (SGI), which has struggled for nearly a decade to attract foreign participation due to strict Chinese capital controls. Hong Kong offers a distinct advantage: no capital controls and a common law system. This allows foreign entities, including those from the Global South and the Middle East, to trade gold in Hong Kong and repatriate funds in U.S. dollars without restriction.

Yung noted that this infrastructure supports the potential for a "Southbound Gold Connect," mirroring the existing Southbound Stock and Bond Connect mechanisms. Such a system could allow Chinese citizens to access the Hong Kong OTC gold market, providing liquidity while keeping capital flows within a controlled loop. Additionally, Chinese commercial banks are phasing out forward gold contracts for retail customers on the Shanghai Gold Exchange (SGE) by July 24, 2026, further shifting activity toward institutional and offshore channels.

### Strategic Outlook: Crosswinds and Investment Advice

The current market environment is characterized by significant "crosswinds." While the India import situation is expected to reverse, other risks such as the unwinding of the yen carry trade and a potential burst in the AI or tech bubble could exert downward pressure on silver prices.

Despite these headwinds, Yung advised that silver's retreat from highs near $120 to the current $60 level presents a compelling entry point for long-term investors. He recommended dollar-cost averaging (DCA) for those looking to build positions, emphasizing the importance of maintaining dry powder and exercising caution due to the volatile macro landscape. For mining stocks, the advice remains measured, with liquidity risks necessitating a careful approach to equity exposure.
