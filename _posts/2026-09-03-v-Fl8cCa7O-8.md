**David Rosenberg on the risks of aggressive monetary policy, the AI boom, and the coming bond market correction**

## Introduction

David Rosenberg, President of Rosenberg Research, argues that the Federal Reserve is on the verge of making a "big blunder" by raising interest rates into a slowing economy and an ongoing oil price shock. In a recent interview, Rosenberg, who is launching a new ETF, the ROSY ETF, on September 9th, detailed why the current hawkish pivot by the new Fed Chair, Kevin Warsh, is disconnected from economic reality. Rosenberg contends that the market is pricing in multiple rate hikes, a move he believes will trigger a "law of unintended consequences," leading to a significant de-risking in equities and a rejection of duration in the bond market.

## The Misunderstanding of Yields and Inflation

Rosenberg challenges the prevailing narrative that rising Treasury yields are driven by fiscal deficits or inflation expectations. He points out that while the 10-year Treasury yield has risen to 4.79%, breakeven inflation rates (around 2.3%) have remained relatively stable. Instead, he attributes the yield spike to a "reset of Fed expectations" following Warsh's hawkish guidance at Jackson Hole.

"Everybody always tries to attach a narrative to the price action," Rosenberg stated. "But what's changed is that we have a new Fed chairman who I think has lost his footing."

Rosenberg highlights a historical pattern where new Fed chairs often face a financial or economic crisis shortly after taking office, a trend he believes Warsh is following. He notes that Warsh's focus on headline inflation and commodity prices, rather than wages and core services, is a fundamental misstep.

## The Two-Pronged Economy: AI and the Equity Wealth Effect

A central theme of Rosenberg's analysis is the fragility of the current US economy. He describes it as a "two-pronged economy" supported by only two factors:
1.  **The AI Capital Expenditure Boom:** A massive spending surge in data center construction and technology infrastructure.
2.  **The Equity Wealth Effect:** Spending driven by the rise in stock market values, particularly among high-net-worth individuals.

Rosenberg argues that this is a precarious foundation. Unlike the late 1990s, when the internet boom was accompanied by broad-based economic growth (3-4% GDP), today's economy is growing at a sluggish 1.5% real GDP. He points to data showing that while AI capex is up 16% year-over-year, non-tech capex is flat, and the construction sector (excluding AI-related projects) is contracting.

"The AI spending boom is siphoning vitality out of the rest of the economy," Rosenberg explained. "It's a tax on the economy. When you get a price shock and you don't get a wage response, you get negative real income growth."

## The Oil Shock and the "Law of Unintended Consequences"

Rosenberg warns that hiking rates into an oil price shock is particularly dangerous. An oil shock acts as a tax on consumers, reducing disposable income. If the Fed hikes rates, it further tightens credit, potentially pushing the economy into a recession.

"We are in a riskier investing environment right now," he said. "That means that you don't really want to take on duration in the bond market and it means you want to at the margins start de-risking in the stock market."

He predicts that if the Fed proceeds with a hike on September 16th, it will be a "pretty big policy mistake." The combination of a slowing labor market (with zero employment growth in the past year) and rising financing costs for the AI sector could lead to a sharp correction.

## The ROSY ETF: A Barbell Strategy

Amidst these macro risks, Rosenberg is launching the ROSY ETF (ticker: RS Y) on the TSX on September 9th. Managed by Corton Capital, the fund is designed as a "barbell" strategy:
*   **Fixed Income:** Short-duration bonds in regions with flat yield curves and high real interest rates, providing a cash flow stream.
*   **Hard Assets:** Exposure to commodities (gold, uranium, rare earths, oil, base metals) as a hedge against goods inflation and supply security issues.

The fund is also 70% inversely correlated to the US dollar, positioning it as a hedge against a potential bear market in the dollar and equities. Rosenberg notes that while he is not an "inflationist" in the traditional sense, he sees goods inflation and supply shocks as a persistent theme that requires a defensive posture.

## Conclusion: A Critical Juncture

Rosenberg concludes that the market is at a critical juncture. The Fed's hawkish stance, combined with the fragility of the AI-driven economy and the oil shock, creates a high-risk environment. He advises investors to be cautious, avoid long-duration bonds, and consider de-risking equities.

"The bar is set really high for the Fed not to move," Rosenberg said. "If they do move, it will be a mistake. We are heading into a period where the 'law of unintended consequences' will play out, and investors need to be prepared for a de-risking event."
