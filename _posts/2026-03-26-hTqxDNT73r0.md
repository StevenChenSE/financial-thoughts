*March 25, 2026 – In a stark interview on Capital Cosm, energy expert Shubam Garg, CEO of White Tundra Investment and Prosper Energy, issued a sobering forecast: the world is sleepwalking into an unprecedented energy crisis that could see oil prices surge to $200 per barrel and trigger widespread economic and social disruption.*

“We’re in unprecedented times here,” Garg stated. “They say sometimes you don’t know you’re living through history until after the fact. And I think we’re in that time right now as we speak.”

His warning hinges on a perilous combination: a long-building structural bull market in oil, now supercharged by the ongoing military crisis in the Strait of Hormuz.

### The Perfect Storm: A Predicted Cycle Meets a Geopolitical Accelerant

Garg argues that today’s soaring prices aren’t merely a reaction to the Strait of Hormuz conflict but the culmination of a years-long setup. For over a decade, global oil demand has grown steadily by 1-1.5% annually (1-1.3 million barrels per day), while the supply cushion has eroded.

“The world is running on no insurance policy,” he explained. U.S. shale, which supplied over 110% of demand growth for years, has hit a wall. Production is now flat or declining. Meanwhile, global inventories—the “kitchen cabinet” of the oil market—have been drained by years of strong demand and strategic reserve sales.

“We had drained our inventories… The world didn’t have that lever to pull and say, ‘Bam, here’s the barrels.’”

Enter the Strait of Hormuz crisis. With an estimated **10-11 million barrels per day of oil currently offline or stuck**, the global system has been hit with a supply shock of historic proportions.

“This has never happened before,” Garg emphasized. “Every single day we're losing 11 million barrels per day.”

### The Domino Effect: From Shortages to Societal Shifts

The immediate impact is being felt most acutely in import-dependent regions like South Korea, Southeast Asia, Australia, and parts of Europe, where gasoline shortages and panic are already emerging.

But Garg sees two broader, lasting impacts:

1.  **A Severe Supply Squeeze:** Even if the crisis abates, refilling depleted inventories will take time and keep prices elevated. “You would need 10 days of an oil oversupply of a million barrels per day to gain one day back right now of the war dragging on.”
2.  **A “Global Regime Change” in Mindset:** Countries and consumers, shaken by how quickly economies can be “held at gunpoint,” will seek their own insurance. This means governments building larger strategic reserves, companies stockpiling fuel, and households keeping extra gasoline on hand. This widespread “restocking” will itself become a new source of demand, sustaining higher prices.

“People are generally very rational,” Garg noted. “They like to protect themselves… These are small price to pay to keep some excess insurance policy in place.”

### North America: Insulated, But Not Immune

While North America is more self-sufficient and better inventoried, Garg cautions against complacency. The region is the world’s largest consumer and is not immune to price shocks.

“The cheap U.S. shale has lulled people into a sense of abundance… North America insulated, but we shouldn't take this for granted.”

He predicts a painful adaptation, where consumers will have to cut spending elsewhere in their budget to afford higher fuel costs, which are essential for the economy. “We’re going to have to find a way to absorb this higher price.”

### The $200 Scenario: A Glimpse into Chaos

When asked about the potential for oil to reach $200—a target cited by some involved in the conflict—Garg didn’t dismiss it, especially if the crisis drags on.

**“If this thing drags on till September, you will see $200 oil,”** he stated bluntly. “You will see absolute demand destruction as maximum as possible. You will see planes grounded. You will see a complete shift in people’s energy consumption.”

He estimates the world has perhaps **“30 [to] 60 days… of still like relative calm,”** before facing “absolute chaos.”

However, he believes the physical and economic impossibility of a months-long disruption will force a resolution sooner. The real question isn’t a fleeting price spike, but where the new equilibrium settles.

“Do we end up being above $80 oil… for the next two years? Do we end up above $100 a barrel for the next two years? That to me is more important.”

### A Final Warning: Time to Wake Up

Garg concludes with a call for awareness and resilience, urging a move away from a just-in-time mindset toward one of preparedness.

“The world has been just sleepwalking… Maybe a bit of a warning signal here that the world should not be so complacent because when these issues occur, they occur unexpectedly and they go on for a lot bigger and a lot longer than people usually expect.”

His final assessment is a chilling summary of the moment: “I don't think the global powers that be have control of the situation as they've been claiming to for the last many years.” The coming months will test whether the world’s energy system—and its leaders—are up to the challenge.
