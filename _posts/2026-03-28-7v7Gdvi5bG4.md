After a volatile week that began with a dramatic selloff, gold and silver closed higher—sparking a pressing question among investors: **has the bottom finally been reached?**

The week opened with a sharp assault on precious metals, seeing gold drop roughly $400 in a single day. Yet by Friday’s close, both metals had not only recovered but posted gains. This reversal, marked by what technical analysts call a **“hammer” candlestick pattern** on the weekly chart, signals a potential rejection of the selloff and a resurgence of bullish control.

“Does that mean the bottom is in? Well, only time will tell,” the host noted. “If we see continued strength in the next couple of weeks, yes, it could be in.”

## The Stagflation Trade Gains Momentum

Friday’s price action is being viewed by many as a **microcosm of a broader shift** toward stagflation investing—a environment where growth stagnates while inflation persists. In such a scenario, hard assets like gold and silver are increasingly seen as the new safe havens, especially as traditional shelters like stocks and bonds struggle.

“I spoke about this a few days ago about the fact that gold and silver are going to become the next safe haven assets,” the host remarked, contrasting them with treasuries and government bonds.

The data from the week supports this decoupling narrative:
*   **Gold** opened the week at $4,472 (spot) and closed at $4,494.
*   **Silver** opened at $67.25 and closed notably higher at $69.72.
*   Mining ETFs surged, with the Global Silver Miners ETF (SLV) up **13.5%** for the week.
*   Meanwhile, major stock indices and long-term treasury ETFs (TLT) finished the week in the red.

## Putting the Correction in Historical Context

To assess whether the recent drop is a major top or a correction within an ongoing bull market, it’s instructive to look at history. The host outlined key pullbacks from the 2001-2011 bull run:
*   **2003:** ~18% correction
*   **2006:** ~25% correction
*   **2008:** ~34% correction (during the Lehman Brothers crisis)

In the current bull market, corrections have been notably shallower until recently:
*   Early 2024: 8.4%
*   2025: 9.26%
*   Summer 2025: 11%
*   Late 2025: 11%

The current decline, measured from a high of $5,439, sits at approximately **24.73%**. “We’ve heard people say, ‘Oh, this is the biggest correction since 1983.’ Well, in nominal dollar terms, yes, but in percentage terms, it wasn't,” the host clarified, referencing the larger 2008 drop.

## Voices from the Analyst Community

The shift hasn’t gone unnoticed by seasoned market watchers. Key insights from respected analysts were highlighted:

**Tavi Costa** of Azoria Capital pointed to a critical breakout in agricultural commodities after a 20-year resistance level, warning of the social and political consequences of rising food prices. More pointedly for metals, he observed:
> “Stagflation is written all over this move today… Gold decisively decoupling from overall equities and rising in step with oil and other hard assets.”

He added that metals remain “supply constrained, historically under-owned relative to financial assets, and deeply undervalued.”

**Peter Spina** of GoldSeek suggested a geopolitical pivot: “There will be a point where gold and silver prices will no longer negatively react to the war in the Middle East but react very positively—and that point may be underway now.”

Perhaps most notably, **Robert Gotautas**, a former bullion banker, turned constructive:
> “I’m getting constructive on gold again… Gold held and bounced hard off the 200-day moving average… Sentiment reset. This is how bases form.”

He identified the key signal: when gold rises despite higher oil prices and rising yields, the liquidation phase may be over.

## The Macro Backdrop: Why Stagflation Looms

The fundamental case hinges on a constrained Federal Reserve. The host argued that central banks will ultimately prioritize financial market stability and employment over fighting inflation.
> “They’re going to keep providing liquidity… and I think the Fed will allow inflation to run hot.”

This environment—characterized by high debt, leverage, and a derivatives-laden financial system—is seen as even more fragile than the 1970s, potentially setting the stage for a powerful, sustained rally in hard assets.

## A Personal Move and the Path Forward

Adding a personal note, the host revealed acting on his conviction by purchasing physical gold during Monday’s selloff, despite an initial plan to wait. “I thought this is just being given away… I will continue to buy because in two, three, five years, I probably will have wished that I bought a lot more.”

The final question remains: Is this the turn, or is one more flush lower needed before the next leg up?
> “Nobody knows, right? But if it does come… I’m going to hold on.”

The takeaway for investors is patience and conviction in the long-term fundamentals. As the host concluded, echoing trader Jesse Livermore, the best results often come not to the smartest, but to the most patient who hold for the long term.

With speculation building around potential explosive moves in silver and the stagflation trade appearing to awaken, the metals market is sending a clear, if volatile, signal: the landscape for safe havens is changing.
