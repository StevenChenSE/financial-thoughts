## The Bull Case for $1 Million Bitcoin Remains Intact

When Michael Turpin appeared on the show at Consensus Miami, his $1 million Bitcoin prediction by 2033 still stands firm—but the path may be rockier than many expect. The founder and CEO of Transform Ventures, dubbed by CNBC as the "godfather of crypto," has spent years developing the Super Cycle framework that reframes how we think about Bitcoin's price trajectory.

Despite short-term turbulence, Turpin sees a clear roadmap to $1 million Bitcoin, driven by structural changes in how institutions and corporations are accumulating the asset. The thesis hasn't changed; if anything, the underlying factors have strengthened.

## Michael Saylor's Strategic Accumulation: Changing the Game

The most significant shift since Turpin's last appearance is MicroStrategy's unprecedented buying program. Saylor has publicly committed to an ambitious expansion strategy—targeting $10 billion per month, then $100 billion monthly, ultimately aiming for $1 trillion and eventually $10 trillion in Bitcoin purchases.

"What that has done is perhaps put a floor on how low it can go," Turpin explains. "I saw him at the Bitcoin conference in Las Vegas where he did a special session for whales after his main speech. His thesis is to get to 10 billion a month, then 100 billion, then a trillion. As that goes up, he can buy more and more Bitcoin. I don't know if he's ever going to get to 10 trillion, but he's certainly going to get to 10 billion pretty quickly."

### The STRAT Dividend Strategy

MicroStrategy's 11.5% dividend yield—nearly three times what T-bills offer—has created a retail phenomenon alongside institutional interest. The key question many ask: how does Saylor pay such a substantial dividend?

"He has to show that if needed, he can sell Bitcoin to pay the dividends because there's a lot of it," Turpin says. "That doesn't mean I see him doing it anytime soon. Most MATs when they sold Bitcoin was because they had to at the bottom of the market. Their boards were in turmoil and crisis—that's not the case with Saylor or MicroStrategy. They're firm long-term believers."

The financial engineering works because borrowing at 11-12% while Bitcoin appreciates more than 20% annually remains profitable with the right structure. This creates what Saylor calls an "inoculation" or "escape valve"—the ability to sell if necessary without fundamentally changing the thesis.

### Why Saylor's Buying Creates a Price Floor

Saylor claims his buying doesn't move the price. Turpin disagrees about the implications: "I didn't say it moved the price the day he buys it. What I think it does is puts a floor on how low it's going to go because I would think that he would buy a lot more if it actually went down to say $39,000 or something like that."

The mechanism works through Over-The-Counter (OTC) purchases, which mask much of the buying behavior. This opacity benefits large accumulators: "OTC masks a lot of buying and selling behavior. Historically, a lot of the volatility when whales control things was that they would buy things OTC and then dump on the market, causing prices down, and then short at the same time. That's behavior Wall Street knows how to do."

## The October Bottom Thesis: Technical Analysis Points to Q4 2025

Turpin has consistently predicted October 2025 as the capitulation bottom. Multiple technical indicators support this view:

### Key Metrics Driving the October Prediction

- **Coin Days Destroyed**: Historical analysis suggests a $42,000 bottom aligned with this metric
- **23-Month Pattern**: Twice in a row, from initial all-time high to capitulation took 23 months—adding 23 months to the March 2024 high points to October 2025
- **200-Week Moving Average**: February came close but didn't quite reach the level that has marked every previous bottom. Bitcoin needed to drop below $57,000 to touch it but only went to $60,000 before bouncing hard

"I've been saying consistently that I thought it was about 70% chance the bottom was not in yet," Turpin states. "Today we actually had the first day we tipped up to $83,000. I think it's a good opportunity to short. My fund is doing that. But we're probably more like a 40% chance the bottom was in."

### Why This Cycle Is Different (But Not That Different)

Every cycle, one or two things change, but when the majority changes, you have to wonder about the cycle overall. This time:

- ETFs have created unprecedented retail access (including through Charles Schwab accounts)
- New derivatives platforms like Hyperliquid allow leverage trading previously impossible in crypto
- MicroStrategy's corporate treasury strategy represents a fundamentally new buyer

"The ETFs have really been providing a little bit of a cushion so that we don't drop quite as low," Turpin explains. "Every halving cycle you've had diminishing returns and you've also had diminishing losses. This was certainly the lowest of the diminishing returns—barely 2x. I thought it'd be 3x with neutral macro. I was expecting we'd be down about 66%, and so far we've only been down like 54% from $126,000 to $60,000."

The floor: "$40,000 is probably off the table now. I think we'll still get down a bit below $60,000, but it wouldn't surprise me if it goes down to somewhere between $48,000 and $55,000, maybe even $57,000. As long as it goes below that $60,000 mark in February and touches the 200-week moving average, then I feel like the cycle is still intact."

## The Four-Year Cycle: Satoshi's Electoral Masterstroke?

Turpin believes Satoshi Nakamoto intentionally designed Bitcoin's halving schedule to align with US presidential elections—a seemingly accidental but brilliantly calculated move.

"He never said specifically in the white paper that he was going to have this, but I believe it's not an accident that the halvings have been 2012, 2016, 2020, 2024. The next one's going to be 2028. I don't believe he planned to have it move out of the presidential year so quickly because 2016, I mean, if you take away 2016, we've been pretty close to the four-year cycle."

The mechanism is elegant: Bitcoin's algorithm creates 10-minute blocks. If blocks arrive faster than 10 minutes, difficulty increases; if slower, it decreases. This dynamic ensures approximately four-year cycles—roughly matching US presidential terms.

"Global liquidity tends to be driven by presidential elections and policies," Turpin explains. "It doesn't matter where this person is from. He realized or she realized that once you get past the first five, six, seven cycles and you get into the super cycle effect, the US is still the most powerful economy in the world that affects all global liquidity."

The result: bull markets occur during election years, bear markets during midterms.

## The Whale Sell-Off: Opportunity in Disguise

The question everyone asks: who's selling while Saylor is buying?

"Whales who are not playing trading games," Turpin answers. "Whales who sold a significant position—you know, pretty much in September, October, November."

These aren't the day-traders. Looking at on-chain data reveals the pattern: "Twice every four-year cycle, they go and sell near the top. They usually get a little bit wrong—usually sell a little bit early. Then they buy back after the bottom's already in. They usually come back a little bit late because they think it's going to go lower. And you saw that very, very clearly in 2021-22. Whales selling at the top and buying back at the bottom."

The thesis of the Four Seasons of Bitcoin—summer, fall, winter, spring—holds that selling at the end of Bitcoin summer yields prices far higher than buying at the start of Bitcoin winter.

"Bitcoin fall is where all your decision-making that makes a difference happens. If you're accurately able to tell the first day of Bitcoin fall, which is the bubble popping, and the last day of Bitcoin fall, which is the final capitulation, you make 4x per cycle."

## AI vs. Bitcoin: Competition or Complement?

The narrative suggests AI is the new "sexy" investment taking capital from Bitcoin. Turpin sees it differently.

"It's two things. First of all, it's a giant pie, right? Yes, there's a lot of stock market investors shifting from other parts of the stock market to the Mag 7 to AI. There's a big part of institutional, venture capital putting 80-something percent of their budgets into AI, but these are within defined asset classes. It's not like gold investors are dumping gold and putting everything into AI. And same thing with crypto, same with Bitcoin."

### The AI Token Opportunity

More interesting is the rotation between crypto subsectors: "AI tokens are going to outperform Bitcoin in the next three years—the top ones at least. A lot of those gains will then roll back into Bitcoin and increase as new money comes into AI tokens from outside the system. Eventually when someone sells, they put into Bitcoin."

Evidence already exists: "The Venice token, VVV, is up 500%—went from like $2 to almost $10. You also have Bit Tensor has doubled. And that's still nowhere near its high from the last time."

## Understanding Diminishing Returns: Why $1 Million Still Works

Every subsequent bull cycle shows lower growth rates from trough to peak. Doesn't this mathematically imply growth approaching zero?

"That's why the Super Cycle part is important," Turpin responds.

The historical progression tells the story:

| Cycle | Return | Drop |
|-------|--------|------|
| First (pre-halving) | 3,000x ($0.0001 to $30) | 97% to $1 |
| Second (post-first halving) | 100x ($12 to $1,200) | 85% |
| Third | 30x | 83% |
| Fourth | 3x (intended 10x, clipped by bad macro) | 75% to $15,600 |

"Next number is 10, right? In that sort of a progression. I was projecting we would have had a 10x increase from the $8,700 halving in 2020. We ended up getting cut short around $68,000, and I think that's because of bad macro—Biden's war on crypto, choke point 2.0, and the rapid rise of interest rates. It only got clipped about 20%, but it did. So it went up 8x instead of 10x."

The ETFs and Saylor's buying have changed dynamics. "We ended up having suppressed demand show up at the end of the fourth cycle, the month before the halving, with the ETFs going live and us hitting $73,000, which is roughly 9x."

## The Threat Nobody Talks About: AI vs. Smart Contracts

Everyone discusses quantum computing threatening Bitcoin. Nobody discusses AI threatening Ethereum—which Turpin considers more vulnerable.

"I'm not talking about quantum and Ethereum. I'm talking about smart contracts built on Ethereum. There wouldn't be so much quantum—it would be some of these newer frontier models in AI like Mythos that they're afraid to release. They think it's so powerful. If that gets out and it gets into the wrong hands, it could find exploits."

The DAO hack in 2016 provides precedent: "$60 million—double-digit amount of the entire value of Ethereum—collapsed the price from $30 to $6. Vitalik made the decision along with the community to roll back the Ethereum blockchain."

This creates risk: "I think it's very possible that you'll have AI crack some significant smart contract and that will be a crisis that will drive the price of Ethereum way down. There'll probably be collateral damage on Bitcoin. I would see that as one of the main things that could happen between now and October that would then be your FTX moment. Let's say LEO cracked—how much is staked on LEO? If we got sucked out and it goes to North Korea, that would definitely cause a lot of collateral damage and I could see that bring us down into the $40,000s."

## Quantum Computing: Not a Bitcoin Problem (Yet)

While smart contracts face AI risks, Bitcoin's architecture provides natural protection.

"Quantum's threat is that it can go in and figure out what a private key is by trying what would normally take billions of years and in the future could take a few minutes to go and guess every single variation of a 45-character alphanumeric string. But I think before that happens—which I believe is still 15-20 years away—they're going to go after other SHA 256 targets like the defense industry, hospitals, banks. These are all built on SHA 256. If you can crack Satoshi's wallet or you can crack JP Morgan, you're probably going to have to go after JP Morgan first."

Additionally, attacking Bitcoin requires attacking wallets one at a time. "You're not going to crack every wallet in Bitcoin. It's a very distributed, decentralized system."

## Wall Street's Dark Arts: Understanding Market Manipulation

The 10 a.m. Jane Street controversy illustrates how institutional players manipulate markets.

"The thesis was that Jane Street was systematically selling at 10:00 a.m.—half hour after the markets open—flooding the market with 'price is going to dump today because of XYZ news.' Well, the price is going to dump because you were systematically selling at 10 a.m., and then they would have shorts in and they would make money. It's been widely reported enough that it probably happened, and it's not happening anymore. Prices have gone up since then."

This reflects broader patterns: "You have a lot of invisible parts of the market in stocks as well—dark pools, OTC desks. Historically, the way that whales would make money in any market would be they would buy OTC and then short on chain in a smaller exchange, and the rest would automatically follow because you have very quick arbitrage between the exchanges."

Large players can manufacture dumps they profit from: "They have enough Bitcoin that they can cause the dump by themselves, and then they buy it back at where they dumped it to, and they also collect money on the short."

## The Super Cycle Thesis: Beyond Simple Halvings

Traditional analysis misses what's happening to Bitcoin's supply dynamics.

"Right now, even though we're only in the fifth cycle, we are 96% out of Bitcoin. The most money to be made was in the first cycle when you could have mined things at effectively below a penny. But Bitcoin has withstood an awful lot of attacks."

The supply schedule becomes increasingly aggressive: "Every four years we're at 0.88%, it's going to go to 0.44%, 0.22%. And gold, they keep finding new gold every year. It keeps around 1.5%. In 100 years, if gold stays at 1.5%, that means 150% more gold, but only 4% more Bitcoin—and then none."

Scarcity compounds: "If we get to one million Bitcoin and we have the same demand, do the math. You're looking at something that is unbelievably deflationary in terms of the amount that gets produced."

## Stablecoins: The Hidden Infrastructure Revolution

The Tether story illustrates how crypto has evolved beyond original expectations.

"I was involved in the dawn of Tether when Brock Pierce and Reeve Collins and the team there—Craig put together Tether in Santa Monica in early 2014 and then they ended up selling into Bitfinex and spun it off. Their thoughts in those days was: we're going to be able to go and have a coin that represents a dollar so that you can trade between exchanges and not wait three days, and we'll make money on interest from banks. That was it."

What happened instead: "I don't think any of them thought that one day Tether would be making $20 billion a year in T-bills with 100 employees."

More importantly: "The more that you have people having stable coins, they're now very easily able to buy Bitcoin, Ethereum, any other token because they finally have a wallet. You've had this mass explosion. Stable coins are again a massive new force in the economics of crypto."

## Market Structure: Why ETFs Changed Everything

ETFs have created a fundamentally different retail investor than previous generations.

"First-generation retail—I'd say is the majority of retail buyers of ETFs—are not people who were already on Coinbase. They're ones who are uncomfortable doing self-custody, buying even on Coinbase. And all of a sudden it's like, 'Oh, I can do it through my Charles Schwab account.' And so they bought."

These investors behave differently: "They typically are a little bit less paperhand in terms of selling when the price goes down. They do sell, but it's been a lower level than four years ago, eight years ago when it was just people who had heard about Bitcoin, bought because their friends were making money, bought at the top, and then panic sold at or near the bottom."

Their brokers help: "They called their broker and said, 'No, no, no, keep this in your IRA for 10 years.' And so you do have less selling at the bottom."
