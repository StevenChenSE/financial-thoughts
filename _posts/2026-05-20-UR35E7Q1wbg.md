## Why Canada Can't Afford to Overlook Gold

When Ranch Premier led the Yukon territory, he witnessed something that would permanently reshape his understanding of Canada's economic resilience: during the COVID-19 pandemic, the Yukon and Nunavut were the only two regions in the country to achieve GDP growth. The driver? Gold mining.

"We had a global pandemic and there were two regions in the country that saw actual GDP growth," Pillai explained. "One of them was the Yukon and the other one was Nunavut. It was gold producers."

This experience crystallized a belief that Pillai carries into his new role as CEO and president of SAVA Mining Corp. (ticker: SEVA on the TSXV): gold isn't just another commodity—it's Canada's economic insurance policy.

## The Bank of Canada Paradox: G7's Only Gold-Free Reserve

Here's a striking fact that puzzles many observers of the Canadian economy: the Bank of Canada is the only central bank among G7 nations that holds no gold in its reserves.

The Bank of Canada began selling off its gold reserves approximately two decades ago in what became a decades-long drawdown process. Today, despite gold trading above $4,600 per ounce—representing a 150% increase from recent lows—the institution has made no announcements about purchasing gold back.

Pillai doesn't mince words about this policy: "I disagree with the policy. I think it's something that we should revisit."

His concern stems from broader global trends. Central banks worldwide are increasing their gold allocations. BRICS nations have dramatically shifted their reserve composition, moving from approximately 11% to 17% of global reserves into gold. New gold ETFs continue launching, including a significant one in Hong Kong just recently.

"I look to see what European banks are saying," Pillai noted. "I'm looking to see what Wells Fargo is saying. The word they're sharing with the investment world is that the $6,000 gold price target is attainable."

The driving forces behind this institutional demand include:

- **Geopolitical instability** and the "resetting of who are allies"
- **De-dollarization trends** as nations seek alternatives to U.S. dollar exposure
- **Historical precedent** as investors return to the asset "humans have moved towards for thousands of years"
- **Supply chain security** concerns following recent global disruptions

When asked whether he expects the Bank of Canada to reconsider its stance—particularly given polling showing 64% of Canadians openly distrusting Americans—Pillai was measured but pointed.

"I wouldn't be shocked if that's not a conversation that's pretty active these days," he said. "The folks at the Bank of Canada and what we're seeing from the federal government is they're watching what's happening globally, watching what's happening with BRICS, watching what's happening in a move away with maybe some concern about the U.S. dollar."

## From Politics to Private Sector: A Strategic Return

Pillai's transition from premier of the Yukon to leading a junior mining company wasn't a career pivot—it was a homecoming.

"I worked in the sector previous to getting into politics," he explained. "I really appreciated the experience I had, had a lot of fun doing the work previously."

The return to mining required retraining his mindset. "You're moving from being in a decade in the policy work now coming back into the mining sector, coming back into private business. You're building a whole different network of individuals—whether it's with capital markets or the technical teams that we rely on."

What does a former politician bring to a management team that traditional mining executives—often geologists or bankers by training—might lack?

Pillai identifies his strength in relationship-building, particularly with Indigenous communities. "I spent a lot of time over the last 15 years either advising First Nation governments, negotiating, whether it was in municipal government or in the territorial government, sitting at the table getting to understand what the priorities were."

The insight is practical: "There's a lot of similarities across this country on where communities want to go, but I think it's just that understanding that you have to really make sure you spend the time and understand what the priorities are, the uniqueness of each community, and then how do you marry that with the supports you can bring to the table."

## The Government Awakening: Mining's Political Renaissance

For investors evaluating Canadian mining jurisdictions, permitting timelines matter enormously. Industry leaders frequently cite frustration with 15-year permitting processes. Yet the political landscape has transformed dramatically.

"180," Pillai responded when asked how federal government attitudes toward mining have changed since his time in office. "I always judged it by whether ministers attended PDAC. In the previous prime minister's administration, he showed up at PDAC and it made a lot of noise. Now we see multiple premiers showing up—not just to show up but making announcements for their jurisdiction, coast to coast."

What catalyzed this shift?

Pillai points to several factors:

- **Supply chain awareness** following recent disruptions that exposed vulnerability in critical mineral sourcing
- **Recognition that countries like Canada play essential roles** in providing offtake security for allied nations
- **The AI-driven economy** that depends heavily on data centers, which ultimately trace back to critical minerals and mining
- **Economic necessity** as traditional manufacturing and auto sectors face challenges

"South of us, we're seeing the U.S. federal government moving with such a sense of urgency when it comes to critical minerals," Pillai observed. "That's something I know Canada's trying to retool how they're approaching things—on policy, on permitting processes. We've seen some new legislation come through trying to make sure there's a one-window permanent process. But all of those things have to be executed. It's not just about talking about that policy. It has to move into a place where it's operationalized."

Ontario exemplifies this new approach. The province has reduced red tape for mining, invested in infrastructure, and established programs supporting Indigenous participation in mining projects. These efforts have positioned Ontario as the world's second-ranked destination for mining investment according to the Fraser Institute.

## Indigenous Partnerships: The Non-Negotiable Foundation

Every mining project in Canada sits on traditional Indigenous territory. This isn't a technicality—it defines the operational reality.

"All of them," Pillai confirmed when asked if Canadian deposits operate on Indigenous lands. "There's not a project in the country where you're not going to be having a dialogue."

SAVA Mining's approach prioritizes Indigenous partnerships above all else. The company operates in Treaty 3 territory in northwestern Ontario, adjacent to four First Nations communities.

The negotiation framework follows a structured progression:

1. **Letter of Intent**: Communicating presence and intentions to affected communities
2. **Exploration Agreement**: Formalizing early-stage cooperation and reporting protocols
3. **Advanced Exploration Agreement**: For deeper drilling and evaluation phases
4. **Impact and Benefit Agreement (IBA)**: Comprehensive economic arrangements for production-stage projects

Pillai emphasizes understanding community priorities beyond financial metrics. "In many cases, some of those priorities may be: we understand there's an opportunity in mining next door, but we really need to make sure our citizens are healthy and supported. A lot of the communities have gone through tremendous trauma, and they're trying to make sure their citizens are okay."

The model SAVA pursues involves:

- **Joint venture opportunities** for Indigenous-owned development corporations in exploration, drilling, road construction, food services, and camp infrastructure
- **Grant programs** helping Indigenous entities access capital for project participation
- **Loan guarantees** supporting Indigenous business development within mining operations
- **Cultural sensitivity** recognizing that trust-building cannot be rushed

"There's no shortcut for you or I to trust each other," Pillai reflected. "But it still can be done in the construct we're working within, knowing there's a lot of pressure to bring projects through and get them into production. If you're doing the projects right, you understand how to mitigate any environmental impacts, and that you're getting to a place where you've got community support and there's real benefit coming to the communities."

## SAVA Mining: Strategic Position in Northwestern Ontario

SAVA Mining acquired its Cameron Lake project from First Mining (which retains 44% ownership), positioning the company in one of Canada's most active gold regions.

The numbers tell a compelling story: approximately $9.6 billion in M&A activity has occurred in the region since 2020. Core Assets recently purchased the Rainy River project located 88 kilometers south of SAVA's holdings. Agnico Eagle, West Red Lake (with the Madsen project), and Nex Gold (with the Goliath property) all operate nearby—all companies with connections to the Fiori Group ecosystem that supports SAVA.

Ontario produces nearly 50% of Canada's gold output. The region offers:

- **Multigenerational workforce** experienced in mining operations
- **Established infrastructure** from historic production centers
- **Proximity to processing facilities** enabling DSO (Direct Shipping Ore) models
- **Strong government support** at both provincial and federal levels

SAVA's current resource stands at approximately 1.25 million ounces (measured and inferred), with significant exploration upside remaining across the extensive land package.

### The DSO Advantage

Unlike competitors building complete mine infrastructure, SAVA pursues a streamlined approach. "We're focused on a DSO model," Pillai explained. "We're not looking to build out all the required infrastructure that a big producing mine would have. We've got options both south and north of us to move our ore to those projects."

This strategy offers several advantages:

- **Lower capital requirements** in the near term
- **Faster pathway to revenue** through toll milling agreements
- **Preserved optionality** for future expansion or strategic alternatives

"We're looking to have discussions over 2026 to see if we could have agreements in place with projects to the south or north of us that could partner with us to mill," Pillai continued.

Pillai outlined three critical objectives for the coming year:

2. **Exploration Season Success**: Complete infrastructure preparation and execute a robust drilling program to expand the resource base
3. **Market Validation**: Demonstrate project viability to attract continued capital support and validate the investment thesis

"We're brand new and so we're at that period of building the foundation of relationships so that we're ready to go," Pillai said. "We want to get a good field season in place. We want to make sure that we can grow the resource at Cameron Lake."

The company maintains a strong treasury position and isn't currently seeking additional financing, though Pillai acknowledges ongoing conversations with interested parties.

## The Foreign Investment Question

With 64% of Canadians favoring restrictions on foreign resource sales according to Ipsos polling, Pillai addressed the implications for SAVA's future.

"Every case is different," he offered. "Where it gets sensitive is around critical minerals and rare earths, where we see countries trying to cobble together and hold them under a sovereign veil."

For gold specifically, Pillai sees less concern. "The companies that tend to be looking at consolidation or acquisition—we got great Canadian companies like Agnico Eagle doing phenomenal work in the country. We have U.S. companies that are really deeply ingrained into the Canadian economy."

He expects continued consolidation interest given SAVA's strategic positioning, but emphasized his focus remains on building value through proper development.

"We think there's something that can create a lot of value if we're in production. We know there's a lot of potential suitors around us. We're just focused on early stage of building good relationships, increasing our value through a number of different avenues, and proving that our thesis can be achieved."

## Diversification vs. Resource Focus: Canada's False Dichotomy

Some economists argue Canada must diversify away from resource dependence. Pillai sees this as a false choice.

"I think we have to do both," he asserted. "We should be looking to diversify. There's some things that are happening in the country—whether it's onshoring more of our defense spending, that's another place where there's a new pie that we can look at instead of slicing the pie up into smaller pieces."

Simultaneously, Canada must maximize its resource advantages. "We should be embracing and continuing to embrace where we are in the resource sector. We should do that and have to figure out how to get that right."

The urgency is real. "We're seeing countries like the U.S. really moving quickly to onshore processing when it comes to critical minerals, ensuring they're supporting the industry with bilateral agreements between state and federal governments. We've had one agreement of its type signed in Canada—between the federal government and British Columbia. Ontario has significant work underway between the province and federal government supporting the Ring of Fire development."

## Key Takeaways

1. **Gold serves as Canada's economic insurance**: Regional GDP data from the pandemic confirms gold mining's stabilizing role when other sectors falter, justifying continued investment in the sector.

2. **The Bank of Canada's gold-free status warrants review**: As global central banks increase gold allocations and geopolitical alliances shift, Canada's position as the only G7 nation without gold reserves appears increasingly anachronistic.

3. **Indigenous partnerships define project success**: With every Canadian mining project situated on traditional territory, building authentic relationships with First Nations communities—through joint ventures, employment, infrastructure support, and cultural sensitivity—is non-negotiable for modern mining operations.

4. **Government support has fundamentally changed**: Federal and provincial governments now actively champion mining as an economic driver, with Ontario specifically reducing permitting barriers and climbing to second globally for mining investment attractiveness.

5. **SAVA Mining offers a compelling regional story**: With 1.25 million ounces, a capital-light DSO strategy, strong institutional backing, and an experienced team, the company sits positioned to benefit from Ontario's gold sector momentum and the broader appreciation for Canadian resource security.
