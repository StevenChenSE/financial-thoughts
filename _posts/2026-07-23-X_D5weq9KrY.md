## Economist Steve Hanke Debunks the $20,000 Gold Theory and Warns of Rising Inflation

### Introduction
In a comprehensive economic analysis, Johns Hopkins University Professor Steve Hanke addresses critical market dynamics, including the feasibility of revaluing U.S. gold, the impact of geopolitical tensions on oil supplies, and the true drivers of inflation. Hanke argues that while political narratives and speculative manias create market noise, the fundamental forces of money supply and physical commodity shortages dictate economic outcomes.

### The Myth of the $20,000 Gold Revaluation
A recent viewer query prompted a discussion on the theory that the U.S. government could revalue its gold reserves to $20,000 per ounce to erase federal debt. Hanke dismisses this as a "bookkeeping entry" with no real economic impact. He explains that the U.S. Treasury owns 261.5 million troy ounces of gold, which is valued on the Federal Reserve's books at $42.22 per ounce under a 1973 statute.

Even if the government revalued gold to match the $39.6 trillion national debt, it would require a book value of approximately $40,000 per ounce. However, the actual market price is currently around $4,000. Hanke emphasizes that market-to-market value is what matters; selling the gold at current prices would yield only about $1 trillion, a fraction of the debt. Furthermore, attempting to sell such a large quantity would crash the market price. Therefore, revaluation is merely "government hocus pocus" that cannot solve fiscal deficits.

### Oil Shortages and Geopolitical Disruptions
Hanke highlights severe disruptions in global oil markets caused by the re-ignition of conflict in Iran. He attributes the effective closure of the Strait of Hormuz to actions taken by the Trump administration, which has led to renewed attacks on cargo ships. Additionally, the Houthis have shut down the Red Sea, cutting off an alternative pipeline route used by Saudi Arabia.

These dual blockades have pushed the crude oil market into backwardation, a rare condition where the spot price exceeds the futures price. This indicates a physical shortage of inventories. Hanke notes that the backwardation spread has widened from 3% to 8.2% for crude, and even more severely for gasoline (nearly 30%) and diesel (16%). While Chinese imports have temporarily softened prices, Hanke warns that China is depleting its inventories and will soon resume aggressive buying, further straining global supply.

### Inflation, The Fed, and Money Supply
Addressing the Federal Reserve's policy, Hanke criticizes the central bank's obsession with interest rates rather than the money supply. He points to Divisia M4 measures from the Center for Financial Stability, which show a 6.7% year-over-year growth rate. This exceeds Hanke's "golden growth rate" of 6%, consistent with a 2% inflation target.

Hanke argues that the "inflation genie is out of the bottle." He believes the recent inclusion of money supply metrics in the Fed's latest report under Chairman Warsh is a positive step, though he maintains that inflation is already "baked in the cake" due to past monetary accelerations. Consequently, he expects inflation to remain elevated and volatile, pressuring the Fed to raise rates despite political headwinds.

### Japan's Monetary Misconception and AI Fatigue
Hanke corrects the widespread misunderstanding of Japan's economy. He argues that Japan's low interest rates do not indicate loose monetary policy; rather, they reflect tight money supply growth, which suppresses inflation and nominal GDP. He predicts that Japanese interest rates and the yen will continue to weaken as the Bank of Japan remains confused about monetary mechanics.

In the equity market, Hanke warns of "AI fatigue." He cites the recent margin calls affecting 1.2 million brokerage accounts in South Korea, particularly in leveraged positions related to chipmaker SK Hynix. He describes this as a speculative mania where retail investors are taking excessive risks. With upcoming earnings reports, Hanke suggests the market may finally confront the reality that AI profits are not yet materializing at the scale investors expect.
