## Macro Analyst Predicts Correction Despite Market Optimism

Jim Welsh, founder and author of Macro Tides, has issued a cautionary outlook for equity markets, predicting that the recent S&P 500 rally is nearing its conclusion. Having correctly forecasted the semiconductor sector crash in July, Welsh is now warning that the subsequent rebound is likely a retracement rather than the start of a new bull trend. He anticipates a pullback of 4% to 7% in the coming weeks as the Federal Reserve prepares to raise interest rates.

### S&P 500 Rally and the Wave Theory

Welsh attributes the S&P 500’s recent rise to a technical pattern known as a triangle. Following a peak at 7,620 on June 2nd, the index chopped sideways. Welsh identified this as a "wave four" triangle, which typically resolves with a strong upward thrust. This movement was fueled by positive earnings, a decision by the Fed not to hike rates immediately, and falling oil prices.

However, Welsh notes that this thrust marks "wave five" of the rally, which historically precedes a significant pullback. While prediction markets like Koshi show a high probability of the S&P ending the year above 7,800 or even 8,000, Welsh believes a correction is imminent. He argues that for the S&P to break above 8,200, specific conditions must be met: the Strait of Hormuz must reopen, oil prices must drop, and inflation data must improve enough to dissuade the Fed from raising rates.

### The State of the U.S. Economy

Welsh describes the U.S. economy as being on "firm footing," though he acknowledges the pain felt by the bottom third of wage earners due to the cost-of-living crisis. He points out that the top 10% of earners, who account for nearly 50% of consumer spending, are driving GDP growth. This "bifurcated economy," combined with a large fiscal deficit and significant AI spending, keeps recession probabilities low in the near term.

Regarding the recent July jobs report showing a loss of 23,000 jobs, Welsh views it as an overstatement of weakness. He cites seasonal adjustments in government education jobs and weak data in leisure and hospitality as distortions. With unemployment claims under 200,000 and Challenger layoffs near two-year lows, he believes the Fed will discount this single report and focus on the broader trend of inflation.

### Fed Rate Hikes and Bond Yields

Welsh is confident that the Federal Reserve is setting the table for a rate hike, likely in September or October. He notes that the 2-year Treasury yield, currently trading above the Fed funds rate, historically moves in anticipation of hikes. He argues that Fed Chair Jerome Powell needs to act on his tough talk regarding inflation to maintain credibility and dismiss claims of political influence.

On the bond market, Welsh sees a long-term trend of rising yields globally. He believes the 10-year Treasury yield will eventually break above 5%, following the 30-year bond which has already surpassed its previous highs. He views the bond market's revolt as a global phenomenon driven by volatility, not just U.S. policy.

### Semiconductor Sector: Recovery or Retracement?

Welsh correctly predicted the semiconductor ETF (SMH) crash in July based on technical patterns and overbought sentiment. He now views the rebound to 578 as a retracement rally within a larger downtrend. He expects SMH to test 590 before rolling over again and potentially taking out the recent low of 503.

Welsh highlights a divergence between price and market breadth. While the S&P 500 is hitting new highs driven by AI-related stocks and the "Mag 7," the internal thrust of the market is weak. He explains that a "weak thrust" means fewer stocks are participating in the rally compared to the strong advance seen after the March 30th low. This lack of broad participation is a warning sign that the rally is running out of energy.

### Gold and Gold Stocks Outlook

Welsh remains bullish on gold and gold stocks, though he views the recent gold price surge to 4,400 as a retracement rally within a larger correction. Using Elliott Wave theory, he identifies the current move as "wave four," suggesting more volatility and a potential decline below 3,945 before the next major bull leg.

However, he is highly bullish on gold miners, represented by the GDX ETF. He notes a falling wedge pattern and positive divergence in the Relative Strength Index (RSI) for GDX. He expects GDX to reach new all-time highs above 117 if gold continues its upward trajectory. He advises buying gold stocks on pullbacks while selling into the strength of the semiconductor rally.

### Geopolitical Risks and Market Psychology

Welsh expresses low confidence in a peaceful resolution to the tensions at the Strait of Hormuz, comparing Iran's strategy to Muhammad Ali’s "rope-a-dope." He believes a re-escalation there poses a significant risk to equity markets, particularly if it disrupts oil flow.

Despite geopolitical risks, high oil prices, and inflation, the market remains resilient due to strong corporate earnings. Institutional investors, biased toward "buy and hold," are justifying higher prices based on earnings growth, largely driven by the tech sector. However, Welsh warns that a failure to resolve geopolitical issues could negatively impact the perception of U.S. global power and trigger market volatility.

### Summary and Trading Strategy

Jim Welsh’s current trading strategy reflects his bearish near-term view on equities and bullish stance on commodities. He is selling into the semiconductor rebound, buying gold stocks on pullbacks, and lightening his exposure to the S&P 500 in anticipation of a 4% to 7% correction. He emphasizes that while the Fed’s forward guidance has historically been unreliable, the market must react to raw economic data and technical signals rather than relying on central bank hand-holding.
