## The Current State of the Market: A V-Shape Recovery

The stock market has experienced a dramatic V-shape recovery following a significant sell-off in July, where semiconductors and Korean stocks led the charge downwards. Despite the Nasdaq seeing an 11% drawdown and momentum stocks experiencing a 50% decline, sentiment has improved dramatically. Jay Singh, founder of Special Situations Report, joins to discuss the big picture, noting that while the S&P 500 has been range-bound, the recovery in August has been driven by fundamental earnings strength rather than speculative leverage.

## Why the Rally Is Sustainable: Earnings and Valuation

Singh argues that the current rally is supported by robust earnings growth, with S&P 500 earnings growth reaching 50% year-to-date, a figure he describes as phenomenal. This growth has effectively lowered the 12-month P/E ratio for the S&P 500 to just 20 times, making the index less expensive than it was last year despite price increases.

Key drivers of this strength include:
*   **Broad-Based Growth:** 86% of S&P 500 companies reported positive EPS, and 76% reported positive revenue surprises.
*   **Inflation Data:** CPI met expectations while PPI came in below expectations, reducing fears of immediate Federal Reserve rate hikes.
*   **Geopolitical Insulation:** The US market remains relatively insulated from the Iran conflict due to domestic oil production and energy imports from Canada, unlike European and Asian markets.

## The AI Capex Conundrum

While earnings are strong, Singh highlights a growing concern regarding the capital expenditure (CapEx) of hyperscalers. Companies like Google, Amazon, and Microsoft are burning through cash to build AI infrastructure, with Google alone raising $85 billion in Q2. According to a Goldman Sachs report, hyperscaler purchase commitments have exploded to $1.5 trillion, with $1 trillion yet to start.

Singh notes that while this "mad dash for compute" is currently driving growth, it has shifted these tech giants from capital-light to capital-heavy businesses. The market is beginning to penalize companies for overspending, as seen in the muted reaction to Google's strong revenue growth. The sustainability of this circular financing, where providers like Nvidia are raising billions to buy their own GPUs, will be a key question for investors in the coming year.

## Stocks to Beat the Downturn

Singh shares his specific portfolio positions that he believes offer value and protection against potential volatility:

### 1. Gold Miners
With real rates potentially peaking, Singh is bullish on precious metals. He is long **AGI (Algo Metals)**, **Kinross Gold**, **Barrick Gold**, and **Agnico Eagle**. He notes that AGI is set to ramp production to over a million ounces, making it a strong candidate for the current rally.

### 2. Software and Cybersecurity
In a contrarian move, Singh bought software names that were heavily shorted by hedge funds as a hedge to their long AI positions.
*   **DataDog:** A strong performer that has benefited from the short squeeze.
*   **Wix:** Bought from the lows due to its low subscription price, making it resistant to AI replacement.
*   **ServiceNow and Snowflake:** Companies with strong moats that have seen significant rebounds.
*   **Atlassian (TEAM):** A speculative software name that surged from the high 50s to $165, though Singh has trimmed his position.

*   **CoreWeave:** A key player in GPU leasing with strong customer prepayments and contracted capacity.

## The Risks: Midterm Elections and Leverage

Singh warns that the "Goldilocks" environment may not last past the November midterm elections. He anticipates a re-escalation of geopolitical tensions and potential new tariffs once the political pressure of the elections subsides.

He also emphasizes the dangers of leverage, citing the collapse of the Situational Awareness hedge fund. The fund, run by Leopold Aschenbrenner, used four times leverage on a $45 billion exposure to AI stocks. When the market corrected, the leverage forced liquidations, resulting in a 45% drawdown. Singh advises against using margin, preferring to maintain a 20% cash position and use options for leverage if necessary.

## Second Half of 2026 Outlook

Looking ahead, Singh expects a pause in tariff and war-related fears for the next four months, allowing for a continued relief rally in risk assets. However, he suggests focusing on themes that are less dependent on pure AI hype:
*   **Power and Energy:** Companies providing grid connections, battery infrastructure, and power generation (e.g., Vistra) will see sustained demand as data centers require constant energy.
*   **Defense:** A global defense supercycle is emerging as countries increase spending as a percentage of GDP.
*   **M&A and Private Capital:** As interest rates stabilize, a rebound in mergers and acquisitions is likely.

Singh concludes that while the market is strong, investors should remain cautious and position themselves in sectors with durable cash flows and tangible assets ahead of potential political volatility in the fall.
