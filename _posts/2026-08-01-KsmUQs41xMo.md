## Inflation, War, and the "Crack-Up Boom"

Florian Grummes, managing director of Midas Touch Consulting, recently joined the show to discuss the complex interplay between inflation, geopolitical tensions, and market performance. With the Federal Reserve keeping rates steady amidst rising CPI data, Grummes attributes current inflationary pressures to a combination of supply shocks—particularly from escalating conflicts in the Middle East and Iran—and persistent global money supply expansion.

He invokes Ludwig von Mises’ "crack-up boom" theory to explain the divergence between soaring stock market indices and struggling real-world economic conditions. According to this theory, as trust in fiat currency diminishes, asset prices inflate while the real economy suffers. Grummes notes that while the Fed attempts to manage interest rates, market forces are increasingly dictating yields, with bond markets demanding higher compensation for inflation and credit risk.

## Gold: Healthy Correction, Not a Trend Reversal

Gold has experienced a significant correction, dropping approximately 30% from its January highs to test the $4,000 psychological support level. Despite this volatility, Grummes maintains that gold’s safe-haven status remains intact. He characterizes the pullback as a necessary and healthy correction following an extreme rally from $1,615 in 2022 to $5,600 in early 2024.

Looking ahead, Grummes is optimistic about a summer rally. He identifies technical improvements on the weekly chart and suggests targets at the 50-day moving average ($4,250) and potentially the 200-day moving average ($4,500) over the next few months. While he acknowledges worst-case scenarios could see gold dip to $3,500, he believes the secular bull market remains intact, driven by long-term demand for protection against purchasing power erosion.

## Silver: Parabolic Rise Leads to Hard Fall

Silver’s trajectory has been more volatile than gold’s. After a parabolic surge from $50 to $121 between late 2023 and early 2024, silver has faced a steep correction. Grummes explains that such rapid price increases inevitably trigger profit-taking and cascading stop-loss orders, leading to sharp declines. Unlike gold, silver lacks central bank buying support, making it more dependent on industrial demand, which is currently facing headwinds.

Grummes highlights a historical pattern where parabolic silver spikes are followed by long bear markets, citing the 30-year decline after the 1980 peak and the 14-year slide after 2011. However, he views the current pullback as part of a larger "cup and handle" breakout pattern. He anticipates silver will consolidate in the $45–$55 range, with a strong possibility of returning to $100 in the future, provided the $50 support level holds.

## Bitcoin: Contrarian Interest Amidst Neglect

Grummes has been largely out of the crypto space since late 2023, citing excessive hype, the proliferation of leveraged positions, and concerns over Michael Saylor’s corporate strategy. However, he is now re-evaluating Bitcoin as sentiment turns bearish and interest wanes. With Bitcoin stabilizing around the $60,000 level and showing an oversold weekly chart, he sees potential for a contrarian bounce.

Referencing the traditional four-year cycle, Grummes suggests a turning point could occur by mid-October. He projects that even a more modest 5x rally from current levels could push Bitcoin toward $300,000 over the next three years. He remains cautious but acknowledges the asset's resilience and the potential for significant upside if historical cycles hold true.

## Stagflation Risks and AI Sector Caution

In Europe, Grummes expresses concern over the European Central Bank’s decision to raise rates amidst a slowing auto sector and rising unemployment in Germany. He warns of a stagflationary scenario, characterized by persistent inflation and economic stagnation, which is particularly detrimental to ordinary citizens.

Regarding the AI and semiconductor sectors, Grummes advises caution. He notes that the recent parabolic run in semiconductor stocks mirrors previous bubbles that ended in sharp corrections. While he acknowledges the long-term potential of AI, he believes the current market dynamics and high capital expenditures create significant downside risks in the short to medium term. Investors are advised to wait for greater clarity, potentially emerging after the Jackson Hole meeting in August.
