In a special Thanksgiving episode of *Capital Cartoon*, host Danny welcomes back financial commentator Bob Moriarty to discuss what he believes is the beginning of a catastrophic, multi-decade global market crash. While many investors are celebrating recent rebounds in tech stocks, Moriarty warns that these gains are illusory and that the true crisis is only just beginning.

## South Korea: The Canary in the Coal Mine

Moriarty’s central thesis rests on the current state of the South Korean economy. He asserts that "it starts in South Korea," comparing the country's recent market behavior to Thailand during the 1998 Asian Financial Crisis. According to Moriarty, South Korea is currently experiencing a severe downturn:

*   **Market Collapse:** The KOSPI (Korean stock index) has fallen approximately 50% from its highs in June of this year, retreating to levels not seen since January.
*   **Treasury Sell-Off:** Reports suggest the South Korean government is preparing to sell $118 billion in US Treasuries to stabilize its own markets.
*   **Regulatory Distortion:** Moriarty criticizes South Korea’s ban on short sales, arguing that short selling is necessary for efficient market functioning. By suppressing short sales, South Korea is masking underlying weaknesses, but the damage is already done.

"We are not going into a new era. We're going into a very bad old era," Moriarty states, predicting that the instability in South Korea will spread globally.

## The AI Bubble and Circular Financing

A significant portion of the discussion focuses on the technology sector, which Moriarty describes as "the biggest bubble in history." He argues that recent gains in companies like Nvidia are merely a "dead cat bounce" fueled by algorithmic trading and news headlines rather than fundamental value.

Moriarty highlights specific examples of what he calls "circular firing squads" within the AI industry:

1.  **Oracle:** Once a software company with high margins, Oracle has pivoted to data centers. Moriarty notes that Oracle’s stock has crashed from $170 to $103 in three months. More alarmingly, credit default swaps on Oracle are "blowing up," signaling that the market believes the company may not survive its debt obligations.
2.  **Nvidia and AI Infrastructure:** The model of borrowing money from Nvidia to buy chips, which are then used to build data centers for other tech giants, is mathematically unsound. Moriarty cites Michael Burry’s correct prediction regarding Nvidia’s volatility, noting that even Burry had to cover his shorts due to market pressure, but the underlying fragility remains.

"When Google loses $500 billion in a day and Nvidia loses a trillion dollars in a week, you got a problem," Moriarty warns. He suggests that while AI as a technology will survive, many of the current corporate structures built around it are fraudulent or doomed to fail, similar to the dot-com bust of 2000 or the railroad bubbles of the past.

## The Liquidity Crisis and the Strong Dollar

Moriarty identifies a "giant liquidity issue" driving current market dynamics. He explains that the strength of the US dollar is not a sign of economic health but rather a "negative chase for liquidity." Investors are scrambling to hold dollars to pay off bad loans triggered by margin calls. This forced demand for cash is suppressing other asset classes, including gold, silver, and energy.

Despite this pressure, Moriarty remains bullish on precious metals in the long term. He points out that gold recently hit a low of $3,400 before rebounding to $3,572, forming a "hammer candlestick" pattern that indicates buyer strength at lower levels. He predicts that the panic selling and margin calls will end within four weeks, marking the bottom for metals.

## Investment Strategy: Buying the Dip in Mining Stocks

For Moriarty, the current downturn presents a tremendous opportunity. He is actively buying gold and silver mining stocks that have been crushed by tax-loss selling season and forced liquidations.

*   **Tax-Loss Selling:** Investors often sell losing positions at year-end to offset gains for tax purposes, creating artificial lows in fundamentally sound companies.
*   **Undervalued Assets:** Moriarty cites examples of solid mining companies trading at cents on the dollar—such as one stock trading at 16 cents that was valued at 34.5 cents two weeks prior. He notes these companies have real assets and management, but are being sold off due to margin calls rather than fundamental deterioration.
*   **The Safe Haven:** Moriarty believes mining stocks will be the ultimate safe haven once the panic subsides. He expects them to rise significantly in the next six to nine months, mirroring their performance during the 2015-2016 recovery period.

He also mentions using the Brent Crude ETF (BNO) as a liquid cash alternative while waiting for these opportunities, though he suspects government manipulation is artificially suppressing oil prices despite geopolitical tensions in Iran and Saudi Arabia.

## Conclusion: A 500-Year Transition

Moriarty concludes with a broader historical perspective, suggesting that the world is at a "500-year transition." He argues that economic power is shifting from the debt-based systems of the West to Eastern powers like China, India, Russia, and Iran.

The message for investors is clear: do not be fooled by temporary tech rallies. The AI bubble is popping, South Korea’s collapse is a leading indicator, and the resulting liquidity crisis will offer rare entry points into precious metals and mining stocks before the market enters what Moriarty predicts will be a "20-year crash" in equities.
