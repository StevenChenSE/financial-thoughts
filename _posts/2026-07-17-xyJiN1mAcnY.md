The Investment Mastermind has returned after a twelve-month hiatus, bringing together Rod, Adam Lawrence, and Manish Qataria to review a volatile quarter and share their top investment picks for Q3 2026. The session covers a turbulent macro landscape, including geopolitical shocks and market corrections, before diving into detailed analyses of precious metals, UK housebuilders, and government bonds.

## Macro Review: Geopolitics, Markets, and Housing

The macro environment over the past year has been defined by a "wall of worry," with markets navigating significant geopolitical and economic shifts. A major catalyst was the late February conflict involving Trump supporting Netanyahu in bombing Iran, which briefly spiked oil prices to $140 per barrel. While fears of a global crash emerged, the team noted that adjusted for inflation, oil would need to reach $250 to match the pressure seen in 2008. The shock caused gilt yields to surge and dashed hopes for Bank of England rate cuts, pushing mortgage rates to 6.5% before settling into an "elevated normal." Inflation missed the 2% target in April, and the job market remains fragile.

In equity markets, the SpaceX IPO generated massive hype, with Wall Street banks reportedly earning around $1 billion in fees. However, the stock has since retreated to its listing price, highlighting the cyclical nature of market sentiment. Meanwhile, US banks are reporting record revenues and profits, bolstered by transaction volumes and the broader market activity.

The UK property market presents a mixed picture. London is experiencing its worst year on record for development, with only 4,170 units completed. Demand from buy-to-let investors has collapsed due to affordability issues, stamp duty burdens, and non-dom tax changes driving private equity families away. Developers are struggling to secure the customary 30% upfront sales. Regionally, there is a glut of stock as landlords sell due to the Renters' Rights Act, though 60% of listings have failed to sell in the last three months. The new policy environment creates a gamble for sellers, as properties sold cannot be re-let for twelve months. Discussions around Mayor Sadiq Khan's proposed Land Value Tax (LVT) continue, with potential to boost transaction volumes but risks of depressing asset values.

## Manish Qataria's Pick: Gold and Silver

Manish Qataria revisits precious metals, selecting both gold and silver based on shared fundamentals but differing risk profiles. Gold is positioned as the "steady Eddie," while silver offers a more leveraged exposure to the same drivers. After a remarkable 50-60% rally in 2025, gold has corrected and is currently testing a critical technical support level at $4,000 per ounce. This level has held firm multiple times since late 2025, providing a potential entry point for investors seeking portfolio protection.

The thesis relies on the inverse correlation between gold and the US dollar, as well as bond yields. Gold tends to perform well when the dollar weakens or when real yields fall. However, Manish cautions that a hawkish stance from new Fed Governor Kevin Walsh could strengthen the dollar and suppress gold prices. He emphasizes that higher bond yields increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like gold.

For execution, Manish recommends ETFs over physical metal due to lower costs, FCA protection, and the ability to utilize options. He notes that investors can "get paid to wait" by selling options on gold ETFs, generating approximately 2% per month in premium income while holding the position. The primary risk remains a sustained strengthening of the US dollar driven by aggressive Fed policy.

## Rod's Pick: Barratt Redrow

Rod selects Barratt Redrow, a UK housebuilder, citing a compelling value proposition. The stock is trading at a 36% discount to its Tangible Net Asset Value (TNAV), with shares at 287p against a TNAV of 433p. This discount accounts for the company's land banks, inventory, and cash, stripping out goodwill from the merger.

Rod addresses several concerns regarding the valuation. Barratt Redrow holds £772 million in cash and has a pipeline forecasting 18,000 completions for the coming year, primarily in regional mid-market segments rather than high-value London schemes. A legacy provision of £300 million for building safety and cladding issues is mitigated by a Supreme Court ruling allowing the company to claw back costs from subcontractors, a process already yielding £38 million.

A key operational highlight is the company's part-exchange strategy, which accounts for 21% of private reservations. This helps overcome the "chain" deadlock in the current market. Rod notes that these acquired properties are valued conservatively at an eight-to-ten-week forced sale price, adding a margin of safety to the balance sheet. Additionally, Barratt Redrow is executing a £350 million share buyback over the next twelve months and offers a dividend yield of approximately 6%.

The investment carries policy tailwinds, including the potential reintroduction of Help to Buy, Home Information Packs, and reforms like the LVT that could stimulate transactions. Adam Lawrence supports the pick as an asymmetric bet, noting that the government relies on major builders for infrastructure and housing targets, making nationalization or land seizures impractical.

## Adam Lawrence's Pick: Low-Coupon Gilts (TG61)

Adam Lawrence chooses the TG61 gilt, a long-dated government bond maturing in 2061, currently yielding around 5.4%. This pick is framed as an asymmetric bet on political outcomes and macro stability. The market appears to be overpricing the risk of Ed Miliband becoming Chancellor, assuming a "Liz Truss-style" fiscal error. Adam argues that Miliband possesses the intellectual capacity to manage the role responsibly, and a confirmation of his appointment could trigger a relief rally, lowering yields and boosting bond prices. Conversely, if Pat McFadden becomes Chancellor, yields are also expected to fall.

Beyond politics, long-dated gilts serve as a hedge against "demand destruction" events and global economic stutters, performing well in crisis scenarios similarly to gold. The pick also offers tax efficiency, as gilts are exempt from Capital Gains Tax in a personal portfolio, with investors only liable for income tax on the modest coupon.

Manish Qataria critiques the duration risk, noting that long bonds are sensitive to inflation and interest rate spikes. He points out that the yield pickup over a two-year bond (around 4.25%) is only about 1.15%, which may not sufficiently compensate retail investors for the additional volatility. However, for institutional liability matching or as a short-term trade on political mispricing, the risk-reward remains attractive. The primary thesis risk is if Miliband implements aggressive fiscal expansion or if inflation proves more persistent than expected, causing yields to rise.

## Summary

The Q3 2026 Mastermind highlights a market navigating geopolitical tension, policy uncertainty, and structural shifts. Manish Qataria advocates for gold and silver as protective assets at technical support levels, Rod identifies deep value in Barratt Redrow based on asset discounts and operational resilience, and Adam Lawrence targets asymmetric upside in long-dated gilts driven by political repricing. Together, these picks offer a diversified approach combining crisis hedging, value investing, and macro speculation.
