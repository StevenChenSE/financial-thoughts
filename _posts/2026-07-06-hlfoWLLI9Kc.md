## Capitalizing on Market Volatility with a Royalty-First Approach

**Introduction**

In a recent interview on InvestingNews.com, Charlotte Mloud sat down with Jordan from Mining Stock Monkey to discuss the current landscape of precious metals and mining equities. With gold, silver, and copper experiencing significant price swings, Jordan outlined his investment philosophy, emphasizing the importance of buying on pullbacks, focusing on royalty and streaming companies, and navigating macroeconomic risks like potential recessions and geopolitical conflicts.

### Gold Market Outlook and Buying on Pullbacks

The conversation began with the recent behavior of gold prices. Jordan noted that gold had experienced a substantial pullback, dropping from a high of approximately $5,500 to below $4,000. Rather than viewing this decline as a bearish signal, Jordan sees it as a prime buying opportunity. He explained that bull markets have historically seen pullbacks of 25%, 35%, and even up to 49%. Based on the current highs, a 49% pullback would place gold around $2,750, meaning the asset is still significantly elevated from the $2,000 levels seen in early 2024.

Jordan's strategy revolves around sentiment and scaling. He avoids buying when prices are at all-time highs and sentiment is overly optimistic. Instead, he looks for moments when market sentiment sours, multiples shrink, and retail investors express pessimism. When conditions align, he scales into positions aggressively, buying all the way down to average into a strong base. He also highlighted the long-term thesis that dollar devaluation inevitably drives the price of gold higher, despite short-term volatility.

### The Royalty and Streaming Advantage

A core component of Jordan's portfolio is gold and silver royalty companies. He favors this segment of the mining sector for its high margins and downside protection. He specifically highlighted **Elemental Royalty**, noting it presents a strong buying opportunity around the $15 share price, and **Royal Gold**, which he considers significantly undervalued and has been accumulating heavily. He also mentioned **Altius Minerals** as a diversified royalty play with exposure to copper assets.

Jordan draws a parallel to Warren Buffett's evolution in investing philosophy. Initially, Buffett sought "cigar butts"—cheap companies with little intrinsic value beyond cash on hand. However, with the influence of Charlie Munger, Buffett shifted toward buying great companies at fair prices. Jordan applies this same principle, preferring to buy high-quality royalty firms at reasonable valuations rather than chasing distressed assets. He noted that when major royalty companies trade at three times their net asset value (NAV), they become too expensive, but current market conditions have brought prices back to attractive levels.

### Navigating Copper and Recession Risks

While Jordan remains bullish on copper long-term, he expressed caution regarding a potential economic recession. He pointed out that the last genuine recession occurred in 2008–2009, dismissing the 2020 downturn as government-induced. With the U.S. government debt-to-GDP ratio soaring to 120% (or 400% including unfunded liabilities), Jordan doubts the Federal Reserve can replicate the aggressive rate hikes of the Paul Volcker era without breaking something in the financial system.

Because copper is highly sensitive to economic cycles, Jordan prefers copper royalties over pure producers during periods of uncertainty. Royalty companies benefit from nearly 100% margins on their assets; if copper prices drop, their revenue declines proportionally, but their profitability remains intact. In contrast, copper producers can see their margins evaporate entirely in a downturn. Jordan suggested that major producers like **Freeport**, **Southern Copper**, and **Ivanhoe Mines** would become excellent buying opportunities only after a recession-induced crash. For those willing to take on higher risk, he mentioned **Hercules Metals**, a copper explorer in Idaho with a significant deposit that is trading at a fair price despite its early-stage risk profile.

### Silver Dynamics and Supply Constraints

Silver has also been a focal point of Jordan's analysis. After spiking to $125 an ounce, silver has retreated to approximately $58. This correction has shifted Jordan's stance from cautious to actively seeking silver investments. He emphasized the difficulty of finding pure-play silver producers that consistently generate cash flow, as many operate at a loss over long periods.

To mitigate risk, Jordan appreciates streamers like **Wheaton Precious Metals**, which secure guaranteed 80% margins on silver ounces. He also highlighted the geopolitical risks associated with silver mining, noting that quality deposits are heavily concentrated in jurisdictions like Russia, Mexico, and Peru, where political instability and anti-mining sentiment can hinder development.

Despite these challenges, Jordan remains long-term bullish on silver. He explained that the majority of silver is mined as a byproduct of copper, lead, zinc, and gold, meaning mine supply does not expand significantly even when silver prices surge. Conversely, price spikes trigger massive selling from recycled silver and rural savers in Asia who use silver as a savings vehicle. However, structural demand from solar farms, electronics, and defense applications, combined with long permitting timelines for new mines, suggests persistent supply constraints that will support higher prices over time.

### Summary

Jordan from Mining Stock Monkey advocates for a disciplined, sentiment-driven approach to mining investments. By focusing on high-quality royalty companies, scaling into positions during pullbacks, and carefully weighing recession risks in base metals, investors can position themselves for long-term gains. His final advice reinforces the fundamental rule of investing: buy low when sentiment is bleak, and sell high when optimism peaks.
