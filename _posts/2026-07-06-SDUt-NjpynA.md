## Silver's Comeback: Has the Real Opportunity Already Passed?

In a recent episode of *Hot Trades of the Day*, trader Daniel offered a detailed technical analysis of silver following its dramatic recovery from the $56 bottom. The discussion, shared ahead of his trip to the Rule Symposium, covered daily and weekly charts, resistance levels, and—perhaps most importantly—why traders who feel they "missed" the bottom may actually be looking at the wrong price.

## Daily Chart Analysis: Recovery Signs

Silver's daily chart shows the market has clearly turned a corner. Over the past week and a half, four consecutive green candles appeared, with six of the last seven trading sessions closing in positive territory. Critically, on June 26, silver did not make a new low—it printed a higher low, which is a constructive sign.

A subtler but equally important pattern emerged midweek. Two consecutive candles showed long upper wicks that tested the downtrend from below, pushing above it temporarily before selling off again and closing under the uptrend. Despite this initial failure, the very next session produced a large green candle. This price action suggests selling pressure is steadily leaving the silver market. Even when the dollar showed strength—a condition that would typically pressure silver—price held firm. The long-term uptrend dating back to March or April 2025 has been reclaimed.

That said, the market remains below both the 200-day moving average and the 50-day moving average, so caution is warranted.

## The Five-Level Resistance Wall

Silver now faces what Daniel calls a "crazy monster resistance zone." Up to five technical barriers converge in the same area:

1. A blue trendline around $67.95
2. The 200-day moving average
3. The 50-day moving average
4. The short-term downtrend line
5. A former support-now-resistance zone (yellow line)

Given that the dollar is gaining strength and gold is providing limited support, Daniel doubts silver will power through this wall immediately. Rather than expecting a return to $80 or $200 silver, volatility should be expected—and that volatility creates buying opportunities.

## Did You Actually Miss the Bottom?

This is where Daniel reframes the discussion. Many traders feel they missed the rally, comparing current prices to the absolute $56 bottom. But as he points out, realistically, very few people bought at $56.

The chart at $56 showed:

- A hammer-like candle, but not a clean one
- Price below the long-term uptrend
- The downtrend still intact
- A large red candle preceding the bottom that looked deeply bearish

Buying there required enormous conviction and risk. The *realistic* entry point was around $59.50—when silver broke the downtrend and regained the uptrend. Comparing current silver prices to a $59.50 entry, the missed gains are minimal.

For silver mining stocks, which sometimes moved 20% during the rally, the realistic missed move is closer to 10% or 12%. In the broader context of silver's long-term potential, this is a small gap.

## Weekly Chart Perspective

On the weekly chart, silver undercut its long-term uptrend—an occurrence Daniel had previously predicted. The current weekly candle is decent, and while the expected long lower tail did not fully materialize, the two smaller tails suggest sellers are largely exhausted.

However, a major concern remains: the 50-week moving average sits at approximately $64.60. Silver has held above this level repeatedly since breaking above it in March 2024, using it as a launching pad for its rally. Until price reclaims this level, the bullish case is incomplete.

## What Would Change the Picture

According to Daniel, things would "really improve" if silver closed above $72. Such a close would mean the market retakes the 200-day MA, the 50-day MA, and breaks the long-term downtrend connecting the intermediate highs. Until then, there is no rush to buy aggressively.

The smart strategy, he suggests, is to readjust expectations. Buy small starter positions on any continued weakness. If the uptrend is retested and holds, that becomes a solid buying opportunity. If the bearish case plays out instead, traders still have dry powder to add to positions at better prices.

## Mining Stocks: Where the Real Opportunity Lies

Daniel's focus going forward is shifting toward silver mining stocks—specifically those showing high relative strength. Mining companies still trading above their 200-day moving averages, particularly larger firms with institutional involvement, represent the "diamonds" of the sector. These names are unlikely to be sold by institutions even in broader market downturns, suggesting lower downside risk and strong potential to lead the next rally.

## Summary

Silver's recovery from $56 shows signs of a genuine shift in momentum, with selling pressure appearing to fade. However, meaningful resistance lies just ahead in the form of five converging technical barriers. Traders who worry they missed the rally are likely comparing prices to an unrealistic entry point; the realistic buying level was around $59.50, which puts the missed gains at roughly $2. For those looking to position themselves now, starting small on any weakness—and focusing on silver mining stocks with institutional backing—offers the best risk-adjusted approach for the coming weeks.
