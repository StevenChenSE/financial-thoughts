## The Sovereign Bond Market and Rising CDS Rates

The relentless rise in government bond yields is no longer an isolated American phenomenon; it extends across the entire G7 bloc and Europe. A particularly alarming development is taking place in European sovereign credit, highlighted by surging credit default swap (CDS) rates. 

A credit default swap acts as an insurance premium against sovereign default. In France, CDS spreads have seen dramatic spikes, moving rapidly higher as investors demand steeper compensation for sovereign risk. This deterioration is driven by fundamental imbalances:

- **Japanese Capital Flight:** Japanese institutional investors, who previously held significant positions in French sovereign debt, have steadily liquidated their holdings.
- **Diverging Debt-to-GDP:** French national debt is expanding at roughly twice the rate of its underlying GDP growth.
- **Contracting Denominators:** When economic output slows or contracts, the debt-to-GDP ratio worsens automatically, diminishing a government’s capacity to service its liabilities.

This dynamic triggers a vicious negative feedback loop: rising yields increase debt service costs, worsening the fiscal position and driving bond yields even higher.

## The Liquidation Cascade and Inevitable Intervention

Should interest rates persist at elevated levels, equity markets face severe downward revaluation. The knock-on effects of an equity crash would rapidly cascade through the banking system:

1. **Collateral Liquidation:** Commercial banks hold an estimated $4 trillion to $5 trillion in equity collateral, which would face mandatory liquidation during a sharp downturn.
2. **Foreign Capital Flight:** Foreign investors holding trillions of dollars in domestic equities would rush for the exits, exacerbating currency and market volatility.
3. **Budget Deterioration:** Slower economic growth leads to declining tax receipts and rising mandatory welfare spending, widening deficits further.

When faced with systemic insolvency across the private and banking sectors, central banks and treasuries have historically prioritized liquidity preservation over currency stability. To prevent widespread bankruptcies, authorities will ultimately have no choice but to suppress yields by restarting massive quantitative easing (QE). This continuous monetization inevitably comes at the expense of fiat currency purchasing power.

- **Policy Responses:** In response to price spikes, governments frequently resort to price controls or emergency subsidies. Rather than addressing root causes, these measures typically create domestic shortfalls and necessitate further credit creation.
