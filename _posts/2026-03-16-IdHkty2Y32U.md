In an assessment that frames our current era in stark historical terms, analysts at CPM Group present a compelling case for the continued rise of gold and silver prices. The driving force, they argue, is a global landscape fraught with peril not seen in generations.

**"We look at the world right now and we see a world where the risks and uncertainties are greater now than at any time since Pearl Harbor, December 1941,"** notes the commentary. The analogy is striking. While the war continued for years after that pivotal event, the ultimate outcome became clearer. Today, by contrast, the path forward is shrouded in doubt. **"The risks and uncertainties are greater now than ever before since 1941. And I don't necessarily see how they get resolved."**

This profound and persistent uncertainty is the engine behind the firm's bullish outlook for precious metals. CPM Group employs various scenarios to model the future, but most converge on a single, powerful investor behavior: a sustained flight to safety.

**"Most of those scenarios involve investors saying, continuing to say for many years, 'I think I want to have more of my wealth in gold and silver.'"**

Consequently, the forecast is clear. The firm sees current high prices not as a peak, but as a plateau from which further gains are likely. **"We see gold and silver prices staying high and moving higher until you see a reduction in political and economic risks."**

The implication is that gold and silver have transitioned from speculative assets to essential hedges in a portfolio. Their value is now directly tied to the global risk thermometer. Until there are tangible, sustained signs of geopolitical and economic stabilization—a de-escalation of conflicts, a return to predictable policy, and diminished financial volatility—the fundamental demand driving these metals upward is expected to remain firmly in place.

In essence, CPM Group’s analysis suggests that in a world searching for anchors, gold and silver are becoming exactly that. Their price trajectory will be a direct barometer of the world’s return to stability, or its continued descent into uncharted and turbulent waters.
