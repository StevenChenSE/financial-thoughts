## Strikepoint Gold CEO Discusses Market Reset, Copper, and Nevada's Mining Boom

Gold prices have corrected significantly from their all-time highs, sparking concern among short-term investors. However, Mike Allen, President and CEO of Strikepoint Gold, believes the worst is over. In this interview, Allen argues that $4,000 per ounce represents a solid new base for gold, driven by fundamental shifts in mining economics, central bank demand, and industrial utility. He also highlights copper as the key commodity for the "western catch-up" trade and details how higher gold prices are transforming exploration and mining operations in Nevada.

### Gold's Correction and the New $4,000 Floor

Gold peaked around $5,500 in late January before correcting to approximately $4,000, with the GDX index down 35% in the quarter. Allen notes that while short-term sentiment is weak, the long-term picture remains strong. He attributes the recent peak to excessive media coverage, such as CNN features, which often signal retail exhaustion.

Allen argues that $4,000 is becoming the new reality for miners. Companies are beginning to adjust their internal models, moving from $3,000 assumptions to $4,000. This shift expands resource estimates, as lower-grade deposits become economic at higher prices. "Time equals comfort," Allen explains. As gold stabilizes at this level, investors and companies will adjust their mindset, viewing daily fluctuations of $100 as minor "chop" rather than significant market events.

### Copper Leads the Western Commodity Catch-Up

Following China's industrialization, Allen identifies copper as the primary beneficiary of the "western catch-up" in the commodity supercycle. He describes copper as essential to modern civilization, powering data transmission, electronics, and infrastructure. While gold serves as a hedge and has growing industrial uses, particularly in electrical applications, copper is the definitive industrial play for this cycle.

### Gold's Role in Central Bank Portfolios

Allen emphasizes gold's critical role as a foundational pillar for central banks. In an era of volatile paper currencies and cryptocurrencies, gold offers stability. He views it as a "solid investment" with limited downside risk and significant upside potential, making it an essential component for balancing growth and risk in national and institutional portfolios.

### Strikepoint Gold and the Hercules Project

Strikepoint Gold (SPT) has seen significant volatility, dropping 56% from its March highs before rebounding 63% in late July following positive drill results. Allen attributes the initial sell-off to broader market sentiment and the pullback in gold prices, rather than company-specific issues. The recent recovery reflects investor recognition of the company's fundamental assets, particularly the Hercules Gold Project in Nevada.

The latest drill results included a headline hole (Hole 5) returning 67 meters at 6.3 g/t gold and 11 g/t silver. This data supports the company's move toward a resource estimate, expected in Q4 this year. Allen notes that the southwest corner of the "Cliff's Target" has revealed a large zone of disseminated material with consistent high-grade intercepts, expanding the project's potential.

### Higher Gold Prices Transform Mining Economics

The rise in gold prices has dramatically improved the economics of mining and exploration. At $4,000 per ounce, a 200-ton truck load of 0.5 g/t ore contains approximately $12,000 in value, double the amount at $2,000. This leverage benefits both senior producers, through massive volume, and junior explorers, by unlocking previously uneconomic deposits.

However, higher prices also bring challenges. Increased competition for skilled labor, including geologists and drillers, can impact the quality of exploration programs. Allen stresses that human expertise remains the single biggest factor in drill program success, and supply chain constraints for rigs and personnel can hinder execution during bull markets.

### Nevada: A World-Class Mining Jurisdiction

Allen highlights Nevada as arguably the top mining jurisdiction globally, rivaling Quebec and Finland. Its advantages include unique geological endowments, a mining-friendly regulatory culture, and world-class infrastructure. Unlike remote Arctic operations, where logistics costs are prohibitive, Nevada offers accessible power, water, and skilled labor.

The recent Anglo Gold discovery at the Arthur Gold project near Battle Mountain exemplifies Nevada's potential for new, world-class finds. Allen, who previously held assets in the area, notes that scientific exploration continues to yield massive deposits even in mature camps with 150 years of mining history.

### Peak Gold and M&A Opportunities

Higher gold prices push back the timeline for "peak gold" production by making lower-grade ores economic. This extends the life of existing mines and incentivizes new exploration. Allen suggests that the recent market pullback has created relative bargains, potentially sparking a new wave of M&A activity. While acquirers may hesitate to dilute shareholders at lower share prices, strong producers with good projects are ideally positioned to acquire weaker competitors.

### Conclusion

Mike Allen remains bullish on gold's long-term trajectory, viewing the current correction as an opportunity for investors to reload. With $4,000 as a new floor, rising industrial demand for copper, and transformative discoveries in Nevada, the mining sector is poised for sustained growth.
