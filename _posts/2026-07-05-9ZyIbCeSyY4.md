## Rick Rule on Oil, Gold, Silver, and the Coming Scarcity Crisis

In a July 2026 discussion with Capital Cosmlin host Danny, veteran resource investor Rick Rule breaks down the hidden realities behind today’s commodity markets. While nominal prices for oil, housing, and other assets may suggest a robust economy, Rule argues that a shift in perspective reveals a fundamentally different story. By using gold as a unit of account and analyzing structural supply deficits, investors can see through the illusion of nominal strength and prepare for a decade of significant dollar depreciation.

Rule’s analysis spans multiple asset classes, from the strategic petroleum reserve’s depletion to the long-term underinvestment in global oil infrastructure. He also addresses the psychological dynamics of silver, the historical parallels of fiat debasement, and why American investors remain drastically underallocated to precious metals. This conversation provides a roadmap for navigating the intersection of macroeconomic trends, commodity cycles, and strategic portfolio allocation.

### The Oil Market: Near-Term Calm, Long-Term Scarcity

Current WTI crude prices hovering around $67 have sparked confusion, especially given recent geopolitical tensions and infrastructure damage in the Middle East. Rule explains that the market avoided a true shortage only because of coordinated releases from the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) and domestic stockpiles in Japan and China. More importantly, the previous spike in oil prices triggered severe demand destruction in emerging markets like Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, and Sri Lanka. Even as supplies normalize, the hangover of that demand destruction keeps near-term prices muted.

However, Rule warns that this calm is temporary. He projects a structural oil shortage beginning around 2029–2030, driven not by war but by chronic underinvestment. Global oil companies, particularly paristatals, are deferring sustaining capital expenditure by approximately $1 billion per day. Compounded by the destruction of Middle Eastern infrastructure and a hiatus in working capital during recent conflicts, the industry is failing to maintain its production base. Unlike the recent war-inspired scarcity, this structural deficit cannot be solved by armistice or reserve releases; it will only be resolved through higher prices and renewed investment.

### Gold as a Unit of Account: Seeing Through Nominal Illusions

Rule emphasizes that investors must mentally maintain a gold standard to accurately assess purchasing power. When commodities, housing, and consumer goods are priced in gold rather than US dollars, they appear astonishingly cheap. For example, while US single-family home prices are nominally near all-time highs, they sit at historical lows when measured in gold, mirroring the nominal bottom seen in 2011–2012. 

This divergence highlights the real issue: the steady erosion of the dollar’s purchasing power. Rule draws a direct parallel to the 1970s, when the dollar lost 75% of its value over a decade according to the Office of Management and Budget. He expects a similar trajectory over the next ten years, meaning a $5,000 basket of goods today could cost $20,000 by 2036. He also addresses the gold-to-oil ratio, noting that while it closely tracks the GDX (VanEck Gold Miners ETF), it fundamentally represents the real price of oil rather than mining margins. As the real price of oil falls when measured in gold, energy consumption as a percentage of GDP declines, further proving that nominal dollar weakness, not asset inflation, drives today's economic anxiety.

### Silver Strategy: Selling the Meltup and Waiting for "Hate"

Addressing silver’s recent 50% drawdown from its peak, Rule outlines a disciplined approach rooted in market sentiment and technical structure. He previously sold 80% of his physical silver holdings, including the iShares Silver Trust (SLV), when the chart exhibited a classic "hockey stick" meltup pattern. Historically, hyperbolic upside moves resolve sharply to the downside, and Rule refuses to overpay into euphoria.

Rule measures market "hate" through two lenses: production economics and crowd sentiment. When silver trades below the total cost of production (including capital costs, royalties, and taxes), it signals industry liquidation and widespread investor disdain—a prime buying opportunity. Conversely, when social media and retail sentiment turn overwhelmingly bullish, the psychological supply has already been exhausted, and the move is likely peaking. Currently, Rule sees no hate in the silver market and prefers exposure through silver mining stocks rather than physical bullion. He also maintains indirect silver exposure through Bank of Montreal, which actively lends to bullion holders.

### Where to Invest: Oil, Gold, and the Precious Metals Gap

For most investors, Rule currently favors the oil and gas sector. He describes it as a more understandable, fundamentally stronger business with clearer long-term tailwinds driven by deferred capex and a declining dollar. While mining stocks offer higher speculative upside, oil equities provide a more stable foundation for retirement or long-term capital preservation.

Simultaneously, Rule highlights a massive opportunity in precious metals. According to JPMorgan Chase data, precious metals and related securities currently make up only 0.5% of total US savings and investment assets, far below the 40-year mean of 2%. If gold and silver merely revert to their historical market share, demand could quadruple. Rule views gold not as a get-rich-quick vehicle but as essential portfolio insurance against sustained dollar depreciation, government debt, and artificially suppressed interest rates.

### Summary

Rick Rule’s market analysis underscores a critical shift in how investors should evaluate asset prices. By stripping away the illusion of nominal dollar strength and measuring commodities against gold, the true affordability of real assets becomes clear. While near-term oil prices may soften due to lingering demand destruction, structural underinvestment guarantees higher energy prices by 2030. Silver remains a sentiment-driven market best approached when fear and liquidation return, while gold serves as essential insurance against inevitable dollar debasement. For investors willing to look beyond short-term noise and embrace resource fundamentals, the next decade presents a clear path to preserving and growing purchasing power.
