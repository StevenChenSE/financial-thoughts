## Insights on Oil Market Dynamics, Real Commodity Pricing, and the Gold Standard

**Introduction**

In a recent episode of Capital Cos, host Danny sat down with renowned natural resources investor Rick Rule to discuss the macroeconomic landscape as of July 2, 2026. The conversation covered critical themes including the current state of the oil market, the importance of using gold as a unit of account, and the long-term trajectory of the US dollar. Rick Rule provided a contrarian perspective on commodity pricing, structural supply shortages, and strategic asset allocation for the coming decade.

### The Oil Market: Temporary Glut vs. Structural Scarcity

The discussion opened with an analysis of WTI crude oil, which was trading at $67. While this price point may seem low given recent geopolitical tensions and infrastructure damage, Rick Rule attributes the current equilibrium to demand destruction and the release of reserves. During the recent conflict, extreme price spikes destroyed demand in developing nations, including Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, and Myanmar. Additionally, the cessation of panic hoarding and releases from the US Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR), alongside domestic reserves in Japan and China, shielded the global market from severe rationing.

However, Rule warns that the current calm precedes a significant storm. He distinguishes between the recent "contrived scarcity" caused by war and the "structural scarcity" that is inevitable within the next three years. The global oil industry, dominated by parastatals, is underinvesting in sustaining capital expenditure by approximately $1 billion per day. Compounding this issue is the destruction of critical infrastructure in the Middle East, including facilities on Kharg Island in Iran, as well as assets in the UAE, Qatar, and Saudi Arabia. Rule asserts that the narrative of oil as a "sunset industry" is false; demand continues to grow inexorably, and the market will soon be forced to ration by price due to systemic underinvestment.

### The Gold-to-Oil Ratio and Real Pricing

Danny presented the gold-to-oil ratio as a proxy for gold mining company margins, noting its correlation with the GDX index. Rick Rule offered a different interpretation, arguing that the ratio represents the real price of oil. He explained that the real price of oil has fallen consistently from 2000 to 2026 when measured against a stable store of value like gold. This decline in real price reflects increasing energy efficiency; energy consumption as a percentage of GDP in the United States has fallen steadily.

Rule emphasized that while alternative energy sources have received between $6 trillion and $11 trillion in investment over the last 45 years, they have only reduced the market share of fossil fuels in power generation from 83% to 81%. He concluded that unless there is a massive real price shock, oil demand will continue to rise, and the eventual supply deficit will drive nominal oil prices higher, buoyed further by a declining US dollar.

### Housing, Commodities, and the Dollar's Decline

A central theme of the interview was the necessity of using gold as a unit of account to understand true purchasing power. Rick Rule has maintained a "gold standard" in his personal accounting since the year 2000. When commodities and housing are priced in gold, they appear astonishingly cheap, revealing the severe deterioration of the US dollar's value.

For instance, while US single-family home prices are near all-time highs in nominal terms, they are at an all-time low when priced in gold, comparable to levels seen in 2011 and 2012. Rule draws a parallel to the 1970s, when the dollar lost 75% of its purchasing power in a decade. He predicts a similar trajectory for the next ten years, suggesting that a basket of goods costing $5,000 today could cost $20,000 by 2036. In this environment, Rule views gold not merely as an investment but as essential insurance to preserve wealth, noting that the dollar is a "floating abstraction" suffering from slow-motion decline due to debt, deficits, and artificially low interest rates.

### Silver Strategy and Sentiment Analysis

Regarding silver, Rule explained his decision to sell 80% of his holdings, primarily the Sprott Physical Silver Trust, during the recent price surge. He adheres to the principle that "hockey stick" hyperbolic upside charts always resolve to the downside. Rule measures market sentiment through "hate" and "love." He bought silver when the market sentiment was overwhelmingly negative, noting that prices were below the total cost of production, including social costs and capital. When sentiment shifted and the "hate" disappeared, he sold.

Currently, Rule is not buying physical silver because the market lacks the necessary negative sentiment. Instead, he prefers exposure through silver stocks or indirect equity interests in institutions like Battle Bank, which actively lends to silver bullion holders. He advises investors to bet against consensus when a single narrative dominates over 80% of market share, as the psychological supply has likely already been exhausted.

### Investment Outlook and the Upcoming Symposium

Rick Rule concluded by promoting his upcoming Symposium, scheduled for July 6th. The event focuses on sub-$2 billion market cap companies in natural resources, offering 46 hours of programming with vetted exhibitors and seasoned analysts. Rule highlighted that the target sector has suffered a 35% to 40% price decline over the last six months, creating attractive entry points.

For portfolio allocation, Rule recommends oil and gas stocks for most investors due to the robust business fundamentals and impending scarcity. However, he strongly advocates for increased exposure to gold, citing JP Morgan Chase statistics showing that precious metals comprise only 0.5% of US investment assets, far below the four-decade mean of 2%. He believes a reversion to the mean could drive a fourfold increase in demand. Rule also reminded viewers of his free portfolio grading service available through Rule Investment Media, where he personally ranks natural resource stocks on a scale of one to ten.

### Summary

Rick Rule presents a compelling case for the deterioration of the US dollar and the inevitability of rising commodity prices. While the oil market currently faces demand destruction and reserve releases, structural underinvestment and war-damaged infrastructure guarantee future scarcity. By pricing assets in gold, investors can see the true state of the market and protect their purchasing power against inflation. Rule advises a dual focus on oil stocks for business fundamentals and gold for wealth preservation, urging investors to prepare for a decade of significant monetary shifts.
