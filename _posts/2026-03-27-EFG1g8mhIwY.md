**By Jesse Day | Commodity Culture**

In a world where geopolitics and commodity markets are inextricably linked, the escalating war in Iran is not just a regional conflict—it is a catalyst reshaping global finance, energy flows, and the future of precious metals. On March 25, 2026, geopolitical and commodities analyst Eric Young joined *Commodity Culture* to break down the complex interplay between Middle Eastern warfare, the staggering silver market, and what he sees as the beginning of the end for the petrodollar system.

## The Strait of Hormuz and the Silver Lifeline

The conflict has effectively closed the Strait of Hormuz to most shipping, a chokepoint for roughly 30% of the world’s seaborne oil. Young recently detailed the implications of this closure for silver in a Substack article, focusing on an often-overlooked byproduct: sulfuric acid.

“A lot of the secondary and third-order minerals produced around the world are dependent on the byproduct of crude oil coming out from the Strait of Hormuz… mainly sulfuric acid,” Young explained. The sulfur-heavy oil from the region is a key feedstock for this vital industrial chemical.

While oil flow isn’t zero—with some tankers reportedly paying Iran a $2 million fee for passage, purportedly in Chinese Renminbi (RMB)—output is severely hampered. Saudi Arabia and Qatar are also redirecting about 30% of their oil via pipelines to the Red Sea.

The sulfuric acid shortage creates a domino effect for silver. “If you don’t have the copper, you don’t need the silver. You need both copper and silver to build solar panels,” Young noted. A complete crash in sulfuric acid output could slump silver demand. However, a partial decline might have the opposite effect, accelerating a rebound as the green energy transition continues unabated. “Silver is the new oil,” Young asserted, “because silver drives the renewable energy push by countries like China.”

## Gold’s Rollercoaster and the Petrodollar Crack-Up

Both gold and silver have sold off sharply since the war began. Young interprets this not as a simple “sell everything” panic, but as a calculated move by insiders aware of a far more significant development: the accelerating demise of the petrodollar.

He points to a critical shift in January 2026. “The information I got is Saudi Arabia started… to settle half, 50% of the oil that it sells to China in RMB.” This, Young argues, is a direct assault on the decades-old petrodollar system, where oil sales in U.S. dollars are recycled into U.S. Treasury bonds, supporting both the dollar and American debt markets.

“Somebody knew in DC… that this de-dollarization by China is accelerating,” he said. The initial gold dump in late January, followed by a recovery, and then a second dump after the U.S. decapitation strike on Iranian leadership, aligns with this thesis. The first sell-off anticipated the war; the second reacted to the disruption of a key trade: “oil for RMB, and then the excess RMB converted to gold.”

But the petrodollar’s foundation isn’t just cracking financially—it’s breaking politically. Young framed it as a broken promise: “The deal is that if ever you get attacked, Uncle Sam is going to step in and save you… What happened in the last couple weeks? That promise has been broken.” He highlighted reports that the U.S. moved missile defenses from Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) countries to Israel during Iranian barrages. “If you’re one of these GCC countries, what’s your incentive to do this all over again?”

## Silver’s Precarious Supply and the Path Forward

Despite the volatility, the fundamental picture for silver remains starkly bullish, defined by critically low inventories. Young cited data showing combined physical silver at the Shanghai Gold Exchange and Shanghai Futures Exchange has plummeted to nearly 600 metric tons, down from a typical 2,000-3,000 tons.

“In my calculations, if they don’t get a refill soon, that’s only going to last three months until there is zero,” he warned.

The situation in London is equally tense. “We’re at that level now around five to six thousand metric tons at the LBMA… a danger zone.” He recalled that in September of the previous year, when LBMA inventories hit 5,000 tons, they were drained to zero within 20 days, coinciding with a violent spike in the silver price from $40 to over $50 an ounce. “Everything is kind of quiet on the surface right now, but there’s a lot of turbulence underneath.”

## Investment Strategy in a Time of Chaos

Given the extreme uncertainty, with the potential for a U.S. ground invasion of Iran and acute liquidity events, Young advocates for prudence and discipline.

“I haven’t sold anything,” he said regarding his physical gold, silver, and mining stocks. For new buyers, his advice is clear: “Dollar-cost average. Don’t blow all your ammunition in one shot… Who knows what’s going to happen next week when the Marines actually land in Iran. Potentially, gold can dip below $4,000.”

Outside of precious metals, Young sees opportunity in uranium, which he prefers over the manipulated oil and gas markets. “The players in the uranium space… simply want uranium to go up because they want to reshore all that supply chain to the US.”

## The Larger Geopolitical Chessboard

Ultimately, Young sees the war as a desperate, multi-faceted gambit. It serves Israel’s regional ambitions, but also aims to slow China’s rise by disrupting its energy supply and, more importantly, staunching the bleed of dollar hegemony.

“This de-dollarization—oil for RMB—has been accelerating,” he said. The war is an attempt to “stop that process being expanded to buy Middle Eastern oil.” The synchronized dumps in gold were the market’s early tremors signaling this profound shift.

For investors, the lesson is to look beyond the headlines and emotions. The realignment of global power, energy, and money is happening in real-time, and its signals are etched in the charts of gold, the inventories of silver, and the breaking of old promises in the Persian Gulf. As Young’s analysis makes clear, in today’s world, geopolitics isn’t a distraction from the markets—it is the market.
