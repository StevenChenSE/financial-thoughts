When asked to define inflation, Paul distinguishes between temporary price fluctuations caused by external events, such as hurricanes, and systemic inflation. He defines true inflation as a monetary policy issue where a central bank operates in secrecy, printing money to fund special interests. "That’s going to continue. He criticized the Federal Reserve’s approach to managing the economy, arguing that raising interest rates is a superficial "gimmick" that does not address the core issue of money supply expansion. Paul expressed a fundamental distrust of the institution, stating, "You shouldn't ever trust the Fed. They shouldn't even exist because they're just part of the problem." He noted that the Fed has allowed inflation to remain above its target for five years, highlighting the institution's inability to maintain price stability while the government continues to spend beyond its means.

## The Iran Conflict and the Military-Industrial Complex

Paul addressed the ongoing conflict with Iran, rejecting the administration's justification that fighting terrorism makes the U.S. safer. He argued that the war actually makes the country more dangerous and serves the interests of the military-industrial complex rather than the American public.

Paul’s central thesis is that the current debt and deficit trajectory is unsustainable.
