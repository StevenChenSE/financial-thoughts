## Gary Wagner Warns: Support at $4,000 Could Hold—But Only If This Happens

Gold has taken a dramatic tumble from its all-time highs above $5,500, leaving investors wondering if the precious metal is headed for a catastrophic 50% correction reminiscent of the 2011-2012 crash. With prices currently hovering around $4,300—representing a 21% decline from record levels—market watchers are closely examining technical charts and fundamental factors to determine whether this represents a buying opportunity or the beginning of a prolonged bear market.

In an exclusive interview, Gary Wagner, editor of thegoldforecast.com, shared his analysis on the critical support and resistance levels that could determine gold's short-term trajectory. Wagner, who has witnessed multiple gold bull and bear cycles throughout his career, offered a nuanced perspective that blends technical indicators with fundamental market forces.

---

## Technical Analysis: Chart Damage Confirmed

From a purely technical standpoint, Wagner acknowledges that gold has sustained significant chart damage. The precious metal has effectively broken and remained below the long-term 200-day simple moving average—a development that, to market technicians, signals a short-term bearish scenario.

"We did find a bottom," Wagner explained. "The bottom we found was right around $4,045, and then it popped back up pretty dynamically. Of course, this happened last week."

The chart analysis reveals a series of lower lows following gold's record high at the end of January. Green arrows indicate support levels that have been established, while a red arrow marks what Wagner believes is currently resistance. Two moving averages are displayed: the shorter-term 50-day and the longer-term 200-day.

"As they widened, that's when we were releasing a huge move," Wagner noted. "You can see how these have narrowed tremendously where the 200-day and the 50-day is coming closer. If it actually would cross, that would be a significant level of chart damage, but we're not quite there yet."

Wagner's technical assessment places $4,000 as a critical floor for gold—a level that aligns with the recent bottom and represents an area of previous support.

---

## The 2012 Comparison: Is History Rhyming?

The $4,000 level gains additional significance when compared to historical bull cycle corrections. In 2011, gold reached approximately $1,900 before embarking on a multi-year correction that wouldn't find its bottom until late 2015 at around $1,144—representing a decline of well over 40%.

"We've had steep corrections," Wagner observed. "The correction that we're looking at now is nowhere near as deep as the move from $1,900 down to $1,000. To do that, we would have to go from call it $5,500 down to just below $3,000."

However, Wagner draws an important parallel between the current market structure and the 2011-2012 period. During that earlier correction, gold topped out, corrected somewhat, bounced back toward its previous high, failed to meet that top, and then crashed by 40-50%. The same pattern has repeated twice in past bull cycles—in 2011 and 1980.

"If this area holds, and that's a big if, it will be a much more shallow correction than many other instances in which gold traded to a new all-time record high and corrected," Wagner said.

The recent rally from August 2023 tells its own story. Gold moved from approximately $1,900, encountering short pauses along the way at $2,400 and $2,700—all new all-time record highs. The pace was described as "highly compressed," with new highs witnessed nearly weekly at one point. The market then experienced higher highs at $3,400, $4,000, and finally nearly $5,600 before the current correction began.

## Central Banks: The Bullish Wildcard

While technical analysis paints a concerning picture, Wagner identifies a fundamental factor that could distinguish this correction from historical precedents: central bank purchasing activity.

"The biggest purchasers of gold this year that are going to be actively accumulating gold are the central banks," Wagner emphasized, citing data from the World Gold Council.

According to a survey conducted by the World Gold Council among central banks, 57% of respondents indicated they are switching gold from its current location for safety reasons while simultaneously becoming active buyers. These institutions are believed to be accumulating vast amounts of gold over the next year.

"This is not like retail investment," Wagner clarified. "It's not even like hedge fund investment. When central banks are buying and they're converting their fiat into that hard asset, one, that tells me that they're losing faith in whether they're overseas working with euros, whether they're in China working with yuan, regardless of their local currency or the currency that's a primary currency in the central bank."

Wagner considers this the most significant factor that could potentially move gold back into positive territory—specifically, pushing it back above the $4,000 level that represents last week's low.

---

## Interest Rates: A Double-Edged Sword

The Federal Reserve's monetary policy remains a crucial driver of gold prices, though Wagner suggests the market's relationship with interest rates may be evolving.

"Obviously, gold is a non-interest-bearing asset," Wagner explained. "So when interest rates go up, that makes gold less attractive because fixed income investments are yielding higher, and so that will move money out of gold and silver the precious metals—even equities to some degree—to move them into the safety of a fixed interest rate-bearing investment."

The market was positioned one day ahead of a Federal Reserve meeting, with expectations that the central bank would maintain a hawkish stance. While no rate cuts were anticipated at that meeting, the CME Fed Watch tool indicated approximately 60% probability of a rate hike occurring sometime that year.

"The last time the Fed raised rates was July of 2023," Wagner noted. "It's been a long time since we've seen the Fed actually raise rates, and that's why once that door is perceived to possibly be open, a rate hike might turn into a cycle of rate hikes."

However, Wagner believes that gold has moved beyond being "just an interest rate story" and now responds to different fundamental forces than in previous market cycles.

---

## Inflation: The Energy Factor

May's Consumer Price Index report showed inflation at 4.2%, with 60% of that figure attributed to higher energy costs. Notably, crude oil prices have been falling significantly, which Wagner believes could bring oil back to the $80 benchmark level.

"If those come down, it will take time to work it in the system," Wagner predicted. "I believe that national gasoline is around $4 to $5 a gallon where a year ago it might have been $3 to $4."

Should energy prices continue declining, inflation could moderate, potentially removing one pressure point that might otherwise force the Fed to maintain its hawkish stance.

However, Wagner cautions that even if inflation moves from 4.2% to 3%, the market would still be "well above the Fed's target" of 2%, and the central bank could continue maintaining elevated rates. This dynamic has created what Wagner describes as the "biggest disparity" in the current economic environment: increased wages in the United States and Canada have not kept pace with inflation.

"Even if people were making 3% more per year, they were pretty much losing money on the buying power of the currency," Wagner observed. "And that doesn't keep your citizenship happy."

---

## Geopolitical Considerations: The Iran Factor

The geopolitical landscape adds another layer of complexity to gold's outlook. Wagner notes that war in Iran, once considered a potential tailwind for gold prices, has been largely "put to the side, believing now we have a peace agreement."

While the durability of this agreement remains uncertain, its potential success could have significant implications for energy markets. The Strait of Hormuz represents a critical passage through which 10-20% of the world's oil supply flows.

"If we see the truce hold, there's going to be a 60-day period where they come to a long agreement," Wagner explained. "Because to me, that's the biggest uncertainty factor is whether or not it will hold."

The resolution of geopolitical tensions could reduce safe-haven demand for gold, though Wagner suggests the market may have already priced in much of this uncertainty.

---

## The Verdict: $4,000 as the Line in the Sand

Despite acknowledging the technical damage and the potential for deeper corrections, Gary Wagner's base case remains constructive for gold—at least in the near term.

"My assumption right now is that around $4,000 will be a floor in gold, and it's simply based upon the low that we saw last week," Wagner concluded.

This view rests on several supporting factors: the technical significance of the $4,000 level, the historical precedent of prior corrections finding support at round-number price points, and the fundamental backdrop of aggressive central bank accumulation. Whether this support zone can hold—and prevent a repeat of the devastating 40-50% corrections seen in 2011 and 1980—will depend on how these factors evolve in the coming weeks and months.

For investors watching gold's next move, $4,000 represents the critical battleground where technical support, fundamental demand, and market psychology will ultimately determine whether the precious metal has found its floor or is merely pausing before another leg down.
