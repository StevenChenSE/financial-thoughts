*An interview with veteran precious metals analyst Ed Steer reveals why the current selloff in silver and gold may be the final shakeout before a dramatic price reset.*

---

## The Setup: A Managed Selloff

On March 18, 2026, with silver breaking below $80 and gold below $5,000, metals investors were rattled. Yet, according to Ed Steer, this decline is not a reflection of fundamental weakness, but rather a familiar pattern of market intervention.

“The only reason that silver and gold prices are going down right now is because the eight large commercial traders are here beating it into the dirt,” Steer stated. “It’s the same old same old that we see all the time. They want to keep people out of precious metals and in paper assets.”

He points to the day’s Producer Price Index (PPI) reading of 7%—higher than expected—as a catalyst that should have sent metals “screaming higher.” Instead, selling pressure began even before the number was released. For Steer, this is evidence of “market management across the board,” orchestrated by what is often called the “Plunge Protection Team,” or formally, the Working Group on Financial Markets.

“There are no markets anymore, only interventions,” Steer said, quoting a past remark from Chris Powell. “What you’re seeing is on a massive, massive scale.”

## The Macro Backdrop: War, Oil, and Suppression

The interview took place against a tense geopolitical backdrop, with the Strait of Hormuz closed, locking an estimated 25% of the world’s oil supply out of the market. While crude prices were rising, Steer argued they—like metals—were being artificially capped.

“WTI should be well north of $100 and everybody knows that,” he said, noting the unusual ~$10 spread between WTI (~$98) and Brent crude (~$109). He sees direct parallels to the metals market, where regional supply and demand create massive premiums, like the 15%+ premium for silver in Shanghai over the COMEX price.

“It’s supply and demand… We’re going to see West Texas Intermediate crude prices well north of $100 before this is over, and sooner rather than later.”

Despite soaring energy costs, Steer isn’t overly concerned about mining company margins. While costs will rise, he believes the larger issue is the suppressed price of the metals themselves, which drags down mining stocks. For investors, he advises diversification through ETFs like GDX or GDXJ rather than trying to pick individual winners.

## The Silver Bull Case: A Setup Like Never Before

Amidst the gloom, Steer laid out a staggeringly bullish case for silver, rooted in the futures market structure.

> “Silver from a COMX futures market perspective is the most wildly incandescent bullish it's ever been in the length of time I've been following these markets. It has no… there's nothing to compare to it.”

His key data point: The large commercial traders (the big bullion banks) hold their smallest short position in silver in over 25 years. After the day’s price decline, he considers the setup “even more wildly bullish.”

This pattern, he explains, is a classic precursor to a major rally. “We’ve seen this movie before… over the last 25 years. Normally we get this big selloff. And once the big sell-off is done, then it’s off to the races to the upside.”

He believes the current selloff is a final effort by these entities to exit their remaining short positions before losing control. The physical market fundamentals support this: the global silver market has been in a structural deficit for over six years, and inventory is fleeing exchange vaults.

“120 million ounces of silver have been shipped out since the beginning of the year… The amount of silver leaving the COMEX and going elsewhere… is just absolutely off the charts.”

He also highlighted critically low inventory levels at the Shanghai Futures and Gold Exchanges, back to 2015 lows. “I expect that someday they're going to put restrictions on silver imports… the moment they even hint that they're going to do that, three-digit silver prices will be realized almost immediately.”

## The Endgame: Reset and Rally

So, what’s next?

“Once this thing is done, then we're going to see prices in a lot of different things reset, including the prices of silver [and] gold,” Steer predicts. He has “no trouble whatsoever” seeing both metals at new all-time highs before the end of the year.

His advice to investors channels the old adage of buying when there’s “blood in the streets.”

“The only reason I didn't buy anything today is because I got up late and I'm not feeling particularly well… If it's like this tomorrow, I'm going to definitely be in the market.”

For someone who is “almost all in” with 85% of his portfolio in silver, his next move might be to “hold my nose and buy gold stocks” for diversification, likely through broad-based ETFs.

## Conclusion: A Moment of Maximum Opportunity

For Ed Steer, the current weakness is a facade. The combination of a historic bullish setup in futures, rampant physical depletion, escalating geopolitical risk, and blatant market intervention has created a tinderbox. The dramatic selloff may simply be the match.

“We’re all sitting here waiting for the big reset in silver… it's just a matter of time before we see that silver break up above $120 and stay there.”
