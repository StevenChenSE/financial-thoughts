### The Final Stretch of the Metals Bull Market

Hunter begins by setting the stage for a significant recovery in the precious metals sector. He believes we are currently in the "final stretch" of the current metals bull market, which will last approximately **5 to 8 months** before a major consolidation occurs.

> "I do think that we're, you know, we're in the final stretch here, meaning the next um 5, 6, 8 months of the metals bull market before they have a big big consolidation through the bust. And then coming out the other side, uh we'll see much much higher highs in in both bevels um in what I think will be a big inflationary uh recovery cycle."

This consolidation, which Hunter defines as a "global bust," is expected to hit nearly all assets except for U.S. Treasuries. However, emerging from this downturn, he anticipates a robust inflationary recovery cycle.

> "So but that's yeah, that's 207 probably 28 29 30, you know, the early 2030s. Um, you could see gold get to 20,000. You could see silver get to I have had a long long held long-term target of 500 on silver."

While he acknowledges the volatility of the next cycle—where silver could drop 60-70% and gold 30-50%—he maintains that the upside potential is immense.

### The Oil Outlook: From $111 to $60, and Beyond

Turning to energy, Hunter addresses the recent surge in oil prices, which have climbed above **$111 per barrel**. While many fear this will trigger a recession, Hunter views it as a temporary spike before a longer-term decline.

> "Uh, so that's spooking some people, but I think the market's selling off in an early way here and basically giving back some of the excitement from yesterday."

He predicts that oil will roll over and return to the **$60s** within the next few months, likely by fall. Looking further into the future, he anticipates oil could plummet to **$30** during the "bust" phase (around 2027), before surging dramatically in the next cycle.

> "Now in the bust, which is mostly a 2027 story now. It could start this year, but it's, you know, late this year, but basically a 2027 story for me. Um when we have that bust hit, I think it can take oil all the way down to 30. Um so, you know, we had a surplus going into this war, a big surplus. Uh and that's why oil was weak. Um this obviously short-term took care of that. Um but I think in the longer you know the next many months that surplus will show itself again and um and then in a a very bad economy next year with demand dropping um I think it will it will hit oil."

Ultimately, he forecasts oil prices reaching **$500** by the early 2030s, making energy stocks and commodities key leaders in the next economic cycle.

> "Um and uh obviously with that kind of pricing power, the oil stock should do very well. And same thing if you think about the the price of goods sold in the metals. I mean if you're selling gold for anything 10 15 20,000 and you're selling silver for you know 500 600 600 and up. Um it's hard for miners not to make money. So it's hard for oil producers not to make money. So um that's where I think the next cycle focuses."

### Strategic Shifts: Beyond Passive Index Investing

Hunter warns that the strategy of simply "buying and holding" index funds, which has been successful for the past 40 years, may not hold up in the coming volatile decade.

> "So the the first message is that um you know if you think through that whole scenario um the index funds which have done so well you know passive investing which has done so well since the mid80s uh when the mantra began of it's time in the market not timing the market that whole mantra of you know particularly people accumulating in uh retirement assets you know just keep putting your money into index funds and letting it Don't try to play the market. Don't get in and out. Don't trade. Just just um you know put it in um index funds."

He explains that if the stock market drops **70-80%** during the upcoming bust, passive investors could face a prolonged period of underperformance.

> "So, if you just ride that out and say, "Well, it's worked for me for the last 40 years. I'm going to continue to just buy and hold." you're going to be pretty sick, I think, when you look and see your portfolio because you're an index fund drop that 70 or 80%."

Instead, Hunter suggests a shift toward **asset allocation** and potentially **market timing**, focusing on sectors with pricing power such as commodities, energy, and precious metals.

> "Um, so so you're still far below water and then um and that that's you know over probably the first couple years coming out of the bottom and then I think index funds are going to struggle from there because interest rates are going up. You may actually see the broad markets going down while the commodities continue to move merrily along. Um, so it's it's going to be very important to understand that what's worked for the last 40 years as a strategy is probably not going to be a good strategy going forward."

### Conclusion

David Hunter's outlook paints a picture of a market that is currently consolidating but poised for a dramatic, inflation-driven recovery. While the path will be rough—characterized by a "global bust" and high volatility—the opportunities in commodities, particularly gold and silver, appear substantial.

> "Um, and I think next cycle we'll see inflation move from, you know, negative to first uh low single digits to the higher single digits. And as we get to the early 2030s, we could be above 20%. And if you've got above 20% inflation, you're going to have interest rates up in the high teens. And that is just not something that a growth stock can handle. It's not something uh a lot of companies that are not going to have pricing power are going to be able to handle. Um but what can handle it are energy stocks, precious metals, uh copper, you know, steel, aluminum, you're going to see a big boom in commodities across the board, probably even a commodities."

For investors, the key takeaway is to adapt to the changing tides of the macroeconomic environment, preparing for a world where traditional equities may lag, but resilient sectors like energy and precious metals lead the way to new heights.
