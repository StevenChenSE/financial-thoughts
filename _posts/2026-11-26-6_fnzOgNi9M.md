## Executive Summary

Felix Zulauf, president of Zulauf Consulting, laid out a sequenced market scenario on *The Julia La Roche Show*: the current correction has already bottomed, a zigzagging rally takes the major indices to new highs by spring 2027, and a second-half-of-2027 crash follows as AI earnings disappoint. That downturn, he argues, forces the "mother of all stimulus" — which sets up a systemic crisis later in the decade.

## The Current Market Bottom

Zulauf's read is that stock markets globally, along with gold and silver, are bottoming now. Bond yields are topping out, and the US dollar still has upside, but primarily in *time* rather than price. He is emphatic that the recent 1987 comparisons were wrong: "this is not 1987. There are some ingredients that are completely different." In 1987 the US saw constant capital outflow and relentlessly rising bond yields; today capital is flowing in and yields are peaking.

His stated technical levels were hit almost exactly:

*   **S&P 500:** he expected a decline "into the range of 6,900 to 7,200. And I think we have bottomed. We may see some retests."
*   **Nasdaq 100:** "we expected a break of 26,000. My ballpark point was 25,200" — a number he had mentioned in private discussions, not published.
*   **Gold:** his projection, set early in the year when gold traded around $5,000, was 3,800 — and it undershot to that level, which he called "a little" early.

## The Final Rally: Spring 2027

"Next year, another rally into spring of next year to new highs. My target is about 8,500 on the S&P, and I think that will conclude" the advance. On Nvidia he was more specific and self-correcting: his earlier target was about $150, the stock went down to $125, and from $150 he sees "the upside over the next 6 months or so is about 50% or more to new highs."

The fuel, in his telling, is the money that just fled. Global fund managers cut their technology overweight from **37% to 12%** in a single month — the biggest positioning swing he had seen. Stanley Druckenmiller, "probably the most successful macro investor," went public at a conference saying his equity book was net and very low in gross terms, and that he had liquidated technology; hedge fund managers then mimicked him, which is why Zulauf saw "a lot of liquidation last week" and believes it "has run its course." Aggressive money, he says, is "way underrated because they were running for cover."

## The AI Boom: Earnings, Not Valuations

Zulauf separates this cycle from 2000: the dot-com era was a valuation bubble, while today's AI boom is an earnings and investment boom — and that is precisely what makes the reversal dangerous.

*   **Scale:** the AI investment boom is bigger relative to the size of the US economy than the 19th-century railroad boom, and "much bigger than the .com boom."
*   **Margin extremes:** technology companies outside the "big six" are posting earnings growth of 100% and net profit margins up to 70% — "companies today with a net profit margin of 70% that used to have a negative profit margin 4 years ago. You see this is very cyclical. It's a cyclical swing."
*   **Supply arriving:** "we will see new capacity coming on stream in semiconductors. So the supply side of semiconductors will come to the market. That will probably mean that the over ordering" disappears and prices fall.

First half of 2027: robust. Second half: "the earnings problem will pop up, come to the surface."

## The Second Half of 2027: The Crash

"Next year in the second half, we will have a big decline down to 5,000 in the S&P and probably down 50% in the Nasdaq 100, a big shakeout because at that time the earnings problem will pop up."

He expects quarters of negative GDP growth alongside it — not from a policy mistake but mechanically: "the market declining more than 20% usually impacts consumer wealth and therefore the spending and saving behavior of consumers, and that would be very normal." The capital-flow reversal runs the other way too: the US is running a current account deficit of almost 5% of GDP, financed largely by Asians (and some Europeans) who in this cycle bought AI and technology stocks through sovereign wealth funds instead of Treasury bonds, rather than the previous pattern.

His own portfolio instruction is blunt: an investor "should go maximum cash, whatever his mandate is, by spring of 27. And then wait for the great opportunity later in the year to buy back at discounted prices."

## Geopolitics and Commodities

### Oil and Iran

Zulauf is bearish oil, targeting about **$60 a barrel**: the world economy is slowing ("China is in recession. You know the numbers that come out of China, the official numbers are fake"), and he expects "minor military interventions, but I do not see a major escalation."

The tail risk is what he flags, not the base case: "A major escalation would rewrite the whole script of what I said. A major escalation would push the oil price to $200 and then we would all find ourselves in a serious depression worldwide." His reason for ruling it out is military, not political: "you cannot win militarily against Iran. They have put themselves in a very strong position. They have the escalation potential. They could sink all the US carriers."

### Multipolar disorder

Zulauf goes as far as saying it is "the first time in over [N] years that the West has lost its power dominance over the world" — the auto-caption drops the number of years he cites, so treat the span as his emphasis rather than a quoted figure. The world is moving from a unipolar US-led order to a multipolar one, which raises systemic risk. He nonetheless calls the recent trilateral summit of "the three big guys" positive: "better talk. And when you talk and better understand the others, then you can probably come to a conclusion" about how to avoid catastrophic conflict.

## The "Mother of All Stimulus" and the Systemic Endgame

The late-2027 crash is not, for Zulauf, the end of the secular cycle — it is the trigger for policy intervention on a scale that then creates the real danger. "Get ready for the mother of all stimulus."

*   **Post-crash run-up:** "another big run-up of probably 100% from 5,000 S&P to 10,000 S&P into the late 20s or early 30s."
*   **The bond market pays:** yields "from then on will explode on the upside, and I could easily see late this decade 8% for 10-year Treasuries."
*   **The endgame:** that is when the systemic risk arrives — he draws the parallel to "1929, 100 years ago, and then a systemic meltdown," with a serious depression lasting several years.

## Investment Strategy

1.  **Equities:** ride the rally into spring 2027, then go to maximum cash — "whatever his mandate is" — and wait to buy back at discounted prices in the second half.
2.  **Gold and silver:** both are bottoming and are buying opportunities. Gold is the hedge that matters because it "is an asset that has no liability. What we call money — dollars, euros, yen — it's not money, it's credit, because it always has a liability."
3.  **Copper:** bearish. It is the base metal tied to "the electrification of the transportation system and the AI build-up, and I think it has been over done."
4.  **Bonds:** bullish near term as yields peak — the turn he made this year and which drew controversy — but wary once stimulus re-ignites the long end.

His own framing of the horizon: he deliberately refused to extrapolate further, saying he "just wanted to focus on the next months, and it's not over yet."
