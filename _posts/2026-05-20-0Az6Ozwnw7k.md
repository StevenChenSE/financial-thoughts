Silver has surged to $89—an eight-week high that has stackers buzzing and traders reassessing their positions. In a recent episode of Capital Kazm, veteran precious metals analyst David Morgan sat down with host Danny to dissect the charts, outline his outlook, and explain the disciplined rules he uses to navigate volatile markets. From technical patterns and price projections to the broader macroeconomic forces at play, here's a comprehensive look at Morgan's analysis and what it means for investors.

## Technical Analysis: Reading the Charts Differently

The most striking observation on the silver chart is the breakout from a falling wedge pattern—a classic technical formation that often signals further upside. Morgan confirmed the pattern is valid, but he emphasized that his approach differs from conventional technical analysis.

After studying under practitioners connected to the original developers of stochastic indicators, Morgan shifted toward what he calls a "horizontal lines" methodology. Rather than relying on complex indicators and channel formations, he identifies key support and resistance levels and watches how price interacts with them.

> "If you draw a line from where we are right now straight across to the left, that right there is the top of the trading range. If you take the bottom... draw a horizontal line there that parallels the top one. We have a very broad trading range."

By drawing horizontal lines at the top and bottom of silver's trading range, Morgan identifies a broad zone between approximately $70 and $90 that has defined price action over recent months. The current breakout above that horizontal resistance marks a significant technical shift.

### Key Technical Observations

- **Falling wedge breakout**: Valid pattern confirming upside momentum
- **Horizontal channel approach**: Uses simple horizontal lines rather than complex channel formations
- **Trading range**: Identified $70–$90 as the established range; current breakout suggests continuation
- **Acceleration phase**: Historical precedent shows sharp moves when price breaks horizontal resistance with momentum

Morgan noted that the January 70% move in silver demonstrated how quickly price can accelerate once it clears such horizontal resistance levels—and suggested the market could see a similar dynamic play out again.

## The Morgan Rule: A Disciplined Entry Strategy

For paid subscribers and public audiences alike, Morgan has articulated what he calls "The Morgan Rule"—a systematic approach to confirming breakouts that removes emotion and guesswork from trading decisions.

### The Three-Day Rule

Morgan's framework requires three specific conditions be met before acting on a breakout:

1. **Three consecutive daily closes above the top horizontal line** (in this case, above $90)
2. **Above-average volume on each of those three days**
3. **Calculation based on closing prices only (On Close Only/OCO)**

Morgan explained the practical application:

> "If we break through that top line and we stay there three days in a row—it must be consecutive days on above-average volume—then we have a breakout with 80% probability. 80 isn't 100, but it's pretty darn good."

Crucially, the daily closing price is what matters—not the intraday trading range. If silver closes at $91 on day one, trades down to $87.50 the next day, but closes at $90.50, that still counts as a valid day two. This flexibility prevents false signals from normal intraday volatility while maintaining discipline.

### Trade Management

For those acting on the signal:

- **Entry**: Confirms breakout after three qualifying closes
- **Stop placement**: Mental stop approximately at the breakout level ($90.50–$91 area)
- **Position sizing**: Morgan didn't specify exact sizing but emphasized keeping stops mental so institutional algorithms can't detect them

> "I'm very open to it. I won't say shocked because I've been around the silver market for decades, but it shows you how silver can scare you out or wear you out. If you were short seven or eight trading sessions ago, you watched silver go from roughly $70 to $90 in about seven or eight trading days. That's pretty spectacular as far as percentage movement."

## Price Projections: From $90 to $150 (and Beyond)

Morgan laid out a clear roadmap for silver's potential path forward, blending technical analysis with fundamental reasoning.

### Near-Term Target: The $100 Stall Point

Morgan anticipates an initial pause around the psychologically significant $100 level:

> "If we get a move from $90 to $100, I do think—and others in my peer group don't—but I think we'll stall out around $100 for a while. I think a small while, a month or a couple weeks. I think there's a lot of people in my age group that missed the $100 sale that'll see it manifest again and say, 'I'm out, man. Sell half my position.'"

This anticipated profit-taking at $100 reflects human psychology in markets—investors who felt they missed the move earlier will take gains when price returns to levels they consider fair. Morgan doesn't fault this approach; he simply acknowledges it's likely to create a temporary ceiling.

### Medium-Term Targets: $150 to $200

Beyond the $100 stall, Morgan projects significantly higher prices:

> "I really think we're going to go to $150 minimum if not $200. And I don't rule out Michael Oliver's $500 target. It's really tough in a debt-based fiat fantasy Ponzi scheme to put a paper price on it."

### Real Asset Valuation: What an Ounce Should Buy

Morgan emphasized that paper price targets are inherently unstable in a fiat currency system. Instead, he prefers analyzing what an ounce of silver purchases in real-world commodities:

- **Silver in terms of oil**: How many barrels per ounce?
- **Silver in terms of wheat**: Bushels per ounce?
- **Silver in terms of housing**: Square footage per ounce?

> "If the standard best silver ever did is X bushels of wheat, and all of a sudden an ounce of silver buys 2X bushels of wheat, hey, it's overvalued in terms of wheat. So I really think—you know me, old grumpy old man—I love to stress the fundamentals because a lot of people are misled."

### Historical Precedent: Jastram's Golden Constant

Citing Professor Roy Jastram's seminal work (*The Golden Constant* and *Silver, the Restless Metal*), Morgan pointed to historical evidence supporting silver's inflationary hedge properties:

> "The conclusion that Professor Jastram came to after studying very diligently gold and silver was nothing—and I mean nothing—will prepare you better for an inflationary environment than silver. Whenever he looked back to the 13th century, when currencies and the monetary system were inflating, the one that did you the best wasn't gold—it was silver."

## Silver vs Gold: The Lead Dog Has Changed

A critical observation in Morgan's analysis is the shift in leadership within the precious metals complex.

### Silver Outperforming Gold

The silver-to-gold ratio has broken through the 33:1 level—historically significant as the best ratio in the past 25 years:

> "Silver's gold silver ratio is 33. That's the best it's been in the last 25 years, and it broke through it. It's going to 16. I'm swapping my gold to silver, and they double their money just by moving from gold to silver."

Currently, silver trades at approximately 1.8% of gold's price. When this ratio rises, it indicates silver outperforming gold—a healthy sign for the overall precious metals bull market.

### Why Silver Takes the Lead in Bull Markets

Morgan explained this dynamic is historically normal and expected:

> "When we get into the major bull market final phase, or even phase two, silver will take over the lead at some point. And that's what we expect in a major bull market. Anyone that follows the precious metals knows that silver almost always exceeds price appreciation relative to gold in the final phase and the end of the run."

Supporting this thesis, Morgan noted that in 2025, gold surged approximately 65% for the year—but both platinum and silver gained 140%, with that outperformance beginning in May.

### Divergence Warning

Morgan expressed what would concern him:

> "What would worry me, Danny, is if we saw a big divergence and we saw gold break through that bottom line and silver was up to $100 or something like that. Why? Because I want confirmation."

He follows Jim Dyne's application of Dow Theory to precious metals, emphasizing the importance of all four metals confirming moves. Currently, that confirmation exists—which he considers "proof that we're in a really strong market."

## Copper: The Industrial Leader

Copper has reached all-time highs at $6.66 per pound, presenting a different but related opportunity.

### Technical Setup

Copper has broken out of a bull flag continuation pattern. Morgan noted that copper's chart also validates his three-day rule:

> "If you draw the channel... when above whatever that top is three days in a row, bang, we're on 80% probability and away we go."

### The AI Data Center Connection

The copper surge reflects industrial demand from an unexpected source:

> "People think the only way to play AI is through buying these tech companies, but in reality, you can also just buy copper miners and silver miners and lithium miners and any other type of resources that go heavily into building out these data centers."

Morgan has personally invested in a Montana copper play alongside an investment banker and geologist—a high-risk, high-reward situation accessible primarily to institutional and sophisticated investors.

### Mining Equities Lagging

Despite copper and silver hitting new highs, the mining equity ETFs tell a more cautionary story:

> "Here's COPX, the copper miners ETF. While copper is making new all-time highs, copper miners are still below their all-time highs. Silver miners—they're still consolidating."

This divergence between metals and mining equities creates both opportunity and caution:

- **If metals continue higher**: Mining equities should follow, offering leverage
- **If metals fail**: Mining equities signal "We need more work to do to build another base"
- **Market wisdom**: Equities often act as confirmation tools—"The market knows more than me"

> "The metals are saying, 'I'm making new highs,' but the equities aren't. If the metals fall off, the equities are saying, 'We're right. You know, we need some more work to do.' If the metals take off, the equities will follow. It's like knowing ahead of time that your horse is going to finish in the top three."

## The Macro Backdrop: Oil, Inflation, and the Bond Market

Morgan placed the precious metals rally within a broader macroeconomic context that warrants attention.

### Oil and Mining Margins

Oil has rallied to $101 per barrel—a critical input cost for mining operations:

> "Oil is the first domino to drop. Modern civilization runs off hard hydrocarbons—diesel, jet fuel, petrochemicals, shipping fuel, fertilizer, plastics, electrical generation."

The relationship between oil and metals had been inversely correlated for two months, but Morgan sees them moving in tandem again:

> "The last two months or so since the start of the war, oil and metals have been kind of inversely correlated. When one goes up, the other goes down and vice versa. Now it seems they're finally back to moving in tandem."

For mining companies, higher oil costs compress margins—but if metal prices rise proportionally, profitability can be maintained.

### PPI Data: Inflation Reigniting

The Producer Price Index (PPI) showed wholesale inflation up 6% year-over-year in April—the largest increase since 2022:

> "Wholesale prices, if wholesale prices rise, doesn't that imply that consumer prices are going to rise? Well, if the producer cost increases, they're not going to sell at a loss. They must, to stay in business, increase the retail price."

Morgan sees this as confirming the inflationary environment that historically benefits silver.

### The 10-Year Yield and Federal Reserve Limitations

The 10-year Treasury yield has broken out to the upside, recently touching 4.5%:

> "I think they defend that level. I'm really concerned. The bond market is the most important market of all. In the financial realm, how the system is run on digits and computers, nothing's more important than the US Treasury market."

Morgan expressed skepticism that the Federal Reserve can indefinitely suppress rates:

> "The Fed isn't as powerful as most people think. If you start to see that regardless of what the bankers want, what the financial markets want, what Wall Street and London want, if the market says, 'Foo on your dollar—I don't trust your dollar in the future. I demand a higher interest rate'—they're going to see a higher interest rate regardless of what the Fed sets for the federal funds rate."

He sees the market increasingly taking determination away from central banks—signaling a fundamental shift in the financial system.

## Final Thoughts: Staying Humble and Disciplined

Morgan closed with reflections on decades of market experience:

> "I've learned from my own experience through this trek of honesty, integrity, truth, and telling like it is in the financial system that a lot of things can go wrong and this crooked system of ours can continue onward. But I've also learned that there are a lot of good companies out there. There's a lot of progress being made. There's a lot of good things that have made our lives better."

His core message: simplicity and discipline trump complexity:

> "I remember when I was your age... I learned over time that analysis paralysis by analysis—you know, this indicator is bullish but this one's neutral, this one's bearish, what do I do? It's like, what is the market doing? What's the market saying now? The market's saying what we just showed you: 'Hey, I'm making new highs, but the equities aren't.'"
