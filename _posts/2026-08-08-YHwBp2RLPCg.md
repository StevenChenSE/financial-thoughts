### The Mechanics of the Intervention

Japan traditionally defends its currency by selling its massive stockpile of U.S. Treasuries to buy yen. However, doing so unilaterally would spike U.S. interest rates, an outcome the U.S. cannot currently tolerate. The solution emerged through a coordinated approach:

1.  **Exchange Stabilization Fund (ESF):** The initial intervention involved the U.S. selling euros and buying yen, effectively providing the liquidity Japan needed without triggering a fire sale of Treasuries.
2.  **Fed Repo Facility:** The next phase involves leveraging the Federal Reserve Bank of New York’s FEMA repo facility. This would allow Japan to borrow dollars against its $1.14 trillion in Treasury holdings. With a current counterparty limit of $60 billion, this tool offers a secured way to defend the yen while granting the Bank of Japan breathing room to raise domestic interest rates.
