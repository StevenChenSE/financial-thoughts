The financial media loves to trumpet all-time highs in the stock market, but what they conveniently omit is that when measured against gold—a more stable unit of value—the S&P 500 hasn't made a genuine new high in 25 years. In this comprehensive market analysis, technical analyst Casper from the Uslink INV YouTube channel joins Danny on the Capital podcast to examine gold, silver, mining stocks, oil, interest rates, and the dollar through a rigorously chart-driven lens. The conclusion is striking: gold has quietly outperformed equities for a quarter-century, and the metals mining sector is positioned to deliver exceptional returns as one critical ratio completes a half-century breakdown.

---

## The Great Illusion: Stock Market "All-Time Highs" in Fiat Currency

The S&P 500 continues to shatter nominal records, sporting a dramatic V-shaped recovery from recent lows. But Casper argues that measuring wealth in depreciating fiat currencies creates a fundamentally distorted picture.

> "If you were to do this in for instance against maybe inflation and/or the gold again we basically haven't made a new all-time high since 2001, I think, at the dotcom bubble... so in theory you haven't really done much in 25 years when it comes to the broader markets if you measure it in my view against a more stable currency that is gold."

When the S&P 500 is priced in gold rather than dollars, the last genuine all-time high occurred during the dotcom bubble of 2000, when equities reached 0.55 on the gold-adjusted chart. Since then, despite nominal gains, the market has underperformed gold by roughly 50-60%.

This matters because gold roughly tracks inflation at approximately 10% per year, while government statistics claim inflation at only 2%. Casper notes:

> "Gold typically tells the truth. So, yeah, yes, we are making all-time highs in the stock market, but against gold on a... monthly candlestick chart, you're actually in a... quite a severe bear market."

The mainstream media's fixation on nominal all-time highs obscures the real story: the purchasing power of fiat currencies has been eroding steadily, and anyone measuring their portfolio solely in dollars, euros, or yen is seeing an illusion of prosperity.

## Gold: The 40-Year Cup and Handle Reaching Completion

After 15 years of consolidation, gold has broken out in dramatic fashion. The technical picture shows a classic bull flag formation following the 2020 peak, with three touchpoints at the top before finally breaking above $2,100.

### Current Technical Position

Gold now trades above $2,100, representing a massive long-term bull market that Casper expects to continue for years. The dollar and bond market collapse is creating the perfect environment for gold appreciation:

> "Overall we are in a very healthy long-term bull market and... this will continue for quite a while because again the dollar and the bond market is collapsing... and gold is basically the inverse of particularly dollar... adjusted for inflation."

### Price Targets and Timeline

Casper's base case target for gold stands at **$8,000**, though he believes this may be conservative:

> "I have a... base target that is 8K for this one here, but I think I'm lowballing it... with quite a big amount, but 8K is kind of like my... standard case, but I'm expecting way higher prices."

The timing for these targets? Roughly 2026-2027:

> "It depends again we could see 6K easily... in 2026 and then 2027 is roughly where I'm expecting 8K to come into play."

### Short-Term Consolidation

While the long-term picture is bullish, gold is currently in a consolidation phase that may see one more leg down potentially below $4,000:

> "You have typically in a bull flag or in a consolidation you have three selloffs. So again, even though we are to make potentially a low... below like 4K, you're still overall in a very good healthy environment."

The measured move from the flag pole suggests prices could reach $7,000 relatively quickly once consolidation completes, but Casper considers even this target "way too low for gold."

## Silver: The Better Play Within Precious Metals

Silver often gets overshadowed by gold, but Casper identifies it as the superior opportunity in the current environment. The critical breakout occurred in April 2024 when silver broke above $26, forming what technical analysts recognize as a cup and handle pattern.

### The $50 Level and Long-Term Potential

Silver famously tested the $50 level in 2011 during the last major precious metals bull market. That level was decisively broken at the end of 2025, with a mini blowoff top into February. But for Casper, the real story began earlier:

> "To me the entire silver... macro bull market actually began in April 2024 when we finally broke 26."

The big picture target from the multi-decade cup and handle formation points toward **$500+**, though timing remains uncertain:

> "If you do a lock scaled measured move from that you're getting into the 500 plus... it has to be precise... it has 666, funny enough... the target for that big 45 year cup and handle."

### The Gold-Silver Ratio: The Most Important Metric

The gold-silver ratio currently sits around **70:1**, and Casper views this as the single most important indicator for tracking the mining sector:

> "The gold silver ratio, which is the most important ratio in my view, if you want to keep track of the mining sector."

The ratio's path forward is clear: expect a collapse toward **30:1**, followed by a bounce, before ultimately reaching the 1980s lows around **15:1 and below**:

> "I think right now we are at roughly... 70... and I do believe we are going to hit again roughly 30 for this leg down and then maybe have one big bounce... before eventually collapsing to the old 1980s low at 15 to 1 and below."

During this ratio collapse, silver will dramatically outperform gold—historically the period when mining stocks shine brightest.

## Mining Stocks: The Coming Outperformance

The GDX (gold miners ETF) has begun breaking out against the broader market, a development that signals transformative opportunities ahead for precious metals investors.

### The GDX vs. SPY Breakdown

A fascinating chart shows GDX divided by SPY, revealing a descending triangle building toward completion:

> "If you were to take one line from this chart... it's actually the 2011 high and then you can see the 2020 here and then we peaked once again at 2023 and then finally... at the early start of 2025 you actually broke the entire downtrend."

Since that breakout, the gold miners have outperformed the S&P by as much as 150%, settling currently around 70% outperformance—a substantial lead that Casper sees expanding further:

> "This one here is just some sort of counter trend move before eventually going higher and... outperform the broader market that is the S&P."

### Mining Sector Profitability: The Oil-Adjusted View

One of the most insightful charts Casper presented shows GDX adjusted for the inflation-adjusted price of oil—a measure of real profitability for gold miners. The picture is remarkably bullish:

> "Even though we have the pullback... looking back 20 years, the gold miners as a whole is actually making a lot more money... as they used to do. And... that will in my view only continue to remain high."

The ratio has broken above a long-term channel, and once the gold-to-oil ratio declines further, miners will become increasingly profitable:

> "At some point in time once we get to 15 to 1 in that ratio... that's it. That's your top right. That is your top. And we are at 70 now. So we have a long way to go."

### Volatility Reality Check

Investors must understand that mining stocks are not for the faint of heart. Casper emphasizes:

> "Sometimes we're getting like a 40% correction in miners in basically two weeks... it is very tricky to be in, but it is... one of the assets that truly will be the best performing assets."

Silver miners (SIL) have broken their 15-year downtrend and are now consolidating in a bull flag that represents outstanding longer-term opportunity despite short-term turbulence.

## Oil: The Breakout That Changes Everything

Energy prices have surged over 50% in just two months, and the technical picture suggests this move has much further to run.

### Multi-Year Wedge Breakout

Oil broke out of a four-year wedge formation driven by Middle East tensions, with most of the move occurring in just 5-10 days. The current consolidation is recharging for the next major move:

> "Right now we're digesting that move... it still an overall... it seems like it wants to recharge and then eventually break higher at some point in time."

The $40 trading range seen recently—from $70 to $120—is unprecedented volatility in oil markets, but Casper sees this as transitional noise before the next leg up.

### Price Targets

The macro target for oil begins at **$125**, which would signal the end of bear market conditions and the beginning of a new structural bull phase:

> "If you start to break roughly 125, 130, that's the start of the journey to 200, 300, maybe even 400... not tomorrow but that is the macro targets that you get once you resolve such a long-term pattern like oil."

Higher oil prices will feed directly into inflation data, supporting the precious metals thesis and putting additional pressure on central banks to maintain elevated interest rates.

## Interest Rates: The Critical Breakdown Approaching

The U.S. 10-year Treasury yield has reached 4.6-6%, approaching a resolution of a three-year consolidation that could redefine the macro environment.

### The 45-Year Bond Bear Market Ending

The bond bull market that began in the 1980s reached its nadir in 2020, when rates went negative in Europe and approached zero in the U.S. That marked the final bottom for bonds:

> "That was the bottom for the bond market and ever since we have basically had a huge snapback breaking the entire downtrend... rates is going to be higher for longer and we haven't seen the high yet for rates."

### The Three-Year Wedge Resolution

After breaking out in 2023, yields have been contained in a compression triangle, now approaching the apex:

> "After this big breakout you can see... we have kind of been contained by that... compressing price this triangle here... once you start to break again this three-year wedge it is only pointing to higher prices."

The target? **6-7% yields potentially by late 2026 or 2027**:

> "That wouldn't surprise me if we break here."

The bond market is the largest market in the world, and when it breaks down, the contagion spreads to stocks, bonds, and crypto as leveraged players are forced to cover positions by selling whatever they can:

> "Remember the bond market compared to every other market is by far the biggest player. So when that starts to look like a very ill patient... that could contagion even into silver and gold S&P Bitcoin every asset basically."

## The Dollar Dilemma: Potential 20-Year Breakdown

The DXY (Dollar Index) is holding its 20-year uptrend but faces potential catastrophic breakdown.

### Critical Support Levels

If the dollar breaks below 95-97, it risks a 20-year trendline breakdown that could send it to **88**:

> "If we start to get too much below roughly... 97, 96, maybe 95, then you start to break this 20-year uptrend. And that's when the dollar is going to have a really, really bad day. And we could see 88, you know, somewhat quickly in 2027, maybe even late 2026."

### The Unprecedented Disconnect

What's unusual is that the dollar isn't following the rates breakdown in typical fashion:

> "I think this is such a moment where we haven't seen this disconnect between rates and the Dixie."

The resolution will come within months. A move above 100-101 would confirm dollar strength; failure to hold means historical weakness ahead.
