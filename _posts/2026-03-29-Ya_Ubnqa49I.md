In a recent episode of the *Shawn Newman Podcast*, renowned economist and forecaster Martin Armstrong returned to discuss everything from precious metals and market manipulation to geopolitical intrigue and the looming breakup of nations. Known for his unorthodox views and decades of experience at the heart of global financial crises, Armstrong didn’t hold back.

## A Startling Admission: “If I Die by Suicide, You Know Who It Really Is”

The conversation opened with Armstrong addressing a recent, ominous tweet: “I have shaken the ground that the neocons stand on. So if I die by a suicide, you know who it really is.” He explained this stems from his direct involvement in high-stakes geopolitical maneuvering.

Armstrong revealed he was personally called upon to draft a peace plan for Ukraine that would be acceptable to Vladimir Putin. “I was asked to write this peace plan… and use my back channels,” he stated. To maintain plausible deniability for his contacts, he published the plan on his blog. “I got it on Putin’s desk,” Armstrong claimed, “and two days later [Putin] came out and said… Russia’s willing to guarantee we’re not interested in going into NATO.”

According to Armstrong, the meeting where he was tasked with this revealed a deep rift within U.S. power structures. He warned officials that the real future enemy was China, not Russia, and that the U.S. should “get the hell out of NATO” to avoid being dragged into a false-flag-triggered world war. To his surprise, he says they agreed.

“I guess I was called in because I’m on the opposite side of the table from the neocons,” Armstrong mused, suggesting his role was sanctioned by one faction opposing another. He later received a thank-you letter from Donald Trump, which he has posted on his X feed. “I’ve been battling against these people since ’98,” he said, referencing powerful neoconservative figures he has known personally.

## A Forecaster’s Journey: From Every Crisis to Prison Intrigue

Armstrong’s credibility, he argues, comes from a career spent in the trenches of every major financial event since the 1970s. He was consulted during the collapse of Bretton Woods, the formation of the G5, the 1987 crash, the creation of the Euro, and the 1997 Asian currency crisis. “I’m not an academic. I’m a trader,” he said, noting that even top universities have admitted to him that “what we teach doesn’t work.”

His opposition to established powers has come at a high cost. He discussed the documentary *The Forecaster*, which details his life and predictions. To stop it, he claims, “They broke into the producer’s offices, stole what they thought was all the film.” A planned Netflix deal was canceled at the last minute after “somebody picked up the phone.”

His imprisonment in the late 2000s became its own drama. While in prison during the 2008 financial crisis, Armstrong says he was advising Congress. “They got a hold of the BOP [Bureau of Prisons]. They had the BOP throw me in the hole to cut off my communications.” Only a congressional investigation into his solitary confinement secured his release. A sequel to the documentary is due out before summer, he noted, to show how his past forecasts have all come true.

## The Flawed System: Manipulated CPI and Wasteful Government

Armstrong pulled no punches in critiquing modern economic management. He explained how government budgeting encourages waste, citing a rule where agencies must spend their entire annual allocation or face budget cuts the next year. This leads to “warehouses at Fort Dix in New Jersey with… the first computers, thousands of them, never used.”

He saved particular scorn for the manipulation of the Consumer Price Index (CPI). “The reason they manipulate CPI is because their budgets are automatically indexed to CPI,” he explained. To keep budgets—and deficits—from exploding, governments have repeatedly changed the CPI formula, such as replacing rising real estate costs with controlled rents. “They have finagled everything,” he said.

## The Inevitable Breakup: Alberta, Europe, and Beyond

Turning to Alberta’s rising independence movement, Armstrong was direct: “Eventually Alberta will split.” He sees this as part of a global trend. “So will Europe, and eventually even the United States will break up.”

His reasoning hinges on the failure of centralized, one-size-fits-all economic policy. He pointed to the original, “brilliant” design of the U.S. Federal Reserve, which had independent regional branches with different interest rates to suit local economies. That changed under Franklin D. Roosevelt, who centralized power to push a socialist agenda. “Canada followed suit,” he noted.

Armstrong recalled the 1980s, when the Bank of Canada raised rates to cool Toronto’s housing boom, thereby “putting farmers and miners into bankruptcy in Alberta.” He calls this the “Texas New York arbitrage” problem—centralized policy inevitably crushes one region to serve another. “Centralized government does not work,” he concluded.

## Precious Metals and the Road Ahead

While the podcast opened with a promo for holding physical gold and silver in registered accounts (with a March 2nd deadline noted), Armstrong’s broader discussion framed precious metals as a traditional hedge in a world of manipulated currencies and failing centralized systems.

His final warning was cyclical and geopolitical: understanding interconnected global economic cycles is key, and those who stand against powerful, manipulative factions do so at their own risk. For Armstrong, the path forward is clear—decentralization, a return to realistic economic analysis, and vigilance against those who would silence dissent.
