Clem Chambers, founder of NewFM, joined David Lin over the Thanksgiving weekend with a market already down about 20% from its October peak — trough "a couple of days ago, earlier this week." The question he puts is not whether the dip is cheap, but whether the money printers have already been switched on.

## The Fork in the Road

Chambers frames the choice in two paths:

1.  **The European road to decline.** "You don't print money. You don't bother with any of that. You let nature take its course." Slow, grinding decline instead of a crash — and he is explicit about the cost of the alternative: "a 50% correction in the NASDAQ would probably break the back of America."
2.  **The all-in reflation path.** "Quite a lot of chaos, but you do get massive economic growth" — printing to fund the AI buildout and onshore industry, with inflation as the price.

His own lean: "I think we'll go down the all-in path. And that would be great for gold, great for silver, great for copper, great for banks." Asked point-blank whether the NASDAQ takes out its previous high this year, he says no; whether it drops another 20%, "I hope not… I'm not convinced because it's all probabilities."

The tell he is watching is timing, not fundamentals: "did they start printing yesterday afternoon? And if they did, are they going to carry on?" His claim about recent price action is blunt — "V-shaped bottoms are not natural market bottoms. They are intervention bottoms where the government's gone: we will print money, and that money will go into assets and up the market will go."

## The Crash Taxonomy

Chambers sorts declines into four tiers, and the current 20% sits just under the first:

*   **25% — "common garden."** "If you get to 25%, that's when the wheels threaten to come off… 25% is your average crash, and crashes cause all sorts of horrible outcomes."
*   **33% — "a bit vicious."**
*   **50% — "almighty."** He notes there have been "a few 50s," and points to the failure to intervene as the source of systemic damage: "if you don't intervene then you get systemic damage like you did in the financial crisis."
*   **75% — "Armageddon."** "There's only two 75s" — the caption drops the years he names.

History disciplines the downside: after the 2000 dot-com bust, "the NASDAQ didn't recover to its pre-1999 peak until 2015." His argument for why that repeat is unlikely is that governments have learned to intervene — hence intervention bottoms.

## The AI Liquidity Squeeze

The mechanism he describes is a cash queue. Under reflation, "all the mag seven get as many trillions as they want to build out AI, and all the companies that are behind them in the queue for cash get what they [get left over]." Smaller businesses lose access to credit — the oxygen of the corporate world.

His analogy for why companies borrow anyway: "I'm going to play chess with you, David… I'll give you 10 grand if you beat me, and you can give me two grand if I beat you, but I'm going to be playing with a chess computer and you're not. How do you like those odds?" Either you adopt AI or you perish — "which is the same calculation that America has to make."

The stress test is severe: "I would say there's probably only [a small number of] companies in the S&P that would survive an environment where they couldn't borrow money. They'd all go." The auto-caption drops the exact count, so treat it as his point about fragility rather than a quoted figure.

## The Geopolitical Gap

The urgency is industrial, and Asia's lead is what makes it urgent: "we haven't seen the top of the AI bubble in Asia… they've got 250% more electricity to drive AI. And that's the critical point for America. Hasn't got enough electricity to do any of this."

*   **Power:** the US lacks the generation to support its own AI ambitions or onshore manufacturing.
*   **Robotics and factories:** "They're ahead of you two, three years on robots. They've got all the manufacturing… the factories where nobody works, and it's all dark factories as they call them. No lights need to be on."
*   **Pharmaceutical inputs:** China makes "75% of all the precursors of all your medicines" — raw materials that become a strategic chokepoint if cut off. "If you want to come back from that, you got to go all in."

## Assets

*   **Gold and silver:** bullish, and he has been repeating the entry level on the show — "How many times did I say $3,500 on your show?" — "every time I've been on." Gold at $3,500–$3,600 and silver around $40 are the levels he works with.
*   **Bonds:** Treasury yields had slipped back below 5%, which is why the host floated bonds as a better play. Chambers reframes falling rates as **financial repression**: "a little bit of financial repression going on there, probably quite a lot. They will be pushing down interest rates and they will be trying to keep up inflation" — rates suppressed below inflation, which is precisely the environment where monetary metals thrive.
*   **Bitcoin:** "No." At $60,000 he is not interested; at $40,000 "I might say, yeah, I told you so"; at $30,000 "you might be getting cheap." The structural reason is legal risk: "Crypto is absolutely suffocated now by regulation. You can't start a crypto with any more market cap than a million and not risk going to jail."
*   **The tell-portfolio:** "make a paper portfolio of banks, copper, gold, silver, and the hyperscalers and all the people in the AI value chain. The day the people that want to give up are in charge, they all go down — copper goes down, miners go down, banks go down, all the people in the AI buildout go down. And when they're not in charge, they all go up together." That basket moving together is his live read on whether the all-in path is active.

## The Limits of AI

On whether AI replaces human creativity, Chambers is a hard no: it can enumerate "all the possible permutations of cake and judge which one I would like the most," but "it can't make the Steve Jobs leap of intuition," and it "can't come up with a new kind of mathematics like Wolfram does or Einstein did. It cannot generate that random spark of intuition. It can only build and synthesize."
