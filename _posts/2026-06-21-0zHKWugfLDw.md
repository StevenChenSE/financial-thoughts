## Legendary Macro Analyst Predicts Parabolic Move as 44-Year Bull Market Nears Its End

David Hunter, a veteran macro analyst with decades of experience navigating market cycles, recently appeared on the *Metals and Miners* show to share his bold outlook with host Gary Bone. Known for his contrarian perspectives and accurate cycle predictions, Hunter delivered a comprehensive analysis that challenges conventional market wisdom—and his forecasts are nothing short of dramatic.

Hunter characterizes the current period as historic, describing it as the culmination of a nearly 44-year secular bull market that he believes will end in spectacular fashion: a parabolic runup into a major top. "I think people will just have to understand that it's going to be hard to want to be out at the top or to want to go the other way when you don't get to those kind of levels," he explained. While he sees significant upside ahead in the coming months, Hunter emphasizes that "there's another side of the mountain coming"—pointing toward what he describes as a global bust next year that will dwarf a typical recession.

## The New Fed Landscape: Warsh Takes the Helm

The discussion turned to the Federal Reserve's leadership transition, with Kevin Warsh now at the helm. Hunter noted that Warsh aligns with Trump's policy direction, which explains his appointment. However, Jerome Powell remains as a voting governor until his term expires on January 31st, 2028—a situation that Hunter says creates some friction but won't derail Warsh's agenda.

"Powell respects the position of chairman enough that he'll be a voting member," Hunter explained. "I don't think he's going to get into big conflicts with Warsh or try to run the committee from a second spot."

Looking ahead, Hunter expects Warsh to pursue rate cuts in the coming months, despite current market pricing that suggests no cuts this year. He believes inflation will roll back over as oil prices decline and geopolitical tensions ease, giving the Fed room to maneuver. Hunter also noted that Warsh wants to take a longer-term view, recognizing that AI and technology will have dampening effects on inflation over time.

## The Bond Market Will Lead, Not the Fed

One of Hunter's core beliefs challenges the traditional narrative about Fed power: "The bond market sets rates, not the Fed." He sees the bond market bottoming now, with rates topping out as the economy shows increasing signs of slowing. "As oil prices head for $60 and inflation looks like it's rolling over, you're going to see a big bond rally and rates are going to come down. The Fed will be following rates down, not leading them."

Hunter also addressed the massive pile of maturing debt that needs to be rolled over, acknowledging that shrinking the Fed's balance sheet will prove extremely difficult given macroeconomic pressures. "Ultimately, I think macro is going to trump any intention to shrink the balance sheet. Between now and that bust, once that bust hits, expanding the balance sheet is going to be the story, not shrinking it."

## Contrarian Outlook: Economic Weakness Meets Market Strength

Perhaps the most counterintuitive element of Hunter's thesis is his "meltup" prediction: despite forecasting economic deterioration and a major bust, he sees markets surging sharply in the near term. "I think we are in that kind of sweet spot where inflation can start rolling over, rates can start coming down, the economy can still seem like it's holding its own," he explained.

During this window, Hunter believes momentum will pull more investors into the market, creating concentrated buying pressure that could push indices up sharply. He dismisses concerns about consumer spending strength, noting that while retail sales appear solid, "under the surface, incomes—not jobs—are not going to be able to prop up spending like that for very long." He points to a deepening "have and have not" economy, with roughly half the population struggling month-to-month while the other half props up economic activity.

Hunter also highlights unprecedented leverage in the system—far greater than during the 2008-09 financial crisis—as the reason this downturn will be far worse than a normal recession. "Because of the leverage in the system, which is far bigger than we've ever had going into a downturn, this is going to be so much bigger than just a normal recession."

## The Consolidation Phase Is Ending

After spending several months consolidating the massive gains from December through January, Hunter believes the correction lows are in. "Those should be your lows for this period, and we come out of that whole consolidation. We come out of it in a very steep way this summer and into the fall."

Miners, he argues, should outperform the underlying metals during this rally phase. "Metals, those are big runs. If you do the math, you're talking about more than a double on silver and a big run-up on gold. I think the miners will outperform that."
