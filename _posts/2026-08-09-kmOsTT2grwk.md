## The Real Constraint: America’s AI Power Problem

Steur raises a critical, underappreciated bottleneck:

- If all planned data centers are built, they could consume roughly half the U.S. power grid by 2030—and we’re already in 2026, nearing 2027.
- The U.S. faces shortages in:
  - Copper
  - Critical minerals (with limited exports from China)
  - Skilled labor (electricians, construction workers)
- Costs are spiking: some electricians working on data centers are reportedly earning as much as $400,000 a year.
- Meanwhile, China is building nuclear capacity and appears structurally better positioned in the short term to support AI energy demand.

His concern:

- U.S. AI companies face rising tokenization and compute costs, while Chinese alternatives could be 50% cheaper with similar accuracy.
- If major firms consider shifting workloads to China’s AI infrastructure, it creates a policy and competitiveness dilemma for the U.S. government.
