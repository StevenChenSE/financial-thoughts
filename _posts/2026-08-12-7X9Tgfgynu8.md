### Momentum Structural Analysis reveals a looming debt crisis and the next explosive phase for precious metals.

In a recent interview, Michael Oliver, founder of Momentum Structural Analysis (MSA), shared his distinctive perspective on the global financial landscape. With over 50 years of experience, Oliver’s approach focuses on momentum rather than price, covering stocks, bonds, commodities, and currencies.

Oliver argues that we are currently at a critical inflection point. While many view recent market movements as weakness, his analysis suggests that gold, silver, and the broader financial system are signaling a major monetary event is underway.

## The Bizarre Correction in Gold and Silver

The conversation begins by addressing the sharp drop in monetary metals seen on the last day of January and the first trading day of February. Oliver describes this as a bizarre phenomenon he has never seen in market history. Although major tops were formed, the speed and nature of the decline appeared unnatural.

Since then, gold and silver have been "teasing" lower. Every time they made a new low below the early February levels, the move was marginal before reversing higher. Oliver interprets this not as weakness, but as a solid foundation.

"Most of the damage was done in 48 hours," Oliver explains. Since February, significant buying has occurred around $4,000 for gold and in the $50s-$60s range for silver. While most market participants have viewed the subsequent rallies as selling opportunities, Oliver believes the current rally is different.

According to MSA’s momentum analysis, signals were generated last week when silver moved above approximately $58. Oliver predicts this rally will sustain and mark the beginning of the next phase in the gold and silver advance—a phase expected to be far more explosive and vertical than the previous one.

## A Global Government Debt Crisis

Oliver emphasizes that the current environment is not just a standard bull market for gold and silver. It is driven by a deeper, more severe underlying issue: a government debt crisis.

This crisis has been building decade by decade, regardless of who is in power. As Jamie Dimon recently noted, we are now facing a situation where the debt must be dealt with, yet it remains off the radar of mainstream financial media.

Japan is already deep in this crisis, impacting the yen. The U.S. Federal Reserve has intervened to support the yen to prevent Japan from dumping U.S. bonds, effectively creating a scenario where "we're printing money to help them print money."

Gold and silver are reacting to this ongoing degradation in the value of fiat money and the perception of government debt markets. This is a massive event that is not being discussed openly. Consequently, we are seeing a shift in asset flows out of traditional classes and into precious metals.

## The Erosion of the 60/40 Portfolio

For the last 50 years, the standard portfolio allocation rule was 60% stocks and 40% bonds. The logic was simple: if stocks looked uncertain, bonds were the safe alternative. However, Oliver argues that the government bond market is no longer a viable alternative.

The bond market continues to weaken, with rising rates and the Fed buying bonds since November to provide liquidity. The Fed’s unwritten mandate is to save the government’s bond market, as the consequences of it failing would be catastrophic.

This relentless printing increases the money supply. Oliver points to the M2 chart on the Federal Reserve St. Louis site as evidence of a "ballistic bull market in the quantity of dollars."

The impact of this monetary expansion is clear in real-world assets:
*   When your grandfather built a house, it cost around $4,500.
*   When your father built one, it cost about $45,000.
*   Today, the median home price is around $450,000.

This reflects the degradation of the money unit. Similarly, the S&P 500’s gains since the 2000 dot-com high have merely matched the increase in the money supply.

Oliver warns that once the stock market shakes and enters a bear market (defined as a 10-20% drop), average investors will feel pain and seek refuge. That is when significant additional flows into monetary metals will occur.

## Vulnerability in the Stock Market

Oliver highlights a specific vulnerability in the U.S. stock market. The S&P 500 is heavily weighted by about 10 major stocks. While the index trades around 7,700, Oliver’s momentum studies identify critical trigger levels.

According to MSA’s long-term momentum triggers, the S&P 500 should not drop to the 7,100 area. If it does, it breaks a major trend structure.

On a price chart, 7,000 might look like support based on previous trading ranges. However, momentum charts reveal that if the index returns to this level, "momentum will be blowing major bridges out from under it." A breakdown here would hasten the flow of money into gold and silver.

## The Case for Gold Miners

Oliver also discusses the performance of gold miners, particularly the GDX (gold miners ETF) and the XAU (Philadelphia Gold and Silver Index).

Historically, the value of XAU relative to the price of an ounce of gold averaged about 25% for decades (from the 1980s through 2008). However, during the gold bear market from 2008 to 2015, this spread collapsed to just 4%.

For the last 12 years, the spread has traded sideways between 4% and 8.5%. It is now pushing toward the top end of that range. Oliver argues that the miners are vastly undervalued.

"If you were just an average technician, you'd look at that action and say that's a base," Oliver says. "You're approaching that ceiling again and I think you're going to go through this time."

When this spread breaks out, miners are expected to explode in value relative to gold, potentially doubling or tripling their current valuation. This event would coincide with vertical price action in all monetary metals. Oliver advises that while gold is the "mama," investors should allocate money to miners because they will outperform.

Silver is similarly undervalued. Currently, silver is about 1.5% of the price of gold, compared to 6.5% during the 1980 peak. Its spread action suggests it is also about to explode.

## The Yen, the Dollar, and the Bond Crisis

The Japanese yen remains a critical factor. Oliver’s momentum analysis suggests the recent plunge in the yen may have been the low. If the yen stabilizes, the U.S. dollar could weaken relative to it.

The U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) is heavily weighted by the euro and yen, which make up over 70% of the index. While the yen’s weakness has kept the DXY from collapsing, Oliver’s momentum work indicates the index has broken major structures and is in a congested holding pattern.

"When it breaks down, the dollar index will be weak," Oliver states. This shift would surprise many who view the dollar as strong, further highlighting the weakness in fiat currencies.

Meanwhile, the 30-year Treasury bond futures have been trying to base since the 2022 collapse. Oliver believes the base will not hold and that the low end (around 108) will be broken. The Fed cannot control long-term bonds with a push of a button, but they must print to prevent the debt market from catching fire. This printing is the lifeblood fueling gold and silver.

## A Widespread Crisis and Political Fragmentation

Oliver warns that the repercussions of this crisis will be widespread, affecting not just investors but the general public. Job losses, home foreclosures, and economic hardship could mirror or exceed the 2008 crisis.

Stock markets in the U.S. and Japan are viewed as bubbles. In contrast, markets like the Shanghai Composite are not considered bubbles by Oliver’s metrics, having risen far less than their U.S. counterparts since 2009.

Beyond economics, Oliver predicts significant political and societal changes. The two-party system in the U.S. is fragmenting, with both major parties losing coherence. Third parties may ascend. Furthermore, there may be a fundamental rethink of fiat currencies and the role of the Federal Reserve.

"I don't think governments are going to solve it," Oliver concludes. "I think this one is the bigger one... I think a lot of changes will occur in things we take as norms."

## Conclusion

When asked if the situation will worsen gradually or resolve quickly, Oliver expects a rapid unfolding of events. The key trigger to watch is the explosion in the miners versus gold spread.

"When that spread breaks out, I'm arguing they will explode and that will not happen in a vacuum," Oliver says. "You'll have peripheral headlines suddenly bang front and right in your face... I think it will unfold very rapidly and I think by the end of the year we will say, 'Yep, it's underway.'"
