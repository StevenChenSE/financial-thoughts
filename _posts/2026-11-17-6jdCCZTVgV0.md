In a recent episode of *Capital Cosm*, host Danny sat down with renowned economist and gold advocate Peter Schiff to discuss the precarious state of the U.S. economy. Recorded on November 16, 2026, the conversation centered on Schiff’s stark warning that Americans are on the brink of a significant financial crisis characterized by bank failures, foreclosures, and a collapse in asset prices. With the national debt surging past $40 trillion and inflation proving sticky despite Federal Reserve actions, Schiff contends that the current market stability is an illusion before a major correction.

## A Broad Market Sell-Off

The interview opened with Danny noting the widespread weakness across financial markets. On the day of the recording, major indices were bleeding: the Dow Jones was down 0.5%, the S&P 500 fell over 1%, and the Nasdaq dropped 1.5%. Commodities were also struggling, with gold down 6%, silver down 5%, and oil down 2%. Bitcoin had fallen approximately 1%, while the dollar remained slightly up—a move Schiff criticized as a misconception among traders who fail to understand the underlying monetary dynamics.

Schiff viewed the decline in precious metals not as a trend reversal, but as a buying opportunity. He noted that gold was trading around $3,850 and silver below $47, levels he identified as potential yearly lows. According to Schiff, the market is incorrectly pricing in the efficacy of Federal Reserve rate hikes while ignoring accelerating inflation.

## The Inflation Illusion and Fed Policy

A central theme of the discussion was the disconnect between monetary policy and real-world inflation. Schiff argued that despite recent hikes by the Federal Reserve, led by Kevin Warsh, monetary policy remains effectively loose. He pointed out that the Fed had only raised rates by 25 basis points in the preceding six months, a pace far slower than the rise in year-over-year inflation.

"Objectively, the Fed has got a looser monetary policy now than it did before that hike," Schiff stated. He predicted that even if the Fed follows through with additional hikes—such as 50 basis points in December and 25 in January—it will fail to slow the "inflation train." By late 2027, Schiff forecasted inflation could reach 7%, while the Fed funds rate might only sit around 4.5% to 4.75%. This results in deeply negative real interest rates, which historically favors hard assets like gold and silver over cash and bonds.

## Political Shifts and Fiscal Irresponsibility

Schiff analyzed the recent midterm election results, where Republicans suffered significant losses despite controlling the presidency. He attributed this defeat to the worsening cost of living crisis. "The problem that caused voters to vote Trump and Republicans... got worse, not better," Schiff explained. Voters felt abandoned by an administration that denied economic struggles while boasting about a booming economy.

Looking ahead to 2028, Schiff predicted a Democratic sweep, including the White House and a filibuster-proof Senate majority of at least 60 seats. This political landscape, he argued, would lead to even more reckless government spending. While Republicans pretend to dislike deficits, Democrats embrace them, leading to a cycle where both parties contribute to ballooning debt. Schiff warned that this fiscal indiscipline is the primary driver of inflation and the affordability crisis.

## The Debt Spiral and Interest Payments

The conversation highlighted the unsustainable trajectory of the U.S. national debt, which Schiff noted would hit $41 trillion shortly and could reach $50 trillion by January 2029. A critical point of concern was the rising cost of servicing this debt. Interest payments on the national debt had already reached $1.4 trillion annually, surpassing defense spending ($1 trillion) and approaching Social Security outlays ($1.7 trillion).

Schiff warned that interest payments could become the single largest line item in the federal budget within a year. As rates rise and debt accumulates, the government faces a "death spiral" where it must borrow more just to pay interest on previous borrowings. This dynamic erodes purchasing power and forces the Federal Reserve to continue printing money, further devaluing the dollar.

## Real Estate and Banking Crisis

Perhaps Schiff’s most alarming prediction concerned the housing market and banking sector. He argued that real estate prices are poised to crash as higher interest rates finally bite into affordability. This collapse would wipe out home equity for baby boomers and trigger a wave of foreclosures.

"There's going to be bank failures, you know, foreclosures, uh, financial crisis," Schiff declared. He specifically pointed to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac, the government-sponsored enterprises that entered conservatorship during the 2008 crisis. Schiff believes their losses are already baked in but unacknowledged, and they will eventually require massive bailouts far exceeding those of the previous cycle. The combination of falling asset values and rising defaults could destabilize regional banks heavily exposed to commercial and residential real estate.

## Crypto Bubble and AI Hype

Schiff offered a skeptical view of two major speculative assets: Bitcoin and AI stocks. Regarding cryptocurrency, he noted that Bitcoin had fallen from its peak of $126,000 to around $64,000. He dismissed the "Strategic Bitcoin Reserve" narrative as political hype, noting that the U.S. government has not actually purchased significant amounts of Bitcoin for the reserve. Instead, he predicted that a future Democratic administration would sell off seized Bitcoin holdings, flipping the narrative from accumulation to liquidation.

On artificial intelligence, Schiff distinguished between the technology and the stock valuations. He believes AI is a genuine innovation with transformative potential, but the market has built a "house of cards" financed by excessive debt. While the science is sound, the corporate structures and valuations supporting the boom are fragile and likely to correct sharply.

## Investment Strategy: Hard Assets Over Paper Wealth

Given his bearish outlook on equities, bonds, and real estate, Schiff reiterated his long-standing recommendation for precious metals. He described gold and silver as essential hedges against government mismanagement and currency debasement. For those with IRAs heavily weighted in bonds—which have lost significant value since 2020—he suggested reallocating into gold and silver to preserve wealth.

Schiff also highlighted the potential of tokenized gold, suggesting that digital representations of physical gold could eventually dominate blockchain applications more than Bitcoin or stablecoins. He views this as a convergence of traditional hard money principles with modern technological efficiency.

## Summary

Peter Schiff’s analysis paints a picture of an economy on the verge of a painful correction driven by decades of fiscal irresponsibility and monetary manipulation. With inflation outpacing interest rate hikes, debt servicing costs exploding, and asset bubbles in housing and tech nearing bursting points, Schiff advises investors to prioritize capital preservation through hard assets like gold and silver. As political shifts promise even more government spending, the window for positioning ahead of the next financial crisis may be closing rapidly.
