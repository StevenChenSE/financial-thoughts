The psychological milestone of $4,000 per ounce has been reached, raising critical questions about the future trajectory of the gold market. Is this a temporary bottom before a collapse toward $3,000, or the foundation for the next leg up? Derek Macpherson, CEO and President of West Point Gold, provides an on-the-ground perspective on the mining industry, the broader commodity cycle, and the specific factors currently supporting gold prices.

## The Paradox of High Rates and Strong Gold

Macpherson notes that the current market environment is "risk-off," characterized by choppy broader markets where only the tech trade seems to be working effectively. Despite rising interest rates—with the 10-year yield north of 5%—and a strong US dollar, gold has held up impressively. Historically, high real interest rates and a strong dollar would be bearish for gold. However, Macpherson argues that gold’s resilience is driven by a structural shift: the slow decline of the US dollar as the world’s reserve currency.

This trend has triggered significant central bank buying. "You can't beat central banks on the buy side," Macpherson states. If they want to hold the price in, they can. This institutional accumulation began two and a half years ago, long before traditional Western buyers entered the market, sparking the initial bull run that peaked near $5,000 earlier this year.

### What Could Pressure Gold Further?

While central bank buying provides a floor, Macpherson identifies specific headwinds that could push prices lower:
1.  **Continued Rising Interest Rates:** If yields climb higher, the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like gold increases.
2.  **Geopolitical Resolution:** A resolution in conflicts such as those in Iran could reduce inflationary pressures and geopolitical risk premiums, negatively impacting gold demand.

Despite these risks, Macpherson believes the underlying trend of de-dollarization remains intact, providing a "put option" for gold prices.

## Gold’s Evolving Role in Portfolios

The function of gold in an investment portfolio has shifted significantly over recent decades. In the past, it was primarily a hedge against specific currency weaknesses. Today, with governments becoming substantially more fiscally undisciplined and inflation persistently running above targets (around 3% versus 5% yields), gold serves as a broader hedge against systemic economic instability.

Macpherson suggests that investors who do not currently hold a 5-10% allocation in gold should reconsider their stance, as the fundamental reasons for holding it—protection against high debt spending and inflation—are actively playing out.

## West Point Gold: Exploration Milestones and Strategy

Amidst this macroeconomic backdrop, West Point Gold is focusing on its flagship asset, the **Gold Chain Project** in northwest Arizona. The company has completed approximately 30,000 meters of drilling and is preparing to release its maiden resource estimate.

### Key Assets and Targets
*   **Tyro Deposit:** The primary driver of the maiden resource. It features high-grade mineralization at shallow depths, with impressive continuity. Notable results include hole WH148 intersecting 66m at 6.5 g/t gold (true width ~40m) at 250m depth, and hole WH49 intersecting 30m at 9 g/t gold just 150m above it.
*   **Satellite Targets:** Additional prospects include Black Dyke, Sheep Trail, Northeast Tyro, Bull 8, and Frisco Graben. These are expected to support a hub-and-spoke processing model centered on Tyro.
*   **Nevada Assets:** The company holds three projects in Nevada, including **Jefferson Canyon**, which is partnered with Kinross Gold. Kinross has recently obtained permits and begun drilling. Under the option agreement, Kinross can earn up to 80% ownership by paying West Point a total of $10 million plus funding exploration expenditures.

### Capital Management and Dilution
Managing capital efficiently is crucial for junior miners. Macpherson highlights that timing financings is key to minimizing dilution. West Point successfully raised $25 million in January under favorable terms (straight shares, no warrants), allowing them to fund the current 30,000-meter drill program without needing immediate additional capital.

Recently, 5.5 million warrants from a previous financing have exercised, bringing in significant cash while slightly diluting share count. Macpherson views this as a positive outcome of early-stage risk-taking by initial investors, noting that the company is now benefiting from its success. Institutional ownership has increased significantly, with Manek Capital becoming the largest shareholder after leading a 2025 financing.

## Jurisdictional Advantages: Arizona and Nevada

Both Arizona and Nevada consistently rank high in Fraser Institute surveys for mining investment attractiveness. This ranking is based on operational feasibility rather than just geology. These jurisdictions host major operations like Freeport’s copper mines in Arizona and Nevada Gold Mines, demonstrating that despite permitting challenges, it is possible to operate at scale.

Macpherson emphasizes that while these areas have been explored for over 150 years, significant gold remains undiscovered due to historical interruptions (such as the War Powers Act during WWII halting non-critical mining) and the fact that many deposits were only economically viable after the end of the fixed gold standard.
