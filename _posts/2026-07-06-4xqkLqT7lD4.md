## David Morgan analyzes the futility of gold revaluation, exposes options manipulation, and provides critical price targets for gold and silver.

In a July 2026 interview with Capital Cosm, David Morgan of the Morgan Report delivered a detailed assessment of the precious metals market, addressing widespread speculation regarding gold revaluation, unusual options activity, and macroeconomic risks. With gold trading near $4,187 and silver around $62, Morgan emphasized that while the market remains in a "limbo" trading range, the long-term bull thesis for metals remains intact. He warned investors to look past political noise and focus on fundamental valuation, structural debt issues, and key technical levels.

### The Gold Revaluation Math: A Drop in the Bucket

Rumors have circulated regarding a potential gold revaluation by the Treasury to address the United States' $40 trillion debt burden. Morgan conducted a rigorous mathematical analysis to demonstrate why this solution is insufficient. The Treasury holds approximately 261.5 million ounces of gold. At current prices above $4,000, this reserves value is roughly $1 trillion. Even if gold were revalued to $20,000 per ounce, the total value would rise to $5 trillion.

Morgan argued that $5 trillion against $40 trillion in debt improves the debt-to-equity ratio but does not solve the structural problem. The U.S. faces fixed obligations for Social Security, military spending, and interest payments that are unchangeable and increasing due to demographics. Revaluation does not reduce these outflows. Morgan cited the perspective of Judy Sheldon, noting that true fiscal order requires cutting government departments, returning functions to private industry, and enforcing strict fiscal restraint, rather than relying on asset revaluation to balance the books.

### The Options Bet Mirage: Manipulation Over Signal

Market participants have noted significant volume in call options for gold with strike prices of $20,000 and $30,000 by year-end, fueling speculation of an imminent revaluation. Morgan dismissed this as likely manipulation rather than a genuine signal. He pointed out that the volume consists of 30,000 contracts trading at a penny per contract, representing a minimal financial outlay.

These options consist almost entirely of time premium with negligible intrinsic value. Morgan explained that sophisticated traders are buying time, betting on volatility or news events that could inflate the option prices without gold ever reaching the strike price. He warned that this activity is designed to manipulate the mindset of unsophisticated investors, creating a false narrative of insider knowledge. If gold were to move significantly, even to $5,000, these options would gain value due to leverage, allowing the holders to profit without the revaluation ever occurring. Morgan advised treating these bets as noise rather than evidence of policy shifts.

### Market Outlook: Trading Ranges and Buying Zones

Morgan described the current market as a long-duration trading range. For gold, he identified a range with a lower bound near $3,900 and emphasized that prices below $4,000 constitute a buying zone. For silver, the range is wider, extending from $52 to $90, with prices under $60 marking a prime accumulation opportunity.

Seasonality plays a crucial role in Morgan's outlook. He expects sideways movement through the summer, noting that August is traditionally the seasonal low for metals. Investors should anticipate dips below $4,000 for gold and $60 for silver one or twice during July and August, offering strategic entry points. Morgan remains bullish on mining stocks, which have recently shown resilience by holding firm while metals declined. He views mining equities as undervalued relative to metal prices and likely to find their bottom first, often serving as a leading indicator for metal price moves.

### Fed Chair Worsh and Inflation Manipulation

The interview also covered the agenda of new Fed Chair Worsh, who has signaled intentions to revamp how inflation is measured. Morgan expressed skepticism, suggesting Worsh aims to implement a new calculation methodology that will artificially lower reported inflation. This could involve aggressive use of hedonic indexing and substitution effects, such as replacing premium items with cheaper alternatives in the basket, thereby masking true cost-of-living increases.

Morgan highlighted that if the Fed successfully lowers the reported inflation rate through statistical manipulation, they can justify maintaining interest rates without acknowledging the real erosion of purchasing power. He referenced Shadow Stats, run by John Williams, which calculates inflation using older methodologies from the 1980s and 1990s. Shadow Stats indicates that real inflation is likely double the official figure, potentially reaching 8% to 9% compared to the reported 4%. This divergence underscores the importance of holding hard assets as a hedge against currency debasement.

### Critical Risks: Yen Carry Trade and Geopolitics

Morgan identified the narrowing spread between the U.S. 10-year Treasury yield and the Japanese 10-year yield as a critical risk factor tied to the yen carry trade. The differential has compressed from a peak of 4.2% to approximately 1.7%. Morgan warned that if this spread breaks below 0.5%, it could trigger a significant market event, as it approaches a technical double-bottom formation. A collapse in this spread would force unwinding of carry trades, potentially causing volatility across global markets.

Regarding geopolitical risks, such as a potential restart of war in Iran, Morgan noted that historical data shows precious metals often sell off at the onset of conflict. Countries facing liquidity pressures may liquidate gold reserves to fund military expenditures, as seen with Turkey. While counterintuitive, this dynamic can suppress prices in the short term. Morgan also downplayed recent large gold deliveries from COMX, calculating that the volume represented only about one-twelfth of 1% of the total global gold supply, which is held primarily by central banks.

### Conclusion and Price Targets

Despite near-term headwinds and manipulation attempts, Morgan remains firmly bullish on the long-term trajectory of gold and silver. He advised investors to maintain exposure to metals as a hedge against inflation and currency instability, recommending a strategy of trimming positions during parabolic moves but never selling out completely.

Looking ahead to the fall, Morgan set specific targets to confirm the continuation of the bull run. He expects gold to reach the $4,500 to $5,000 range and silver to move above $75 by September through November. Achieving these levels would validate his thesis that the market is not yet at the top. Morgan concluded by reminding investors that gold and silver serve as the money of last resort, essential for preserving wealth in an environment of persistent fiscal deficits and manipulated economic data.
