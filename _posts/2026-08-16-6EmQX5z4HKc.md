## Introduction
In a compelling interview, Mark Skousen, a macroeconomist strategist at the Oxford Club and author of *The Greatest American*, warns that the United States has entered an era of permanent inflation. Skousen, who is also the editor of the *Scousin Report*, provides a detailed analysis of the current economic landscape, arguing that traditional measures of price stability are no longer reliable. He outlines the structural shifts that have made inflation a permanent feature of the modern economy and identifies key assets investors should consider to survive the coming monetary challenges.

## The Era of Permanent Inflation
Skousen contends that the post-World War II economic model, which was characterized by periods of inflation followed by deflation, has fundamentally changed. He references a chart by Harvard economists Reinhart and Rogoff, which illustrates a "hockey stick" rise in inflation since 1945. Several factors have contributed to this shift:
*   **Monetary Policy:** The Federal Reserve has shifted from a mandate of price stability to a target of 2% inflation, effectively normalizing price increases.
*   **Fiscal Policy:** The adoption of Keynesian economics has led to chronic deficit spending, even during economic booms.
*   **Global Currency Status:** The 1944 Bretton Woods agreement established the US dollar as the world's reserve currency, allowing the US to export its monetary inflation.

Skousen notes that while the Consumer Price Index (CPI) may show declining rates, the "real" cost of living remains high. He cites *The Economist's* "Barbecue Index," which suggests that a typical July 4th meal for four has increased by 12% year-over-year, a figure that far exceeds official CPI reports.

## The Debt Crisis and Potential Monetary Shock
A central theme of the interview is the looming threat of a fiscal crisis driven by the US national debt, which is approaching $40 trillion. Skousen explains that the US Treasury must refinance roughly $7 trillion in debt annually. He warns that if global investors lose confidence in US Treasuries, the government may be forced to raise interest rates dramatically to attract buyers.

He draws a parallel to the 1992 Swedish crisis, where interest rates spiked to 500% to defend the currency. In such a scenario, Skousen suggests that gold and Bitcoin would be the primary havens for investors. He points out that central banks are currently buying gold at a record pace, now holding more gold than US Treasuries, which signals a shift in global monetary policy toward hard assets.

## The "Roaring 20s" of Technology
Despite his warnings on inflation and debt, Skousen remains bullish on the US stock market, particularly the technology sector. He compares the current era to the "Roaring 20s," but driven by technology, AI, and space exploration rather than industrialization. He argues that the NASDAQ is following a similar trajectory to its rise in the 1990s and is currently only halfway through its bull market, potentially lasting until 2029 or 2030.

However, he cautions that this boom is fueled by easy money policies implemented during the pandemic. He likens the market to a field of "wheat and chaff," where legitimate technological growth (wheat) is mixed with speculative bubbles (chaff) created by fiat money. He believes that a correction is inevitable, likely triggered by the Federal Reserve tightening monetary policy to combat inflation, similar to how rate hikes in 1999 and 2007 preceded major market corrections.

*   **Dividend Stocks:** He highlights Main Street Capital (MAIN) as a favorite income stock, noting it is the only company that pays both monthly and quarterly dividends.
*   **Technology ETFs:** He points to the XLK ETF as a way to capture the ongoing growth in the tech sector.

Skousen also emphasizes the importance of looking beyond GDP to "Gross Output," a metric that measures total spending at all stages of production. He argues that Gross Output is a better leading indicator of economic health and has recently shown signs of recovery, suggesting that the supply chain is finally stabilizing after being impacted by tariffs and trade wars.
