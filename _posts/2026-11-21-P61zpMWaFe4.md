Anthropic, the company behind the AI model Claude, made history this week with what is described as the largest Initial Public Offering (IPO) ever. The company raised approximately $93.5 billion, surpassing the previous record set by SpaceX earlier in the summer. However, the debut has been marked by intense price discovery, significant volatility, and growing questions about whether a $2 trillion valuation can be sustained given current financial realities.

## The IPO Performance: From Premium to Correction

Anthropic priced its shares at $83 on NASDAQ under the ticker **NT**. Trading began on Thursday with an opening price of $111.50, representing a 34% premium over the IPO price. However, by Friday, shares had fallen approximately 13% from their opening level, trading around $85–$97 depending on the specific intraday moment cited.

This fluctuation has sparked debate among investors and analysts. Is this merely normal price discovery, or are markets beginning to question the fundamental valuation? The stock’s movement mirrors the pattern seen with SpaceX’s recent IPO, where initial excitement gave way to consolidation as the market sought a clearing price.

## Insider Selling and Lockup Periods

A primary concern for new shareholders is the potential supply overhang from insiders and early institutional investors. While most Anthropic shareholders have agreed not to sell for up to 180 days, specific provisions allow employees, executives, and directors to sell up to 350 million shares starting on the first day of trading.

Will Ryan, an expert joining the discussion, noted that while there is no immediate evidence of heavy insider selling in the first two days, the predefined lockup expirations are well-telegraphed. As these periods end in the coming weeks and quarters, investors should watch for increased volume from insiders who acquired shares at much lower price points.

## Financial Reality: Explosive Growth vs. Massive Losses

Anthropic’s financials present a stark contrast between top-line growth and bottom-line reality.
*   **Revenue Growth:** The company has achieved the fastest revenue ramp in history for a public company. Full-year revenue reached exactly $33 billion, up from just under $5 billion in 2025.
*   **Net Losses:** Despite this growth, Anthropic recorded $104 billion in net losses. While much of this is attributed to non-cash expenses and revaluations, the magnitude has raised concerns about sustainability.

The core investment thesis rests entirely on future projections. With a $2 trillion valuation against $33 billion in revenue, traditional multiples do not apply. Investors are effectively discounting future growth to today’s price, betting that Anthropic will continue its extraordinary trajectory.

## Strategic Partnerships and the "Bank of Nvidia"

The IPO was heavily anchored by strategic investments from hyperscalers and tech giants. Notably, **Nvidia** has shown interest in purchasing an additional $10 billion worth of shares post-IPO. This move is not about vertical integration but rather extending financing to a key customer, similar to Microsoft’s early support for OpenAI.

However, the landscape is shifting. With Jensen Huang stepping back from certain leadership roles at Nvidia, the company is becoming more selective in its financing partnerships. This "Bank of Nvidia" approach means that while top-tier companies like Anthropic will continue to receive capital and compute resources, smaller neoclouds and less established AI firms may face tighter credit conditions.

## New Investment Vehicles: Leveraged ETFs

To cater to the high volatility surrounding Anthropic, **Granite Shares** is launching two new Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) on Monday:
1.  **AIL (2x Long Anthropic):** Provides 200% leverage to daily gains.
2.  **ANS (2x Short Anthropic):** Provides 200% leverage to daily losses.

Unlike some competitors that use options to achieve leverage—which can suffer from decay—Granite Shares utilizes swap contracts with major investment banks to precisely replicate the daily return. This structure allows for more accurate tracking but requires careful management of counterparty risk.

## Market Cycle and Future IPOs

The concentration of mega-IPOs in the tech sector has led some to wonder if we are at a market top. Ryan argues against this view, suggesting that companies like Anthropic and SpaceX are going public primarily out of necessity to raise capital on a scale that private markets can no longer support. The total commitments for AI infrastructure are estimated around $250 billion, forcing these entities into the deeper liquidity pools of public markets.

The success of Anthropic’s capital raise—oversubscribed and exceeding expectations—is likely a positive signal for other potential IPOs, including **OpenAI**. While OpenAI has not announced a specific date, its decision to go public will likely be influenced by how well Anthropic performs post-IPO and whether the market appetite for AI stocks remains robust.

## Key Risks and Catalysts

For investors looking to position themselves long or short on Anthropic, several factors will drive future performance:

*   **Bull Case:** Sustained adoption of AI, ability to meet compute demand, and maintaining strong margins despite heavy infrastructure costs.
*   **Bear Case:** Commoditization of Large Language Models (LLMs) leading to price wars, inability to secure sufficient compute resources, regulatory headwinds, and broader macroeconomic slowdowns affecting AI spending.

As the market digests Anthropic’s debut, the interplay between insider selling, Nvidia’s strategic moves, and the new leveraged ETFs will define the near-term trajectory of one of the world’s most valuable companies.
