## Clem Chambers on AI Infrastructure, Gold, and the Investment Shift

**Introduction**

In a recent market discussion, Clem Chambers, founder of Newf, joined host David Lin to dissect the current volatility in tech stocks, the pullback in AI-related assets, and the broader geopolitical implications for gold, oil, and crypto. Chambers argues that while sentiment is shifting, the underlying fundamentals of the AI infrastructure boom remain robust, offering significant long-term opportunities for investors who look beyond short-term noise.

## Why AI Infrastructure Still Wins

Chambers emphasizes that the current market confusion stems from a misunderstanding of Artificial Intelligence. Many investors believe AI is solely about large language models (LLMs) and software companies like OpenAI or Anthropic. However, Chambers contends that AI is fundamentally a hardware play.

"Elon Musk didn't buy empty trillion zillion dollars worth of Nvidas because he thinks the models are important," Chambers explains. "It's you got to have computers to run it."

He points out that the real value lies in the infrastructure required to power AI: chips, CPUs, hard drives, air conditioning, cables, networks, and the physical buildings housing them. While software models may face valuation questions, the demand for the physical equipment to run them is insatiable. Chambers advises investors to focus on the hardware suppliers—companies like HP, Cisco, and those in the memory, cooling, and construction sectors—rather than just the model developers.

## Gold, Oil, and the Iran War

The discussion shifts to precious metals and energy, particularly in light of reignited tensions with Iran. Chambers offers a contrarian view on gold's performance during wartime. He argues that "gold is for war," but with a specific nuance: investors buy gold *before* a war starts as a hedge, driving prices up. Once the war begins, nations must sell gold reserves to fund military operations, causing prices to drop.

"Gold is for war. We're going to have a war. We better buy gold. Up it goes. Oh, we're in a war. We better start selling it to buy stuff... Gold goes down," Chambers states.

Regarding oil, which has risen to around $81 per barrel amid attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, Chambers suggests the market is signaling that this is not yet a "big hairy deal." He predicts a "siege" scenario where alternative pipelines and routes are rapidly developed, reducing reliance on the strait over time. He advises investors to listen to the numbers rather than the headlines, noting that oil prices have not spiked to $100, indicating that the supply disruption is currently manageable.

## Where Markets Go From Here

Addressing the recent selloff in semiconductor stocks, which are down 20% since mid-June, Chambers remains bullish on the long-term trajectory. He believes the AI trade is not slowing down; rather, the demand for intelligence is unlimited. "There is no second place," he warns. "They will boil the oceans."

Chambers highlights the democratization of coding through AI, noting that even non-technical individuals can now build software, while skilled programmers can increase their output exponentially. This surge in productivity will drive economic growth, inflation, and further investment in AI infrastructure. He advises investors to take a long-term view, looking for assets that could 10x over the next decade, similar to the internet boom of the 1990s.

## Why Investors Are Too Pessimistic

Chambers criticizes the prevailing pessimism in the media and among the public. Citing a CNBC survey showing high economic anxiety, he dismisses the sentiment as fear-driven noise. "Fear is the mind killer," he says, urging investors to avoid doom-scrolling and instead focus on action.

He draws parallels to historical periods of rapid technological change, such as the steam engine revolution, arguing that those who embrace new technologies thrive while those who resist fall behind. Chambers advocates for an active investment strategy, leveraging AI tools for research while maintaining a positive, forward-looking mindset.

## The Biggest Investment Opportunity of the Next Decade

When asked about the biggest structural shift for investors, Chambers points to the global competition in AI and energy. He notes that China currently has 250% more electricity capacity than the US, a critical advantage in the race for AI dominance. This disparity forces the US and Europe to "go all in" on energy production and technological advancement.

"If America wants to remain America the number one guy on the block, he's got to get his skates on," Chambers says. He believes this will lead to massive economic growth, re-industrialization, and significant investment opportunities in the AI value chain.

## Gold, Silver, and Crypto Outlook

Chambers provides specific price targets for precious metals, suggesting that gold around $3,500 and silver under $50 represent the "roof of the basement." He recommends dollar-cost averaging into these positions over the next two years, anticipating a significant rally in the medium term.

On cryptocurrency, he expresses skepticism about its future as an "anarchist" tool, noting that it has been co-opted by Wall Street and is prone to theft and criminal activity. While he sees potential in stablecoins and new use cases, he warns that the original vision of crypto as independent money has been diluted by institutional integration and security risks.

## Conclusion

Clem Chambers concludes by reiterating his long-term strategy: build a diversified portfolio focused on AI infrastructure, ignore short-term market noise, and remain active and optimistic. He advises investors to look beyond the next week or month and position themselves for the technological and economic shifts that will define the next decade. As he puts it, "In 10 years time, you'll be the smartest kid in your street."
