## Expert Reveals the Bottom and the Path Forward

**Introduction**

In a volatile market characterized by a selloff in tech stocks, flat Bitcoin prices, and geopolitical tensions involving Iran and oil, investors are seeking clarity. Clem Chambers, founder of Newf, joins the discussion to dissect the current market sentiment. He argues that while sentiment is fearful, the underlying trends in artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure present a massive opportunity. Chambers emphasizes that the AI revolution is not about large language models (LLMs) alone, but about the vast hardware ecosystem required to run them.

## Why AI Infrastructure Still Wins

Chambers asserts that the market fundamentally misunderstands AI. Many investors worry about the valuation of companies like OpenAI and Anthropic, fearing that models might become commoditized or given away for free. However, Chambers points out that AI is ultimately a hardware play. "Elon Musk didn't buy empty trillion zillion dollars worth of Nvidas because he thinks the models are important," Chambers explains. "It's you got to have computers to run it."

The real value lies in the equipment: chips, CPUs, hard drives, air conditioning systems, cables, networks, and the physical buildings that house them. As demand for intelligence grows without limit, the infrastructure supporting it will "go off the dial." Investors can buy into this value chain through companies like Dell, Cisco, hard drive manufacturers, memory suppliers, and even financial institutions like Goldman Sachs that facilitate these massive build-outs. Chambers predicts that this realization will fully sink in by September, driving significant growth in these sectors.

## Gold, Oil & the Iran War

The discussion turns to commodities, specifically gold and oil, in the context of the ongoing conflict in Iran. Chambers offers a contrarian view on gold, stating, "Gold is for war." He explains that before a war, central banks buy gold as a hedge, driving prices up. Once a war begins, countries must sell gold to fund military operations, causing prices to drop. Currently, gold is under pressure because nations involved in the conflict are liquidating reserves.

Regarding oil, prices have rebounded to around $81 per barrel due to attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz. However, Chambers argues that the market is telling us this is not a "big hairy deal." If the situation were critical, oil would spike to $200 a barrel. The current price suggests inconvenience rather than catastrophe. He views the situation as a "siege," where the adversary is being starved out, rendering the Strait of Hormuz less relevant over time as alternative pipelines are built.

## Market Sentiment and the K-Shaped Economy

Addressing the widespread pessimism reflected in recent surveys, Chambers dismisses media-driven fear as a "mind killer." He compares the current era to 1915, noting that life expectancy has improved dramatically, yet people are more depressed than ever. He advocates for an active, optimistic approach to investing. "No pessimist ever got rich," he states.

Chambers describes the economy as "K-shaped," where the active participants who engage with new technologies like AI will thrive, while the passive observers who remain stuck in negativity will fall behind. He encourages investors to look beyond short-term news cycles and focus on long-term structural shifts. The media often reports doom and gloom, but this is a "pestilence" that investors should ignore. Instead, they should focus on the breakthrough technologies that are reshaping the world.

Chambers identifies the AI infrastructure build-out as the most significant investment opportunity of the next decade. He draws parallels to the internet boom in the late 1990s and early 2000s. Just as buying Amazon in 1998 or Nvidia in the early days would have yielded massive returns, investing in the AI value chain today offers similar potential.

He advises investors to "stake out the AI infrastructure" and build a diversified portfolio across the entire chain, from LLMs down to the physical hardware and financial services. This is a long-term play, potentially spanning 10 to 20 years. The key is to dollar-cost average into these positions, ignoring short-term volatility. "Don't watch tomorrow's news or next week's news," Chambers advises. "Find the positions for the next 10 years and stake them out."

On precious metals, Chambers sees gold near $3,500 as the "roof of the basement." He suggests buying ounces under $3,500 and silver under $50, building positions slowly over the next couple of years. He expects a boring consolidation period before a significant move higher.
