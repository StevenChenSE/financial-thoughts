## The Announcement That Shocked the Internet

In an earnings call that sent crypto Twitter into a frenzy, Michael Saylor announced that Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy) was prepared to sell Bitcoin to fund its "stretch" credit dividends if necessary. The announcement seemed to contradict Saylor's famous mantra: "never sell your Bitcoin."

But according to Saylor, the market misunderstood the announcement entirely.

"The most important thing is we want the market to understand that Bitcoin capital gains fund stretch credit dividends," Saylor explained during a joint interview with tech investor Bonnie Cheung. "When we sell STRC credit, we sell a million dollars of it, we turn around, we buy a million dollars of Bitcoin. We expect Bitcoin to appreciate about 30% a year."

The confusion stemmed from a fundamental misunderstanding of Strategy's business model—one that Saylor has now clarified with remarkable precision.

---

## The Core Business Model: Digital Capital, Digital Credit

### How the System Works

At its foundation, Strategy operates like a sophisticated real estate development company, but with Bitcoin as its underlying asset:

1. **Raise capital** by issuing credit instruments (STRC) or equity (MSTR)
2. **Acquire Bitcoin** with that capital
3. **Allow Bitcoin to appreciate** (historically at 30-40% annually)
4. **Strip the first ~11% of capital gains** as a credit dividend
5. **Continue accumulating** more Bitcoin than ever sold

"Think of it like a real estate development company," Saylor illustrated. "They raise capital by issuing credit instruments, buy land at $10,000 an acre, develop it, it's worth $100,000 an acre, and then monetize that appreciation. Nobody questions a real estate development company that makes a capital investment with credit income. What we're doing is the same thing with Bitcoin."

### The Dividend Funding Question

For much of Strategy's history, the company sold common equity to fund dividends. While MSTR equity typically trades at a premium to Bitcoin as a derivative instrument, skeptics began questioning whether this approach was sustainable—and whether it meant the Bitcoin on the balance sheet had "zero value."

"If you had $65 billion worth of something, and people wanted to value it at zero, it's not very good, right?" Saylor acknowledged. "We don't want the credit rating agencies to think the company has zero dollars of assets. We want the credit rating agencies to think we have $65 billion of assets."

The solution? Make it explicit that Strategy can sell Bitcoin itself to fund dividends—not just equity.

## "Never Be a Net Seller of Bitcoin"

### Reframing the Mantra

Saylor's famous "never sell your Bitcoin" proclamation has become gospel in crypto circles. But Saylor himself admits the phrasing was imprecise.

"I'm very famous for saying never sell your Bitcoin, and that's why the internet went crazy when we said we might sell it," Saylor admitted. "If I was being more precise, I'd say never be a net seller of Bitcoin. It just wouldn't have been so viral or so catchy to say never be a net seller of Bitcoin."

The distinction is crucial: Strategy may sell Bitcoin, but only while simultaneously buying substantially more.

"In these periods, even if we were to sell one Bitcoin, we'd be buying 10 to 20 more Bitcoin. So, you're really talking about a situation where we buy 10 Bitcoin, sell one Bitcoin, buy nine net Bitcoin, and continue to accrete Bitcoin."

### The Math Behind the Strategy

Saylor broke down the mechanics with specific numbers:

- **Number one Bitcoin accretion engine**: STRC (Stretch) credit
- **April STRC issuance**: $3.2 billion → converted to $3.2 billion in Bitcoin
- **Monthly dividend obligation**: approximately $80-90 million
- **Example ratio**: When raising $3 billion, they'd need $80-90 million for dividends, effectively buying 30 Bitcoin while selling one

The critical metric is the **break-even rate of 2.3%**. This means:

> If Strategy issues STRC equal to just 2.3% of its Bitcoin holdings, it will remain a net buyer of Bitcoin forever—even after selling Bitcoin to pay dividends.

"Currently, we've sold about $5 billion of STRC in the first 4 months of the year," Saylor noted. "We're running on a pace to have an issuance rate of 15 or 20% this year. Since 2.3% is the break-even, you buy 20, you net 18. The company, as long as it grows, is going to buy more Bitcoin than it sells."

## Addressing the Critics: Ponzi Scheme Accusations

### Peter Schiff's Challenge

One of Bitcoin's most vocal critics, Peter Schiff, weighed in on the announcement: "Yesterday Saylor admitted MSTR would sell Bitcoin if needed to pay the dividend on STRC. I think that type of commitment is needed to keep the so-called Ponzi going longer, but my guess is when the time comes he'd suspend the dividend and crash STRC rather than crash Bitcoin."

Saylor's response was direct:

"Peter thinks Bitcoin's a Ponzi scheme. Peter is not really a lover of anything in this space. Bitcoin is digital capital, and we've created a digital treasury company by selling equity and credit instruments to buy capital."

The core rebuttal: If you don't acknowledge Bitcoin as legitimate, you can't acknowledge any derivative on top of it as legitimate. But for those who do understand Bitcoin's value proposition:

"We overcollateralize it. For every $5 of Bitcoin, we sell a dollar of credit. The dollar of credit has a defined yield."

### The Stable Yield Proposition

Saylor emphasized that STRC serves investors who believe in Bitcoin but can't tolerate its volatility:

"There are a lot of people that believe Bitcoin is a legitimate asset. They just can't stand the volatility. They don't want to take money they need for their kids' tuition in the fall and invest it in Bitcoin because they need to pay a bill in 12 weeks. For them, digital credit makes a lot of sense because the principal is protected, it's stable, and then they're getting paid three or four times money market."

## Market Dynamics: Why Strategy Can't Move Bitcoin

### A $50 Billion Daily Market

One interviewer raised an interesting observation: traders have noticed that whenever STRC issues a dividend, the ex-dividend price trades below par for a day or two, then recovers to par—prompting some to front-run Strategy's Bitcoin purchases.

Saylor dismissed concerns about front-running in the Bitcoin market:

"The Bitcoin derivatives market is $50 billion a day. Do I think someone has enough capital to move that market? Not really. Bitcoin is kind of like tech capital squared."

### The Liquidity Reality

Saylor shared specific data points illustrating Bitcoin's depth:

- Strategy has purchased **$100 million of Bitcoin in an hour** without moving the price
- **$200-300 million in an hour** has occasionally moved prices
- A billion dollars represents roughly **1/50th of daily volume**
- "If you talk to traders, they'll say the spot market is $20 billion, but derivatives is $80 billion sometimes. What's $100 million in a $40-50-60 billion dollar bucket? It's a very deep liquid market."

"Bitcoin is the world's deepest, most liquid capital market," Saylor declared. "On the weekend, you want to put a billion-dollar trade on with 20x leverage, you can do it in Bitcoin. You want a billion dollars of credit in an hour, you can do it in Bitcoin."

### What Actually Drives Bitcoin

Rather than company-specific factors, Saylor identified macro forces as the primary drivers:

- Trade wars and geopolitical tensions
- Currency wars and interest rate expectations
- Federal Reserve monetary policy
- Global events (citing the Strait of Hormuz and Iran situation as examples)

"It's flattering to suggest that we're systemically important," Saylor concluded. "I don't think so. Bitcoin has a life of its own, and that's why we have confidence in it—because there is no one actor that's either supporting it or holding it back."

## Digital Credit: The Killer App of Bitcoin

### A Year of Realization

When asked if his thesis on Bitcoin had changed, Saylor's core conviction remained intact—but one realization crystallized over the past 12 months:

"At least one of the killer apps of Bitcoin is digital credit. If the digital capital is the highest-performing capital asset—and it is, right? It's outperforming the S&P by a factor of two to three—then it stands to reason that we can create the highest-performing credit asset on top of that capital asset."

### STRC by the Numbers

The results speak for themselves:

| Metric | STRC Performance |
|--------|------------------|
| Dividend yield | 11.5% |
| Sharpe ratio | 2.5-3.0 |
| Market position | Most liquid preferred stock in entire market |
| Year-over-year dominance | Issued **60% of all preferred stock in the United States** |

For context on those Sharpe ratios:

- **Highest credit instrument Sharpe ratio**: 0.5
- **Highest equity Sharpe ratio** (Nvidia): ~1.7
- **S&P 500 Sharpe ratio**: ~0.85-0.9
- **Best hedge funds**: rarely exceed 2.2

"Digital credit is actually a better risk-adjusted return than every other financial strategy and every publicly trading instrument in the public capital markets," Saylor stated. "Twelve months ago, I couldn't tell you that. But it makes sense: if Bitcoin is the highest-performing capital, convertible bonds backed by Bitcoin become the best-performing convertible bonds, and credit instruments like Stretch become the best-performing preferred stocks."

### The Emergence of Digital Yield Tokens

The innovation isn't limited to institutional credit instruments:

- **Apex**: Created a stable, dollar-pegged yield token that reached $300 million in just 8 weeks
- **Saturn**: Another yield token reaching $110 million in 6 weeks

"There's an explosion of innovation in the digital asset space and the crypto space and the trad-fi space—all being driven by digital credit," Saylor observed. "Bitcoin is the thing that makes digital credit possible."

## The AI Connection: Digital Intelligence Meets Digital Capital

### How AI Enables Digital Credit

When asked about Bitcoin miners pivoting to AI data centers, Saylor drew an unexpected connection:

"We created digital credit with digital intelligence, right? We never could have created Stretch without AI. I used AI to create Strike, Strife, Stride, and Stretch."

The process mirrors physical asset development:

- **Bitcoin = pure financial energy** (digital capital)
- **AI = the tool to machine that capital** into structured credit instruments
- **Output**: A digital credit product with defined risk profile, volatility characteristics, yield, and currency denomination

"If you're a Bitcoin treasury or digital treasury company, the smartest thing you can use is digital intelligence to carve out digital credit from digital capital. And that is the stepping stone—it's like the financial fuel to create digital money and digital yield."

The market for this digital money and yield? **$100 trillion**, according to Saylor.

## Looking Forward: The Compounding Machine

### Capital Flows as Economic Engine

Saylor described the self-reinforcing nature of the system:

"Every time we come up with another $10 billion of capital, we buy the entire year's supply of Bitcoin from miners. If a bank creates $10 billion of credit, that's one turn on the axle. If we sell $10 billion of STRC, that's the second turn on the axle. When $10 billion flows into Bitcoin, that's the third turn on the axle."

With approximately $10-12 billion in annual organic supply from miners (~450 Bitcoin daily), and Strategy capable of absorbing that supply multiple times over, the accumulation thesis remains intact.

### Tactical Flexibility

Perhaps most importantly, Saylor emphasized that Strategy maintains optionality:

- **Will trade STRC for MSTR** (common stock)
- **Will trade BTC for MSTR** if advantageous
- **Will buy back undervalued corporate bonds**
- **Will use whatever asset is most undervalued** to meet obligations

"We don't sell assets that are undervalued. We buy assets that are undervalued, and then we arbitrage whatever the inefficiency is. If the market knows we're going to do that, then the market's going to actually fairly value all those assets."

The result: a self-reinforcing engine where Strategy's actions gradually eliminate mispricings, benefiting all investors across its various instruments.

### The Origin Story

Before closing, Saylor shared how science fiction shaped his trajectory:

"In first grade, my parents gave me 10 cents for every book I read. I had a comic book addiction—comic books cost 25 cents. So I had to read two and a half real books to get one comic book. I was very motivated. I read about 100 books that summer."

Through library visits and voracious reading, Saylor discovered Heinlein, Clarke, and Asimov. One book stood out: Robert A. Heinlein's *Have Space Suit—Will Travel*.

"The story: an alpha male fixes up a spacesuit, gets picked up by a spaceship, gallivants across the universe, saves the human race from bug-eyed monsters, and returns home to receive a full tuition scholarship to MIT."

"I figured if MIT was good enough for the guy that saved the human race, it was probably good enough for me. And so, gosh darn it, I was going to go there."

## Key Takeaways

1. **"Never be a net seller"** is the operative principle. Strategy may sell Bitcoin, but only while buying 10-20x more—the math ensures perpetual net accumulation.

2. **The 2.3% break-even rate** is the most important number. As long as STRC issuance exceeds 2.3% of Bitcoin holdings, the company remains a net buyer of Bitcoin indefinitely.

3. **Digital credit is Bitcoin's killer app**. STRC achieves a 2.5-3.0 Sharpe ratio—three times better than the best traditional credit instruments—making it the highest-performing preferred stock in the entire market.

4. **Bitcoin is too liquid to manipulate**. With $50 billion in daily derivatives volume and global macro factors as primary price drivers, no single entity—including Strategy—can sustainably move the market.

5. **The compounding engine accelerates**. With $10-12 billion in annual organic Bitcoin supply and multiple institutional and retail channels absorbing it multiple times over, the digital capital flywheel continues gaining momentum.

*"The market for digital money and digital yield is a hundred trillion dollars, and it's spreading virally right now."*
