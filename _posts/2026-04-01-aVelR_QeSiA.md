*Based on an exclusive interview with John Fick, Financial Analyst and CEO of FC Consulting Group, for Palisades Gold Radio.*

In a rapidly shifting global landscape marked by geopolitical tensions and supply chain vulnerabilities, John Fick, a seasoned financial analyst with over 30 years of mining experience, is urging investors to look beyond traditional growth sectors. Instead, he is spotlighting "defense metals"—specifically tungsten, antimony, and rare earths—as critical assets poised for permanent growth amid potential global conflicts.

Fick, who serves as the CEO of the FC Consulting Group, recently joined *Palisades Gold Radio* to discuss his bullish outlook on the defense metals sector, the volatility of silver, and emerging opportunities in the energy and helium sectors.

### The "Head Fake" of US-China Cooperation

The conversation began with a retrospective on Fick's predictions from December 2025, when he was bullish on silver, gold, and defense metals. His thesis remains consistent: the geopolitical relationship between the United States and China is fragile.

> "I think that's something also that the average investor doesn't understand is that hey look there could be a short-term pullback. That's your opportunity to buy. I mean like you're not going to get great opportunities to buy this when China controls so much of the production."

Fick describes the recent "Trump and China high five" in November 2025 as a "joke."

> "There's no way the US is going to do business with China at this juncture. That was all, you know, for for optics in my opinion. So, you know, you have to pick a path as an investor and you have to say, was that a was that a real, you know, development or is this just a head fake? And to me, that is a head fake and a buying op when you can see the US at any time saying we're getting along with China. I just don't believe it."

According to Fick, the defense sector—and the metals required to fight wars—will remain protected even during a potential global recession.

> "And I think the defense sector as well as defense metals is going to be somewhat protected from that sell-off because there's just no metal out there."

### Tungsten Supply: A Global Bottleneck

At the heart of Fick's strategy is the scarcity of tungsten. China currently dominates the market, producing approximately **81% of the world's supply**. Furthermore, China's production levels have fluctuated; in 2025, production was lower than in 2024, suggesting a strategic hoarding of resources.

> "Well, China's production in 2025 was lower than 2024. So even they aren't producing as much. They're just keeping it in the ground and they know what to do when they need it, right? The US doesn't have that ability. They don't have the ability to do this."

This scarcity creates a perfect environment for Western companies with existing assets. Fick highlights **Western Star Resources (WSR)** as a prime example.

> "Right now, we're we're heavily focused on tungsten. And the reason is, again, there's no publicly traded companies. I mean, it's it's like 10 to 15 names globally. So, we love Western Star Resources, uh, which is WSR if in the States and WSR in Canada."

Fick discovered that Western Star holds more resources than currently disclosed on their website, offering significant room for expansion without heavy shareholder dilution.

> "They already own properties. So they have a gold property in Canada. They have a Nevada tungsten property, and then there's room for growth."

Currently trading around **45 cents Canadian**, Fick sees potential for significant appreciation.

> "I mean, this is implying that the spot price has gone up exponentially in just 3 months. As of Friday's close, March 27th, the the closing average price was 2975, which is insane because the closing average price January 1 or two was 920."

### Silver: Volatility and the Path Forward

While defense metals are the primary focus, silver remains a major component of Fick's portfolio. Going into January 1st, his firm held an **18% position in silver**, which contributed to an **18% retail gain** and up to **360% in his private account** the previous year.

However, silver markets have experienced extreme volatility. On January 30th, silver prices crashed, dropping **28.5% to 29% net**, and at one point fell over **34%**.

> "JP Morgan has already claimed that they were short on January 30th when the crash happened. And when you have a crash, not a decline, it messes with people's psyche, right? Like, you're not going to see people just, including myself, to be like, 'Okay, sure. I'm just going to put all my chips back in.'"

Fick notes that silver miners are not yet showing the expected leverage against the metal price.

> "I would agree with you that there should be a 2 to 3x kind of leverage in a silver equity on the upside and downside, you know, against the silver metal itself, but we haven't really seen that."

He identifies **$50** as a key support level for silver.

> "If you're in that 50 to 54 range, yeah, you better start watching that chart because if it breaks below 50, it's not good. But if we break below it for a day or two, who cares, right? I mean, it's a volatile metal and and that might happen."

Despite the volatility, Fick sees long-term potential, particularly for companies like **Silver 47**, which possesses over **300 million silver equivalent ounces** and is well-cashed up for drilling.

> "If the silver goes from 70 to 100 again, which I think is very possible this year, you're going to see a company like that go up way more than 30%. because they just have a lot of leverage to a run in silver."

### Rare Earths and Other Critical Minerals

Beyond tungsten and silver, Fick discusses the rare earth sector. While crowded last year, the sector saw a correction due to US-China dynamics and tax laws.

> "It was a crowded trade last year and and the uh news with Trump and China in November just wiped people out and I think that combined with tax laws selling really just put a huge, you know, damper on things in Q4."

Fick highlights **Eastport Critical Minerals (EVI/EVIF)** as a favorite micro-cap play in this space.

> "To be quite honest, I haven't really looked back at too many of those names since then because I've been so busy with gold and silver and tungsten this year. But I will say that uh our favorite name in that sector in the micro cap space is Eastport Critical Minerals."

He also touches upon **Helium Exploration (HHF/HEX)**, a US-based micro-cap company with operations in Montana, noting its deep expertise and potential for growth.

> "We bought the stock after the interview around 30 cents and it's trading at 55 now US. I think it's going a lot higher."

### Energy Sector: Oil and Helium

Fick also analyzes the energy sector, noting that oil prices are expected to remain elevated despite futures curves predicting a drop to **$75 by year-end**.

> "Right, too early to tell right now. I would just point out to people if you're looking at the ETF world, you have to be careful with some of these oil ETFs. Some of them generate K1s, which would not be good for some people's tax situations. Um, but I'm just going to give you a ticker to look at. You know, I think this generates a K1, so be careful, but it's USO."

He points to companies like **Anchor Resources (ANK)** and **NKOF** as promising micro-cap plays with long-term leases and deep teams.

> "And what people don't understand is that it's not like tungsten or copper or gold where you have to, you know, wait five to seven years through a permitting process. when oil comes out of the ground, it flows and you just make money."

### Conclusion

As the interview concludes, Fick emphasizes that while the market may experience short-term pullbacks, these present buying opportunities for investors willing to navigate volatility.

> "So I'm I'm starting to, you know, buy some charts here because you can't wait forever. You're never going to get the perfect entry point. you you just have to look at some of your favorite names on sale and say, 'I have to go buy more of that.'"

With China controlling the majority of critical mineral supplies and the US potentially accelerating permitting processes for domestic projects, the stage is set for a new era of resource-based investment.
