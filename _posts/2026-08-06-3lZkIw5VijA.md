## Positive Chart Signals and Technical Breakdown

Gold and silver have seen significant price action across Asian and European trading sessions, raising the question of whether the six-month bear market is concluding and a new rally is beginning. Analyst Daniel Wilhelmi identifies two key positive signals in the gold charts. First, if current European trading gains hold during U.S. sessions, gold has marked a higher high intraday. Combined with two higher lows, this forms a textbook triple bottom pattern. This structure marks the first sign of a trend change in six months, as gold had previously failed to make higher highs despite higher lows.

Second, if this price action holds on the close, it would represent a new monthly high close. While extended bear markets can see rallies, a monthly high close alongside a triple bottom and a break of the March downtrend is a strong bullish indicator. Additionally, the Relative Strength Index (RSI) shows positive divergence. The RSI is now at 55, above the critical 50 level, indicating buying momentum is gaining traction. This is the highest RSI level seen since March, surpassing the highs of April and May.

## The Critical Resistance Level: 50-Day Moving Average

Despite the bullish signals, a major warning exists. Gold has slammed into the 50-day moving average, currently around $4,160. This level has been the primary driver of the bear market since the breakdown in March led to a waterfall selloff. If gold is rejected at the 50-day MA, profit-taking and short-term shorts could push prices lower, potentially to the $4,370–$4,450 range. However, a break above the 50-day MA would confirm buying dynamics, likely pushing gold to $4,215 and potentially into the $4,320–$4,375 resistance zone. Breaking this level would also reduce the probability of a "W" top pattern and further validate the triple bottom formation.

## The U.S. Dollar Bull Trap and Intermarket Factors

A crucial intermarket factor is the U.S. Dollar Index (DXY). The dollar’s uptrend began on January 27, leading gold’s major selloff on January 29. The recent dollar breakout above its sideways range was a severe bull trap, resulting in a triple fake-out that trapped traders on both sides. The dollar has now broken below its 50-day moving average and is testing the uptrend line from late January.

A break below 99.00 in the DXY would invalidate the 200-day moving average and the uptrend, potentially sending the dollar down to 97.00. Given the negative correlation between gold and the dollar, such a breakdown would provide fundamental fuel for gold to break above its 50-day and eventually 200-day moving averages.

Traders are advised to play the percentages rather than waiting for a guaranteed final panic selloff.
