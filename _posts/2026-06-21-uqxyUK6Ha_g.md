## A Geopolitical Shift That Could Reshape Precious Metal Markets

The geopolitical landscape of the Middle East is undergoing a seismic transformation. An MOU (Memorandum of Understanding) scheduled to be signed between Iran and the United States represents far more than a diplomatic agreement—it signals a fundamental power shift that could have profound implications for gold and silver investors worldwide.

On the Capital Account show, Alasdair Macleod explained that this deal essentially marks America and Israel as defeated in their objectives. "The only thing that America seems to be getting out of this, apart from eventually the Straits of Hormuz being opened, is a return to the situation where Iran isn't producing nuclear-grade uranium and the JCPOA inspectors back in there making sure they don't," Macleod noted. "And that's it. America and Israel are defeated. Put it bluntly, that's actually the situation."

## Israel's Sticking Points and the Deal's Fragility

The MOU faces significant obstacles, with Israel's non-participation being the most critical. "Israel is not a cosignatory to this. She does not feel bound to by it in any way," Macleod observed. "And if she is forced to retreat, in effect, it's the end of Netanyahu, as I see it. I think the very future of Israel will be being called into question if this deal goes through."

Netanyahu faces considerable parliamentary support for continuing operations against Hezbollah in southern Lebanon, creating an incentive structure that works against peace. "I think if I put myself in Netanyahu's shoes, it would probably be in my interest to step up the bombing in southern Lebanon. That would scupper the deal," Macleod suggested. "Israel will try and do everything she can to disrupt it."

## The Regional Power Shift

The geopolitical realignment extends far beyond bilateral negotiations. Macleod identified a broader trend: "The days of American meddling in this area, the Middle East—what the Asians call West Asia—I think those days are over."

Gulf Cooperation Council nations are rapidly repositioning themselves. "We're already seeing the GCC members beginning to align themselves more with Iran because they can see that Iran basically has more or less eliminated the functionality of American bases within the Gulf," Macleod explained. "They're going to bend with the wind. And as America gets quite clearly shown the door, the Arabs were fair weather friends for Israel. No longer. Israel's going to be very isolated."

Pakistan emerges as a crucial intermediary. "Pakistan I think has been very much a stepping stone for China and Russia in trying to sort out this mess," Macleod noted, adding that Pakistan likely will supply Chinese fighter jets to Saudi Arabia—a clear signal of shifting allegiances.

## Implications for Gold and Silver Buyers

Perhaps most compelling for precious metal investors is the evidence of wealth preservation strategies already underway among Middle Eastern elites. Macleod shared a revealing anecdote from his time at GoldMoney in 2014: "They were very busy at the time working 24 hours a day, 7 days a week... They were getting LBMA bars being sent to them from the Middle East. These are not governments. These tend to be the very big wealthy families, the sheikhdoms, the sheikhs and all the rest of it."

These wealthy families were instructing the refinery to recast LBMA 400 oz bars into 1 kilo Chinese standard bars. "The answer obviously was they could see that the geopolitical drift was that selling oil in particular to America was lessening and the China and all the rest of it, their demands for oil were rising and rising and rising," Macleod explained. "So, where's our future lie? Our future lies with China and possibly Russia as the hegemonic powers rather than America."

## The Economic Factors Driving the Deal

Trump's motivation for pursuing the agreement stems primarily from economic pressures. "Trump wants out. He realizes now the damage to his economy—I don't think he gives a damn about anybody else's economy, but the damage to the American economy from this failure, I think he sees this as going to threaten his presidency big time," Macleod observed.

The closure of the Straits of Hormuz and potentially the Red Sea threatened significant inflation spikes, undermining Trump's desire for lower interest rates. "You got to hit the economy in the sense also that you find the economy stalling, probably going into some sort of recession," Macleod explained. The military situation had deteriorated substantially—over 110 days into what was supposed to be a three or four-day operation, with helicopters shot down, fighter jets not returning, and massive destruction to American bases within the Gulf.

## Summary

The MOU between Iran and America represents a watershed moment in Middle Eastern geopolitics. Key takeaways for investors include:

- The decline of American regional influence creates uncertainty that typically supports precious metals
- Middle Eastern wealth has already been repositioning toward China and Eastern-aligned assets since at least 2014
- Israel's opposition to the deal and potential disruption attempts add further instability
- Economic pressures on the US presidency are driving diplomatic solutions that may reshape regional alliances
- The bond market, while often ignored by politicians, remains the key indicator of underlying economic stress

For gold and silver buyers, this geopolitical realignment suggests that the traditional safe-haven dynamics of precious metals remain relevant, particularly as regional power structures shift and wealth continues flowing toward Eastern markets.
