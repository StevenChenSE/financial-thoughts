## Introduction
On Election Day 2025, markets held their breath amid predictions of a potential "blue sweep" in Congress. While many investors feared immediate volatility, veteran trader Todd "Bubba" Horwitz offered a sobering perspective: the election is merely a catalyst for an overreaction that will precede a significant decline. Having correctly called the recent tech selloff, Horwitz now warns of a potential "Black Christmas" scenario reminiscent of 2018, driven by hidden debt in the AI sector, rising defaults, and structural economic weaknesses masked by government statistics.

## The Election: A Catalyst for an Overreaction
Horwitz dismisses the idea that election results will have a lasting fundamental impact on market direction. He draws parallels to 2016, when markets dipped briefly upon Trump’s victory before rallying sharply. Similarly, he expects any post-election move—whether toward a rally or a sell-off—to be an overreaction.

Historically, markets tend to rally after midterm elections due to seasonality and relief from campaign noise. However, Horwitz distinguishes between the investor mindset and the trader mindset:
*   **As an Investor:** He remains net long, having bought heavily into the recent dip without selling a single position. His strategy is based on mathematical models that compound gains over time.
*   **As a Trader:** He is actively shorting the market daily. He anticipates a "sellable rally" in the next three to four weeks, after which he expects prices to head significantly lower.

Horwitz emphasizes that while prediction markets (such as Koshi) show declining odds of the S&P 500 reaching 8,000 points by year-end, these numbers are influenced by arbitrage and market makers. Regardless of the immediate sentiment, his core thesis remains that the path of least resistance for the market is down.

## The AI Bubble: Hidden Debt and Structural Risks
A central pillar of Horwitz’s bearish outlook is the state of the technology sector, particularly artificial intelligence (AI) companies. He argues that the current enthusiasm for DCAing (Dollar-Cost Averaging) into AI stocks is dangerous.

### Key Concerns in Tech
*   **Off-Balance Sheet Debt:** Horwitz highlights that AI companies have accumulated trillions of dollars in off-balance sheet debt, which is not fully reflected in standard earnings reports.
*   **Lack of Real Profits:** While companies report good earnings, they are not generating sufficient cash flow to cover their mounting debt obligations.
*   **Consumer Impact:** The displacement of workers by AI threatens the very consumer base that fuels the market. With Microsoft buying out 60,000 employees and other tech giants automating roles, there is a growing disconnect between corporate efficiency and household income.

Horwitz predicts a 40% to 60% haircut for the broader tech market, noting that some lower-cost AI stocks are already down 70-80%. He warns that we have not yet seen "capitulation," where panic selling leads to a true bottom. Until there is "blood in the streets," he sees significant downside risk remaining.

## Economic Indicators: Defaults and the K-Shaped Economy
Horwitz is skeptical of official government economic data, labeling it as unreliable due to frequent revisions. Instead, he points to hard evidence of distress among lower-income consumers:
*   **Credit Card Delinquencies:** 25% of the population is 90+ days past due on credit cards.
*   **Loan Defaults:** Auto loan defaults stand at 10%, while mortgage defaults are at 8%.
*   **Housing Market:** Mortgage companies offering 0% down loans is a familiar warning sign from previous bubbles.

He describes the current economy as a "K-shaped" recovery, where the wealthy thrive due to asset ownership and lower borrowing costs relative to inflation, while the middle and lower classes struggle with stagnant wages and high debt burdens. This divergence creates instability that markets are ignoring.

## Commodities: Oil and Gold Strategies

### Oil: Bearish Outlook
Horwitz maintains a bearish stance on crude oil, viewing recent rallies as selling opportunities. He notes that despite geopolitical tensions in Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, every spike has resulted in a lower high.
*   **Supply Glut:** There is an oversupply of oil globally, evidenced by China buying at discounts.
*   **Demand Destruction:** High prices at the pump are reducing consumption, a key indicator of economic slowdown.
*   **Futures Curve:** The market is in "backwardation," where future contracts are cheaper than spot prices, signaling expected lower prices. Horwitz projects oil trading at $77–$78 per barrel a year from now.

### Gold: Long-Term Buy
Gold recently fell below $4,000 an ounce, hitting levels not seen since November 2025. While the chart pattern looks bearish in the short term, Horwitz sees this as a buying opportunity for long-term investors.
*   **Margin Calls:** The drop in gold was partly driven by investors selling liquid assets to meet margin calls on equity losses.
*   **Support Levels:** He suggests $3,600 may be tested but views current levels as attractive.
*   **Long-Term Target:** Horwitz expects gold to return to new highs, potentially reaching $5,500, driven by inflation and monetary policy concerns. He advises scaling into positions with money that does not need to be accessed for immediate bills.

## Bonds and the Fed: The Yield Curve Warning
The 10-year Treasury yield peaked around 5.3-5.5% before declining. Horwitz is currently selling 10-year futures, betting on higher rates, but acknowledges a complex scenario. If yields stay low while the Fed hikes short-term rates, it leads to a flattening or inversion of the yield curve—a classic recession indicator.

He argues that the Federal Reserve must prioritize controlling inflation over stabilizing stock markets, noting that 90% of the population has little exposure to equities but is directly hit by food and energy prices. High interest rates primarily affect the middle class through mortgages, while inflation devastates everyone.

Todd Horwitz’s strategy for the end of 2025 is clear: protect capital as a trader by shorting rallies, but maintain long-term positions in quality companies and precious metals. He warns against trying to "time the market" or rotate into defensive stocks merely because advisors recommend it.

While a "Black Christmas" sell-off may occur, Horwitz believes that for those who are not over-leveraged, the resulting downturn will provide generational buying opportunities in both equities and commodities.
