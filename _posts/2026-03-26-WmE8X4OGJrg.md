**A sharp drop in gold prices isn’t a crisis—it’s an opportunity.** That’s the contrarian view of veteran resource investor Rick Rule, who sees recent volatility as a chance to build positions in high-quality gold miners and physical metal.

In a recent interview, Rule addressed gold’s steep decline—what some called its “worst week in 40 years”—with characteristic calm. For long-term buyers, he argues, fear in the market is a gift.

---

## The Buyer’s Mindset: “Worst Week” vs. “Best Week”

When asked if the sell-off made gold attractive again, Rule was unequivocal.

> “Yeah, as you know from prior interviews, I am one who believes that at least a nominal price of gold does very well for the next 10 years. The consequence of that is that structurally I'm a buyer. So when you say the worst week, I say the best week because I'm not a seller. I'm a buyer.”

He recalled warning about the dangers of a parabolic rise in gold earlier this year, noting that such steep rallies often correct with similar severity. The flip side, however, is that a parabolic drop can set the stage for a powerful recovery.

Rule avoids trying to “call bottoms.” Instead, he maintains a shopping list of stocks with target prices. When the market offers them at a discount, he buys.

> “I went from having no live bids in the market to having a fairly robust menu of live bids in the market… Does this have to do with the fact that some companies that I would like to own became substantially cheaper?”

---

## The Strategy: Look Beyond the Next Quarter

Rule emphasized that his approach is not focused on short-term timing.

> “My strategy is not geared to Q2, 2023 or Q3, 2023. It's geared to a 5-year period commencing March 24th, 2026 and ending five years from now, six years from now, or seven years from now.”

He looks for high-quality companies selling at discounted net asset value (NAV) multiples—those that could rise on their own merits or become takeover targets. Recently, he noted, many such companies have fallen 40–50% from their 2026 highs.

> “Sometimes stuff is cheap enough.”

He illustrated this with a personal story about Exxon Mobil. When Exxon was removed from the Dow Jones Industrial Average during the COVID oil crash, he knew it was time to buy—but waited for it to fall further. It didn’t.

> “I ended up paying 15% more than I should have for my favorite company on the planet. That is really, really, really stupid… Sometimes things are cheap enough.”

---

## Mergers & Acquisitions: A Temporary Halt, Then a Surge

The plunge in gold and mining stocks has temporarily frozen merger and acquisition (M&A) activity, Rule explained, because both acquirers and targets see their share prices fall. But history suggests this sets the stage for a powerful rebound in deals.

> “In the very near term this doesn’t slow M&A, it halts it. In the intermediate term, however, if this market follows the same direction that past markets have taken, this will be a great impetus to M&A because the best of the best will recover quicker.”

Higher-quality companies typically rebound first, widening the valuation gap between potential acquirers and targets—making deals more attractive.

---

## The Macro Picture: War, Deficits, and the Long-Term Case for Gold

Rule turned to the broader economic landscape, particularly the fiscal impact of ongoing global conflicts. He noted that war spending is exacerbating the U.S. budget deficit, which could ultimately lead to more monetary easing.

> “The aftermath of the fiscal difficulties that are being caused and will be caused by this war will be quantitative easing and a further loosening of interest rates. Mr. Trump is very, very, very clear about this. And this is extremely good for gold in the long term.”

He was careful to clarify:

> “I'm not advocating war as a tool for gold, by the way. Don't get me wrong. I'm just saying that what you do in a democracy rather than deal with a problem is you kick it down the road.”

That “kicking the can” likely means more money printing and artificially low rates—conditions that historically benefit gold.

Rule also pointed to a concerning signal in the bond market: while the government manipulates short-term rates, long-term yields are rising anyway.

> “The fact that… the long yields are rising anyway despite their redeeming the long end of the curve—that's a very, very, very important thing. The last time the bond markets began to exert discipline over federal government spending was in the Clinton administration.”

Loss of control over long-term rates could ripple through the economy, affecting everything from mortgages to housing affordability.

---

## Energy Markets: War, Disruption, and Holding Oil Stocks

Shifting to energy, Rule discussed how conflict has disrupted global oil and liquefied natural gas (LNG) markets. Attacks on export facilities in the Persian Gulf have damaged critical infrastructure, with repairs estimated to take 2–5 years.

The impacts extend beyond oil and gas:

*   **Helium:** 35% of global supply (critical for semiconductors) moves through the affected region.
*   **Fertilizers:** 40% of the world’s nitrogen-based fertilizers come from Persian Gulf gas fields.
*   **Aluminum & Sulfur:** 30% of aluminum and 35% of sulfur/sulfuric acid production is impacted.

Rule hasn’t sold his oil stocks, noting that the expected multi-year move happened much sooner due to the war. If the conflict persists, he believes oil rationing by price is inevitable.

Finally, Rule promoted his annual **Rule Symposium** (July 6–10 in Boca Raton, Florida, and via livestream), emphasizing its focus on deep research and vetting.

Every speaker and exhibiting company is pre-interviewed, with videos available to attendees beforehand. Every exhibitor is also a company Rule personally owns.

> “At every other investment conference on the planet, qualification to be an exhibitor is a check that cashes. At our conference, those companies have to be owned in our accounts.”

He stands behind the value with a unique guarantee:

> “If you think for any reason that I haven't delivered good value for money, simply email me and I'll give you your money back.”

## The Bottom Line

For Rick Rule, market panic is a time for disciplined buying, not selling. The long-term drivers for gold—monetary expansion, fiscal stress, and geopolitical uncertainty—remain firmly in place. While volatility may continue in the near term, he sees current prices as an opportunity to build positions in quality assets with a multi-year horizon.

As he puts it:  
> “I am one who believes that at least a nominal price of gold does very well for the next 10 years. The consequence of that is that structurally I'm a buyer.”
