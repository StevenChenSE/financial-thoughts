**A sharp drop in gold prices isn't a crisis—it's an opportunity.** That’s the contrarian view from veteran resource investor Rick Rule, who sees recent volatility as a chance to build positions in high-quality assets.

In a recent interview, Rule addressed gold’s steep decline—described by some as its worst week in decades—and explained why he’s not just a buyer, but an enthusiastic one.

## The Contrarian Mindset: "Worst Week" vs. "Best Week"

When asked if the drop made gold attractive again, Rule was clear:

> “Yeah, as you know from prior interviews, I am one who believes that at least a nominal price of gold does very well for the next 10 years. The consequence of that is that structurally I'm a buyer. So when you say the worst week, I say the best week because I'm not a seller. I'm a buyer.”

He noted that parabolic rallies often end with equally steep corrections. The recent plunge, in his view, simply reset the market, creating a “fairly pleasant upside” from here.

## Strategy: Have a Shopping List, Not a Crystal Ball

Rule avoids trying to time the exact bottom. Instead, he relies on a disciplined, value-based approach:

> “What I do is I have a shopping list of stocks and I have an approximation of what I think they're worth and what I'll buy them up to. When the market allows me the opportunity to buy goods that I believe are on sale, I do it automatically.”

He went from having no live bids in the market to placing a “fairly robust menu” after prices fell. For him, it’s about buying when quality companies become substantially cheaper—not predicting short-term moves.

His time horizon is long-term:  
> “My strategy is not geared to Q2, 2023 or Q3, 2023. It's geared to a 5-year period commencing March 24th, 2026 and ending five years from now, six years from now, or seven years from now.”

## What’s on the Shopping List?

Rule’s interest spans the entire mining spectrum:
- **Majors:** He’s a “perma buyer at the right price” of giants like Franco-Nevada and Agnico Eagle.
- **Intermediates & Juniors:** He focuses on “high-quality companies selling at discounted NAV multiples” that are 40–50% off their highs.

He shared a cautionary tale about Exxon Mobil during COVID, when he waited for it to get cheaper and ended up paying 15% more:  
> “That is really, really, really stupid, right? … Sometimes things are cheap enough.”

## Mergers & Acquisitions: A Temporary Halt, Then a Surge

The sell-off impacts M&A activity, but not in a straight line:

> “In the very near term this doesn’t slow M&A, it halts it. In the intermediate term, however, if this market follows the same direction that past markets have taken, this will be a great impetus to M&A because the best of the best will recover quicker.”

Higher-quality companies typically rebound first, widening the valuation gap between potential acquirers and targets—setting the stage for a wave of deals.

## The Macro Picture: War, Deficits, and the Long-Term Case for Gold

Rule addressed the broader economic landscape, particularly the impact of ongoing conflict in the Middle East. While he explicitly stated he does **not** advocate war, he noted its fiscal consequences:

> “The aftermath of the fiscal difficulties that are being caused and will be caused by this war will be quantitative easing and a further loosening of interest rates. Mr. Trump is very, very, very clear about this. And this is extremely good for gold in the long term.”

He believes democracies tend to “kick the can down the road” via monetary easing, which devalues currency and supports gold. However, he highlighted a critical tension: while authorities manipulate short-term rates, long-term yields are rising anyway—a sign the bond market may be reasserting discipline over government spending.

> “If they continue to lose control of the long end of the curve, that has really profound implications.”

## Energy Markets: A Wildcard with Global Impact

Rule also discussed oil and LNG, noting that prolonged conflict could severely disrupt global supply. Key chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz handle half the world’s export crude and significant volumes of LNG, helium, fertilizers, and sulfur.

> “If this continues, make no mistake, we're going to have to ration oil by price.”

While he hasn’t sold his energy stocks, he acknowledged the recent rally brought forward gains he expected years later. The situation remains fluid, heavily dependent on the war’s duration.

## Final Thought: Focus on Value, Not Noise

Rule’s philosophy echoes Warren Buffett’s wisdom:  
> “Trying to comprehend the incomprehensible is a waste of time. If it amuses you, by all means, call it a hobby, but don't spend money on it.”

For investors, the message is clear: use volatility to accumulate quality assets at a discount, keep a long-term perspective, and ignore the short-term noise. In Rick Rule’s world, a “worst week” for gold is simply a sale—and smart buyers are loading up.
