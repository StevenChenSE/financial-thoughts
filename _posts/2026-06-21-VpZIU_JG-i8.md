## Legendary Investor Reveals His Top Picks as Gold Faces Critical Crossroads

Rick Rule, one of the most respected voices in natural resource investing, recently joined Danny from Capital Cos for an in-depth ranking episode covering major gold and silver mining companies. The timing couldn't be more critical—gold is trading around $4,362, having experienced significant volatility amid shifting interest rate expectations. Rule's analysis draws striking parallels to 1975, a historical period he believes offers the most relevant roadmap for where gold prices may be heading.

The conversation delved deep into individual company fundamentals while maintaining a sharp focus on the macroeconomic forces shaping the precious metals landscape. Rule's proprietary 10-point ranking system—where one represents the strongest investment opportunity and ten the weakest—provides investors with a framework for evaluating relative value across the sector. This ranking methodology, which Rule has applied to nearly 100,000 portfolios over 35 years, offers invaluable insight into how the industry's top operators compare against one another.

## Understanding the Current Gold Price Action

From a technical standpoint, Rule observes that gold's recent weakness stems primarily from higher US interest rates. "Gold is denominated in dollars," he explains. "Higher interest rates generate a stronger US dollar. Ergo, something denominated in dollars tends to fall in nominal pricing." This relationship between interest rates and gold prices has been a defining characteristic of the market throughout history, and Rule sees the current chart painting a familiar picture—one that closely resembles 1975.

The comparison to the mid-1970s is particularly instructive for investors seeking to understand the current environment. During that period, inflation became a pressing political issue in the United States, prompting the political class to allow interest rates to rise significantly. The result was devastating for gold prices, which fell approximately 50% from their highs. "It also destroyed the long bond market, did substantial damage to the equities market, and was harmful for new home sales and consumer durables like automobiles," Rule recalls.

However, the historical parallel doesn't end with the correction. Nine months into that 1975 experiment, Congress lost their nerve and pressured the Federal Reserve to lower interest rates precipitously. The outcome was extraordinary: gold embarked on a six-year rally, moving from approximately $100 per ounce to $850 per ounce—a remarkable 9x increase. Rule believes a similar scenario could unfold today, though the timing remains uncertain.

The 10-year Treasury yield, currently hovering around 4.4% after briefly testing 4.5%, provides a crucial barometer for gold's near-term direction. Rule anticipates that the political class will eventually "lose their courage" on maintaining elevated interest rates, particularly given the mounting cost of servicing US debt. "I am anticipating at some point in time, perhaps it's six months out, perhaps it's nine months out, that the political class has their way and the interest rate gets cut," he states. "If that happens, then I think you'll see a very, very different gold chart."

## Gold Mining Stock Rankings

### Newmont (NYSE: NEM) — Ranked 5

Newmont currently trades around $111, representing approximately an 18% decline from its January peak of $135. The stock has experienced a more significant 31-32% correction from its lows of around $92. Despite this price weakness, Rule has actually upgraded Newmont from a six to a five, reflecting the company's improving operational performance.

"The price chart does not reflect the internal improvements taking place in Newmont, nor does it reflect the benefit that shareholders will receive from the sell-off of most of their tier two mines and the wonderful development schedule that they have in front of them," Rule explains. "Their pipeline is probably the best it's ever been."

Rule sees potential for further ranking improvements if gold prices remain soft through the summer and Newmont continues delivering strong quarterly results. He also notes an intriguing strategic possibility: the potential combination of Newmont's North American assets with those of Barrick, creating what would be "easily the most valuable gold mining company on the planet." While he emphasizes this remains speculative, the implications for shareholders could be substantial.

### Agnico Eagle (NYSE: AEM) — Ranked 4

Among all the companies discussed, Agnico Eagle holds a special place in Rule's evaluation. "I have Agnico as a four," he begins. "The price action in Agnico is wonderful because I'm trying to buy more." His enthusiasm extends beyond recent price performance to encompass the company's fundamental qualities.

"Agnico is, I think, operationally easily the best gold mining company on the planet," Rule declares without hesitation. His assessment draws from five decades of personal relationships with Agnico's leadership, including all three chief executive officers over that period. The company's reputation for mine-building excellence and disciplined capital allocation has distinguished it among major producers.

The company's five-year production growth pipeline and extraordinary capital discipline reinforce Rule's conviction. From a technical perspective, the stock has formed a descending wedge pattern—a continuation formation that historically suggests upward resolution. This combination of fundamental strength and potentially constructive technical setup makes Agnico Rule's favorite gold mining company and his top-rated silver stock as well.

### Barrick Gold (NYSE: GOLD) — Ranked 5

Barrick has experienced roughly a 20% correction from its January high of $55, currently trading around $43. Rule rates Barrick as a five but sees a clear path to a four if certain strategic initiatives materialize. "The idea that they're going to spin off their North American assets is a good one," he observes. "The question is what becomes with the rest of the company."

Several potential catalysts could improve Barrick's ranking. The possible merger of Barrick's African assets with Endeavour Mining's African operations could create significant synergies. Similarly, the potential sale of Reko Diq—a massive porphyry copper project in Pakistan—to Chinese mining giant Zijin could unlock substantial value for shareholders. If these transactions proceed as contemplated, Rule would raise his ranking to a four.

### Franco-Nevada (NYSE: FNV) — Ranked 4

Rule's assessment of Franco-Nevada carries perhaps the strongest endorsement of any company discussed: "I believe Franco-Nevada is the finest gold-oriented company on the planet." The royalty and streaming company receives a four in the current ranking, with potential to improve to a three if prices continue to decline.

The investment thesis for Franco-Nevada involves dispelling common misconceptions about valuation. "By conventional metrics, it always seems overpriced because it's valued on a net present value basis, and the company enjoys 30- or 40-year long assets," Rule explains. Standard NPV calculations at an 8% discount rate essentially assign zero value to cash flows occurring after year ten, creating an optical overvaluation appearance. However, "five years out, the net present value calculation is always the same as it was at year one," suggesting the market systematically undervalues the company's long-duration asset base.

Franco-Nevada also boasts the industry's lowest general and administrative expenses as a percentage of revenue—a testament to operational efficiency. Rule sees additional upside from anticipated major transactions. "I believe that the $250 billion that the copper industry must spend in the next 10 years to maintain current copper production will involve massive sales by the copper producers of gold and silver streams," he predicts. The recent BHP sale to Wheaton Precious provides a template for the type of transactions that could benefit Franco-Nevada going forward.

### Gold Fields (NYSE: GFI) — Ranked 4

Gold Fields has experienced one of the more dramatic corrections among major gold producers, falling approximately 46% from its peak around $61 before recovering somewhat. Currently trading at roughly a 33% discount to its January highs, the stock presents an intriguing opportunity at current levels.

"I have Gold Fields as a four because their production profile is so bullish," Rule notes, though he admits he does not currently own the stock. The company's track record of balancing sustaining capital requirements with new project investment positions it well for improved profitability. "What you'll see two years out is a company with substantially higher production and substantially higher profitability," he predicts.

## The Strategic Advantage of Streamers and Royaltiers

The conversation highlighted a structural advantage that royalty and streaming companies like Franco-Nevada and Wheaton Precious maintain over traditional mining operators. These companies provide upfront capital to producers in exchange for the right to purchase metals at predetermined prices, creating diversified exposure to numerous mining operations without the operational risks of mine management.

This business model becomes particularly valuable during commodity downturns, as streamers can acquire new streaming agreements at favorable terms when miners face capital constraints. The anticipated copper industry capital expenditure cycle that Rule describes—requiring $250 billion over the coming decade—will likely force many copper producers to monetize their gold and silver byproducts through streaming arrangements. Companies with strong balance sheets and established relationships stand to benefit disproportionately from this dynamic.

For investors with appropriate time horizons, current weakness in gold mining equities may represent an accumulation opportunity rather than a reason for alarm.
