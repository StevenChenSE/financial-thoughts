*Copper prices hovering near all-time highs reflect a structural supply deficit that won't resolve quickly—and one Yukon miner is positioning itself to benefit from this momentum.*

---

When copper climbed to $5.99 per pound in early May 2025, it wasn't just another commodity tick. For Colin Jadry, CEO of Selkirk Copper Mines and a geologist with decades of experience in base metals, the price movement told a deeper story about an industry fundamentally out of balance.

"The investments in mines and in major new copper production have really lagged over the last decade," Jadry explained in a recent interview. "Many of the big mines that have been stalwarts of the producing environment for many years—they're getting older, grades are declining. It's getting more expensive and harder to expand and meet that production platform that was there before."

The result is a supply-demand squeeze that analysts have been warning about for years, but which is now becoming impossible to ignore.

---

## The Perfect Storm Driving Copper Prices

### A Decade of Underinvestment

The current copper price strength traces its roots to years of insufficient capital deployment in new copper development. According to Jadry, several factors contributed to this shortfall:

- Major mining companies shifted focus toward operational efficiency and shareholder returns rather than greenfield exploration
- Declining ore grades at established operations reduced output without proportional new discoveries to offset the decline
- Increasing regulatory complexity and permitting timelines extended the time between discovery and production

"We're just not replacing existing production with new production quickly enough," Jadry noted. "And that's because demand has just increased."

### Demand From Every Direction

The demand side of the equation has intensified across multiple sectors:

- **Electrification**: Electric vehicles contain approximately four times more copper than internal combustion engine vehicles
- **Energy transmission**: Grid infrastructure upgrades require substantial copper for power distribution
- **Battery storage**: Large-scale energy storage installations are copper-intensive
- **Renewable energy**: Solar and wind installations require significant copper components

"Per capita growth consumption globally is not just about electrification," Jadry emphasized. "It's the transmission, it's the use, it's battery storage. How the economy is using copper is just increasing at a pace that demands more production."

### The Gold-Copper Correlation

One phenomenon catching investor attention is the near-lockstep trading relationship between copper and gold over recent months. While not a perfect correlation historically, the two metals have moved in the same direction roughly 70% of the time over the past 30 years.

Jadry attributes this to several converging factors:

1. **Geological association**: Many major copper deposits contain significant gold mineralization, creating natural supply linkages
2. **Demand pressure**: Both metals face similar supply constraints, with above-ground inventories declining
3. **Portfolio flows**: Investors increasingly view metals as an asset class, moving between gold and copper based on macro sentiment

"Many of the major companies that we hear about—they're all talking about whether they're a major gold producer wanting more copper exposure, or a major copper producer wanting more gold exposure," Jadry observed. "I think that's a little bit of why we're seeing this correlation strengthen."

### The $6 Question: Is It Enough?

With copper approaching the $6 mark, industry observers are asking whether this price level provides sufficient incentive to catalyze new production.

"I think the $6 is more about what incentive price is required to bring new copper into production," Jadry said. "We're right now in a place where the incentive price is excellent and it should generate a lot more activity across the complex."

However, Jadry cautions that the market's response may lag the price signal. "Most of the big projects that you've seen executed over the last number of years have been late, slow, and more costly. And that's not because they have to be that way. I think we're just out of practice building."

## Geopolitical Factors Reshaping the Copper Landscape

### Tariffs and Stockpiling

Beyond the structural supply-demand dynamics, near-term price action has been influenced by geopolitical developments:

**Tariff-related stockpiling**: US copper users have been building refined copper inventories ahead of anticipated tariff implementation, tightening near-term availability and supporting prices.

**Smelter capacity constraints**: Concentrate markets are experiencing historically unusual conditions. Treatment charges and refining charges (TCRCs) have turned negative for the first time in decades, reflecting intense competition among smelters for limited concentrate supply.

"You're seeing negative TCRCs. It's the first time that's really happened in the industry for decades, if not ever. And that's a reflection of the lack of primary supply in concentrate. These smelters just need that material so they're pulling it as hard as they can," Jadry explained.

### The Critical Minerals Dimension

Copper's designation as a critical mineral by the United States in 2025 adds another layer of strategic importance. With China dominating global copper refining capacity, North American policymakers are seeking diversification sources—but this takes time.

"China dominates global copper and critical minerals refining," Jadry acknowledged. "What you have to do is hope that the metal flows back out the way that it should. And right now, the incentive for the Chinese is to make sure that metal is available, at least copper."

## Why Yukon Is Becoming a Copper Destination

For Selkirk Copper, the macro copper thesis converges with a specific jurisdictional advantage: the Yukon territory.

### Yukon's Mineral Endowment

The Yukon represents one of Canada's most underexplored but geologically prospective regions:

- **Type deposits**: The territory hosts world-class zinc, tungsten, and copper occurrences
- **Emerging gold potential**: Recent exploration has revealed exceptional gold deposits advancing through development
- **Frontier character**: Large areas remain undrilled, with some Selkirk properties seeing no exploration activity for over 50 years

"The mineral endowment in the Yukon is exceptional," Jadry stated. "From an endowment perspective, it's a great place to be. It's a great place to go and aspire to discovery."

### Infrastructure Advantages

Not all Yukon locations are created equal. Selkirk Copper has positioned itself on what Jadry describes as the "infrastructure advantage side" of the territory:

- **Existing roads**: Direct access reduces logistics costs
- **Grid power**: Eliminates the need for standalone power generation
- **Previous operations**: Existing infrastructure provides a foundation for restart

"That allows us to explore a little more cost-effectively," Jadry explained. "When we do find something, we don't have to spend as much money building the non-mine infrastructure like roads and power."

### Labor Market Dynamics

With Yukon unemployment at 3.9%—significantly below the national average of approximately 6.7%—potential labor shortages concern some investors. However, Jadry sees this as manageable:

- **Fly-in/fly-out workforce**: Historically standard for northern mining operations
- **Asset appeal**: The Minto site has positive associations, with former workers actively seeking return opportunities
- **Local development**: Selkirk is committed to maximizing local employment and training programs

"I get phone calls from people who operated and worked at the asset before, asking to come back," Jadry noted. "It's in a beautiful part of the country. The work is meaningful."

## Selkirk Copper: Drilling Results and the Path Forward

### The Phase One Drill Program

Selkirk Copper's recent drilling campaign at the Minto Copper Gold Silver Mine represents the largest exploration program in Yukon over the past decade:

- **Program size**: 52,288 meters across 175 drill holes
- **Success rate**: 87% of holes intersected target mineralization
- **Drilling productivity**: Average of 94 meters per drill per day, with some holes exceeding 200 meters per day

"This was targeted to be a 50,000-meter program in phase one—important because phase one means there'll be a phase two," Jadry explained. "This one just happens to be bigger, and it surprised me as well."

### What the Results Reveal

The drilling accomplished three key objectives:

1. **Grade confirmation**: In known high-grade zones (Minto North and Copper Keel), drilling validated and augmented existing resource understanding
2. **New discoveries**: Higher-grade intercepts were identified in the 118 zone and Ridge Top deposit area, expanding open-pit potential
3. **Depth extensions**: Beneath existing pit designs, new mineralization was discovered in the main zone and at depth

"We've had about five main zones that we're targeting our drilling at—around extension and expansion, a little bit of infill drilling where appropriate, and then discovery within the main property zone," Jadry said. "The program has done what it's intended to do."

### Quality Assurance: Why Reanalysis Matters

In high-grade copper-gold systems, assay verification is critical. Selkirk's latest release included a reanalysis that delayed results by several weeks:

"The main thing is to have a process in place and to act on it," Jadry explained. "This is even more relevant for higher-grade samples. We are intersecting often zones where we'll get grades over a meter thick that will be as high as 20% copper."

The company's quality control protocols include:

- **QA/QC insertion rate**: Approximately 15% of all samples
- **Standard protocols**: Industry-standard procedures for checking and rechecking results
- **Transparency**: Full disclosure of reanalysis protocols in public releases

"By adding more QA/QC doesn't necessarily help you," Jadry noted. "That number is a perfectly appropriate insertion rate."

## The First Nation Partnership Model

### A Unique Structure

Selkirk Copper represents what Jadry believes is the first publicly-traded mining company in North America with majority equity ownership held by a First Nation group:

- **Ownership**: Selkirk First Nation holds approximately 22% of total shares outstanding
- **Board representation**: Two Selkirk-nominated directors serve on the board
- **Executive presence**: First Nation citizens hold senior management positions with equal voice

"This is a commercial operating partnership," Jadry clarified. "It's not a joint venture in the legal sense, but it operates as a true partnership."

### How the Relationship Works

The partnership operates on multiple levels:

**Governance**: The First Nation nominates directors who participate fully in board decisions, holding management accountable to the operating plan.

**Policy development**: Selkirk seeks direct input from the First Nation on policies and procedures, integrating Indigenous cultures and knowledge into operations.

**On-the-ground operations**: First Nation citizens serve in executive roles with real responsibilities—not ceremonial positions.

**Framework agreement**: A cooperation agreement (similar to an impact benefits agreement) guides contracting, employment, training, and community contributions.

### Why Equity Plus Royalty?

Some investors question why Selkirk offers both equity participation and a 1.5% net smelter return royalty to the Selkirk First Nation. Jadry explains:

"This nation acquired this asset with 100% of their funds when it went into bankruptcy. They vended their asset into the vehicle because they realized they needed a mechanism to raise capital that could advance the restart. They also needed a mechanism to attract a management team and professionals—they know they're not a mining company."

The royalty, which predates Selkirk's formation, creates ongoing alignment:

"When this asset is in production, there's a royalty that pays directly to the First Nation. That's a further incentive for them to assist de-risking the permitting or re-permitting this asset."

### Permitting Implications

Perhaps most significantly for the project's timeline, the First Nation relationship provides a permitting advantage:

"Our approach is there are no surprises here. The First Nation will know exactly what our plans are, what we're proposing for the re-permitting. They will already have agreed to it in principle," Jadry said.

"They know as much about permitting in the Yukon as I do. They've lived it. They've watched it. They've been frustrated by it. But they also are very constructive in their approach to it."

The First Nation's support doesn't guarantee permits—they retain their right to voice concerns—but it substantially reduces the risk of unexpected opposition.

## The 2028 Production Timeline: What Investors Should Know

### Where the Target Stands

Selkirk Copper has maintained its target of production commencement in mid-2028, with feasibility study completion targeted for mid-2027. The path forward includes:

- **Mineral resource update**: New resource estimate incorporating all phase one drilling
- **Preliminary economic assessment (PEA)**: Mid-year release, representing the first economic model without the gold and silver stream that complicated previous analyses
- **Feasibility study**: Q3 2025 commencement following PEA
- **Permitting**: Re-amended permit applications targeted for early Q4 2025 submission

### The Permitting Reality

Jadry is candid about the permitting timeline's uncertainty:

"The main one is permitting. That's still probably the one," he acknowledged when asked what could shift the timeline.

Critically, permitting is not entirely within the company's control. Selkirk's approach involves:

- **Project management**: Treating permitting like a construction program, with clear milestones and communication protocols
- **Resource allocation**: Staffing with experienced permitting professionals who understand regulatory processes
- **Relationship building**: Ensuring regulators have the information they need when they need it

"We've approached this in a very transparent way. We're trying to set out a plan of engagements and communications with the different regulatory authorities such that everybody knows what's going on and they can resource it appropriately," Jadry explained.

The company is pursuing a Category 2 assessment through YESAB (Yukon Environmental Socio-Economic Assessment Board), with an estimated 9-12 month processing timeline once applications are submitted.

### Why Not Produce Now?

Some investors question why a restart can't happen immediately given existing infrastructure. Jadry addresses this directly:

"The permits don't allow us to do that the way I think they should. We have many authorizations that allow us to do certain things, but not on an integrated basis that allows us to keep this mill filled at 4,100 tons per day."

The previous operator's failure included assumptions about permits they didn't actually possess. Selkirk won't repeat this mistake:

"We're not going to be producing at 40% throughput rates on the mill because we will run out of money and we'll go bankrupt just like the last group. So we're going to get these permits right."

## From Teck to Selkirk: Applying Large-Company Experience

Jadry's background includes seven years leading Teck Resources' copper development portfolio before joining Selkirk. The transition, he notes, is less jarring than expected:

"People ask me, 'Oh, you've come from a really big company mentality to a now small, nimble junior.' I think the history of the Teck group of companies is actually the reverse of a big company mentality. When I grew up in that organization, it was very entrepreneurial. It was very hands-on, very familial, lots of incentives to take risks."

What translates directly from his Teck experience:

- **Technical rigor**: Applying the same study standards used at major producers
- **Commercial thinking**: Maintaining focus on value creation
- **Permitting experience**: Understanding how to navigate regulatory processes
- **Silo-breaking**: Ensuring geology, metallurgy, mining, and environmental functions communicate effectively

"The difference is we're commercially nimble," Jadry noted. "I think this is about the perfect mix right now. I'm really enjoying this transition because it's kind of going back to my roots."

## Key Takeaways

1. **Structural supply deficit**: Years of underinvestment in copper exploration and development have created a supply-demand imbalance that prices at $5-6 per pound may only begin to resolve. The incentive price for new production is here, but the industry is "out of practice" building new mines.

2. **Copper-gold correlation strengthening**: As major deposits increasingly contain both metals and investors view commodities as an asset class, copper and gold are trading more closely together, reflecting shared supply constraints.

3. **Geopolitical complexity**: Tariff-related stockpiling and China's dominance in copper refining create near-term volatility while reinforcing long-term incentives for North American supply development.

4. **First Nation partnership as competitive advantage**: Selkirk's relationship with Selkirk First Nation (22% ownership, board representation, executive participation) provides permitting de-risking that many peers lack—a potentially decisive factor in achieving the 2028 production target.

5. **Drilling validates asset quality**: A 52,288-meter program with 87% success rate confirmed and expanded known mineralization while making new discoveries at depth, setting up an updated resource estimate and PEA for mid-2025.
