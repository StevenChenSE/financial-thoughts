## Navigating the Global Energy Reset

The global energy landscape is undergoing a significant transformation, characterized not by a sudden collapse but by a slow, steady decline in availability and rising prices. In an interview with Capital Cosm host Danny on July 23, 2026, Shubam Guard, CEO of Prospera Energy, provided deep insights into the current market dynamics, the geopolitical factors driving oil prices above $100 for Brent and north of $90 for WTI, and his company’s unique strategy for capitalizing on legacy assets in Canada.

### The State of the Oil Market

Guard describes the current environment as a "reset" where both bulls and bears have seen partial victories. While initial buffers—such as hidden shipments through the Strait of Hormuz and larger-than-reported Chinese Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) stocks—provided temporary relief, the world is now back in crisis mode. Hostilities have restarted, featuring precision strikes on US bases and ships in the Red Sea, alongside asymmetrical warfare that restricts tanker movements.

Despite these disruptions, supply additions have been modest, with US rigs added slowly. Meanwhile, demand remains robust, causing US inventories to deplete significantly. Guard emphasizes that oil was never designed to be a "just-in-time" system; the global economy relies too heavily on it. As buffers drain, the world is moving toward a scenario of reduced availability and higher prices, leading to demand destruction in specific pockets rather than a global sudden stop. He notes that China’s reduction in imports is unprecedented, yet energy needs will eventually drive violent rebounds in demand.

### Backwardation and Market Misunderstanding

A key indicator of the current tightness is backwardation, where spot prices for oil are higher than future contracts. Guard argues this is often misunderstood. He attributes the lack of proper energy policy and security to a general lack of understanding among policymakers and the public, contrasting the current era with 2012–2013 when scarcity was recognized, and no SPR was released. Today, there is a reflexive tendency to dump reserves whenever prices spike, which undermines long-term security.

Guard points out that algorithmic trading often misinterprets backwardation, leading to dislocations in equity markets. He advises investors to watch daily data rather than relying on long-term predictions, noting that the current tightness for prompt delivery is real and driven by global geopolitical shifts, including conflicts in the Black Sea and Africa.

### Prospera Energy’s Strategy: Reactivating Legacy Assets

Prospera Energy, trading as PEI on the TSXV with a $21 million market cap, focuses on a low-risk, high-reward strategy of reactivating vertical wells in Saskatchewan, Canada. Guard founded the company after observing that many oil pools were abandoned during the industry downturns of 2014–2018 and 2020. Larger companies consolidated, leaving behind non-core assets, while private equity and banks exited the junior oil space.

The company’s approach is simple: clean out sand, install new progressive cavity pumps (PCP) or pump jacks, and produce from existing wells. There is no drilling or exploration. Each reactivation costs approximately $150,000 CAD, with a payback period of 6–8 months at $75 oil, and a second payback in 12–14 months. This creates a self-financing cash flow machine, unlike shale wells which have high initial drops and long payback periods for subsequent returns.

Guard highlights several advantages:
- **High NPV:** Wells produce reliably for 20–40 years.
- **Low Operating Costs:** Currently $38–$40 per barrel, with variable costs around $15–$18.
- **Transparency:** The board owns 52% of the company, and regular "key wells reports" provide detailed production data.
- **Scalability:** With 140 more wells to reactivate, the company aims to grow from 800 barrels per day to 1,400 and eventually 3,000 barrels per day.

### Financial Health and Risks

Prospera Energy has successfully completed 17 projects in 2025, with 16 achieving success. The company owns its equipment, reducing downtime and costs. Guard notes that the company is heavily torqued to oil prices, with every $10 increase generating approximately $350,000 in additional revenue. While interest rates are a concern, the impact is manageable given the company’s strong cash flow and friendly debt structure.

Key risks include oil price volatility, execution risk on new wells, and harsh Canadian winters. However, Guard mitigates these by running wells at 60% capacity to ensure longevity and avoiding sanding issues. He also emphasizes the stable political environment in Alberta and Saskatchewan, and improved egress capacity via pipelines and rail.

### Vision for the Canadian Oil Sector

Guard’s vision extends beyond Prospera Energy. He aims to prove that junior oil companies can succeed sustainably, inspiring other entrepreneurs to revive the Canadian oil patch. By demonstrating a repeatable, profitable model, he hopes to normalize valuations and bring back confidence to the sector. With oil prices elevated and global supply constrained, Prospera Energy is poised to capitalize on its large war chest of assets, offering investors a transparent and reliable path to energy sector exposure.
