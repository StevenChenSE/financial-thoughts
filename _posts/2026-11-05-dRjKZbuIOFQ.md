## Introduction

The day after the 2026 midterm elections has brought significant volatility and rapid shifts across major asset classes. In a special episode of *Capital Cosm*, host Danny sat down with Chris Vermeulen, founder of The Technical Traders, to dissect the immediate market reaction. While initial fears of a "blue wave" caused an early sell-off, markets rebounded strongly as clarity emerged. However, beneath the surface rally lies a critical divergence: technology and AI stocks are showing signs of exhaustion, while capital flows into dividend-paying stocks and precious metals seek refuge.

## Election Results and Market Certainty

The midterm elections resulted in what Vermeulen described as a "complete obliteration" for Republicans, with Democrats securing control of the Senate (projected at 56-44) and gaining roughly 260 seats in the House. Despite initial panic that drove indices down by 2% overnight, markets rallied significantly during the trading day. The Dow Jones rose approximately 2.8%, the S&P 500 gained 1.7%, and the Nasdaq climbed 1.86%.

Vermeulen emphasized that this rebound was driven by "relief." Investors dislike uncertainty, and the election results provided a degree of certainty regarding gridlocked governance. With divided government expected in 2027, major legislative changes are unlikely to pass. Historically, markets tend to perform well under gridlock because it freezes policy extremes, allowing investors to allocate capital without fear of sudden regulatory landmines.

## The Tech Sector Unwind and AI Bubble Concerns

A central theme of the discussion was the weakness in the technology sector. While the broader market rallied, tech stocks significantly underperformed. Vermeulen pointed to the semiconductor ETF (SMH) as a key barometer, noting it broke below its July lows—a bearish signal.

### On-Balance Volume and Institutional Selling
Vermeulen highlighted the use of On-Balance Volume (OBV) to detect institutional behavior. The OBV in the semiconductor space is declining sharply, indicating that large institutions are selling big blocks of shares and moving away from tech. Goldman Sachs reportedly noted that October was the largest month for hedge fund selling in technology on record.

### The AI Reality Check
The narrative around Artificial Intelligence is shifting. Vermeulen argues that the AI trade has lost its shine due to several factors:
*   **Overvaluation:** The sector feels crowded and expensive.
*   **Lack of Profitability:** Major AI companies are spending fortunes without turning a profit yet.
*   **Technological Shifts:** The rise of local, downloadable AI models that do not require constant internet connectivity or massive cloud token spending may disrupt current business models.

Vermeulen noted that while Anthropic shows strong revenue growth (12x year-over-year), the broader sector is facing a "kiss goodbye" pattern—a final rally into resistance before potential breakdowns. If tech breaks down significantly, it could drag the entire market lower, countering the typical post-election rally.

## Precious Metals: A Technical Bounce, Not a Trend Reversal

Gold and silver miners saw explosive gains, with gold mining ETFs popping up 8-9%. Vermeulen cautioned that this is largely a technical bounce from oversold conditions rather than a confirmed bull market reversal.

### Gold Analysis
Gold rallied due to:
1.  **Technical Support:** It bounced off a Fibonacci support level after being deeply oversold.
2.  **Flight to Safety:** Investors moved into precious metals amid uncertainty.
3.  **Oil Correlation:** The crash in oil prices helped boost the case for precious metals by reducing inflationary pressure concerns temporarily, though the geopolitical risk premium remains complex.

Vermeulen stressed that gold, silver, and miners are still in a bear market downtrend. This rally is "noise within the trend." For a new bull market to begin, price must hold above key moving averages and build a foundation, which has not yet happened.

### Silver's Struggle
Unlike gold, silver showed a muted reaction, failing to make a significant move. Vermeulen noted that investors seem to have "given up" on silver after it broke down from significant resistance levels, leaving it struggling to find a bid.

## Oil Crashes on Geopolitical Relief

Crude oil prices plummeted from over $100 to approximately $88.91 in less than 24 hours. Vermeulen attributed this crash to the market pricing in relative peace or reduced volatility in the Middle East, specifically regarding the Iran conflict. With gridlocked government limiting drastic foreign policy shifts, traders bet against continued escalation.

### Economic Ramifications
Falling oil prices act as a tailwind for the economy:
*   **Lower Costs:** Reduced shipping and transportation fees help slow inflation.
*   **Bond Yields:** Lower energy costs can lead to lower inflation expectations, potentially stabilizing bond yields.
*   **Equities:** Generally, falling oil is positive for the broader stock market by improving corporate margins.

However, Vermeulen warned that oil remains in an uptrend overall. The crash hit a critical support level, and short-covering could cause a sharp rebound if geopolitical tensions flare again.

## Bonds and Global Markets

The bond market remained relatively stable, with TLT (the 20-year Treasury Bond ETF) up only half a percent. Vermeulen noted that bonds are in a bear market, mirroring the inverse of the 10-year yield, which is hovering near 4.9%. He expects yields to trade sideways around the 5% mark for the time being, suggesting limited upside for long-term bonds.

Globally, European indices like the DAX and Europe 600 rallied in tandem with US markets. Vermeulen explained that international markets largely follow the S&P 500 due to heavy overlap in major multinational stocks. Therefore, the global rally is an extension of US market sentiment rather than a fundamentally different trend.

## Conclusion: Let the Market Digest

Vermeulen concluded that this is "only day one." Markets typically take three to four days to fully digest major events like elections. The initial moves are driven by emotion and FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out), which often leads to overreactions.

Investors should focus on following price action rather than predicting news outcomes. While the post-election rally is historically common, the underlying weakness in tech and the technical bear market in commodities suggest caution. The key question remains whether tech can hold its gains or if a breakdown will neutralize the broader market's upside potential.
