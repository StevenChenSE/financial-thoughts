The American market is displaying extreme bipolar characteristics—seemingly capable of launching into a historic bubble, with SpaceX serving as the starting pistol that signals the beginning of that surge. This paradox of simultaneous euphoria and genuine concern defines the current investment landscape, where 30-year Treasury yields have climbed to 19-year highs while equity markets continue their relentless rally. Understanding this dynamic—and what it means for your portfolio—may be the most important financial decision you make in the coming months.

## The Bond Market's Warning Signal

The confirmation of Kevin Worsh as the next Federal Reserve chair on May 13th has triggered a significant bond market selloff, with the US 30-year yield reaching just under 5.2%—a level not seen in nearly two decades. Simultaneously, the 10-year yield has jumped to 4.6% since the beginning of March. This dramatic shift raises a critical question: what exactly is the bond market telling us?

The answer, according to CLM Chambers, founder of CLM and veteran market observer, points to a confluence of factors:

- **Elevated inflation expectations** reflecting structural changes in the economy
- **Higher anticipated economic growth** driven by massive infrastructure spending
- **Potential bond market dysfunction** ahead if these trends persist

The uncertainty around Worsh's positioning adds another layer of complexity. While some reports characterize him as a hawk who views quantitative easing as "a big mistake," the actual policy response remains uncertain. "The solution to all these problems with interest rates is called QE," Chambers notes. "If you got a guy with his hand on the lever that refuses to pull it and says, 'No, no, no, let the bond market do its thing,' then that would be pretty interesting."

## Welcome to the New Capex Era

The real story, however, extends far beyond Fed policy. America is entering what Chambers calls "a very, very big change"—a fundamental restructuring of its economic foundations that will require unprecedented levels of capital expenditure.

Three massive forces are converging to suck capital out of the system:

1. **The onshoring of American industry** — After two generations of offshoring manufacturing capacity, the entire American supply chain must be rebuilt domestically. This isn't optional; it's a strategic imperative that demands astronomical funding.

2. **The AI buildout** — Artificial intelligence infrastructure requires enormous capital investment in data centers, power generation, cooling systems, and networking infrastructure. This isn't a niche sector; it's becoming the backbone of the modern economy.

3. **Corporate capital needs** — Normal companies across all sectors will find themselves competing for the same limited capital pool as these mega-projects. "If all the money gets sucked out for AI and for other projects, well they're going to need money too, aren't they?" Chambers observes. "So they're going to have to print some for them."

This combination creates what Chambers describes as an "inflationary printer" scenario. When you pair massive fiscal spending with a potentially hawkish Fed that refuses to monetize it through QE, the result is clear: higher interest rates and sustained inflation.

"The big deal is all these buildouts that are going to suck all the money out of the system and into basically capex—massive amounts of capex," Chambers emphasizes. "America's not used to massive amounts of capex. It's used to the opposite. And now it's got to reverse that trend."

## Investment Implications: Where the Opportunities Lie

Given this environment, where should investors actually put their money? Chambers has a clear framework:

### Second and Third-Order Beneficiaries of the Buildout

Rather than chasing the obvious plays that have already appreciated significantly, Chambers focuses on companies that supply the buildout participants:

- **Equipment suppliers** — Anyone making "big switches, transformers, and big cables" for the electricity grid infrastructure
- **Energy companies** — Particularly those involved in power generation, including nuclear
- **Commodities** — Especially copper, which Chambers views as having "a long way to run"

### The Copper Thesis

Chambers is particularly bullish on copper, which he sees as the critical enabler of the AI and onshoring buildouts. "Copper is going to be a much bigger deal," he says. "So that's got a long way to run. Anything copperish is good. Copper miners are going to be good."

However, he cautions against overly simplistic approaches. "You could buy a company that makes wires. You think they're going to want wires, but unfortunately the cost of the wires is going to go up so much they might not be able to pass the cost through."

### What to Avoid

- **Utilities** — While they stand to benefit from increased electricity demand, they're also targets for government criticism and regulation. "You're bad people. Stop that," Chambers mimics potential political attacks on utilities that can't keep pace with demand.
- **Obvious AI plays at current valuations** — Companies like Nvidia have already priced in enormous future growth, leaving less margin of safety.

## The American Market: Bubble Territory

Chambers doesn't hedge when asked about the current state of the equity market. "I think the American market is in a bubble now, which is a very risky situation and also a highly profitable one," he states flatly. "But you are guaranteed that there'll be a crash. It's just a question of when—and I think that's about two years away."

The SpaceX angle represents, in Chambers' view, "the biggest opportunity in markets of all time because it's the whole universe to play for." But when someone starts saying that publicly, it's also a warning sign. "That's the sign of the sort of enthusiasm that leads to people in a period of time going, 'Oh, it wasn't me. I didn't do it. They said he was going to do this and it's all their fault.'"

His bubble timing framework is specific:

- **Current phase**: We're already in bubble territory
- **Duration estimate**: 18 months to 2 years remaining
- **Exit signal**: "You'll know when it's got three or four months to run because there'll be a load of crazy IPOs come out of nowhere... Just like it was in 2000, you will see a load of crazy IPOs hit the bricks and that will be the time to leave."

## The Case for International Diversification

One of the most compelling segments of the conversation focuses on what traveling teaches us about investing. Chambers, who was interviewed from Hokkaido, Japan, draws striking observations:

- **Global progress is more advanced than Americans realize** — Istanbul Airport makes Heathrow look like a dump. Mumbai transforms every 90 days.
- **The UK market is deeply undervalued** — "One time sales, look at America, same company, four time sales. Something's wrong with that picture."
- **Value opportunities exist in overlooked markets** — The UK market is "completely broken. The companies aren't—companies are international, but they're operating in a local failing stock market and that makes those companies prone for takeovers."

This mispricing extends to the fundamental thesis. "I love the UK market which is completely broken," Chambers says. "I like to think that it's a good investment to buy the cheap ones and wait for them to be no longer cheap."

For investors with substantial assets, his geographic diversification framework includes:

- 25% in productive assets in your home country
- 25% in international equities
- 25% in cash held outside your country
- 25% in reserve "in your back pocket"

## Gold: From Asset to Signal

After a stellar performance in previous years, gold has underperformed the S&P 500 in 2024. Chambers, who sold near the top, has shifted his view: "Gold is a signal for me now, not an asset."

The reasoning involves complex geopolitical dynamics:

- **China doesn't want to be the global reserve currency** — Rather than accumulating yuan that would carry that responsibility, China pays trading partners like Iran and Russia in gold
- **Sanctions-driven gold flows** — Iran and Russia's use of gold as their "deposit account" in China represents approximately 16-18% of global annual gold production
- **The trade could evaporate** — If geopolitical tensions ease and these nations return to the global financial system, this demand source disappears

"If Iran played nice and was allowed to reenter the global fellowship, or for that matter Russia if that war ended and Russia made nice—that trade will go away, and that would be really, really, really negative for gold," Chambers explains.

His technical analysis suggests:

- Gold is in a "post-boom bubble situation"
- It could trade sideways for an extended period
- An aftershock rally is likely in approximately two years, "around the same time you think the stock market's going to crash"

## Prediction Markets and Sentiment

The conversation touched on prediction markets like Kowi, which allow trading on real-world events from economic data to political outcomes. While Chambers acknowledges their entertainment value, he's skeptical about their predictive power.

"The price is a sentiment indicator but not necessarily a predictor of the future," he says. "After all, if it was, people wouldn't play it, would they? Because there would be nothing that they could see that wasn't already in the price."

His view on market sentiment is characteristically blunt: "Sentiment will always get you in trouble because it's a group of men acting like one fool. When sentiment runs away from reality, that's where you've got opportunity to make money."

## Lessons from Intel: The Market's Inefficiency

One of the more surprising observations from Chambers involves his Intel trade:

- He bought Intel at $20, expecting it could reach $60
- The Gulf crisis and general market uncertainty caused him to exit at $45, which he considered exceptional
- The stock subsequently hit $120

"That surprised me because that tells you how badly wrong the American market can get something very obvious," Chambers reflects. "It shows you that it's quite a highly strong market and can move very quickly. And I would have expected the American market to be hyper-efficient, hyper-quick. And no, it's got all sorts of opportunities lying around in it."

This inefficiency extends to other names like Nokia, which also took much longer than expected for the market to properly value.

## The Bigger Picture: Geopolitics and Stability

Chambers sees recent global tensions as a potential catalyst for future stability:

- China has received a "wake-up call" over Taiwan
- America has received a wake-up call over China
- Russia is "about to get a wake-up call"
- The UAE's rapid progress on a pipeline bypassing the Strait of Hormuz demonstrates how "capitalism always finds a way"

"If they can do the right thing for their people and muddle along, we got a great future," Chambers says. "Absolutely great future. If they absolutely insist on messing about and increasing hostilities, then it's going to get really bad."

The technological advances occurring across all sectors—AI, space exploration, energy—create enormous productive potential. "As long as we don't go down the road of natural stupidity, the future is very, very rosy," he quips.

## Key Takeaways

1. **The bond market is signaling a fundamental shift**: With 30-year yields at 19-year highs and a potentially hawkish Fed, investors should prepare for an environment of structurally higher interest rates and persistent inflation driven by massive AI and onshoring capex requirements.

2. **Second-order beneficiaries are the play**: Rather than buying expensive AI infrastructure companies directly, look for suppliers—equipment makers, cable manufacturers, copper miners, and nuclear builders that enable the buildout without facing the same regulatory and competitive risks.

3. **Copper is the commodity to watch**: While gold has completed its run and serves better as a geopolitical signal than an investment, copper has "a long way to run" as the essential material for electrification and AI infrastructure.

4. **The bubble has room to run, but prepare for the exit**: Chambers sees 18 months to 2 years of continued upside before crazy IPOs signal it's time to exit. SpaceX represents the ultimate opportunity—and the ultimate warning sign.

5. **International diversification reveals hidden value**: The UK market trades at a fraction of US valuations for identical businesses, creating takeover candidates and historically cheap entry points for patient investors.
