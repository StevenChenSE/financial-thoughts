## Polar Stratospheric Clouds and Dynamic Weather

The next morning brings a surprise. Before sunrise, the world appears white, but then a spectacular display of colors emerges. The sky is filled with shades of purple and blue, accompanied by a rare phenomenon: polar stratospheric clouds. These clouds form at altitudes of 15 to 20 km when temperatures drop below minus 78 degrees Celsius. They catch the sun's rays, displaying a pearl-like luster. It is a rare sight, visible only in winter, and a testament to the unique atmospheric conditions of the Arctic.

However, the weather in the Lofoten Islands is notoriously unpredictable. Just as the sky clears, fog rolls in, followed by snow, then rain. The "blue time" in Norway, the period before sunrise, lasts much longer than in southern latitudes—about an hour and a half compared to half an hour elsewhere.
