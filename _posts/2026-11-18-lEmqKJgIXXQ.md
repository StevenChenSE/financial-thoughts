## Introduction

As stock markets decline from their October highs, renowned economist and Johns Hopkins University professor Steve Hanke returns to discuss the precarious state of the US economy. In a candid interview, Hanke reiterates his warning that the AI sector is in a massive bubble and predicts a potential 70% collapse in tech stocks. He argues that because the US economy is effectively "running on fumes" without AI investment, a crash in this sector could trigger a severe recession with no easy escape route for policymakers.

## The AI Bubble: Bigger Than Railroads?

Hanke’s primary concern is the disproportionate impact of the AI boom on US GDP. He reveals that while the economy grew at an annualized rate of 3.4% in the third quarter, **96% of that growth** was driven by AI investment. Excluding this sector, the rest of the economy is essentially flat, with critical sectors like housing already in a recession.

To illustrate the scale of this boom, Hanke compares current AI investment to historical technological shifts:
*   **AI Investment:** Currently accounts for **5.2% of all GDP**.
*   **Railroad Boom (1870–1890):** Accounted for only **2.2% of GDP** at its peak.

Hanke notes that the current AI boom is more than twice as large relative to GDP as the railroad era, a period that famously ended in significant bankruptcies and economic disruption. With stocks like Nvidia down approximately 40%, Intel down 62%, and CoreWeave down 80% from their peaks, the market is already signaling volatility.

## The Risk of Financial Crisis

Is this boom destined to bust? Hanke points to a 2022 study in the *Journal of Finance* by Greenwood et al., which analyzed 42 countries. The study found that when high rates of credit growth and valuation booms concentrate in one sector, there is a **40% probability** of a subsequent financial crisis.

Hanke argues that the conditions for a bust are forming:
1.  **Rising Interest Rates:** The cost of capital is increasing.
2.  **Credit Spreads:** The price of insuring against corporate bond defaults has risen significantly, indicating that bond investors are skeptical of the debt being issued to fund AI infrastructure.
3.  **Political Pushback:** Recent midterm elections showed voters rejecting data centers in ballot measures across the country, signaling potential regulatory and political headwinds for further expansion.

## No Rescue: The Policy Dilemma

If the AI bubble bursts and triggers a recession, Hanke warns that the US government lacks the tools to mitigate the damage effectively. He predicts a slow, painful recovery rather than a quick "V-shaped" rebound due to two major constraints:

*   **Fiscal Policy Constraints:** The US is already running at very high deficit levels. Increasing spending or cutting taxes to stimulate the economy would widen the deficit further, potentially causing a negative confidence shock in the bond market and driving yields even higher.
*   **Monetary Policy Constraints:** The Federal Reserve is still battling inflation. With the Divisia M4 money supply growing at **7.4%** (well above the 5–6% needed for a 2% inflation target), easing monetary policy too quickly could reignite inflation.

Hanke concludes that with neither fiscal nor monetary levers available to stimulate growth without adverse side effects, the US faces a "long slog" out of any potential recession.

Beyond domestic economics, Hanke touched on several geopolitical factors influencing market stability:

*   **Cybersecurity Concerns:** Recent outages at major banks like Chase, Bank of America, and Wells Fargo have raised fears of cyberattacks. Hanke suggests that as the US engages in proxy wars with Russia and direct conflict with Iran, infrastructure vulnerabilities are becoming critical risks.

*   **Iran Conflict:** Hanke argues that Iran has effectively won its conflict with the US, leading to a US military retreat from the region and complicating future security arrangements in the Gulf.

## The Paraguay Bank Collapse

The interview also covered the recent collapse of the digital bank Oeno in Paraguay, which froze accounts for 60% of the adult population. Hanke described it as a case of "crony capitalism," where the bank, linked to former President Santiago Peña, received massive government deposits and issued self-dealing loans. While concerning, Hanke reassured investors that contagion risk to the broader Latin American region is low due to Paraguay’s small economic footprint compared to giants like Brazil or Mexico.

## Summary

Steve Hanke’s analysis presents a stark warning: the US economy is dangerously over-reliant on AI investment. With a 40% historical probability of financial crisis following such credit booms, and with government policy tools constrained by deficits and inflation, a collapse in tech valuations could lead to a prolonged recession. Investors should watch for signs of credit tightening and political resistance to data centers as key indicators that the AI boom is nearing its peak.
