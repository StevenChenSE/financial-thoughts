### An Industry Veteran on Supply Constraints, Capital Discipline, and District-Scale Silver Development

While macroeconomic analysts often focus on currency debasement and central bank demand when forecasting precious metals, the supply side tells a crucial part of the story. Building a producing mine requires extensive capital, years of permitting, and technical discipline. In a detailed interview, Brad Langille, President and CEO of GoGold Resources (TSX: GGD), shared insights gained from over three decades of building mines in Mexico, examining whether the industry has reached "peak gold" and outlining GoGold's self-funded path to intermediate producer status.

---

## Has the Industry Reached Peak Gold?

The concept of "peak gold"—the point at which global mine output reaches its structural maximum—has gained renewed traction. Langille believes that even if global geological limits haven't been hit definitively, the sector has effectively reached peak production for the current cycle.

A decade of underinvestment in exploration and development by both majors and junior miners has left a pipeline gap. Because lead times for permitting, engineering, and constructing new deposits now span several years, higher spot prices cannot immediately translate into increased supply. Combined with monetary debasement and sustained central bank buying, this structural supply bottleneck creates one of the most compelling macro setups for precious metals in decades.

## Operating and Permitting in Mexico

Langille's 30-plus-year career has centered on Mexico, where he and his team have built four mines and recently broke ground on their fifth. While some foreign investors attach a higher risk premium to the jurisdiction, Langille highlights several pragmatic advantages:

* **Deep Local Talent:** Mexico possesses a rich mining heritage, providing world-class engineers and geologists without requiring heavy reliance on foreign technical labor.
* **Permitting Evolution:** While regulatory requirements have become more stringent, recent administrative processes under President Claudia Sheinbaum have addressed backlogs and cleared new major permits, including GoGold's Los Ricos South.
* **Community Relations:** Long-term operating success relies on genuine community buy-in and high environmental standards, such as dry-stack tailings and backfilling underground voids with cemented paste.

## GoGold's Growth Engine: Parral and Los Ricos

GoGold Resources operates a distinctive, dual-pronged business model combining a high-margin operating asset with district-scale development projects.

### 1. Parral Tailings
Operating continuously for 12 years, the Parral project reprocesses historical tailings using proprietary technology. The operation generates approximately $50 million to $70 million in after-tax free cash flow annually, providing the company with an internal funding engine.

### 2. Los Ricos District
Acquired in 2019, the Los Ricos district in Jalisco State covers over 24,000 hectares with extensive historical workings. 
* **Los Ricos South:** Currently under construction as a 2,000-tonne-per-day underground operation. The mine plan targets average grades around 330 g/t silver equivalent, producing doré bars on site. First production is scheduled for mid-2028.
* **Los Ricos North:** Advancing through preliminary economic assessments, planned as the sequential follow-up development funded by cash flow.
* **Production Potential:** Combined, Los Ricos North and South are projected to produce 15 to 17 million silver equivalent ounces annually, generating an estimated $400 million to $475 million in after-tax annual free cash flow.

Across its portfolio, GoGold's revenue composition stands at approximately 60% silver and 40% gold on a value basis.

## The Conservative Approach: Building with Cash

A hallmark of GoGold's corporate strategy is its robust balance sheet. With approximately $284 million in cash, ongoing operational cash flow from Parral, and zero debt, the company is fully funding the $227 million to $265 million initial capital expenditure for Los Ricos South internally.

By completing 85% of detailed engineering prior to breaking ground and locking in fixed-bid contracts, management aims to insulate the project from common industry pitfalls such as capital blowouts and unexpected timeline delays. 

Instead of pursuing speculative M&A or waiting passively for an acquisition, GoGold focuses on operational execution. By moving systematically up the Lassonde curve and derisking its assets through production, the company retains control of its corporate destiny while positioning itself to deliver substantial shareholder value.
