The market is hitting all-time highs, Bitcoin just reclaimed the $80,000 level, and retail investors are euphoric. Yet one of Wall Street's most seasoned traders sees a ominous storm gathering on the horizon.

Todd Bubba Horowitz—former Chicago Board of Trade floor trader and founder of BubbaTrading.com—appears on the David Lin Show from Consensus Miami with a stark warning: the current rally is a "retail rally," and when the big institutional players return, they may be selling into the strength. Bubba remains steadfast in his prediction: a potential 40-60% selloff is coming.

"I've been looking for that same 40 to 60%," Bubba explains. "But again, it's not going to be tomorrow. Until the markets give us an indication that they're going to be sold, we're looking at what I call a retail rally right now."

## The Anatomy of a Retail Rally

The market's current strength masks a troubling reality, according to Bubba. While equities and Bitcoin push higher, the big money isn't participating—at least not yet.

"When I was a floor trader, when the markets got a little bit too out of whack, the traders would walk out of the pit so that they would allow the markets to go where they were going to go and then come and participate. I believe that's what you're seeing now in the equity market—the big money is waiting on the sidelines looking more to sell than to buy right now."

This pattern reveals itself in the lack of institutional volume accompanying the price appreciation. The rally is being sustained by:

- Pension funds
- 401(k) contributions
- IRA money flows
- Retail momentum chasers

These participants only buy—they don't sell systematically. But when the major institutions return with their selling pressure, the equation changes dramatically.

## The Economic Undertow: What's Really Happening Beneath the Surface

Bubba is dismissive of political spin about economic strength. "I don't care that President Trump keeps touting how good the economy is. We are definitely seeing the K is getting smaller on the top end. We are seeing lack of jobs. We've seen housing prices starting to drop in certain areas of the country."

These aren't isolated issues—they're interconnected warning signs:

- The labor market is weakening, particularly at the higher end
- Real estate is softening in key markets
- Consumer spending is under pressure from persistent inflation
- AI technology is replacing jobs across multiple sectors

Bubba adds another dimension: "Besides oil, AI is replacing a lot of jobs as well. Meta is creating robots to do household work which will destroy another bit. What we're really doing with all of this technology and with the high price of oil is we're eliminating a lot of jobs because there's not a need to have those people working."

## Oil: The Selling Opportunity of a Lifetime

At the time of recording, oil traded around $92 per barrel. Bubba sees it falling much further—potentially into the $60s before year-end.

"If everything works out properly, I would expect to see oil back in the 60s before the year is out. That's what shows up in the chart. In December, oil is under $70 a barrel right now."

This prediction rests on a fundamental disconnect: "The economies under normal circumstances without the fear factor of the war cannot support the price of oil."

### The Demand Problem

The United States is exporting record quantities of oil, yet a supply glut persists. This imbalance signals insufficient demand to sustain current price levels. The ripple effects will cascade through:

- **Auto industry**: Already struggling, now facing customers who can't afford new vehicles or fuel
- **Farming sector**: Input costs remain elevated while margins compress
- **Consumer inflation**: Oil touches approximately 80% of economic activity—from driving to shipping to plastics manufacturing
- **Small businesses**: Transportation and operational costs squeeze profitability

"We're in pretty sad shape," Bubba states flatly. "There's a glut in the United States and we're exporting record numbers and we still have a glut because it's telling you that there's not enough demand."

## The AI Revolution: Changing the Trading Landscape

When David Lin asks about the fundamental shift in trading since Bubba's floor trading days, the transformation is stark. "There is no more floor traders. So there's no more true order flow or paper flow that a guy like me could read off of. You have a lot of bots trading, pushing money back and forth."

The institutional edge has grown dramatically. AI systems can analyze fundamental and technical data simultaneously, execute at superhuman speeds, and eliminate many independent traders from the equation. When asked at the conference whether retail traders could ever match institutional technology, a frank answer came back: "Whatever you think you have at home, Citadel and Jane Street have on steroids, and they've had it longer than you."

Yet Bubba sees a critical limitation in even the most sophisticated algorithms: "It still takes a market participant, the trader, the investor, the person on the street who's going to push that button to sell when they start to panic. Just because it's trading off of a bot or AI doesn't mean it's going to go directional in the same direction. We still have to have a price discovery mechanism. We're still going to have panics that will force markets much lower."

The human element—fear, greed, panic—cannot be fully engineered away. Markets will still make their violent moves.

## Reading Parabolic Moves: When to Sell the Breakouts

Recent market action has produced several dramatic surges. Intel soared 13% on rumored Apple chip discussions, having run up 160% since early April. Micron jumped 20% overnight on earnings. These parabolic moves trigger Bubba's sell signals.

"This is always a warning sign, David, when you get these kind of moves. This is very much like the '90s. You get these gigantic moves, everybody's happy, they're all over leveraged, and then suddenly something changes and really nothing changed, but the buyers have run out and the sellers have shown back up into the market."

For Bubba, the mathematics are clear: "The rate of change, the rate that it is moving up cannot be sustained. There's not enough money to sustain a move like that. Put up a chart of silver, put up a chart of crude, put up a chart of gold, put up any one of those, you see the same action. And I expect the same action in Micron and Intel and all the rest. These moves are unsustainable—very much as they were in '87 and '01 and of course in '08."

His approach when identifying parabolic moves:

- **Sell parabolic breakouts** to the upside
- **Buy blowoff bottoms** to the downside
- Look for the pattern that indicates momentum has exhausted itself

"This is no different than a bear market rally or a short squeeze. It's just turning the chart upside down. These moves are meant to be bought or sold."

## Hedging Strategies: Protecting Against the Big Move

For investors who disagree with Bubba's bearish outlook but want protection, options provide the solution. "I teach people: buy whenever you want. It doesn't matter because now I can use options and use what we call a 1x2 backspread against it. I have unlimited upside but I know to a mathematical certainty—for example, in Intel—I would be risking 3% to the downside."

The mechanics involve:

- Selling a put option
- Buying two put options for every 100 shares of stock
- Net short 100 shares but covered for much of the downside
- Can continue accumulating more shares as price falls, using the credit or coverage to hedge

"If Intel should probably fall 30 or 40% at some point here, but again, 10 years from now it'll probably be higher than it is right now."

This approach limits risk while maintaining upside exposure—a disciplined method for trading around core positions.

Despite the overall bearish outlook, specific markets warrant strategic positions:

"I think gold has made a bottom. That blowoff bottom back a couple weeks ago at $4,100 was it. I think you look to be a buyer of gold on all dips. I think that we're probably going to see $6,000 in gold before the year is over."

His framework for gold:

- Look to buy on all significant dips

"If it went to $6,000 in two months, I would do what I told you when it was up at $5,500—I'd be a seller. If it grinds up, which is what I expect it to do, then I'm going to be much more bullish."

## The Technical Trader's Toolkit: Moving Averages and Chart Reading

For Bubba, charts are maps that reveal market truth. His core framework:

- **Higher highs and higher lows**: Uptrend in place
- **Double tops and double bottoms**: Market in consolidation
- **Lower highs and lower lows**: Downtrend established
- **Blowoff patterns**: Meant to be sold (top or bottom)

The practical tool he recommends: moving averages. "Pick a moving average—whether it's the 20-day, 50-day, or 200-day. The markets themselves base off the 200-day moving average. The old Dow theory is: be long above the 200-day moving average and be short below it."

A simple system for any market:

1. Buy when price holds above your chosen moving average
2. Hold until price breaks below that average
3. Exit and look for re-entry opportunities

"When markets get too far overextended, they come back and revert to the mean."

## Risk Management: The Golden Rules of Survival

When asked to define risk, Bubba's answer is layered:

**From an investment perspective:**
"Risk is what you can afford. Investing capital should be money that you can put away and sustain through a massive meltdown in the market and not panic. Most people have trouble doing that."

**From a trading perspective:**
"Your risk is determined by your time frame. If I'm trading on a 4-minute time frame, I'm willing to take a couple hundred dollars worth of risk. If I'm trading on a daily time frame, I'm willing to take a couple thousand dollars worth of risk."

His non-negotiable rules:

- "Your ability to make money should be bigger than the amount of risk that you're willing to take"
- "Never use the leverage they give you. Just because you can buy X amount of futures or X amount of options doesn't mean that you should buy them because leverage is great when it's going in your favor but extremely painful when it's not"
- "Too many people have a tendency to get overleveraged in markets trying to get the big score"
- "Trading is a grind of earning the right to trade bigger size"

The line between trading and gambling? "You have to be willing to take the loss and not bet more than you can afford. You have to be able to have a loss and be able to feed your family, pay your bills, pay your mortgage."

Bubba's thesis weaves together multiple threads:

1. The economy is fundamentally weak despite political messaging
2. Oil prices cannot sustain current levels without geopolitical fear premium
3. AI is destroying jobs faster than new positions are being created
4. The big institutional money is positioned to sell, not buy
5. Parabolic moves always revert to the mean
6. The retail crowd—the last buyers—typically arrives at the worst possible time

"This is very much like the '90s. You get these gigantic moves, everybody's happy, they're all over leveraged, and then suddenly something changes."

The S&P 500 looks structurally similar to the Intel chart—both extended, both running on momentum with thin institutional support. "I think this is the biggest rally in history in the period of time we've had. And certainly I'm not going to tell you it's going down tomorrow, but certainly I'm looking for a place and a reason to sell it."

2. **A 40-60% market correction remains Bubba's base case**: This won't happen tomorrow, but the conditions are building. The parabolic moves in individual stocks (Intel, Micron) are warning signs of excess leverage and euphoria.
