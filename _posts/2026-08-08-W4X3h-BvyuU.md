## The Unprecedented US Move to Save the Yen

The US government has intervened in currency markets on a scale not seen since the 1998 Asian Financial Crisis, buying Japanese yen to prevent a collapse. This move comes after warnings from investor Michael Gayed, founder of Lead Lag Media, who predicted in 2023 that Japan's currency issues would trigger a global financial unraveling. While markets initially cheered the intervention as a stabilizing force, Gayed argues this is merely a temporary fix for a much deeper structural problem. He believes the US is trying to prevent Japan from selling US Treasuries to support its own currency—a move that would spike US interest rates and trigger a domestic crisis.

## The Reverse Carry Trade and the Threat to US Treasuries

At the heart of Gayed’s thesis is the "reverse carry trade." For years, investors have borrowed cheaply in Japanese yen to buy higher-yielding US assets. As the yen weakened and US rates remained high, this trade became extremely profitable. However, Gayed warns that if the yen continues to fall, Japan will be forced to sell billions of dollars in US Treasuries to buy yen and support its currency. This would flood the market with US debt, causing yields to spike and triggering a credit crunch in the US. The recent US intervention is an attempt to prevent this scenario, but Gayed believes it cannot succeed in the long run because the fundamental interest rate differential still favors the carry trade.

## A Currency Crisis Will Precede the Next Stock Market Crash

History shows that every major equity crisis is preceded by a currency crisis. Gayed points out that while the US and Japanese governments are trying to manipulate the market, they cannot change the underlying economic fundamentals driving the yen lower. He expects the yen to eventually retrace its recent gains, leading to a panic that will spill over into global stock markets. Unlike previous crises, this one is driven by the insolvency of Japan’s banking system, which relies heavily on Japanese Government Bonds (JGBs) as collateral. If Japan raises interest rates to support the yen, it will expose massive losses in its banking sector, creating a "Godzilla margin call" scenario that could last longer and be more painful than the brief market crash seen in August 2024.

## Oil, Iran, and the Hidden Inflation Threat

Beyond the yen, Gayed is highly bullish on oil due to the ongoing conflict in Iran. He dismisses the idea that the war will end quickly, drawing parallels to the Iraq War, and warns that any disruption to oil supplies could cause a sharp price spike. He suggests that Iran could even bomb its own oil fields to hurt the US economy, as oil is a critical input for American consumers. A sudden spike in oil prices could lead to higher inflation or, if the move is too fast, a deflationary shock as companies cut costs to survive. This geopolitical risk adds another layer of uncertainty to the global economy, making oil and energy stocks attractive investments with positive tail risk.

## Why Tech and AI Stocks Are at Risk

Gayed also expresses deep skepticism about the current tech and AI boom. He believes that AI will soon face severe regulation after a major, unforeseen incident—such as a cyberattack or harmful decision by an AI model—captures public attention. This regulation could drastically reduce the profitability of AI companies, causing a repricing of tech stocks. Since the S&P 500 is now heavily concentrated in tech, a decline in this sector could drag down the entire index. Gayed advises investors to diversify away from tech-heavy indices and consider small-cap and international stocks, which have shown resilience.

## How to Position Your Portfolio for the Crisis

To prepare for a potential reverse carry trade unwind and the associated market turmoil, Gayed recommends a defensive portfolio focused on assets that benefit from a flight to safety. He suggests going long on:
*   **Japanese Yen:** After another attempt at the lows, as the carry trade unwinds.
*   **Gold:** As a hedge against currency devaluation and geopolitical risk.
*   **US Treasuries:** Specifically longer-duration bonds, which will rise in value if yields drop during a crisis.
*   **Oil:** To protect against upside shocks from geopolitical tensions.

Gayed acknowledges that this view is unpopular, especially with Treasuries, but believes that hated asset classes often offer the best returns during crises. He urges investors to ignore the complacency in the stock market and prepare for a potential global financial event that could reshape the economic landscape.
