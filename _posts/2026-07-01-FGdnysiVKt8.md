## Technical analyst reveals his strategic approach to precious metals in volatile market

The gold market is experiencing one of its most turbulent periods in recent memory. After breaking below the critical $4,000 level for the first time since November, the precious metal is testing key support zones that will determine its next major move. Christopher Muan, chief market strategist at The Technical Traders, recently shared his detailed technical analysis on Kitco News, offering a contrarian perspective that challenges the conventional "hold at all costs" wisdom.

Unlike most market commentators who advocate holding gold through volatility, Muan sold near the top and is now sitting in cash, waiting for a specific price level before re-entering. His approach is built entirely on technical analysis, discipline, and risk management rather than macroeconomic narratives.

---

## The $4,000 Line in the Sand

When gold broke below $4,000 last week, it signaled significant damage to the chart, according to Muan. "When we zoom back on the daily chart, we really saw precious metals obviously early this year just go completely ballistic," he explained. "It broke out of a bull flag chart pattern and everyone really just chased it and drove it higher."

The current price action represents a classic euphoric phase correction. The bull flag pattern that propelled gold higher has now reversed, creating sharp movements that are testing investor resolve. Muan emphasizes that the chart is now at a critical juncture where the next move could be decisive.

"This is a very significant pivot point for gold right now," Muan stated. "It is clinging on to breaking through this consolidation that we saw late last year. We had a spike low early in March. It had a bounce there a few weeks ago and right now it is—if it lets loose here, we're going to see it unwind fairly quickly."

---

## Fibonacci Analysis Points to $3,600

Using Fibonacci theory, which Muan describes as "based on kind of a power in the universe that more or less tells you based on one movement and its reaction, it shows you where the next two likely movements are to be," the technical picture becomes clearer.

According to Muan's analysis, gold has pulled back to the 618 Fibonacci level, also known as the golden ratio. "This is the most powerful kind of value really in the universe," he noted. "If you pause and bounce at the 618, which we have, we almost always see a commodity or asset continue to move to the 100% measured move."

The target based on this analysis is approximately $3,600. "Right now, the chart of gold is looking like it wants to go to about $3,600. That is going to be a sweet spot in terms of—if it drops down to this level, to me, it's fair value or it's undervalued. I think it's a great long-term investment."

The strategy is straightforward: Muan and his team exited gold above $5,000 and are now waiting to reload at the $3,600 level before accumulating physical metals again.

---

## The Long-Term Vision: $8,600

Despite identifying significant downside risk in the short term, Muan remains bullish on gold's long-term trajectory. "When gold gets its traction here, the next upside target should be around $8,000 to about $8,600 for gold," he predicted. "So there's lots of upside potential. There's a nice double from pretty much where we are right now."

This long-term perspective aligns with what Muan calls a "giant bull flag pattern pointing to much higher pricing" when viewing the chart from a broader timeframe. However, he cautions that timing matters significantly because gold and silver have historically taken 5, 10, 14 years or even longer to return to previous highs.

"I like to move my money into an asset that is generating return while that commodity is figuring itself out," Muan explained. This disciplined approach allows him to potentially buy back in at much lower prices while capital works elsewhere.

---

## Silver's More Volatile Path

Silver, which dropped about 2% more than gold on the day of the interview, presents an even more dramatic technical picture. "Silver is really volatile," Muan noted. "It has a very significant support level. It was been trying to hold through this $65. It has clearly broken down."

Following the same Fibonacci methodology, silver has hit the 618 level multiple times and is now heading toward the 100% measured move. "This is a long way down from where we are. This is $40 silver," Muan warned, though he emphasizes this represents an eventual buying opportunity.

The upside target for silver, once it finds traction, is approximately $165-$175 per ounce. Muan previously sold silver at $111 and is watching for entry opportunities at significantly lower levels.

---

## The Dollar's Role in Precious Metals Weakness

One of the key drivers behind gold and silver weakness is the US dollar's strength. "We've been looking for the dollar saying, 'Hey, if the dollar breaks to the upside and breaks out of this multi-year kind of base, look out because metals could get hit very hard,'" Muan explained.

The dollar has broken out of a significant base and is pointing toward approximately 109, representing roughly a 9% move higher. "It also might mean the stock market and the economy actually might be starting to come to an end here, and we might start to see a big sell-off," Muan added.

This inverse relationship between the dollar and precious metals has been playing out historically, with dollar strength typically correlating with metals weakness.

---

## S&P 500 at a Crossroads

The stock market presents a mixed technical picture. "There's no doubt we're still in a bull market," Muan stated. "The short-term trend is still up for the S&P 500, and the long-term trend is still up. We've had a series of bounces."

However, Muan noted that the market is showing signs of exhaustion. His team has moved entirely to cash, waiting to see if the current level represents a temporary top or if the market will continue higher.

The potential exists for additional upside: the S&P could rally approximately 13% and the NASDAQ about 20% before potentially reaching a final euphoric phase. "I think it could be like maybe that final euphoric phase where everybody kind of piles in, and we start to see a big sell-off," Muan suggested.

---

## The Japanese Yen Warning

Muan also highlighted concerns about the Japanese yen's decline to a 40-year low, despite Japan's reported spending of tens of billions to prop it up. "This world is extremely fragile," Muan warned. "We've got a raging bull market in smaller micro caps and technology right now, but I mean, we are it's super delicate."

The potential for a carry trade unwind similar to what occurred in 2024 could shock multiple markets, including gold. "When a major currency breaks down like that, is there a bigger danger here? A blow up or kind of a carry trade unwind?" Muan asked rhetorically.

---

## Risk Management: The Real Edge

Throughout the interview, Muan emphasized that the biggest mistakes investors make stem from poor risk management and emotional decision-making. "The biggest thing for me is protect our time, protect our capital, and make sure—if our money's at work, whatever we put it to work in, it's got to meet rules in our strategy, our criteria to put our money at risk."

His personal approach includes always having an exit plan and stop orders in place. "We always have a position. We always have an exit plan. We always have an order placed saying, 'Hey, if there's a catastrophe and things just start to drop and collapse, we want to make sure we get exited on the way down.'"

Muan's own experience with bankruptcy and blown-up accounts years ago taught him the importance of preserving capital. "It is like takes a bad trade and you blew up a huge chunk of your account," he recalled. "So, I focus very very much so on risk management."

The key psychological barrier many investors face is the reluctance to take losses. "People just look at a loss as taking a hit and losing money," Muan explained. "For their ego's sake, don't want to take a loss because it means you're wrong. Unfortunately, we're going to be wrong all the time in the markets because we don't know where it's going."

---

## Seasonality and Smart Money Flows

Looking ahead, Muan sees seasonal patterns potentially reinforcing current weakness. "We're in a seasonality time where we see June, July, well, right through to October, we tend to see both precious metals and the stock market start to lose their shine," he noted. "That's what happens on average."

Additionally, smart money is visibly rotating from growth stocks to defensive sectors. "We are definitely seeing money looking for safety, smart money, and it's coming out of the Magnificent Seven," Muan observed. "Utilities have been going up."

Large institutional investors who must remain invested are moving to "slower sectors" like utilities, which provide steady demand regardless of economic conditions. "It doesn't matter what happens in the world. We need electricity. We need running water and all those things," Muan explained.

## Key Takeaways

Muan's analysis offers a disciplined, contrarian approach to precious metals investing:

1. **Gold downside target:** $3,600 based on Fibonacci analysis at the 618 golden ratio level
2. **Gold upside target:** $8,000-$8,600 once the correction completes
3. **Silver outlook:** Breaking down toward $40 before potential rally to $165-$175
4. **Dollar strength:** Breaking out toward 109, pressuring metals
5. **Stock market:** Potentially one more rally to euphoric highs before significant correction
6. **Strategy:** Wait for specific price levels rather than chasing momentum

For those holding physical gold as insurance rather than a trade, Muan acknowledges these signals may not apply. But for active traders and investors, his approach emphasizes that patience, discipline, and technical analysis can provide significant advantages in volatile markets.

As Muan put it: "The only thing you can really do is wait for it to start to leave the station and be like, 'Oh my god, that's going in the direction I want to go.' And you hop on it. I would rather jump into something even at a higher price and just catch the big move and have kind of instant gratification and growth than just sit on it and it goes the wrong way for a while."
