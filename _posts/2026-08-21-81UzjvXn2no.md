## Gold and Bitcoin: News-Driven Bounces vs. True Trends

The recent surge in gold and Bitcoin has been fueled by the same news cycle that affected the bond market. Gold rallied to nearly $4,580, while Bitcoin surged over 15% to break above $72,000. Despite these impressive gains, Vermeulen remains skeptical of the sustainability of this rally.

He emphasizes that both assets are currently in a long-term downtrend, characterized by a series of lower highs and lower lows. The 150-day moving average, which serves as his long-term cycle indicator, is sloping down, with price trading below it. For Vermeulen, this is the definition of a correction phase. He warns that news-driven moves create "FOMO" (Fear Of Missing Out), leading to parabolic spikes that often unwind quickly.

To confirm a true bullish shift, Vermeulen looks for two key factors:
1.  **Higher Pricing:** Price must break above previous resistance levels.
2.  **More Time:** The move must be sustained over a longer period, ideally following a "staircase" pattern rather than a sharp spike.

He notes that the 20-day and 50-day moving averages need to cross above the 150-day moving average to signal a change in the major cycle. Until then, he remains on the sidelines, viewing the current rally as a "relief rally" or a bounce within a downtrend.

## Trading Strategy: Asset Revesting and Risk Management

Vermeulen’s trading philosophy is centered on "Asset Revesting," a strategy that involves holding assets only when they are rising in value. He does not believe in holding assets that are in a downtrend, as this defies the purpose of investing. His approach involves:
*   **Trend Following:** Entering trades only when the long-term trend is up.
*   **Statistical Targets:** Using historical data to determine where an asset is likely to run before hitting resistance.
*   **Scaling Out:** Trimming profits as price targets are hit to reduce exposure.
*   **Capital Preservation:** Prioritizing the protection of capital over maximizing returns in a bull market.

He also discusses his book, *After the Rally*, which explores the risks of traditional investing strategies like buy-and-hold and dividend reinvestment. He argues that these strategies carry significant risk in the later years of an investor’s life, as a single major correction can wipe out decades of growth. The book aims to educate investors on the "other side" of the market, highlighting the importance of adapting to changing market conditions.
