Following recent midterm elections, market dynamics displayed an unusual divergence. Consequently, distrust in governments, political classes, and banking systems is driving investors toward assets free from these ties: physical metals.

## The "Quickening" of Gold and Silver Demand

Morgan describes the current state of precious metal demand as the "quickening." He notes that while central banks have been buying gold hand over fist for some time, retail and institutional participation has historically lagged. Morgan uses a walking analogy to describe this progression:

1.  **Soft Walk:** Central bank accumulation begins.
2.  **Brisk Walk:** Awareness grows; investors begin to distrust bonds (e.g., Japan selling Treasuries to boost the Yen).
3.  **Jog/Run/Sprint:** Mass migration into physical metals as the system destabilizes.

Morgan believes we are currently at the "brisk walk" phase, where market participants are realizing that bonds are no longer a reliable store of value during crises.

## The Threat of Bank Bail-Ins and Capital Controls

Perhaps the most alarming warning from Morgan concerns access to funds in banks. He draws parallels to historical events in Argentina, Cyprus, Lebanon, and recently Paraguay, where banks imposed strict withdrawal limits or confiscated deposits.

Morgan emphasizes that having money "in the bank" does not mean you have control over it. In a crisis, authorities can dictate withdrawal terms (e.g., limiting withdrawals to $2,000 per month regardless of account balance) or execute "bail-ins," where depositor funds are used to recapitalize failing banks. He notes that FDIC insurance limits ($250,000) do not protect against capital controls or bail-ins that affect even insured amounts.
