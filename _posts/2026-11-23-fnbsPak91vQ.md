For over a century, the Premier deposit in Northern British Columbia held the title of North America's richest gold mine. Today, it stands silent, its massive SAG mill quiet as a new management team works to correct past mistakes and restart production. This is the story of Cambria Gold Mines, a company navigating the "Lassonde Curve" to transform historic assets into a modern, high-grade mining operation targeting production in late 2027.

## The Hub-and-Spoke Strategy: Premier and Red Mountain

Cambria Gold’s strategy relies on a "hub-and-spoke" model centered around the existing Premier mill. The hub is the 3,000-ton-per-day SAG mill, which requires significant ore throughput to operate efficiently. Historically, Premier alone produced only about 400 tons per day at its peak, insufficient to feed this modern facility.

To solve this, Cambria is developing the Red Mountain deposit, located near the Cambri Ice Field at a mile-high elevation. Discovered in the Mark Zone, Red Mountain boasts 544,000 ounces of gold at nearly 9 grams per tonne. It is described as the best high-grade underground bulk-minable gold deposit in Canada. With ore bodies up to 25 meters wide, Red Mountain will provide the necessary tonnage via underground stopes.

Ore from Red Mountain will be transported down the mountain first by road and later by an aerial tram line to the valley bottom, then shipped to the Premier mill in Stewart, BC. This combination of Premier’s high-grade veins and Red Mountain’s bulk tonnage is expected to ramp production up to 150,000 ounces per year.

## Correcting Past Mistakes: From Ascot Resources to Cambria Gold

The current crisis was precipitated by the failure of the previous operator, Ascot Resources. According to CEO David Tait, who grew up in Stewart and has family ties to the mine dating back over 100 years, Ascot’s management made fundamental errors that led to insolvency with $80 million in unpaid liabilities.

Tait identifies three critical failures by the previous regime:
1. **Geological Mismanagement:** Ascot created an incorrect geological model, failing to drill at the required 12.5-meter spacing. Without precise modeling of wiggling veins and breccias, miners risk striking solid rock instead of ore.
2. **Infrastructure Imbalance:** They built a "crown jewel" mill but neglected underground development, leaving insufficient working faces to feed the plant.
3. **Financial Mismanagement:** The company leased equipment and facilities unnecessarily, deferring capital costs into operating expenses, which drained cash flow.

Cambria negotiated a rescue package with creditors rather than allowing bankruptcy. A key reason for avoiding bankruptcy was preserving Red Mountain’s environmental assessment certificate from 2018. Under BC law, projects must be "substantially started" within 10 years or re-permit from scratch—a delay that could have killed the project.

## The Lassonde Curve and Market Context

Investors often view development-stage miners through the lens of the Lassonde Curve, a concept named after Pierre Lassonde, founder of Franco-Nevada. The curve illustrates how share prices rise with discovery, fall during the risky development phase (the trough), and surge again upon commercial production. Cambria is currently in this trough, where capital expenditures are high but revenue is zero.

With a market cap around $330 million and $122 million in cash as of late September, Cambria faces a "show me" story. The company has raised approximately $250 million including warrant proceeds, which may leave them slightly short of total capital needs due to unforeseen working capital requirements for harsh winter conditions.

## Beyond BC: Mount Margaret and US Critical Minerals

While the focus is on British Columbia, Cambria holds a significant asset in Washington State: Mount Margaret. Tait describes it as potentially the most important undeveloped copper-gold deposit in America. Located north of Mount St. Helens, it has a historic resource of 570 million tons at 0.72% copper equivalent.

Recent drilling has doubled the depth extent of this resource, revealing continuous copper and gold mineralization. The US government’s push for domestic critical minerals supply chains makes Mount Margaret strategically vital. Cambria is utilizing the federal "FAST-41" permitting process, which mandates timely regulatory reviews, to accelerate development on these federally administered lands.

## Operational Reality: Geology in Action

Inside the core shack at Premier, geologists work tirelessly to ensure data integrity. They analyze drill core for indicator minerals like galena (lead), sphalerite (zinc), and pyrite (iron sulfide). While gold is rarely visible, "VG" (visible gold) intercepts of 500 grams per tonne confirm high-grade zones.

The team uses advanced logging templates to study breccias, a key host for mineralization. By combining technical logging with engineering data like fracture counts and hardness tests, they build the precise models needed for safe and profitable underground mining.

## Capital Structure and Future Outlook

Cambria’s capital structure includes $30 million in debt (paid in shares) and $24 million in converts. The largest shareholder is a Peruvian private mining family owning 36%, providing strong strategic support. Other institutional investors include Franklin Templeton and the Fury Group.

Energy costs are among the lowest in the world, with BC Hydro providing prepaid power infrastructure. The primary remaining capital expenditures are road construction to Red Mountain, additional underground development at Premier and Silver Coin, and environmental compliance, particularly water treatment.

If all goes according to plan, the quiet mill at Premier will roar back to life in Q4 2027. For CEO David Tait, whose grandfather worked these same tunnels, it is not just a business turnaround; it is the restoration of a family legacy and a return to production for one of history’s most prolific gold mines.
