*By Danny, Capital Cosm | March 13, 2026*

In a world where geopolitical conflict typically sends investors rushing toward gold and silver, analyst Eric Jung presents a more nuanced—and contrarian—view. In a recent interview, Jung argued that the ongoing war in Iran and the Middle East may not trigger the surge in precious metals prices that many expect. Instead, he outlined a complex, multi-phase scenario where short-term pressures could even depress prices before any sustained rally takes hold.

## The Current Reality: A War of Attrition

Jung began by grounding his analysis in the observable facts on the ground. “The war is dragging on,” he stated, noting that initial plans for a quick resolution have faltered. Despite reported successes against Iranian air and naval assets, attacks on Israel and U.S. assets continue daily. This grinding stalemate forms the backdrop for his metals thesis.

A critical flaw in mainstream analysis, according to Jung, is the assumption that the Strait of Hormuz is completely closed, halting oil exports. “In reality, we’re seeing that Iran is actually shipping a lot of oil to China and apparently they are now shipping stuff to Russia and also to India,” he explained. Satellite imagery and Chinese confirmations suggest the strait may still be operating at 25-50% of pre-war capacity.

## Phase One: The Dollar Scramble

If significant oil is still flowing, Jung’s first-phase theory activates. “The bigger participants, the oil importing countries, they might try to sell a lot of their assets for US dollars,” he said. The reason? To buy whatever marginal oil is still exiting the strait, which is priced in dollars.

This creates a counterintuitive dynamic: a rush into the U.S. dollar, not out of it. “Sell US dollar assets that includes gold and silver, get US dollars, and buy as much of the marginal oil denominated in US dollars.” This, Jung argues, is supported by a strong U.S. Dollar Index (DXY) hitting 100 and spiking bond yields. “We’re in the middle of that what I call phase one.”

## Phase Two: The Gold Pivot & Sulfuric Acid Squeeze

Once the initial panic subsides and a grim equilibrium sets in, Jung envisions a shift. Oil-producing Gulf states, witnessing a perceived failure of U.S. military protection, may begin diversifying their dollar proceeds into physical gold. “That then we might actually get a physical gold price ramp up in the second phase.”

Simultaneously, a severe second-order effect emerges: a sulfuric acid crisis. Most of the world’s sulfuric acid is a byproduct of refining sour crude oil from the Middle East. With exports potentially halved, sulfuric acid production plummets. This is catastrophic for industrial metals, as Jung detailed:

> “Sulfuric acid is a very important agent that… metals refiners, mostly base metals… use to extract the metals out of the ore.”

This shortage could initially create a “rush” for copper, zinc, and uranium, and by extension, silver—which is largely mined as a byproduct of these base metals. This could benefit primary silver producers. “There’s a ramp up between now and then… it’s a race to make that critical electronics, critical solar panels, critical weapons.”

## Phase Three: The Industrial Collapse Scenario

Here, Jung’s analysis takes a darker turn. If the conflict worsens and oil flow drops below 20%, the sulfuric acid crunch becomes severe.

> “You have a severe supply crunch of the sulfuric acid and then your base metals refinement is in serious trouble, right? Extraction and refinement in serious trouble.”

The world would then burn through above-ground copper stockpiles for a few months. Once depleted, industrial production could crash. This is where silver faces a paradoxical threat:

> “Then it might it might crash the price of silver because if you can’t make solar panels or like EVs because you don’t have the copper then you don’t need the silver even if the silver is available.”

Jung criticizes experts who skip from the war’s start directly to this apocalyptic stage, missing the intervening phases of scramble and scramble-induced demand.

## Phase Four: The New Equilibrium & Monetary Reset

Eventually, supply chains would adapt—building rail lines, bypassing the strait, with Russia increasing market share. In this new equilibrium, Jung believes the fundamental loss of faith in U.S. security guarantees would catalyze a lasting shift.

> “Gold and silver would accelerate their rise in prices because… the world realizes that Uncle Sam can’t really protect them. So why would they hold US dollars?… US dollars may simply become a trade token like a hot potato. Once they get the US dollar, they convert it into physical gold, physical silver, and other precious commodities.”

## Base Case vs. Bull Case

When asked for a base case, Jung pointed to the ongoing stalemate. “What I just told the audience is probably the base case of what’s going to happen. It’s already happening.” He noted gold at $5,080 and silver at $82 (front-month futures), with oil (Brent) capped around $100.

The bull case? A swift end to the war. But even then, “the US loses credibility… and then countries just diversify more into gold and silver.” However, he views this quick resolution as less likely.

## Personal Strategy: Hold, Don’t Trade

Given the extreme uncertainty and volatile, rumor-driven news flow—like speculation about Benjamin Netanyahu’s status—Jung’s personal strategy is straightforward: hold.

> “I’m holding on to all my gold and silver… I think it’s extremely difficult to trade this stuff… It’s pure gambling at this point.”

For those under-allocated, he suggests dollar-cost averaging, as pinpointing a bottom is impossible. But for himself, “I’m not buying.”

## Conclusion: A Web of Complexities

Jung’s analysis underscores that in a globalized economy, war’s impact on commodities is not a simple matter of “risk on, gold up.” It’s a tangled web of energy flows, industrial chemistry, currency dynamics, and geopolitical trust. The path for gold and silver, therefore, may be a turbulent journey through several distinct phases—including potential downdrafts—before any lasting flight-to-safety rally can truly begin.
