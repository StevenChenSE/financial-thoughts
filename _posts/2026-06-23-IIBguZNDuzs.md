## Extreme Valuations Signal Historic Bubble Ready to Burst

Renowned economist and chief market strategist Peter Schiff has issued a stark warning about the US markets, arguing that current valuations have reached extremes never seen before in history. Speaking on his show, Schiff contends that while markets have been "melting up," the eventual correction will be severe.

"The valuations are at extremes that have never existed before," Schiff stated. "We've had excess valuations and they've always ended the same way with a major decline. This time the valuations are even more extreme than they've been at prior peaks."

## The SpaceX IPO: A Bell Ringer for Peak Insanity?

Schiff points to SpaceX's recent IPO as a potential harbinger of market froth. The company went public trading at over 100 times revenue with a trillion-dollar-plus market cap—extraordinary metrics for a company of its size.

"They only sold about 4% of the company in the IPO," Schiff explained. "So we're determining the price of all the shares based on the 4% that are available to trade. It really allows for an excess valuation."

Schiff believes this signals peak insanity in the markets, noting that such valuations have historically preceded major downturns.

## The Crypto Collapse: A Preview of What's to Come

Schiff argues that the crypto market provides a crucial leading indicator for broader market bubbles. "Bitcoin and most of crypto have no earnings. They have nothing. They have no underlying value. It's all just hype. It's all greater fool."

Bitcoin has already declined approximately 50% from its peak of $126,000 to around $64,000. More significantly, Schiff notes that Bitcoin is now lower than it was in April 2021—meaning the cryptocurrency has gone nowhere for five years.

## Strategy's Implosion: A Canary in the Coal Mine

Schiff dedicates significant attention to Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy), calling it "the poster boy" for crypto excess. The stock has plummeted roughly 70% and now trades at a 25-30% discount to its underlying Bitcoin per share value.

The company's founder Michael Saylor continues selling stock to purchase more Bitcoin, a strategy Schiff finds fundamentally flawed: "Why would you sell your stock for 80 cents and then only have 80 cents to buy Bitcoin and lose Bitcoin per share? What is the point?"

Schiff explains that Saylor originally generated "Bitcoin yield" by selling stock at a premium and using excess proceeds to buy Bitcoin. But now, with the stock trading at a discount, he's destroying Bitcoin per share while maintaining the illusion of yield.

"He has a negative yield now," Schiff emphasized. "The only purpose is so he can keep buying Bitcoin to prop up the price, but he has to sacrifice his own shareholders to do it. So that's the game over."

Strategy's preferred shares, which pay 11%, now trade below $90 when par value is $100, creating additional financial pressure Saylor cannot easily address without raising dividends.

## Japan's Role as the Flash Point

While the discussion covers multiple market concerns, Schiff suggests Japan could be the next trigger for a US market crisis. Japanese monetary policy and its interactions with global markets represent a significant risk factor that investors may be underestimating.

## Dollar Strength vs. Traditional Havens

The interview also addresses the curious phenomenon of the US dollar strengthening while both gold and Bitcoin decline simultaneously. Schiff suggests this isn't merely a crypto story but represents a rotation into the dollar amid broader uncertainty.

## The Iran War: Strategic Failure

Schiff offers a sharp critique of the Iran policy, arguing the US lost strategically despite military operations. "We threatened to get rid of the regime and the regime is still standing," he noted. The resulting deal economically empowers Iran through oil sales, investment capital, and shipping fees—with no meaningful restrictions on how Tehran spends its money.

## The Federal Reserve's Inflation Trap

Addressing monetary policy, Schiff argues that inflation is fundamentally a choice made by central bankers. "We choose inflation. Powell chose inflation. Yellen chose inflation. Bernanke, Alan Greenspan—he wrote the playbook."

Schiff contends Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Walsh will make the same choice: "Because if you choose not to have inflation, then you choose a massive recession, a huge drop in stocks, real estate, bonds—maybe you choose a financial crisis."

## The Government Spending Crisis

Schiff highlights the unsustainable fiscal trajectory: "The government is about 50% bigger now than it was before COVID. It's spending 50% more money. We're spending like $7.5 trillion a year, but we're barely collecting $5 trillion a year in taxes."

"Where's that extra money coming from? It's debt and it's being monetized," he continued. "If the Fed doesn't create that debt, there's no way the government can service it."

## Energy Sector: The Hidden Winner

Despite broader economic concerns, Schiff sees US oil companies as positioned to benefit from higher oil prices resulting from Middle East instability. Higher prices are good for oil companies, and Schiff owns shares in several US producers.

However, he cautions that the broader American economy won't benefit equally: "The benefits of higher oil are concentrated in the oil companies and people who own stock in oil companies. The majority of Americans don't produce oil. Most Americans are going to suffer higher oil prices."

Additional pressure comes from historically low strategic petroleum reserves and surging energy demand from AI data center construction.

## Looking Ahead

Schiff believes the market has yet to experience the full reckoning that extreme valuations and fiscal imbalances will eventually demand. While timing remains uncertain, the fundamentals suggest the "meltup" cannot continue indefinitely without a corresponding "meltdown."
