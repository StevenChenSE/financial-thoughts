## Navigating Volatility in Late 2024

The stock market’s recent performance has been defined by intense intraday swings and growing uncertainty. As of mid-November, the S&P 500 and NASDAQ have experienced significant pullbacks from their October highs, driven by a confluence of factors including geopolitical news, AI sector anxieties, and rising bond yields. Chris Vermeulen, Chief Market Strategist at The Technical Traders.com, joined the discussion to provide a technical outlook on whether these dips represent buying opportunities or the beginning of a deeper correction.

## Volatility Driven by News and Rotation

According to Vermeulen, the current market whiplash is largely a reaction to major news events. The recent midterm elections triggered a portfolio rebalancing event, while fears regarding AI data center expansion and chip restrictions have heavily impacted the technology sector. 

Despite the sharp declines in tech stocks, Vermeulen notes that capital has not left the stock market entirely. Instead, it has rotated into defensive sectors such as consumer staples, healthcare, utilities, and real estate. "Money is not leaving the markets. It's scared. It's just looking for safer, better bets at this point," Vermeulen explains. This rotation suggests that while sentiment is bearish, a total exodus from equities has not yet occurred.

## The Nvidia Factor and AI Sector Risks

Nvidia, often viewed as the bellwether for the AI trade, has faced significant selling pressure due to potential government restrictions on advanced AI chip sales. Vermeulen advises against "catching a falling knife" with Nvidia or similar high-flying tech stocks. While short-term oversold conditions might trigger a technical bounce, the trend remains down until the price stabilizes and forms a bullish chart pattern. 

The fear is that if the AI narrative cracks, it could lead to contagion across other semiconductor and tech subsectors. Vermeulen emphasizes waiting for confirmation of strength before entering new long positions, rather than assuming a bottom has been reached simply because prices have dropped significantly from recent highs.

## NASDAQ Outlook: A Flat Year?

The NASDAQ 100 is currently only about 5% off its January 1st close, but with only six weeks left in the year, finishing positive requires a strong rally. Prediction markets indicate an 84% chance that the NASDAQ will finish the year positive, and Vermeulen agrees with this sentiment, though he anticipates a flat or slightly positive close rather than a stellar performance.

Technically, the market is showing signs of capitulation, evidenced by volume spikes and a rise in the put/call ratio. These are often precursors to a bottom. However, Vermeulen cautions that until the trend reverses and provides clear buy signals, traders should remain on the sidelines or maintain bearish positions. The S&P 500 shows similar volatility patterns, suggesting a potential stabilization followed by a grind higher, but confirmation is key.

## Bond Yields: The Rising Tide Threatens Stability

Perhaps the most critical indicator for the broader economy is the 10-year US Treasury yield, which recently spiked to over 5.1%. Vermeulen’s technical analysis indicates a "rising tide" environment for yields, with chart patterns pointing toward significantly higher levels. 

Using Fibonacci extensions and measured move targets, Vermeulen suggests that yields could potentially reach 6.5% or even as high as 8.16%. Such an increase would likely trigger a financial crisis, impacting highly leveraged sectors like AI data centers and the broader stock market. The 30-year and 5-year yields are also projecting similar upward trajectories, indicating broad-based pressure on fixed-income markets.

## Precious Metals and the Strong Dollar

The relationship between rising yields, the US dollar, and precious metals is under scrutiny. Historically, higher interest rates diminish the appeal of non-yielding assets like gold and silver. Vermeulen projects a significant downside for precious metals if yields continue to climb:
*   **Gold:** Could fall to approximately $3,300 per ounce (a ~15% decline from recent levels).
*   **Silver:** May unwind to $36–$39 per ounce.

Simultaneously, the US Dollar Index is strengthening as global capital flows into the dollar for safety and higher yields. Vermeulen notes that in times of market stress or economic slowdowns, the dollar often rallies sharply (potentially reaching 1.20 against major currencies). A strong dollar typically exerts downward pressure on commodities and emerging market assets.

## Real Estate: More Pain Ahead?

For those looking at real estate, particularly in Canadian markets like Toronto and Vancouver, Vermeulen advises patience. He believes the housing market has not yet bottomed out and could see another 15–25% decline over the next one to two years. 

Key signals for a real estate buying opportunity include:
1.  **High Inventory:** A surplus of homes on the market, eliminating bidding wars.
2.  **Tight Credit:** When it becomes difficult to secure a mortgage due to bank caution and economic pressure.

Vermeulen emphasizes that waiting for "pain" in the real estate market allows cash-rich investors to capitalize on distressed valuations later.

## Conclusion: Discipline Over Emotion

The core message from Chris Vermeulen is one of disciplined risk management. In bull markets, buying dips works well, but during a potential regime change or bear market onset, holding onto declining assets can erode years of gains. Investors should watch for trend reversals and confirmation signals rather than trying to predict exact bottoms. With bond yields rising and the dollar strengthening, preserving capital and waiting for clear directional cues may be the wisest strategy in the current volatile environment.
