*   **The Wealth Effect:** The US stock market is heavily propped up by retirement accounts, with over 40% of the market lifted by these funds. If the market were to crash, the negative wealth effect would devastate consumer spending. Therefore, the Fed cannot allow a significant market correction.

## The Silver Case: Structural Deficit and AI Demand

While gold is performing well, Holter is particularly bullish on silver. He attributes this to two main drivers:

1.  **Structural Deficit:** The silver market has been in a structural deficit for six consecutive years. Supply simply cannot keep up with demand.
2.  **AI and Data Centers:** The rapid expansion of AI and data centers requires massive amounts of silver. As these projects come online over the next 6 to 18 months, industrial demand is expected to skyrocket.

Holter notes that silver has broken its long-term downtrend line. Although it hasn't yet broken out of its large descending triangle, the speed and magnitude of the recent rally suggest a powerful shift in market sentiment.

## Institutional Money Is Buying Miners

A key indicator of the market's direction is institutional ownership. Holter points to ETFs like GDX (gold miners), GDXJ (junior gold miners), SIL (silver miners), and SILJ (junior silver miners). Institutional ownership in these funds has been steadily increasing, with silver miners seeing particularly aggressive buying.

Holter explains that smart money is favoring silver miners because:
*   Silver has more "juice" and volatility than gold, offering higher profit potential in a bull market.
*   Miners benefit from operating and financial leverage, meaning their stock prices can rise much faster than the underlying metal.
*   Many mining companies are now highly profitable even at lower metal prices, allowing them to stockpile inventory and fund expansion through internal cash flow.

## How to Choose Mining Stocks

When evaluating mining companies, Holter emphasizes the importance of the property itself. He looks for:
*   **Undrilled Potential:** Properties that have shown promise but have not been fully explored.
*   **Financial Strength:** Companies with no debt that can fund drilling and expansion through internal earnings, avoiding the need to dilute shareholders by issuing new stock.
*   **Proximity to Producers:** Exploration companies located near existing producing mines have a higher probability of success.

Holter recommends that novice investors look at the holdings of major mutual funds and ETFs to identify reputable companies, while more experienced investors can dig deeper into junior exploration plays.

## Physical Certificates vs. ETFs

Holter strongly advises investors who buy mining stocks to hold physical stock certificates rather than relying solely on ETFs or brokerage accounts. ETFs cannot be held as physical certificates, leaving investors vulnerable to broker insolvency.

To secure their holdings, Holter suggests using Direct Registration Services (DRS). Investors can request their broker to transfer their shares electronically to the company's transfer agent and then request physical paper certificates. This protects against the risk of a broker failing and taking funds with them.
