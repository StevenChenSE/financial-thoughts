# The Oil Market at $100: Why Energy Assets Are the New AI

## Inside the Structural Shift Reshaping Global Energy Markets

The world runs on energy—no matter how you slice and dice it. This fundamental truth is becoming increasingly impossible to ignore, even as electric vehicles, hybrids, and electrification efforts dominate headlines. According to Shubham Garg, oil expert and returning guest on Danny's show, the U.S. consumer has stalled at approximately 22 barrels per year of consumption, regardless of technological advancements. "Not everybody wants to drive motorcycles and ride bikes," Garg explains. "People want to consume. That's why people make money."

What makes this moment particularly significant is that the paradigm has shifted fundamentally. "Owning hard assets is going to be the next AI, whatever you want to call it," notes the host, highlighting how even AI companies have begun acquiring hard assets like data centers—and notably, natural gas producers. An AI company purchased a natural gas producer just days before this recording, demonstrating that the energy sector has become a strategic priority across industries.

## Oil's Rangebound Market: What's Really Driving Prices

As of May 21st, 2026, oil has been consolidating in the $95-$110 range for approximately 60 days. Currently trading just south of $100, the market has shown remarkable resilience despite ongoing geopolitical tensions now entering day 83 of the conflict that began in early 2026.

The price action remains highly sensitive to news flow. A reported final draft ceasefire agreement briefly pressured prices lower, yet conflicting reports from Iranian sources denying any final agreement underscore the uncertainty. "You've got two parties here, none of whom want to lose," Garg observes, referring to both the Iranian regime, "out for 47 years," and what he describes as "the biggest ego ever" in Donald Trump.

Yet the price suppression comes at a significant cost. Behind the scenes, the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve—described as "a planning error, really, really big error"—has been depleted substantially. Over 200 million barrels have been drawn down since the 2020 timeframe, and the depletion continues at an alarming pace.

## The Hidden Hand: How SPR Releases Mask True Oil Prices

The current $100 oil price exists largely due to artificial support mechanisms. Danny points out that 20 months' worth of Strategic Petroleum Reserve builds have been drained in just seven weeks. Without these releases, oil prices would be considerably higher.

This phenomenon extends beyond the United States. Garg explains: "It's not just the US SPR. It's the Japanese crude inventories, it's the global crude inventories, it's the product inventories, it's the oil in water, it's the Chinese SPR, and then demand destruction. You've got all these sort of buffers that were sitting in the system."

The speaker emphasizes that in 2022, global markets "blew through a lot of these buffers and nobody really realized it." The commentary dismissed concerns as fear-mongering, but the reality proved otherwise. "You gave up your insurance policies. You blew through 200 some million of SPR. The US drilled uncompleted count got absolutely blown down—Eagleford as well as Permian—almost a year's worth of drilled uncompleted wells were completed in the last six years. That was your spare buffer there. That's gone."

## Why the Structural Bull Case Remains Intact

The fundamentals supporting higher oil prices haven't changed—and neither has the market's ability to absorb elevated price levels. At $100 oil, demand destruction occurs at the margins but remains limited compared to what would occur at $140 or $150.

"What you see is effective job owning of the market has been quite successful," Garg notes, crediting both market dynamics and strategic intervention. "Trump is a really good job owner. He is absolutely incredibly effective at using whatever tools—whether it's Twitter, but then the paper markets behind Twitter—to sort of keep that oil price within a reasonable range."

The critical question becomes: how long can these artificial buffers sustain? Beyond the geopolitical conflict, inventory depletion continues unabated. Over a billion barrels have already been lost, with more to follow as the situation persists.

"The world needs to believe that the Permian cannot add a million barrels per day in that one-year time frame at $100 oil," Garg explains of the structural bull thesis. Evidence is building in that direction. Devon recently paid over $300,000 per acre for unrillled Permian acreage, while U.S. shale response remains tepid. "Nothing from sort of the bigger boys," Garg notes. "Yes, Diamondback added a couple rigs, but not like, 'Oh, we're going to add 20% production growth all of a sudden. The geology just isn't there."

## The Price Floor and Ceiling: Where Markets Find Support

As the conflict continues, demand destruction will ultimately give way to demand recovery. A ceasefire—whatever form it takes—will take time for supply to come online, while demand simultaneously rises to meet available barrels.

The price floor becomes apparent at lower price levels: "If we see price go down to 90, 85, 80, you will see that incremental buyer step in—whether it's Singapore, whether it's Japan, whether it's South Korea, whether it's the US SPR itself—sort of step in and take over that barrel and be willing to pay."

The difference between $65 and $80 is "more or less meaningless to these governments and consumers," Garg emphasizes. However, "the difference between $80 and $150 is unbearable—not just for the personal consumer, but because people start rioting. You have not just energy shortage but food shortage, and that's the last thing governments want."

## Investment Implications: Identifying Market Dislocations

For investors paying attention, this environment presents unique opportunities. "A lot of the people are still lazy, fair," Garg observes. "They have no clue about oil price. They don't understand oil supply demand. But beyond that, they don't even know which companies to invest in."

The result is "skewed risk-reward scenarios that pop up in certain equity names, in certain leveraged equity names, in different kinds of options." While Garg doesn't recommend rushing into the most torqued names, understanding these dislocations becomes increasingly valuable as the structural bull market matures.

"Owning hard assets as an investor, not just as a producer, but for the sake of humanity—the capital is moving there," the host summarizes.
