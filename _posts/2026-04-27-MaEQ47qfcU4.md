The federal government just made a move that could reshape the investment landscape for critical minerals and mining companies—and remarkably few people are paying attention.

On April 20th, a presidential memorandum was released invoking Section 303 of the Defense Production Act of 1950, formally designating grid infrastructure equipment and supply chain capacity as essential to national defense. This isn't just another policy paper gathering dust on a shelf. It represents an official government declaration that the United States cannot produce the materials needed to electrify and modernize its electrical grid—and that Washington intends to flood capital into the sectors that can fix this problem.

For investors, this is a significant signal. When the government commits to something as a matter of national defense, budget constraints become essentially irrelevant. There is no profit motive driving procurement decisions, no quarterly earnings pressure limiting expenditure. There is only the machinery of national security moving resources toward a designated goal.

The memo makes no bones about the problem: domestic production capacity is limited, procurement timelines are extended, supply chains remain dependent on foreign sources, and capital investment has been insufficient for years. The Defense Production Act invocation means the executive branch now has expanded authority to use purchase commitments and direct financial support to accelerate domestic production of these critical materials and technologies.

Here's what this means for your portfolio.

## The Presidential Memorandum: What It Covers

The April 20th determination is sweeping in its scope. It designates the following as critical technology items essential to national defense:

- **Transformers** — the backbone equipment for voltage conversion in the grid
- **Transmission lines and conductors** — the physical infrastructure carrying electricity across distances
- **Substations** — critical nodes in the electrical distribution network
- **High voltage circuit breakers** — safety and control mechanisms for grid management
- **Power control electronics** — modern grid management and smart grid technologies
- **Protective relay systems** — automated systems that detect faults and isolate problems
- **Capacitor banks** — equipment for power factor correction and grid stabilization
- **Electrical core steel** — specialized steel used in transformers and electrical equipment
- **Related raw materials and manufacturing tools** — the upstream supply chain feeding all of the above

But the implications extend far beyond the equipment itself. All of these components require the raw materials that make them possible—and those materials are the investment opportunity hiding in plain sight.

## The Capacity Crisis: Why the Government Is Acting Now

The memorandum includes a remarkably candid assessment of the situation. It explicitly states that "without presidential action under section 303 of the act, US industry cannot reasonably be expected to provide these capabilities for the needed industrial resource material or critical technology items in a timely manner."

The reasons are laid out plainly:

- **Limited domestic production capacity** — U.S. mining and manufacturing infrastructure has atrophied over decades
- **Extended procurement timelines** — supply chains that once moved quickly have become stretched
- **Foreign supply dependence** — critical minerals have become strategically vulnerable chokepoints
- **Insufficient capital investment** — the memo essentially admits that private capital has failed to fund the necessary expansion

This is a government admission that the market alone cannot solve this problem. The capex hasn't been there. For the past 10-15 years, capital expenditure in the mining sector has been gutted. Weak companies have perished in a Darwinian filtering process, and while plenty of subpar operators remain, the landscape is far more concentrated than it would have been with sustained investment.

The implication is clear: the federal government intends to pour capital directly into these mining companies and their supply chains to get domestic production up to speed.

## Battery Metals: The Sectors Positioned to Benefit

The investment thesis centers on the raw materials required to build out and electrify the grid at scale. Every transformer, every transmission line, every piece of power electronics requires metals—and the companies mining those metals are the direct beneficiaries of government-mandated capital deployment.

### Rare Earth Metals (REMX ETF)

The VanEck Rare Earth/Strategic Metals ETF (ticker: REMX) offers exposure to companies involved in rare earth mining and processing. Technically, the chart shows an interesting setup:

- The ETF has been bumping against resistance but has formed a **double bottom** pattern at recent lows
- In technical analysis, double bottoms are typically predictive of upside continuation
- Since the start of the year, REMX is up approximately **161%** from its bottom, currently sitting near the lower portion of a rectangle consolidation pattern
- A triple top has formed, which is initially bearish, but gold itself made a quadruple top before breaking out in 2023—sometimes accumulation patterns resolve with powerful moves higher

The rare earth space remains a critical component of the electrification story. These metals are essential for permanent magnets in wind turbines, electric motors, and defense applications—all front and center in the national defense designation.

### Copper: The Grid's Essential Conductor

Copper is indispensable for electrical transmission, and the metal itself is currently trading around $6 per pound. The spot price has been:

- Consolidating sideways since mid-January after a significant move
- Showing relative strength despite broader market weakness
- Having experienced a selloff during the Iran conflict but recovering to hold key levels

The copper miners tell a more compelling story. The Global X Copper Miners ETF (COPX) is showing a **descending wedge** pattern—a historically bullish continuation formation. Mining stocks typically front-run the underlying metal price, meaning the technical setup in COPX could be predictive of copper's next move.

COPX broke out of an earlier descending triangle in March and has since formed a gentler descending wedge. This pattern, when it resolves, typically moves to the upside—not always, but with sufficient probability to warrant attention.

### Nickel Miners: The Battery Metal Play

Nickel is one of the key battery metals driving the EV revolution, and the NASDAQ Nickel Miners Index (NGS) shows compelling technical action:

- A **descending megaphone pattern** has formed—another bullish continuation structure
- The index is currently trading around 836, with support near 711
- Over the past year, nickel miners are up approximately **94%**
- Another test of the lower support trendline may occur before continuation higher

Nickel miners have already posted strong gains but may still have runway if government spending accelerates. The battery demand story remains intact, and government-mandated grid buildout will require nickel-containing stainless steel and specialty alloys alongside the battery applications.

### Lithium: The Outperformer

Lithium has been the clear leader among battery metals. Unlike copper, nickel, silver, platinum, and gold—all of which have experienced corrections since January—lithium has barely paused its advance:

- The metal has been essentially moving in one direction: up
- A prior descending wedge resolved to the upside as expected
- Currently forming what appears to be a **bull flag** on the daily chart
- Could be setting up for another descending wedge before the next breakout

The lithium miners have tracked this strength, and the case for continued outperformance remains solid. The federal push to electrify the grid creates direct demand for lithium in grid-scale battery storage, on top of existing EV and consumer electronics demand.

Taking a position at current levels requires accepting that you've missed the initial move. However, if the Defense Production Act mobilization drives a sustained capital influx into these mining companies, the early stages of that investment cycle could produce another leg higher.

## The Bottom Line

This presidential memorandum is not administrative housekeeping. It is a formal declaration that grid infrastructure and its supporting raw material supply chains constitute a national defense priority—complete with the expanded procurement authorities and funding mechanisms that designation implies.

The government has explicitly acknowledged that private capital has failed to build the capacity needed, and that federal intervention is required. When Washington decides to solve a problem with unlimited budget and no profit motive, the sectors it targets tend to see sustained capital flows.

Battery metals—rare earths, copper, nickel, and lithium—sit at the foundation of this initiative. They are the raw materials without which transformers cannot be built, transmission lines cannot be strung, and grid-scale battery storage cannot be deployed. The mining companies that produce these materials are the direct beneficiaries of the policy shift now underway.
