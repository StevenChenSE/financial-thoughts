## Equinox Gold: Tactical Mergers and Ross Beaty’s Return
Rick Rule discusses Equinox Gold, acknowledging its recent 50% price drop. He holds the stock as a "4" but is considering an upgrade to a "3" if the price falls further. He critiques Equinox's recent mergers with Calibre and Orla as tactical moves to increase size rather than strategic ones for operational synergy. ## Yukon Explorers: Banyan and Snowline
Addressing questions on Yukon explorers, Rule is long in both Banyan Gold and Snowline Resources. He praises Banyan for its existing infrastructure, including paved roads and proximity to the Victoria Mine. Snowline, while lacking infrastructure, boasts astonishing grades. Rule advises investors to review his pre-conference interviews with both management teams to understand the exploration upside. He notes he will not upgrade Banyan until a Preliminary Economic Assessment (PEA) is released, expected in Q4.

## Saturn Metals and the "Orphan" Valuation Anomaly
Rule expresses strong interest in Saturn Metals, holding a reasonable position. He identifies a valuation anomaly: Saturn operates an open-pit heap leach oxide target in Australia, a deposit type well-understood by American analysts but rare in the Australian market. Consequently, American investors avoid it due to location, and Australian investors ignore it due to lack of understanding. Rule values the involvement of Jonathan Goodman and Dundee Precious Metals, whose technical and financial support mitigates risk.

## Centaurus Metals and the Nickel Market
Rule rates Centaurus Metals as a "4," noting it is a speculative play on a nickel deposit that is currently in a "boring" permitting phase. He expects the stock to remain stagnant until final financing and permitting are announced. Regarding the recent pullback in nickel prices, Rule disagrees with the view that inventory drawdowns are the primary cause. Instead, he attributes the price drop to high oil prices acting as an economic tax and higher interest rates increasing the cost of hoarding metal, leading to liquidation of speculative holdings.

## Gold, Silver, and Interest Rates
When asked about catalysts for precious metals, Rule points to US interest rates. He draws a parallel to 1975, where high rates initially crushed gold prices but eventually forced the Federal Reserve to cut rates due to economic damage. Rule believes 90% of gold's current weakness is due to the strong US dollar driven by high nominal interest rates. He expects rates to eventually fall, triggering a significant rise in gold prices.
