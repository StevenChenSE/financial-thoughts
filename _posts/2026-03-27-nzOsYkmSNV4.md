Technical analyst Patrick Karim returned to Northstar Barts for a deep dive into the current state of markets, with a particular focus on precious metals. In a conversation that challenged popular narratives, Karim dismantled the conventional wisdom that gold automatically rallies during geopolitical crises, urging investors to ignore the "clickbait" and focus instead on the mathematical reality of the charts.

## The "Safe Haven" Trap

Karim began with a blunt critique of a common investor pitfall.

> "The number one trap is when people hear narratives out there and they say, 'Ah, there's war. This is good for gold.' Like, what? Gold is a safe haven. How are they defining that? What do you mean it's a safe haven?... That stuff's a whole bunch of... nonsense."

He argued that geopolitical events are merely catalysts that accelerate existing chart patterns. If an asset is "stretched" and ready for a correction, bad news can trigger the sell-off. Conversely, if it's at a bottom, the same news can mark a reversal point.

"The news will just be a catalyst," Karim stated. "People will just dump. Gold was just ready to enter a correction now."

## Reading the Gold Chart: Stretched and Needing a Reset

Karim shared his primary analytical tool: measuring the distance of gold's price from its 36-month moving average. This "stretch" indicator showed that gold was at historically extended levels before the recent conflict began.

> "Look how stretched we were... Gold was stretched from the 36-month moving average all the way up here when the war started."

He contrasted this with gold's powerful breakout in June 2019, which occurred with no major headlines. "What was the headline news narrative back in June of 2019?... There's nothing happening back there." That quiet breakout launched the major bull run. Conversely, the peak during the "silver squeeze" mania and the recent war-driven spike coincided with extreme stretches above the moving average—classic topping signals.

The current technical picture is mixed. On the monthly chart, the long-term uptrend remains intact as long as price holds above key rising support. However, the momentum has broken. On the daily chart, gold recently became "crazy stretched" *below* its 36-day moving average, leading to a bounce. But Karim warns this bounce faces multiple "walls" of overhead resistance.

The core issue is the need for a prolonged reset.

> "The closer the price lets the moving average catch up, the better, the stronger, the higher conviction I'm going to be if we break out... This correction could last a year, two years."

For traders, the message is clear: wait for a "launch pattern." The low-risk, high-reward entries like those seen in late 2023 or June 2019 simply don't exist right now.

## Silver's More Severe Reality

The picture for silver is even more stark. Karim displayed a chart showing silver's violent vertical rally and subsequent crash.

> "We crashed. We crashed it. Like unless this month there's a miracle, that ascending trend line, that momentum as defined by the angle of that line is gone. That's like silver needs a miracle."

He emphasized that explosive vertical moves are typically preceded by large, sustained bases.

> "What created these crazy vertical moves? You see them after large base breakouts, right?... Where is my base today? There's no base. I'll say it one more time. There's no base."

Without a base to build energy, the path forward requires a lengthy consolidation. Silver is in "100% correction mode" on the monthly chart, and the daily chart shows it trading below its key moving averages with heavy overhead resistance. Any hope for an immediate V-shaped recovery is "your heart speaking... you're seeking confirmation bias."

## The Strategic Cost of Holding Through Corrections

A key part of Karim's philosophy is the opportunity cost of holding an asset through a prolonged correction.

> "You could have been playing something else... oil that's breaking out, sugar, coal, corn... Why try to catch these bottoms because you're in love with that instrument?"

He illustrated that after a sharp initial drop, an asset often trades sideways for months or even years before resuming its uptrend. Holding through that period ties up capital that could be deployed in stronger, breaking markets. The smarter play is to step aside during the correction, preserve capital, and re-enter only once a new launch pattern confirms the uptrend is resuming.

## Equities Are in the Same Boat

When asked about the general stock market amid rising energy prices and recession fears, Karim applied the same principles. The S&P 500, on its daily chart, has broken key support and is in a clear correction.

"It's lost a whole bunch of rising support lines. It's in correction mode 100%," he said. Notably, the index is not yet "stretched" to the downside, suggesting the correction may not be over. Like gold and silver, it needs to resolve its pattern before a new sustainable move can begin.

## The Bottom Line: Discipline Over Narrative

Patrick Karim's analysis serves as a powerful reminder for investors: price action and mathematical indicators like moving average distance are more reliable than compelling news stories. The long-term bull case for precious metals remains, but the short-to-medium term requires patience and discipline.

The best opportunities arise from low-risk setups—coiled springs emerging from solid bases—not from chasing headlines or trying to catch a falling knife. For now, both gold and silver are charts in need of repair, and the most prudent course is to wait for the market itself to signal the all-clear.
