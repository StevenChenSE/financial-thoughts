### Adrian Day on macroeconomic stress, rising Treasury yields, geopolitical myths, and the long-term outlook for precious metals.

Macroeconomic signals continue to baffle investors as persistent inflation, climbing long-term Treasury yields, and an increasingly overstretched equity market raise concerns about systemic stability. Some analysts have begun drawing direct parallels to the market environment preceding the 1987 Black Monday crash. 

Adrian Day, President of Adrian Day Asset Management and Portfolio Manager of the EuroPacific Gold Fund, joined David Lin to discuss these growing risks. He evaluated whether current interest rates threaten stock valuations, why geopolitical conflicts rarely create sustained bull markets in gold, and where opportunities remain across precious metal equities.

## Are We Heading for a 1987-Style Market Correction?

Concerns surrounding high valuations—particularly in artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure—coupled with rising debt issuance and elevated rates have sparked fears of a sharp equities drawdown reminiscent of 1987. 

When evaluating whether fixed-income yields are high enough to trigger a massive rotation out of equities into bonds, Day noted that long-term yields have not yet crossed the threshold required to drain institutional capital from the stock market:

> *"I don't think interest rates are high enough at the moment for a large transfer from the stock market to the bond market, particularly at the long end. Would you lend money to the US government for 30 years at 5.5%? I know I wouldn't."*

While short-term paper remains an appealing venue for cash preservation, longer maturities offer insufficient compensation when considering long-term inflation risk and the deteriorating fiscal deficit.

## The Bond Market Dilemma and Fed Maneuverability

The Federal Reserve faces structural limitations in how much further it can tighten monetary policy. Higher rates disproportionately burden three major economic segments:

1. **The Federal Government:** With nearly $4 trillion in debt maturing and rolling over annually, refinancing older 10- to 30-year issuances at prevailing rates significantly exacerbates interest expenses.
2. **Commercial Real Estate and Private Credit:** Sectors heavily dependent on variable-rate financing are experiencing compounding credit strain.
3. **Low-to-Middle Income Consumers:** More than half of U.S. households lack emergency savings. When the Fed hikes short-term rates, credit card APRs quickly climb well past 20%, straining discretionary budgets and raising default risks.

Because each incremental quarter-point rate hike adds disproportionate pressure to these vulnerable areas, the Fed's room to maneuver remains severely constrained.

## Gold's Surprising Resilience and the Geopolitical Myth

Under standard economic models, a regime defined by positive real interest rates, rising bond yields, and a strong U.S. dollar should trigger a severe downturn in gold prices. Instead, gold has held up remarkably well.

Day attributes this strength to steady sovereign and institutional accumulation, notably persistent central bank buying across Asia. However, he cautioned against relying on geopolitical crises to drive sustainable multi-year rallies:

* **The Dollar Effect:** During military conflicts or global shocks, capital frequently rushes to the U.S. dollar as the primary global reserve asset, creating dollar strength that suppresses dollar-denominated gold.
* **Emergency Liquidity Needs:** Entities directly affected by geopolitical crises frequently liquidate gold to access cash or stabilize domestic currencies (such as the Central Bank of Turkey).
* **The "Buy the Rumor, Sell the News" Cycle:** Gold typically rallies during the build-up to a conflict and peaks once official military actions commence.

A sustained breakout in gold will not depend on conflict headlines, but rather on a sustained macro pivot: an easing of energy prices, a softening U.S. dollar, and the eventual realization that the Fed must ease monetary policy in the face of persistent inflation.

```
Geopolitical Conflict Cycle vs. Gold Price:
[Tensions Build / Saber-Rattling] ──► Gold Rallies
[Conflict Commences / Bombs Drop] ──► Local Liquidity Needs & Dollar Surge ──► Gold Corrects
```

## Gold Equity Strategy: Navigating the Mid-Cycle Phase

Within precious metal equities, Day sees the broader market operating in a mid-cycle consolidation rather than the end of a bull run. His current allocation approach emphasizes selective, bottom-up opportunities:

* **Targeting Idiosyncratic Overreactions:** Taking advantage of steep sell-offs driven by company-specific headlines—such as the regulatory and operational issues surrounding the Cobre Panama mine that impacted streaming royalty companies like Franco-Nevada.
* **Leaning Toward Senior Producers:** Favoring higher-quality, large-cap producers with strong balance sheets over high-risk exploration juniors until retail sentiment and macro clarity improve.
* **Managing Cash Reserves:** Deploying capital deliberately while maintaining elevated cash levels to capitalize on sudden volatility and potential year-end tax-loss selling.

## Summary

The global macro landscape is defined by competing crosscurrents: rising sovereign debt burdens, constrained central banks, and persistent inflation pressures. While high real yields and dollar strength present near-term headwinds for gold, the metal's underlying resilience points to robust structural support. For resource investors, patience and disciplined, idiosyncratic stock selection remain key while waiting for broader macro shifts to take hold.
