The world is currently undergoing one of its largest military buildups in decades. With Japan considering a $500 billion defense buildup, Germany aiming for Europe’s strongest conventional military by 2039, and China increasing its official budget to approximately $280 billion, nations are pouring enormous sums into weapons, missile defense systems, drones, and artificial intelligence.

Amidst this escalation, a potential historic diplomatic meeting between President Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping, and Russian President Vladimir Putin could alter the geopolitical landscape. But as countries race to modernize their armed forces, what will the next generation of warfare actually look like? And where does Canada fit into this trillion-dollar defense boom?

To answer these questions, we spoke with Harjit Sajjan, Canada’s former Minister of National Defense (2015–2021) and currently Executive Chairman and Co-founder of Juno Industries, alongside his co-founder and CEO, Hunter Scarf.

## The Global Security Deterioration

Harjit Sajjan notes that while it is sad that nations must significantly increase defense budgets, the deterioration of the rules-based order has forced this reality. He expresses skepticism about high-profile diplomatic meetings yielding immediate results, noting that past talks have produced little stability. However, he remains an optimist regarding dialogue, even as conflicts in Ukraine and tensions in the Pacific persist.

The security environment is shifting from a "peace dividend" era to one of active threat management. Sajjan highlights that threats identified during his tenure—including those in Ukraine, the Pacific, and the Gulf—were precursors to today’s instability. The public is now realizing that the peace dividend was an illusion, and significant threats have been rippling through global security for years.

## Canada’s Defense Industrial Strategy: "Masters of Our Destiny"

A pivotal shift in Canadian defense policy is the move toward sovereign capability. Historically, 70% of Canada’s defense budget went outside the country, primarily to US contractors. The recent federal budget, titled "Masters of Our Destiny," aims to reverse this trend by building a robust Canadian defense industrial base.

Prime Minister Mark Carney’s government is prioritizing:
*   **Sovereign Control:** Ensuring Canada controls its own data and systems rather than relying on foreign permissions.
*   **Economic Benefit:** Keeping defense spending within Canada to stimulate the economy.
*   **Interoperability:** Maintaining compatibility with NATO and Five Eyes partners while ensuring domestic sovereignty.

Hunter Scarf emphasizes that this diversification away from US-centric procurement is a key theme, though partnerships with Europe and the Asia-Pacific remain critical.

## The Pace of Innovation in Modern Warfare

Hunter Scarf points out that the most shocking aspect of recent conflicts, such as those in Ukraine and Iran, is the pace of innovation. Technology used on the front lines can become obsolete in less than six weeks. This rapid cycle demands a fundamental change in how militaries procure equipment:
1.  **Speed:** Faster procurement processes are essential.
2.  **R&D Appetite:** Larger risk-taking in research and development.
3.  **Small Business Engagement:** Working with small and medium businesses to deploy advanced technology quickly.

Sajjan adds that industry must be a trusted partner, delivering capabilities on time and within budget. Juno Industries was founded to address these urgent operational needs by leveraging Canadian talent and AI to build superior defense technologies rapidly.

## Juno Industries: Sensor Systems and Sovereign C4ISR

Juno Industries, which listed on the TSX Venture Exchange under the ticker **JUNO** in October 2025, focuses on two core sovereign capabilities outlined in Canada’s Defense Industrial Strategy: sensor systems and digital systems.

### The Guardian System
Traditional sensor arrays are large, expensive, and vulnerable to drone attacks. Juno’s **Guardian** system uses micro-sensors no bigger than an iPhone, dispersed across operating areas (front lines, borders, the Arctic) on a distributed mesh basis. This approach offers:
*   **Durability:** Much harder to destroy than centralized systems.
*   **Reliability:** Maintains situational awareness even in active conflict zones.
*   **Data Collection:** Currently collects radio frequency and acoustic information, with plans for additional modalities.

### The Logan Platform
The **Logan** platform is a C4ISR (Command, Control, Communications, Computers, Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance) system designed to create a "common operating picture" of the battlefield. It allows operators to process data on a sovereign basis, enabling effective decision-making without relying on foreign infrastructure.

## Future Priorities: The Arctic and Multi-Domain Warfare

Looking 5 to 10 years ahead, Scarf identifies the **Arctic** as a primary focus for Canadian defense. With new shipping lanes opening due to climate change, Canada lacks current detection and monitoring capabilities in the region. Significant investments are planned to build infrastructure and defense systems to protect sovereignty in the Arctic.

Juno aims to become a multi-domain company, addressing conflict across land, sea, air, cyber, and space. This includes integrating capabilities via drones, satellites, and autonomous vehicles. The goal is to provide advanced, integrated situational awareness for Canada and its allies.

## Geopolitical Alliances: EU and Asia-Pacific

Canada is exploring closer ties with the European Union, including potential "associate member" status for defense cooperation. Sajjan notes that this aligns with efforts made during his tenure to integrate Canadian capabilities into European defense spending. As the only non-European country invited into the EU’s safe arrangement for defense procurement, Canada has a unique opportunity to win contracts in Europe.

Simultaneously, the Asia-Pacific region remains crucial. With half the world’s population nearby and tensions rising, Juno is also building relationships in this sector, leveraging its Vancouver base to engage with Asian markets.

## Conclusion: A New Era of Sovereign Defense Technology

The global arms race is not just about quantity but about speed, innovation, and sovereignty. Canada is waking up to the reality that it must manage its own defense capabilities to ensure true sovereignty. Juno Industries represents a new wave of Canadian companies leveraging AI and distributed sensor technology to meet these urgent needs.

As procurement processes modernize, small and medium businesses like Juno are positioned to deliver critical technologies faster than traditional large contractors. The next decade will likely see a resurgence in Canadian defense innovation, with companies providing sovereign solutions not just for Canada, but for trusted partners worldwide.
