In a stark and sobering analysis, Chris MacIntosh, publisher of Capitalist Exploits Insider, warns that the world is barreling toward a period of sustained high energy costs and severe food shortages. The catalyst? The ongoing closure of the Strait of Hormuz and the cascading collapse of interconnected global supply chains.

### The Energy Crisis Is Just Beginning

At the time of recording, oil was trading at $96. MacIntosh argues this elevated price is not a temporary spike but a new reality. The common belief is that once the Strait reopens, oil flows will normalize. However, MacIntosh dismantles this assumption.

"The issue... is that in order for the oil to flow, we need to have all of the ships in place. We need to have the core infrastructure operational," he explains. "Those two main elements are not at all what they were prior to this war."

The shipping fleet is scattered, having sought alternative routes, and critical infrastructure like refineries and storage tanks has been damaged. Repairing this will take "many months to years." Even if the Strait opened tomorrow, MacIntosh cites shipping analysts who say it would take about two months just to reroute vessels.

Furthermore, he believes reopening is unlikely soon. "Iran essentially controls that straight at the moment... There's a cost asymmetry that exists." He notes that for every dollar Iran spends on attack drones, Gulf states spend $15 to $35 on defense—an unsustainable equation that leaves the Strait effectively closed to Western traffic.

This physical supply crunch is creating a dangerous market dynamic. MacIntosh highlights a growing "backwardation," where the spot price outpaces futures, indicating intense demand for immediate delivery. While paper markets can be manipulated, "with a commodity that you don't actually have... you can't do that or you can do it to a certain degree before it fails."

### From Fertilizer to Famine

The energy crisis is the first domino. The next to fall is global food production.

"Moving into the planting season and if that fertilizer doesn't move, which it doesn't look like it's going to," MacIntosh states bluntly, "I don't want to be the bearer of bad news, but we'll have a famine... We will have global food shortage."

The logic is inexorable. The biggest costs for farming are diesel and fertilizer. Both are derivatives of oil and natural gas. With energy flows choked, fertilizer production plummets. MacIntosh details a hidden choke point: sulfur, a byproduct of processing "sour crude" from the Gulf.

"We're losing about 8% of... sulfuric acid components. And that throttles fertilizer production as well. Wastewater treatment, metals extraction, there's a lot of stuff there."

This shortage is already causing real-world shutdowns. In Africa, the Democratic Republic of Congo and Zambia—producers of one-sixth of the world's copper and 70% of its cobalt—are declaring *force majeure* on the copper belt because they can't get the sulfuric acid needed for extraction. "You can't get your electric vehicle battery without your copper and your cobalt," MacIntosh notes wryly.

### Regional Winners and Losers

The impact will be deeply uneven, reshaping global power dynamics.

*   **The Western Hemisphere:** North and South America are relatively insulated. "They got the feedstock... You've got natural gas, plenty of it. You've got oil... an ability... to produce food, to have all the components of energy."
*   **Asia in Crisis:** Southeast and South Asia face extreme peril. "The majority of their feedstock comes... from the Middle East from the Gulf." MacIntosh highlights India, which reportedly buys 40% of its urea phosphates from the region, as a high-risk area for "large-scale malnutrition."
*   **The Semiconductor Shock:** A stunning vulnerability is Taiwan. While the world worries about its chip manufacturing, MacIntosh points out a deeper flaw: "Taiwan imports 98% of its energy. Qatar supplies 30% of its LNG. They got storage of 11 days. So now... their power grid is going to start failing." This directly threatens TSMC, the world's most critical chipmaker.

### The Economic Domino Effect

MacIntosh foresees a violent economic chain reaction:

1.  **Margin Collapse:** Soaring input costs for everything from industrial chemicals to tires will brutally compress corporate profit margins. Companies won't be able to reprice finished goods quickly enough.
2.  **Credit and Equity Market Fallout:** Squeezed margins will hit corporate earnings, spilling into credit markets and then equity markets. "Your average American... has most of their money sitting in the S&P 500 or the NASDAQ," he warns, predicting declines of 40-50% in retirement accounts.
3.  **Concentration Trap:** He sees extreme risk in the market's concentration in a few mega-cap, money-losing tech companies. "If you add... Anthropic, Open AI, and SpaceX... this concentration is about 50%. We've never seen a concentration like that before... none of these make any money."

### A World Redrawn

MacIntosh concludes that we are witnessing the accelerated unraveling of the just-in-time global supply chain and the US-led world order. "This is a catastrophic failure for the US empire," he says, pointing to withdrawn missile systems from South Korea and alienated Gulf allies.

The new world will be one of regional blocs, material cost inflation, and a fundamental shift in investor psychology. "People will be much more interested in a return *of* capital than a return *on* capital."

His final assessment is grimly pragmatic. While the chaos is "not fun," it was anticipated. "We're making a lot of money... I'd rather be making money in the chaos than not making money in the chaos. Do I want chaos? No, I don't. But that's not a choice that I get to make."

The message is clear: the storm that began in the Strait of Hormuz is now a global hurricane, and its path leads directly through the world's dinner plates, power grids, and financial markets.
