**By Chris MacIntosh, Publisher of Capitalist Exploits Insider**

In a recent interview on Capital Cosm, I outlined a stark reality: the world is barreling toward a period of sustained high energy costs and severe food shortages. This isn't a temporary market blip; it's the result of deep structural fractures in our global supply chains, exacerbated by geopolitical conflict. The shutdown of critical maritime chokepoints like the Strait of Hormuz is merely the spark on a pile of dry tinder that has been accumulating for years.

### The Oil Equation: Elevated for the Long Haul

At the time of our discussion, oil was trading around $96. Many hope prices will retreat once the Strait reopens. This is a dangerous misconception.

"I think oil is going to stay elevated for far longer than most people anticipate," I stated. The infrastructure required to move oil—tankers and core facilities like refineries and storage tanks—has been severely disrupted. Ships are out of position, and critical infrastructure is damaged. "Those two main elements are not at all what they were prior to this war... It would take about 2 months to just reroute and get the ships all back," even if everything opened tomorrow.

Furthermore, a peaceful resolution is unlikely. "Iran essentially controls that straight at the moment and it's not likely to be opened anytime soon." The cost asymmetry is staggering: for every dollar Iran spends on attack drones, Gulf states spend $15 to $35 on defense. This unsustainable dynamic, coupled with the potential for further escalation, locks in high energy prices.

We're also seeing a critical divergence between paper and physical markets. "You can manipulate the paper price, but with a commodity that you don't actually have, you don't have the supply... you can't do that." This backwardation—where spot prices outpace futures—signals a desperate scramble for real barrels, not financial contracts.

### From Fertilizer to Famine: The Coming Food Crisis

The energy crisis cascades directly into our food supply. Modern agriculture runs on two key inputs: diesel and fertilizer. Both are derivatives of oil and gas.

"Moving into the planting season and if that fertilizer doesn't move, which it doesn't look like it's going to... I don't want to be the bearer of bad news, but we'll have a famine. We will have a global food shortage."

The interconnected, just-in-time supply chain has critical, concentrated choke points that are now failing. The shortage of sulfur—a byproduct of oil refining—is throttling global sulfuric acid production. This acid is essential for manufacturing phosphate fertilizers and for mining critical minerals like copper and cobalt.

The impact will be regional. My research indicates that North and South America, with their own abundant feedstocks of natural gas and oil, will fare "relatively better." Southeast Asia and South Asia, however, are in the crosshairs.

"India made a terrible, terrible decision there to back the Zionists... they will pay the price," I noted. AI analysis suggests India buys 40% of its urea/phosphates from the Gulf region and faces "large scale malnutrition risk for the poor."

### The Domino Effect: Chips, Credit, and Collapsing Margins

The breakdown doesn't stop at food. It threatens the very foundation of modern industry.

Consider Taiwan, the epicenter of global semiconductor manufacturing. "Taiwan imports 98% of its energy. Qatar supplies 30% of its LNG. They got storage of 11 days. So now their power grid is going to start failing." A failing grid in Taiwan doesn't just mean no chips for your phone; it means no chips for cars, medical equipment, or defense systems.

This leads to what I call "real material cost inflation." When you can't source key components, or their price explodes, corporate profit margins get violently compressed.

"You can't reprice your goods higher to account for the higher cost of production... So now you have a real supply constraint." This will spill over into credit markets as companies struggle, leading to disappointing earnings seasons. The average investor, heavily exposed to a hyper-concentrated S&P 500—soon to be inflated by money-losing companies like SpaceX and OpenAI—faces a devastating wealth effect. "People in their 401k plans wake up one day and they're down 40-50%."

### The Canary in the Coal Mine: Private Credit Freezes

We are already seeing early warning signs in the most illiquid corners of finance. "We've got seven or eight now private equity or private credit companies which have all gated and frozen up so you can't get your money out." They are trying to roll debt at untenable rates. This illiquidity is a precursor to broader market stress.

### Conclusion: A World Reordered

We are witnessing a rapid reconfiguration of the global order into regional blocs. The era of efficient, globalized supply chains is ending. What emerges will be less efficient, more resilient, and defined by resource access.

For investors, the priority must shift. "People will be much more interested in a return *of* capital than a return *on* capital." In this chaotic environment, being positioned in real assets—particularly in energy and within resilient regional blocs like the Americas—is not just an investment strategy; it's a necessity for capital preservation.

As I concluded, "It's a shitty situation, but you got to make the best of it... I'd rather be making money in the chaos than not making money in the chaos. Do I want chaos? No, I don't. But that's not a choice that I get to make."

The storm is here. It's time to prepare for a prolonged period of scarcity, inflation, and systemic strain.
