In a recent interview at the Vancouver Resource Investment Conference, Daryl Thomas spoke with David Hunter, Chief Macro Strategist at Contrarian Macro Advisors. Hunter, known for his contrarian views on market cycles, outlined a stark outlook for the global economy: a final, steep rally in equities followed by a "global bust" larger than 2008, leading to unprecedented money printing and a subsequent inflationary cycle that will drive gold and silver to historic highs.

## The Final Leg of the Secular Bull Market

Hunter argues that the secular bull market in stocks, which began in August 1982 following Paul Volcker’s defeat of inflation, is nearing its end. While he has been warning about this top for several years, external shocks like the pandemic and geopolitical tensions have stretched the cycle. However, Hunter believes we are currently in the final stages.

He posits that the recent market correction has already bottomed out. Looking ahead 3 to 6 months, Hunter forecasts a "very big run" in equities, driven by negative sentiment that he views as fuel for a contrarian rally. His cycle-end targets are aggressive:
*   **S&P 500:** 10,000
*   **Nasdaq:** 36,000
*   **Dow Jones:** 70,000
*   **Russell 2000:** 4,000

These targets imply a 33-42% upside from current levels, occurring rapidly. Hunter notes that while financials and healthcare may participate, energy and consumer staples are likely to lag in this final phase.

## The Catalyst: A Global Bust and Leverage

Following this equity peak, Hunter predicts a "global bust" more severe than the 2008–2009 financial crisis. The primary driver for this collapse is extreme leverage—both global debt and derivatives—which he describes as being at unprecedented levels. When this leverage unwinds, it will exacerbate the downturn, leading to bank failures and a rush for liquidity that will drag down all asset classes, including gold and silver initially.

Hunter’s contrarian view on interest rates is particularly notable. While many fear a new era of high rates, Hunter believes we have already seen the highs for this cycle. He predicts that during the bust, central banks will be forced to implement massive Quantitative Easing (QE) to save the financial system. This will drive the 10-year Treasury yield down to new lows, potentially below the 0.4% levels seen in 2020, with short-term rates turning negative.

## The Inflationary Aftermath and Precious Metals Targets

The consequence of this massive money printing—Hunter suggests the Fed’s balance sheet could expand by up to $20 trillion—is not deflation, but hyperinflation. He describes a cycle where the bust leads to deflation, followed by aggressive monetary stimulus that ignites a new inflationary cycle exceeding the levels seen in the early 1980s.

This environment creates the perfect storm for precious metals:
*   **Short-Term (Cycle Peak):** Hunter targets **$7,000 for Gold** and **$200 for Silver**. He notes that silver has already retested its long-term breakout level near $50, which he views as a bullish signal.
*   **During the Bust:** Metals will likely fall sharply due to liquidation pressures. Gold could drop to $3,500 and silver to $50–$60.
*   **Long-Term (Post-Bust Inflation):** After the money printers are fully engaged, Hunter forecasts gold reaching **$20,000** and silver hitting **$1,000** by the early 2030s.

For miners, Hunter sees significant upside in ETFs as well, with targets of $180 for GDX, $250 for GDXJ, $220 for SIL, and $90 for SILJ. He emphasizes that mining companies are currently undervalued relative to their production capabilities and cash flows.

## The Dollar, Commodities, and Societal Impact

Hunter expects the US Dollar Index (DXY) to peak and then decline significantly, targeting a range of 80–84 as confidence in fiat currencies wanes. This weakness in the dollar will further support commodity prices. He predicts oil could spike to $500 a barrel by the mid-2030s due to supply constraints and surging demand from data centers and reshoring efforts, despite limited new greenfield infrastructure capacity.

The societal implications of this cycle are profound. Hunter warns that the inability of governments to service their debt at high interest rates will force austerity measures. By the mid-2030s, he predicts a "systemic meltdown" where social programs like Medicare and Social Security face severe cuts, potentially leading to civil unrest. He argues that the current financial system is a "Ponzi scheme" that has been sustained by perpetual money printing, but eventually, capital markets will refuse to fund government deficits, forcing a hard reset.

## Investment Strategy for the Coming Decade

Hunter’s strategy for navigating this complex cycle involves:
1.  **Riding the Equity Rally:** Participating in the final surge toward his stock market targets.
2.  **Hiding in Bonds During the Bust:** Treasuries will perform well as rates fall to zero during the deflationary crash.
3.  **Accumulating Metals for the Inflationary Era:** Holding gold and silver through the bust phase to capture the massive upside when inflation returns.

Hunter warns that the mantra of "time in the market" is dangerous in a secular bear market environment. Investors who hold equities through the coming bust may never see their capital recover, whereas precious metals holders can weather the volatility and benefit from the monetary reset.
