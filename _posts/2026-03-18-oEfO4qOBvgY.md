**Chief Market Strategist Gareth Soloway returns with a striking set of predictions for key assets in 2026, drawing parallels to 2008 and warning of stagflation risks.**

In a recent interview, Gareth Soloway, Chief Market Strategist and President of Verified Investing, laid out his outlook for gold, oil, Bitcoin, and equities amid rising geopolitical tensions and economic uncertainty. Soloway, who earlier this year accurately called oil’s breakout, now sees dramatic moves ahead—including a steep drop in gold and a significant oil pullback—while labeling Bitcoin the “most bullish asset in the near term.”

## Oil: From Breakout to Pullback

Soloway first gained attention for his bullish oil call in late December, when he identified a compelling chart pattern. “I talked about it being potentially the first half best performer of any major asset class,” he recalled. The technical setup featured a downsloping trendline and clear support, with the breakout occurring on January 9. While Soloway didn’t predict the Iran conflict, the chart “was telling me something was going to happen.”

Oil subsequently spiked to $120 a barrel, but Soloway believes the highs are in. “I would expect a drawdown in the next 3 to 6 months back to $70 a barrel,” he stated, citing both geopolitical de-escalation and political pressure ahead of the U.S. midterm elections. “The president needs to get oil down probably within a month or two significantly to curb the inflation that would be hitting around the midterm elections.”

He also highlighted the psychological dynamics of such spikes. “When you get these explosive moves… I usually counter trade them.” Soloway shorted oil around $100, exiting as it pulled back toward $75–80. “A good technician stays very logical and watches others’ emotion to trigger the inverse move.”

## Gold and Silver: A Sharp Reversal Ahead

Gold’s recent volatility—including a rare 2.5% intraday drop to around $4,800—has left many questioning its safe-haven status. Soloway argues that gold has become emotionally driven, attracting retail investors seeking quick gains rather than safety.

“It now has become a ‘I’m going to get rich quick’ type asset, which is not a risk-off asset,” he explained. This shift in psychology, combined with bearish chart patterns, leads him to a contrarian year-end forecast: “I think gold by the end of this year is back to $3,500 per ounce.” For silver, he sees a drop to $50–$54.

Despite the near-term bearishness, Soloway remains a long-term buyer of both metals. “Fiat currencies, the government printing presses… you have to take that into account.” He prefers gold for its relative stability but will accumulate both at his target levels once weak hands are flushed out.

## Equities and the Looming Shadow of 2008

Soloway is net bearish on the S&P 500, pointing to a “rounded top” formation that reminds him of 2008. “You keep telling people to buy the dip, but institutions are selling into it,” he said. The parallel extends to oil’s spike and stress in private credit markets. “You’re seeing oil pop just like ’08 and you’re seeing private credit markets start to have upheaval very, very eerily similar.”

He also highlighted the risk of stagflation—rising inflation alongside a slowing economy—which he began warning about in December. Recent PPI data showing 0.7% month-over-month inflation supports his view. “Multiply that by 12, you’re talking about 7 to 10% PPI inflation if it were to stay that way.”

Labor market weakness and AI-driven layoffs add to his concerns. Companies like Block, Oracle, and Meta are cutting significant portions of their workforce. “Imagine if every company lays 20 or 30% of their workforce off. That’s going to create a spike probably north of 10% unemployment.”

## The Fed’s Precarious Position

With a new Fed Chair arriving in May, the central bank faces a difficult balancing act. Earlier expectations of immediate rate cuts have faded amid persistent inflation. Soloway believes the Fed has learned from 2008, when the ECB raised rates into a crisis, but notes the market may be moving regardless. The 10-year yield has risen about 30 basis points since early March, reflecting inflation expectations and effectively tightening financial conditions naturally.

## Bitcoin: The Near-Term Bright Spot

When asked which asset he’s most bullish on in the near term, Soloway didn’t hesitate: “Absolutely Bitcoin.” While he didn’t elaborate further in this segment, the statement underscores his view that cryptocurrencies may outperform traditional safe havens and equities in the current climate.

## Final Thoughts

Soloway’s analysis blends technical patterns, macroeconomic trends, and market psychology. His calls are bold—gold dropping over $1,000, oil retreating to $70, and equities facing a rounded top reminiscent of 2008. Yet his track record with oil’s breakout lends credibility to his warnings.

As he put it, “Charts tend to give you a heads up something’s going to happen.” Investors will be watching closely to see if his latest forecasts prove equally prescient.
