## The Bond Market’s Hidden Danger: Duration Risk
A critical technical discussion focuses on the bond market, referencing an article by Harley Bassman titled "A Fat Cat in a Small Door." Kientz explains a fundamental flaw in the US Treasury futures contract, created in 1976 with a target interest rate of 6%.

*   **The Mechanism:** When rates are below 6%, bond holders (shorts) deliver bonds without significant risk. However, when rates approach or exceed 5.5%–6%, the risk profile shifts dramatically due to "duration risk."
*   **Duration Risk Defined:** As interest rates rise, the value of existing long-term bonds (15-30 years) falls. If rates rise, bond holders face steep losses if they hold.
*   **The Fire Sale:** Because the futures contract does not adequately account for yield changes above 6%, institutional investors and pension funds are forced to dump long-duration treasuries as yields hit this critical zone. This creates a fire sale, potentially adding $110 billion in supply monthly to a market already struggling with Treasury auctions.

This dynamic threatens to destabilize pensions and retirement funds heavily allocated to bonds, especially for those aged 40-60.
