As Google’s latest open-source model, **Gemma 4**, makes waves in the AI community, a critical question arises for enthusiasts and professionals alike: How do the heavy hitters of the GPU world stack up when running these models? 

To find out, we put the NVIDIA RTX 3090, 4090, and the highly anticipated 5090 to the test. With all three cards boasting at least 24 GB of VRAM, we set the stage for a fair, side-by-side comparison using the `llama.cpp` inference engine on a Linux environment.

## The Contenders and the Models
Gemma 4 is available in four sizes: 2.3B, 4.5B, 31B, and a 26B Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model. For this benchmark, we focused on the two largest variants:
*   **31B Dense Model:** A heavy-duty model requiring significant computational power.
*   **26B MoE Model:** A Mixture-of-Experts architecture where only 4 billion parameters are active at any given time, allowing for significantly faster inference.

### Test Setup
To ensure consistency, we built `llama.cpp` from scratch for all platforms, ensuring the exact same build environment across all three GPUs.

---

## Benchmark Results: 31B Dense Model
When running the 31B model, the performance gap between generations became immediately apparent. The RTX 5090 proved to be a clear outlier, leaving its predecessors in the dust.

**Generating Speed (Tokens per Second):**
*   **RTX 5090:** 64.8 tokens/s
*   **RTX 4090:** 42.3 tokens/s
*   **RTX 3090:** 35.7 tokens/s

"The 5090 is just an outlier which performs much faster than both 4090 and 3090," noted the testing team. While the 3090 and 4090 maintained a relatively close performance bracket, the 5090 demonstrated a significant leap in raw throughput.

---

## Benchmark Results: 26B Mixture-of-Experts (MoE)
The performance landscape shifted when we switched to the 26B MoE model. Because this architecture is optimized for efficiency, all three cards saw a massive boost in speed, with every card breaking the 100 tokens-per-second barrier.

**Generating Speed (Tokens per Second):**
*   **RTX 5090:** 182 tokens/s
*   **RTX 4090:** 147 tokens/s
*   **RTX 3090:** 120 tokens/s

The efficiency of the MoE architecture meant that the hardware was less bottlenecked, allowing the cards to shine.

---

## Deep Dive: `llama-bench` Analysis
Using the `llama-bench` utility, we analyzed prompt evaluation (the time taken to process the input) versus token generation. 

During the test, we monitored the RTX 5090 (equipped with 32 GB of VRAM). Even under load, the model used only about 17 GB of VRAM, with GPU utilization hovering around 90%, leaving plenty of headroom for other tasks.

| Metric | RTX 3090 | RTX 4090 | RTX 5090 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Prompt Eval (Tokens/s)** | ~4,000 | 8,600 | ~10,000 |
| **Token Generation (Tokens/s)** | 129 | 160 | 190 |

"Because the model is a mixture of expert model, it doesn’t depend a lot on the VRAM," the report noted. "All three of them have enough VRAM, so the generating speeds are much closer than the previous dense model."

---

## Final Thoughts
All three cards—the 3090, 4090, and 5090—are highly capable of running Gemma 4 efficiently. However, if you are looking for the absolute peak of performance, the RTX 5090 is in a league of its own, particularly with dense models.
