In a recent interview, Tavi Costa, co-founder and CEO of Azurite Capital, discussed the severe impact of high interest rates on global economies, using Brazil’s recent election as a case study for market volatility. He warned that the US debt burden is becoming unsustainable at current rate levels and highlighted precious metals as a critical asset class in an era of fiscal dominance.

## The Brazilian Election Shock: A Market Case Study

The immediate aftermath of the Brazilian presidential election served as a stark reminder of how political uncertainty impacts emerging markets. Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, seeking his fourth non-consecutive term, defeated conservative leader Bolsonaro with 51.8% of the vote to 48%. Despite predictions favoring Bolsonaro’s re-election with an 80%+ probability, Lula secured victory.

The market reaction was violent. The EWZ Brazilian stock market ETF dropped approximately 20% in a single day, marking its worst performance since the pandemic. Costa, who is Brazilian and actively invested in the region, noted that the "political premium" associated with a potential Bolsonaro win—expected to foster a more market-oriented environment—was completely wiped out.

Costa revealed that he had reduced his exposure to Brazil by nearly 70% prior to the election. While he did not predict Lula’s victory, he anticipated a "buy the rumor, sell the news" scenario where markets might decline even if Bolsonaro won due to excessive optimism. He emphasized that in emerging markets like Brazil, nothing is certain; corruption and electronic voting complexities add layers of risk that developed market investors often underestimate.

## Long-Term Thesis: Hard Assets and Commodities

Despite the short-term political setback, Costa maintains a bullish long-term thesis on Brazil centered on hard assets rather than politics alone. He argues that the secular bull market in commodities is the primary driver for investing in resource-rich nations like Brazil.

Costa pointed to historical precedents, noting that Brazil experienced a significant secular bull market in the early 2000s under Lula’s leadership. This suggests that political ideology does not necessarily preclude strong market performance if the underlying commodity cycle is favorable. He views the current downturn as a testing ground for this thesis and sees it as a significant contrarian opportunity, though he advises gradual re-entry into the market rather than rushing in.

## The US Debt Trap: A "Suicidal" Tightening Cycle

Shifting focus to the United States, Costa issued a dire warning about the Federal Reserve’s current policy stance. He described the Fed’s tightening cycle as "suicidal," arguing that the US cannot sustain current interest rates given its massive debt load.

Key points in his analysis include:
*   **Interest Expenses:** Interest payments are rising as a percentage of government expenditures, which Costa likens to a "cancer" in the system.
*   **Nominal Growth vs. Debt:** The US economy currently grows at about 5% nominal rates just to service existing debt. If growth decelerates to 2-3% or turns negative during a recession, the system will break.
*   **Financial Repression:** Costa predicts that the Fed will eventually be forced into financial repression—keeping real rates low relative to inflation—to manage the debt burden. He argues that no matter how hawkish policymakers like Paul Volcker may be, markets will force a pivot when they "break."

## Precious Metals: The Ultimate Hedge Against Fiscal Dominance

Costa identifies precious metals, particularly gold and silver, as the most mispriced assets in the current environment. As long-term interest rates begin to roll over and the US dollar weakens, the macroeconomic backdrop becomes increasingly favorable for hard assets.

He notes that mining stocks are currently "extremely oversold," with valuations at historical lows. Costa believes this is unsustainable. The core argument for precious metals rests on the inevitability of continued fiscal spending by both political parties in the US. With an economy addicted to fiscal stimulus, stopping spending would halt growth, forcing politicians to choose between inflation and recession. This dynamic ensures a long-term tailwind for gold and silver.

## Conclusion: A Window of Opportunity

Tavi Costa concludes that we are at a critical inflection point in global markets. With long-term rates rolling over, the dollar weakening, and precious metals deeply oversold, he sees a 12-to-24-month window of significant opportunity. While short-term volatility remains high due to elections and geopolitical tensions, the secular bull market in commodities and hard assets appears intact, offering investors a chance to capitalize on the impending shift toward financial repression and monetary debasement.
