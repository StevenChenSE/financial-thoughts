## The Case for a Market Meltdown
Horwitz argues that the current economy is a "K-shaped" system where the wealthy benefit from asset inflation while the middle class suffers. He criticizes the Federal Reserve for its decades-long practice of keeping interest rates artificially low, a policy he believes has created massive bubbles. He contends that the only way to restore fairness to the economy is to let the market collapse, forcing a "wash out" of debt and resetting asset prices to their true value.

He dismisses the idea of government intervention, arguing that it has never worked and only benefits the wealthy. Instead, he advocates for a return to a true free market where companies that cannot make money are allowed to fail, making way for new innovation.
