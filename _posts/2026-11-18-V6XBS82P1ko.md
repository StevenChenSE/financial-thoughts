Rubino argues that the current environment is not just an AI problem, but a "peak complexity" problem. As new systems struggle to integrate with obsolete infrastructure, the risk to individual investors’ digital assets—bank accounts and brokerage portfolios—is rising sharply.

The immediate catalyst for the discussion was a wave of global disruptions that occurred in rapid succession. Rubino highlighted several critical incidents that illustrate the fragility of modern systems:

*   **US Air Traffic Grounding:** The US grounded all flights for days due to system incompatibilities between old and new air traffic control technologies.

Rubino posits that these are not isolated incidents but symptoms of a broader issue: things are getting too complicated for the people managing them to handle effectively. New AI models are becoming increasingly proficient at hacking, while aging infrastructure becomes easier to exploit over time. There is no immediate fix for this dynamic, creating a persistent risk environment.

## The Vulnerability of Digital Wealth

For the average investor, the implication is stark: digital financial accounts may no longer be safe havens for liquidity or generational wealth. Rubino warns that if you view your bank account as your primary source of liquidity, you are exposed to significant cyber risk. Similarly, betting a family’s future entirely on brokerage accounts (equities) carries the dual threat of market downturns and potential hacking incidents.

The rise of AI agents in banking further complicates matters. Banks have recently had to ban or restrict AI agents that were autonomously buying and selling for customers, unsure of how to manage their unpredictable behavior. This unpredictability, combined with the fact that AI-driven hacks are becoming more frequent and sophisticated, suggests that who knows what will happen to accounts at major institutions like Charles Schwab going forward.

## Hedging Against Cyber Risk: The Case for Physical Assets

If digital assets are vulnerable to both market crashes and cyber-attacks, how should individuals hedge? Rubino advocates for a different kind of diversification—one that moves away from financial assets entirely.

### Precious Metals and Cash
Rubino recommends holding **physical gold and silver** outside of the financial system. These real assets cannot be hacked in the same way a digital ledger can. While they do not offer the same liquidity as cash, they provide security against systemic failure.

He also emphasizes the importance of **physical cash**. While paper money loses purchasing power over time due to inflation, it remains pure liquidity. In a crisis where banking apps are down (as seen with recent outages at Chase, PNC, US Bank, and others), having a box of $20 bills can be the difference between being stranded and moving forward.

### Real Estate
A well-chosen piece of rental real estate is another tangible asset that is harder to "confiscate" or hack remotely. Rubino notes that while housing prices may crash, physical property remains a solid store of value compared to digital entries in a brokerage account.

*   Negative yields on many world bonds.
