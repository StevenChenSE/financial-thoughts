## Accelerating Liquidity, Yield Curves, and Commodity Forecasts in a Shifting Macro Landscape

In a July 10, 2026 episode of *Capital Cause*, host Danny spoke with Professor Steve Hanke to dissect the current macroeconomic environment. The discussion covered rising US Treasury yields, the accelerating money supply, the emergence of dual bubbles in equity markets, risks associated with the Japanese carry trade, and bullish forecasts for energy and precious metals.

### US Yields and the Inflation Sequence

The conversation opened with the US 10-year yield, which has breached the 4.5% level long considered a "no-go zone" and recently touched 4.6%. Hanke emphasized that this move signals the "inflation genie is out of the bottle." He outlined a clear causal sequence: an acceleration in the money supply leads to higher inflation after a lag of 12 to 24 months, which is subsequently followed by rising yields. Currently, the market is witnessing the yield response to monetary expansion that has been building for over 18 months.

### The Money Supply and Stock Market Bubbles

US dollar M2SL money supply has been accelerating since a local bottom in March 2023, now reaching approximately $23 trillion. While the year-over-year growth rate sits between 6% and 7%, short-term annualized rates are climbing faster, with commercial banking money growth exceeding 8%. Hanke noted that this liquidity surge is a primary driver of asset prices.

Hanke identified a "deadly cocktail" of two simultaneous bubbles in the stock market:
1.  **Valuation Bubble:** Characterized by very high P/E ratios.
2.  **Earnings Bubble:** Earnings are currently running about 60% above their trend rate of growth.

When controlling for the money supply, the S&P 500 divided by M2 is tied with levels seen during the 2000 bubble. Hanke warned that while liquidity fuels these bubbles, normalization of valuations and earnings will eventually cause the bubble to pop, particularly if the Federal Reserve tightens monetary policy.

### Japan, the Yen, and the Carry Trade Warning

The Japanese 10-year yield is rising while the yen tanks to lows unseen since 1986. Hanke clarified that the Japanese story is distinct from the US in terms of causality. Japan is experiencing tight monetary policy with slow money growth, contrasted by extraordinarily loose fiscal policy and a debt-to-GDP ratio over 200%. The Bank of Japan's misguidance has created a drag on the economy and pressure on the currency.

A critical macro indicator is the spread between the US and Japanese 10-year yields, which has contracted to 1.8% from a high of 4.2% in October 2023. This spread facilitates the Japanese carry trade, where investors borrow cheap yen to invest in higher-yielding assets abroad. Hanke pointed out that historical dips in this spread preceded market crashes in 2008, 2012, and 2020. He labeled the current contraction a "yellow light," advising investors to monitor the carry trade unwinding as a potential trigger for market volatility.

### Energy Markets and Oil Mispricing

WTI crude oil is trading around $71–$72, but Hanke argues the market is mispriced. Inventories are dangerously low, placing the market in backwardation, where spot prices exceed futures prices. This condition, rare in the last 20 years, indicates tight supply. Hanke suggested that market participants were misled by perceptions that the Straits of Hormuz were open, but Iranian drone attacks on both Iranian and Omani routes have effectively shut the straits.

With demand recovery underway and governments likely to refill Strategic Petroleum Reserves (SPRs), Hanke forecasts a base case for WTI to reach $80–$85 by the end of the year. He recommends staying long on oil and refined products through year-end, expecting the market to shift toward surplus and contango in 2027 once storage levels normalize.

### Gold and Central Bank Demand

Gold remains in a secular bull market supported by robust central bank buying. China recently set a record for monthly purchases, and the majority of central banks have indicated they will continue buying over the next 12 months. Gold is currently consolidating above $4,000 per ounce. Hanke projects that the end of this bull market could see prices reach approximately $6,000 per ounce.

### Summary

Professor Steve Hanke stressed that the accelerating money supply is the first-order condition driving current asset strength, inflation, and yield increases. However, the presence of dual valuation and earnings bubbles requires vigilance. Investors should monitor the US-Japan yield spread and carry trade dynamics as potential warning signs. While liquidity supports markets now, Hanke reiterated that bubbles historically pop when monetary policy tightens, urging caution and attention to macroeconomic shifts.
