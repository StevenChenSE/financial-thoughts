## Surging Treasury yields, energy spikes, and narrowing equity breadth are pushing the global financial system toward a decisive macroeconomic turning point.

In a comprehensive market discussion with host David Lin, Mike McGlone, Senior Commodity Strategist at Bloomberg Intelligence, broke down the intensifying pressures across global asset classes. Drawing from insights at the Greenwich Economic Forum, McGlone argued that elevated Treasury yields and geopolitical energy spikes are destabilizing broader financial markets in a manner reminiscent of the 1987 bond market collapse.

---

### Peak Energy Crisis and the Oil Conundrum

Crude oil has experienced sharp volatility, oscillating between rapid surges driven by Middle East tensions and deep pullbacks. Despite recent spikes in West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude and Brent, McGlone views the rally as an unsustainable late-stage move rather than a structural bull market.

According to McGlone, the fundamental drivers point toward an eventual price collapse:

* **Massive Supply Surpluses:** North America alone produces an immense liquid fuel surplus, with US and Canadian net exports approaching 9 million barrels per day—more than double the levels seen during previous market peaks.
* **Structural Substitution:** Sustained high prices accelerate the adoption of electric vehicles (EVs) and alternative energy sources, notably driven by manufacturing in China.
* **Production Economics:** The average US production cost for crude sits near $55 per barrel, establishing a natural gravitational pull for prices once speculative geopolitical premiums fade.

McGlone noted that commodity markets characteristically form price peaks when inventories trough and geopolitical rhetoric reaches fever pitch. The steep backwardation across oil and diesel futures indicates that the broader market expects significant price normalization over the coming year.

---

### The 1987 Parallel: Bonds at a Generational Crossroads

One of the most consequential developments in the macro landscape is the relentless rise of US Treasury yields, with the 5-year, 10-year, and 30-year yields all testing or exceeding the 5% threshold—levels not sustained for over two decades.

McGlone described the aggressive liquidation in government debt as a classic "buy when they're crying" scenario. Comparing current conditions to the collapse of the bond market preceding the crash of 1987, he emphasized that leveraged positions are being forcibly cleared out.

With safe-haven government paper offering equity-like fixed yields above 5%, the pressure on non-yielding and risk-heavy assets continues to compound. In McGlone’s view, high yields will persist until the broader stock market finally experiences a meaningful correction, which would rapidly trigger a sharp drop in yields.

---

### "Stock Puppets" and Underlying Equity Fragility

A recurring theme in McGlone’s analysis is that virtually all risk assets have become subservient to equity valuations. Total US equity capitalization sits near $82 trillion, representing roughly 2.5 times US gross domestic product (GDP)—an unprecedented level of economic concentration.

While headline indices like the S&P 500 have remained resilient, market breadth reveals significant internal weakness:

1. **Hidden Bear Markets:** More than 40% of individual S&P 500 components have already fallen 20% or more from their 52-week highs, particularly across defensive sectors like utilities and consumer staples.
2. **Tech Concentration:** Benchmark performance is largely sustained by a handful of mega-cap technology leaders.
3. **High Correlation:** Industrial metals like copper and broader commodity indices are trading with near-record correlation to equities.

McGlone noted that if the S&P 500 were to drop 10% to 20%, high-beta risk assets would likely experience disproportionate pullbacks.

---

### Bitcoin vs. Gold: Divergent Strength and Sentiment

When assessing risk assets versus traditional hedges, McGlone remains cautious on cryptocurrencies while maintaining a structural preference for precious metals.

* **Bitcoin as High-Beta Risk:** On a 12-month relative basis, Bitcoin has underperformed standard equity benchmarks while trading at more than triple the volatility. McGlone warned that overwhelming bullish consensus among crypto proponents at industry conferences is typically a contrarian warning sign. If broader equities stumble, Bitcoin could face substantial downside.
* **Gold as the Monetary Store:** Gold remains the sole commodity with a multi-century track record of structural capital preservation. While gold faces immediate headwinds from high real yields, its risk-adjusted resilience remains superior to speculative digital assets in a liquidity crunch.

---

### Summary and Macroeconomic Outlook

Mike McGlone maintains that the global economy is in the final stages of a temporary energy-driven inflation spike that will ultimately resolve into broad deflation.

* **Bonds:** Historic sell-offs in Treasuries offer asymmetric upside once equity volatility re-emerges and forces yields lower.
* **Commodities:** High oil prices are generating their own cure through demand destruction, technological substitution, and mounting Western hemisphere supply surpluses.
* **Equities & Risk Assets:** Elevated valuations against GDP leave markets vulnerable to sharp drawdowns, which will serve as the primary catalyst for the next major deflationary shift.
