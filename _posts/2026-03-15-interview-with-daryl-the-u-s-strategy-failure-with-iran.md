In the complex theater of Middle Eastern geopolitics, one retired U.S. military officer, Daryl, offers a blunt and sobering diagnosis of American policy toward Iran: a failure rooted in the absence of a coherent strategy. His analysis points to a toxic blend of ignorance and arrogance in Washington that has led the United States into a protracted, unwinnable position.

“When there is no strategy in war,” Daryl observes, “there is no end to the war. We never had a strategy.”

## A Fatal Misreading of an Ancient Power

At the heart of this strategic failure, according to Daryl, is a fundamental misunderstanding of Iran itself. U.S. policymakers, he argues, consistently underestimated both the nation’s capabilities and its resilience.

“We did not have a high opinion of Iran and its military capabilities. We didn't understand Iran,” he states. “It's been around for 2,700 years. This is a large civilizational state. And this state is not going to roll over for Israel or us.”

This perspective frames Iran not as a temporary regime, but as an enduring entity with deep historical consciousness and national pride—factors often overlooked in Western capitals.

## The "Enforcer" for Unrealistic Demands

Daryl pulls no punches in identifying what he sees as the driving force behind recent U.S. pressure on Tehran: the adoption of Israeli objectives as American policy.

“Remember that all the demands that President Trump has set forth are Israeli demands,” he asserts. “All we have been doing is acting as Israel's enforcer.”

He characterizes the demands placed on Iran as a sweeping ultimatum for total submission, far beyond the scope of nuclear non-proliferation:
“Israel's demands were never realistic because effectively you give up not just your uranium enrichment, you're going to surrender your ballistic missile arsenal. You're going to stop cooperating with your friends in the Gulf, whoever they are, and you're going to basically worship at the altar of Israel.”

The ultimate goal, in this view, was not merely containment or agreement, but hegemony: “You're going to do what we tell you. You're going to accept our military and political hegemony over the region.”

## An Impossible Proposition

Daryl’s conclusion is stark and unequivocal. Such an approach was destined for failure because it asked for the impossible from a sovereign, ancient nation.

“Well, Daryl, that's not going to happen,” he says, summarizing Tehran’s certain response. “That's an impossibility.”

His analysis suggests that without a fundamental shift—away from enforcement of another nation’s maximalist demands and toward a strategy that acknowledges Iran’s history, sovereignty, and regional role—the United States will remain trapped in a conflict with no clear objective and no viable path to resolution. The lesson is clear: in statecraft, as in war, a strategy built on misunderstanding and overreach is no strategy at all.
