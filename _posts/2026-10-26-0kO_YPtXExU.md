## The Lessons of History: Capital Preservation First

Starting his career just as the "Nifty 50" era culminated in the brutal 1973–1974 bear market, Hunter learned early that risk management matters more than chasing returns. During that period, institutional consensus held that blue-chip corporate giants were "one-decision" investments that could be held indefinitely regardless of valuation. When that paradigm collapsed, it wiped out portfolios and taught Hunter that capital preservation must always take precedence over the pursuit of gains.

Throughout subsequent decades—from the 1987 crash while at Fidelity to managing pension capital at Textron during the pivotal bottom of 1982—Hunter observed that market psychology rarely changes:
- **Momentum Shapes the Narrative:** Market sentiment almost always follows price action. When yields or asset prices break out, analysts construct consensus explanations justifying the move after the fact.
- **Markets Discount the Future:** Current news is typically already priced into assets. Successful investors anticipate the rate of change several months ahead rather than reacting to headlines.
- **Mistakes Are the Ultimate Teacher:** While inexperienced retail investors often repeat the same behavioral errors in every cycle, enduring market participants adapt their emotional discipline to avoid repeating past missteps.
