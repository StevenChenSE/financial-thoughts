## Milton Burke Predicts Aggressive Fed Action Amidst Market Volatility

With the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting imminent, markets are sliding intraday, prompting questions about the Fed’s next moves and their long-term implications. Milton Burke, founder of MB Advisors and a veteran Wall Street fund manager, joins the discussion to dissect the expectations for the Fed, the potential for rate hikes, and the technical indicators suggesting a market correction rather than a bear market.

### The Fed’s Mandate and Kevin Warsh’s Leadership

Burke begins by expressing strong confidence in Kevin Warsh, anticipating that he may become one of the greatest Federal Reserve Chairmen in U.S. history. Unlike his predecessors, Burke views Warsh as a "real Wall Streeter" with a deep understanding of monetary policy who is not swayed by political pressure. Burke argues that Warsh is committed to fighting inflation, which has remained above the 2% target for five years.

Contrary to the consensus that the Fed will hold rates steady at the current meeting, Burke believes there is a greater than 50% chance of a rate hike. He argues that the Fed historically follows the 2-year Treasury yield and will not let it get ahead of them. Furthermore, Burke suggests that Warsh may eventually lower the inflation target to 1% to better protect savers, emphasizing that maintaining the currency's value is the Fed's primary mandate, with employment being a secondary factor that naturally improves with price stability.

### Quantitative Tightening and Balance Sheet Reduction

Beyond interest rates, Burke highlights the importance of the Fed's balance sheet. He believes the balance sheet is excessively large, a remnant of wartime fiscal policy, and that quantitative tightening—selling bonds to reduce the balance sheet—will have a more direct and significant impact on inflation than rate hikes alone. He advises investors to focus on these fundamental monetary shifts rather than transient factors like oil prices, which he views as political rather than monetary issues.

### Market Technicals: Correction vs. Bear Market

Addressing the current market decline, with the NASDAQ down 1.4% and semiconductors experiencing significant drops, Burke characterizes the action as a correction rather than the start of a bear market. He points to several technical indicators:

*   **Montgomery Cycles:** Citing the work of Paul McCracken Montgomery, Burke notes that we are currently in a cycle period where markets are prone to bottoming. This led his firm to take a small 1% long position in the KOSPI (South Korea’s index), which has declined nearly 44%, viewing the orderly decline and lack of major downside gaps as signs of exhaustion.
*   **Sector Divergence:** While the S&P 500 equal-weight index hit new highs, the NASDAQ and semiconductor ETFs (SMH) have fallen sharply. Burke warns that this divergence is not necessarily bullish; historically, such patterns have preceded further declines in the broader market, as seen in January 2022.
*   **Volume and Gaps:** The lack of panic selling volume and major downside gaps suggests that the market has not yet bottomed. Burke’s models require evidence of a reversal, which has not yet appeared.

### Tactical Positions and Historical Precedents

Burke reveals that his firm is tactically short on the NASDAQ, S&P 100, and semiconductor indices, having entered these positions in June. However, he remains long for retail clients using a buy-and-hold model that only exits during confirmed bear markets. He contrasts the current environment with 2022, arguing that the 2022 bear market was driven by geopolitical fears and started before the Fed tightened, whereas the current correction is driven by valuation and monetary policy adjustments.

He also debunks the notion that high P/E ratios alone signal a crash, noting that markets value growth and sentiment. However, he warns that rising interest rates could negatively impact "hyperscaler" tech companies that are increasingly relying on debt for AI infrastructure, unlike the cash-rich tech giants of the past.

### Gold and Commodity Analysis

Burke shares his precise timing in selling gold on January 29, 2026, based on several factors:
1.  **Gold-to-Oil Ratio:** Gold reached its highest historical ratio relative to crude oil, suggesting it was overvalued.
2.  **Gold-to-Housing Ratio:** Gold prices were historically high compared to median home prices, inconsistent with hyperinflation scenarios.
3.  **Gold-to-CPI Ratio:** Gold was significantly outpacing inflation, a deviation from its historical average of outperforming CPI by only 1.5% annually.

Currently, Burke is long gold, silver, and the GDX, betting on a tactical rally following the sharp correction, though he remains bearish on these assets long-term.

### Conclusion and Investment Strategy

In summary, Milton Burke expects the Fed to take action, likely through rate hikes or balance sheet reduction, to combat persistent inflation. While he acknowledges bearish technical signals in specific sectors like semiconductors, he views the broader market movement as a corrective phase rather than a bear market. Investors are advised to monitor for confirmation of market bottoms, such as panic selling volume, before reversing short positions. For retail investors, Burke promotes a disciplined, model-based approach to avoid emotional trading and capture long-term gains while mitigating bear market risks.
