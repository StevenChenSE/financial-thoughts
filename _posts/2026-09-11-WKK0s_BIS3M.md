**Gary Wagner analyzes the critical technical support at $4,400 and the macroeconomic threat posed by rising crude oil prices.**

Gold has found a critical floor at $4,400, but the metal faces a significant macroeconomic headwind from the recent surge in crude oil prices. In a recent interview, Gary Wagner, editor of GoldForecast.com, detailed how the interplay between technical analysis and inflationary pressures is defining the current market environment. As the market awaits key inflation data, the Federal Reserve is positioned to potentially raise interest rates, a move that could fundamentally alter gold's trajectory for the remainder of the year.

## The Crude Oil and Inflation Connection

The primary driver of current market volatility is the sharp increase in crude oil. Brent crude has breached the $100-per-barrel mark, while WTI (West Texas Intermediate) futures are trading near $97. Wagner emphasizes that crude oil is not just an energy cost but a pervasive component of the economy. Because much of the production and supply chain relies on petroleum, a spike in oil prices acts as a "huge inflationary pressure across the board."

This inflationary pressure creates a complex dynamic for gold. Historically, gold thrives when inflation is increasing, as it serves as a hedge against the eroding purchasing power of the currency. Wagner notes that the recent rally in gold, which began when the price dropped to $4,000 in early August, is moving in tandem with crude oil. The correlation between the two assets has been high over the past year, with both rising and falling around the same time. However, the mechanism is indirect: rising oil increases the likelihood of inflation, which benefits gold, but it also increases the likelihood of the Federal Reserve raising interest rates to combat that inflation.

## Federal Reserve Outlook and Rate Hike Probability

With the CPI (Consumer Price Index) and PPI reports scheduled for release in the days following the interview, the market is closely watching for signals on the Federal Reserve's next move. Wagner believes there is a "very high probability" that the Fed will raise rates at the upcoming FOMC meeting. The FedWatch tool, which polls traders and bond traders to gauge yield expectations, has been moving steadily upward, with the probability of a hike reaching approximately 60% or higher.

Raising rates is the Fed's primary tool for addressing inflationary pressures, as it slows economic activity and reduces the potential for prices to spike further. For gold, higher interest rates are traditionally a negative factor because they make fixed-income assets more attractive relative to non-yielding assets like gold. Therefore, while the inflationary environment is bullish for gold, the resulting rate hikes create a bearish counter-force. Wagner suggests that if the Fed follows a 25-basis-point hike with another hike in December, it would significantly change the overall outlook for the metal.

## Technical Analysis: The $4,400 Support Level

From a technical perspective, Wagner identifies $4,400 as a "really strong level of support." This level is critical because it represents a confluence of two major technical indicators: the 61.8% Fibonacci retracement of the recent rally and the 50-day simple moving average.

Gold recently experienced a steep correction, falling from an all-time high of approximately $5,700 to a low of $4,000. The subsequent rally saw the price climb to nearly $4,800 before pulling back. Wagner notes that the price has held above the 50-day moving average since August 5th, a key indicator of bullish sentiment in the short term. However, the market has not yet overcome the long-term bearish sentiment, as the current high of $4,760 was not sustainable.

For the bullish case to continue, gold needs to hold the $4,400 support and eventually break above the previous high of $4,760. If it fails to do so and forms a lower high, it could signal a continuation of the downward trend. Wagner also utilized a Heikin Ashi chart to analyze crude oil momentum, noting that the increasing candle size indicates that the upward trend in oil is accelerating, not weakening.

## Conclusion

The outlook for gold is a battle between technical support and macroeconomic forces. The $4,400 level serves as a vital floor, backed by the 50-day moving average and Fibonacci retracement. However, the rising price of crude oil is a double-edged sword: it fuels inflation, which is bullish for gold, but it also pressures the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates, which is bearish. Investors should monitor the upcoming CPI and PPI reports closely, as these data points will determine whether the Fed is likely to maintain its hawkish stance. If the Fed commits to a series of rate hikes, the long-term outlook for gold could shift significantly, potentially leading to a break below the $4,000 psychological threshold.
