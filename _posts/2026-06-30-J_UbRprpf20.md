The monetary debasement trade is far from dead—it's just catching its breath. That's the core message from Lawrence Lepard, managing partner of Equity Management Associates, in this exclusive interview where he doubles down on his bold predictions for hard assets.

## Revisiting the 2024 Predictions That Hit the Mark

In April 2024, when gold was still hovering just over $2,000, Lepard made what seemed like an audacious call: gold would double to $5,000, and Bitcoin would triple to $150,000. The results speak for themselves. Gold has indeed approximately doubled, touching around $5,400 before pulling back. Bitcoin reached $125,000—missing the $150,000 target by about 25%, but the directional call proved remarkably accurate.

"Gold doubled, Bitcoin went to 125—I missed the Bitcoin call," Lepard admits with refreshing candor. "But the direction was right and the level was right before they retraced."

The thesis driving these predictions was straightforward: governments would continue running massive deficits and printing money. Nothing has changed that fundamental reality. In fact, events since that prediction have only reinforced the thesis.

## What's Turbocharging the Move Now

When Trump entered office at the beginning of 2024, many expected fiscal responsibility and budget balancing. "A couple months of that and Elon said, hey, I'm out of here," Lepard recalls. "Everyone kind of realized, gosh, they're just going to continue spending money, running deficits and printing the money."

The Middle East situation alone added approximately $80 billion to the budget. Overall budget deficits remain solidly over $2 trillion annually. The numbers haven't improved—they've deteriorated.

"When the mainstream press says the debasement trade is dead, I kind of chuckle," Lepard says. "Have they balanced the budget? No. Are they spending less money? No. They just can't stop printing money, and until they do, the debasement trade lives."

## Updated Forecasts: What Comes Next

Despite recent corrections, Lepard maintains conviction in hard assets reaching substantially higher levels:

**Bitcoin**: Currently down roughly 50% from its highs, carving what Lepard believes is a bottom. "I think when this one ends, we're going to have $150,000, $180,000, $200,000 on the next run up," he predicts. "Most of these Bitcoin bear markets last about 12 months, and we're about nine months into this." The power law model suggests we're near the bottom, possibly at $58,000 or dipping to $54,000 before recovery.

**Gold**: After touching $5,400, gold has pulled back to the "low fours." Lepard expects this consolidation to resolve higher. "Next year we'll be at $6,000, maybe seven," he says. Even a pullback to $3,800-$3,900 would be absorbed before breaking to new highs.

**Silver**: Following Michael Oliver's analysis, Lepard sees silver reaching $150 or north of $200. "Silver hit 120 and lost half its value—it's down at 60 now," he notes. "Even more volatile, but the same story."

## The Debasement Trade Explained

At its core, the debasement trade rests on a simple observation: governments running large deficits must monetize that debt to keep bond markets calm. "They have to print money," Lepard explains. "That's the big picture level."

The mechanics are straightforward. Before Jerome Powell left his position last December, he pivoted and started printing again. "It looked like QE," Lepard observes. "They started growing the Fed balance sheet, which adds reserves into the banking system, which then gets a multiplier effect and grows M2."

And when Powell's successor took over? The playbook remained unchanged—create inflation and lie about it. "The job of the Fed is to create inflation and lie about it," Lepard states bluntly. "They've got to create enough inflation to keep the system running because they don't want the system to collapse. And in an ever-growing system with a lot of credit, you got to make more money or else you can't pay back the credit and it would collapse. But in turn, everyone hates inflation, so they have to lie about the inflation."

## Why the Current Correction Doesn't Change the Thesis

The current narrative suggests inflation picking up means the Fed will raise rates, making bonds more attractive and reducing the need for monetary debasement protection. Lepard thinks this narrative is wrong.

"The Fed's going to claim inflation is lower than reported using the Dallas Trim PCE, which conveniently removes outliers," Lepard explains. "They'll say the numbers are high, but backward-looking. Forward-looking, creating supply by bringing rates down will bring inflation down. That's the spin."

The incentive is clear: the administration and Fed are essentially supply-siders who believe lower rates stimulate investment, create supply, and eventually reduce inflation—Alan Greenspan's 1996 argument revived. "They're going to lie about it," Lepard predicts. "And that's going to allow them to cut rates."

## Bottom Line: Conviction Over Certainty

Many investors spend excessive time seeking the perfect entry point or waiting for certainty that markets never provide. Lepard's approach differs: act when conviction is high, not when certainty is guaranteed.

"I've been through 90%, 80%, 70%, 60% drawdowns in Bitcoin," Lepard notes. "This one's a little over 50%, and the drawdowns are getting slightly smaller each one. When this ends, we're going much higher."

The fundamentals haven't changed. Deficits continue growing. Money printing continues. The dollar continues being debased. Hard assets remain the historical hedge against precisely this environment.

"Understanding an investment thesis and actually positioning for it are two very different things," Lepard concludes. The question isn't whether the debasement trade is dead—it's whether you have the conviction to position for it while everyone else is throwing stones.
