In a recent episode of the *What the Finance* podcast, host Anthony Fattuta welcomed back renowned contrarian analyst David Hunter to discuss his provocative market outlook. Despite current market volatility and widespread skepticism, Hunter maintains that we are in the final stages of a powerful bull cycle that will culminate in a historic "melt-up" before a significant global economic correction arrives in 2027.

## The Final Leg Higher: Aggressive Index Targets

Hunter’s primary thesis rests on the idea that current market downturns are merely corrections within a larger, accelerating uptrend. He argues that each successive rally since 2022 has been steeper than the last, signaling that the market is approaching its terminal velocity.

To illustrate this bullish conviction, Hunter raised his price targets for major US indices:
*   **S&P 500:** Target raised to **10,000**.
*   **Nasdaq:** Target raised to **36,000** (despite trading below 24,000 at the time of the interview).
*   **Dow Jones Industrial Average:** Target raised to **70,000**.
*   **Russell 2000:** Target set at **4,000**.

Hunter acknowledges these figures seem "crazy" given recent whipsawing markets but insists that the fundamentals—particularly strong earnings and the enduring momentum of Artificial Intelligence (AI)—remain intact. He views current fears regarding political shifts in Congress, data center regulations, and Fed policy as noise that is creating a "wall of worry," which contrarian investors should view as a buying opportunity.

## The AI Narrative: Fear vs. Fundamentals

A significant portion of the market’s recent anxiety stems from concerns about the AI sector. Fears have been fueled by political opposition to data centers, potential restrictions on semiconductor sales to China (impacting companies like **Nvidia** and **AMD**), and worries that a new Congress might unwind pro-growth policies.

Hunter dismisses these concerns as overblown psychology. He notes that:
1.  **Demand is Insatiable:** The AI build-out was already overbooked; slowing down regulatory approval or political pushback won't erase the backlog of orders for chips and infrastructure.
2.  **Market Breadth:** While tech stocks have corrected, non-AI sectors like financials, home builders, and basic materials are showing strength. The equal-weighted S&P 500 is down only ~5%, indicating that the broader market is not collapsing, but rather rotating.
3.  **Sentiment Bottoming:** Hunter believes sentiment has hit a bottom similar to levels seen in 2022, suggesting a reversal is imminent.

## The Metals Crash: A Microcosm of Leverage Unwind

The conversation turned to the brutal sell-off in precious metals, particularly silver and gold. Silver, which had surged parabolically to **$122** in January, crashed back to the low $40s—a 65% decline from its peak.

Hunter admits he underestimated the severity of this correction but attributes it primarily to **speculative excess and leverage**.
*   **Leverage Unwind:** The rapid rise attracted highly leveraged retail investors and hedge funds. As prices turned, forced selling (margin calls) created a "death spiral," driving prices down far below fundamental support levels.
*   **Technical Support:** Silver broke through key psychological levels ($62, then $50), testing historical breakout points from 1980 and 2011. Hunter views this retest as a normal technical correction, albeit one exaggerated by leverage.
*   **Long-Term Bull Case:** Despite the crash, Hunter remains a massive bull on metals. He maintains a long-term target of **$200 for silver** and sees gold finding support around **$3,500**. He argues that fundamentals remain strong, but short-term supply/demand dynamics driven by panic selling have temporarily overwhelmed value.

## The Global Bust: Why 2027 is the Danger Zone

Perhaps Hunter’s most critical warning is his timeline for a major economic crisis. He does **not** believe we are in a recession now. Instead, he predicts:
1.  **2026:** The market continues to rise, completing the melt-up phase.
2.  **Second Half of 2027:** A global bust begins.

Hunter argues that central banks, particularly the Federal Reserve, will make a critical error in 2027. Fearing inflation and trying to avoid repeating the mistakes of 2008 and 2020, the Fed may be reluctant to inject liquidity quickly when the crisis hits. This hesitation could allow leverage-driven deleveraging to spiral out of control before policymakers are forced to act.

Once the crisis peaks, Hunter expects a response far more aggressive than previous interventions:
*   **Massive QE:** Potentially up to **$20 trillion** in new quantitative easing globally.
*   **Rate Collapse:** The 10-year Treasury yield could drop from ~5% to **0%**, and the 30-year yield to **0.5%**.

This massive money printing would ultimately stabilize markets but would also set the stage for future asset inflation, reinforcing his long-term bullish view on hard assets like gold and silver (with a decades-long target of $1,000/oz for silver).

## Conclusion: Stay the Course Through Volatility

David Hunter’s message to investors is one of psychological discipline. He acknowledges that current losses are painful, especially for those using leverage in metals or tech stocks. However, he urges investors to understand that market sentiment swings from extreme fear to extreme greed.

*   **Short Term:** Expect a reversal and a steep rally as the "wall of worry" is climbed.
*   **Medium Term:** Prepare for a significant global economic contraction starting in late 2027.
*   **Long Term:** Hard assets (gold, silver) and equities will benefit from inevitable massive monetary expansion.

Hunter’s final advice: Keep your wits about you, avoid panic selling at the lows, and recognize that we are likely near a bottom in both stock and metals markets before the next leg up begins.
