## Gary Wagner Assesses Support Levels, Resistance Zones, And Macro Drivers Shaping Gold's Near-Term Trajectory

On July 14, gold markets responded to a lower-than-expected Consumer Price Index (CPI) print of 3.5%, which beat the consensus forecast of 3.8% and followed a previous reading of 4.2%. The cooling inflation data triggered a rally across risk assets: the S&P 500 and Nasdaq climbed, Bitcoin surged 3.5%, and gold spiked 1.4% on an intraday basis. Gary Wagner, editor of goldforecast.com, discusses whether the recent rebound confirms a bottom at the psychological $4,000 level and outlines the technical structures and macroeconomic factors influencing the precious metal's path forward.

### Technical Analysis: $4,000 Support and Candlestick Patterns

Wagner identifies $4,000 as a tentative but definitive level of support. Technical analysis of the price action reveals that gold touched a low near $3,990 before recovering quickly, with the "real body" of the candlestick closing significantly above the open, suggesting bullish momentum. This recovery follows a roughly $100 sell-off the previous day, indicating that buyers are stepping in at this threshold.

The chart structure is forming a potential double bottom or tweezer bottom pattern, with recent lows aligning closely with support established on June 24. Wagner notes that while $4,000 serves as a psychological landmark, the structural integrity of the level is reinforced by multiple tests where gold has held without a decisive break. The duration of time gold has spent above $4,000 throughout 2026 further strengthens the argument that this level is a significant area for accumulation compared to higher thresholds like $5,000.

### Key Resistance Levels and Trend Confirmation

Despite the support at $4,000, Wagner cautions that technical confirmation of a bottom requires a sustained move above specific resistance zones. Immediate resistance sits between $4,080 and $4,090, based on the bottom formed in October 2025. The critical level for confirming a trend reversal is approximately $4,180 to $4,190.

Gold has been in a corrective phase characterized by a series of lower highs and lower lows since its all-time high above $5,600. To signal that this correction has ended, price action must break above the $4,190 region to initiate a sequence of higher highs. Major resistance looms higher at $4,380 to $4,400, which matches previous tops from November. Wagner emphasizes that a close above $4,190 is necessary before investors can confidently assert that gold has resumed a bullish demeanor.

### Geopolitical Risks and Inflation Outlook

Macroeconomic drivers continue to play a pivotal role in gold's valuation. The 3.5% CPI print suggests fewer rate hikes may be necessary later in the year, providing a tailwind for assets. However, Wagner highlights geopolitical tensions as a counterforce to cooling inflation. He describes the situation in Iran and the Strait of Hormuz as a "tinderbox," noting that renewed military activity could reignite inflation concerns.

Crude oil prices have begun to tick upward after forming a bottom, and Wagner stresses that in a petroleum-based society, oil impacts the dollar and all asset classes. The current inflation reprieve may be short-lived if geopolitical instability drives energy costs higher, which would likely support gold as a hedge against currency debasement and rising prices.

### Historical Context and Fibonacci Analysis

Wagner draws parallels to historical market cycles, referencing the correction from the 2011 peak of $1,900 to the 2016 bottom at $1,044. He notes that during that period, reports of accumulation by major players like George Soros coincided with the market bottom. The current cycle saw a massive spike from roughly $1,800 to $3,400 before reaching the all-time high, and the correction from $5,600 to $4,000 represents a decline of approximately $1,600 per troy ounce.

Fibonacci retracement analysis of the move from the all-time high to current lows suggests a 23.6% retracement level at $4,300. Wagner argues that overcoming this level, along with the $4,190 resistance, would be indicative of a healthy recovery. While the speed of the decline mirrored the ascent, the formation of a base at $4,000 suggests the selling pressure may be exhausting, provided higher resistance levels are conquered.

### Conclusion and Trading Strategy

Wagner concludes that the current environment does not warrant a bearish stance. The formation of a base at $4,000 offers strong support, and the market is likely to challenge higher levels. However, traders should exercise patience until gold demonstrates the ability to close above $4,190, which would confirm the end of the correction.

If support holds, the path to recovery involves sequential tests of resistance at $4,080, $4,190, and eventually $4,400. Conversely, if selling pressure resumes, Wagner points to secondary support levels near $3,935 to $3,920. For now, the focus remains on whether gold can establish higher highs to break the downtrend structure and validate the $4,000 floor as a launchpad for a new rally.

### Summary

Gold has established tentative support at $4,000 following a bullish reaction to cooler CPI data and rising geopolitical risks. Gary Wagner emphasizes that while the double bottom pattern and duration of price action support this level, technical confirmation requires a break above $4,190. Investors should watch for higher highs to signal the end of the correction from $5,600, while remaining aware of resistance at $4,400 and macro drivers like oil and Iran tensions.
