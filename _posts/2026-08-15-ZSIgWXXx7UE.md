## The S&P 500: Technicals and the 8,200 Ceiling

Soloway identifies a clear technical setup for the S&P 500. The index has broken out of a parallel channel that existed since the 2020 lows and 2021 highs. The previous resistance level has now flipped to support, confirming a bullish trend. However, Soloway projects that the next logical parallel resistance level sits at approximately 8,100 to 8,200.

A more complex analysis involves the S&P 500 divided by the M2 money supply. This ratio measures how expensive the market is relative to the amount of money in the system. Soloway notes that if the S&P reaches 8,200, the S&P/M2 ratio would top out, reaching levels more expensive than the dot-com bubble peak. This suggests that 8,200 is a critical ceiling where a significant drawdown is likely to occur.

## Macro Headwinds: Stagflation and the Fed

Despite the bullish chart action, Soloway points to deteriorating macroeconomic fundamentals. Retail sales recently missed expectations, coming in at -0.6% versus a consensus of 0.1%, indicating consumer weakness. Meanwhile, the labor market is cooling, and inflation remains sticky at 3.4%. This combination of slowing growth and persistent inflation creates a "mini stagflation" risk for 2027.

The Federal Reserve's role in this dynamic is central. Soloway argues that Fed Chair Kevin Warsh is unlikely to raise rates further, despite hawkish rhetoric from some committee members. Soloway believes Warsh is politically and economically constrained, and that the bond market, rather than the Fed, is currently driving interest rates. He predicts the Fed will cut rates in 2027 as the labor market continues to weaken.

## Gold: The Ultimate Hedge

Soloway is highly bullish on gold, viewing it as the primary beneficiary of the coming economic turmoil. He notes that gold has broken out of a long-term wedge pattern, a classic technical signal for a major upward move. While he expects a pullback to the $3,900–$4,000 range, he advises buying these dips.

His long-term target is $13,000 per ounce by 2029–2031. Soloway bases this on the unsustainable trajectory of U.S. debt, which has recently hit $40 trillion. The interest payments on this debt already exceed $1 trillion annually, a burden that will eventually force a monetary reset. Gold, as a store of value, is positioned to outperform all other assets in this environment.

In contrast to gold and stocks, Soloway is cautious about Bitcoin. He notes that Bitcoin’s recent breakout from its downtrend is one of the weakest he has seen, lacking the momentum of gold’s rally. However, Soloway remains near-term bullish on Bitcoin. He expects President Trump to push for the Clarity Act to pass before the November midterms to court crypto voters.
