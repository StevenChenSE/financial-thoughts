## The Psychology of "Bottom Fishing": Cognitive Drive vs. Greed

When considering whether to "bottom fish" a stock like Oracle (ORCL), investors must first honestly assess their intrinsic motivation. Is the drive to buy based on a deep cognitive understanding of the company's value, or is it merely a psychological urge to "get a bargain" because the price has dropped significantly? True value investing requires distinguishing between a forced sale due to liquidity crises (margin calls, cash needs) and an active sell-off by long-term institutional funds due to fundamental deterioration. If the selling is driven by fundamentals, blindly buying the dip is dangerous.

Furthermore, market inertia is strong. As trading master Mark Minervini notes, a stock that has dropped 50% has a high probability of falling another 50-80%. Investors often fall victim to survivorship bias, backtesting only the stocks that survived and rebounded, while ignoring those that went bankrupt or were delisted. Therefore, the core strategy should not be "buying because it's cheap," but rather identifying mispricing caused by forced liquidation in high-quality assets.
