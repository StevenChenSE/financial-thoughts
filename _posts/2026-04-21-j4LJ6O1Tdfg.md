## An Unusual Market Dynamic Captivates Investors

In a market environment that has defied conventional wisdom, gold has climbed back toward $4,900 per ounce while crude oil simultaneously plummeted from $106 to just $83 per barrel. The S&P 500, meanwhile, closed above 7,100 points near record highs. This confluence of events raises an intriguing question: has gold found its bottom, and what does this unusual correlation mean for precious metals investors?

Gold market analyst Gary Wagner, editor of GoldForecast.com, recently joined a financial news program to dissect this anomalous behavior. "Really, really interesting times, especially with typical relationships that we would have with crude and gold," Wagner observed. "We're not seeing that. We're actually seeing the opposite, and that's what makes this such a fascinating time to be involved in the markets."

## Gold's Atypical Behavior: A Safe Haven Acting Like a Stock

### The Breakdown of Traditional Correlations

Historically, gold has maintained predictable relationships with other asset classes. When crude oil rises, inflationary pressures typically follow, creating headwinds for gold. Conversely, declining oil prices have traditionally taken gold lower as well. Yet the current market environment has inverted these expected dynamics.

"What we're seeing is an inverse reaction to crude oil right now," Wagner explained. "And I think it's the uncertainty factor more than anything else. If the truce holds, if we see the strait continue to have free passage, I think we will see a normal relationship between crude oil and gold kind of emerge—not the opposite."

The Iranian tensions have deescalated somewhat in recent news, which has contributed to lower oil prices while simultaneously supporting gold prices. This represents a complete reversal of the traditional correlation, where geopolitical uncertainty typically supports both commodities simultaneously.

### The Dollar's Role in the Rally

Adding to the complexity, the U.S. dollar has experienced significant weakness, moving from over 100 on the Dollar Index down to approximately 96.97—a 3% decline across a basket of major currencies. Wagner emphasized that gold is "very, very sensitive to moves in both" the dollar and crude oil, creating a powerful confluence of supportive factors.

"Gold acting like a risk-on asset going up alongside stocks and going down whenever oil spikes," Wagner noted, summarizing the market's perplexing behavior. "It's been climbing up precisely because tensions in Iran have deescalated somewhat in the news."

## Elliott Wave Analysis: Has Gold Bottomed?

### The ABC Correction Pattern

Wagner, who accurately called gold's rally throughout the previous year, has identified a classic ABC correction pattern following gold's all-time record high of $5,600 reached on January 29th. His Elliott Wave analysis provides a framework for understanding the dramatic price swings.

"Looking at my chart, we had the all-time record high that came in on January 29th above $5,600," Wagner explained. "We then got our A-wave and that moved down to $4,674. Our B-wave back up to $5,465. And the low that you're referring to at $4,100 is the conclusion of an ABC correction."

The correction itself proved dramatic. Gold fell approximately 21% in less than two weeks, bottoming around $4,100 in late March before recovering nearly 18% in just two to three weeks. Wagner noted that the bottom occurred "just below a 61.8% Fibonacci retracement" of the prior move from $3,400 to $5,600, which he described as "textbook" behavior for this technical model.

### Understanding Elliott Wave Structure

The Elliott Wave Theory provides insight into market psychology and price movements. According to Wagner, the structure breaks down as follows:

- **Motive Phase**: Waves 1, 3, and 5 move in the prevalent trend direction (upward in this case)
- **Corrective Phase**: Waves 2 and 4 provide counter-trend movements
- **Current Position**: A new primary first wave has begun following the ABC correction completion

"What's exciting is the fifth wave would have to by the actual way the model works would to be above this fifth wave, which was the all-time record high," Wagner stated. "Longer term, and I'm looking at maybe 3 to 4 months out as this structure completes, we've got our one, two, our three should take us above $5,200. A fourth wave and the final fifth wave needs to conclude above $5,600."

Wagner acknowledged that the path would not be parabolic, noting, "We will see corrective waves two and four in between that move." The timeframe, he cautioned, is approximate: "Elliot wave does not account for time in the same way it accounts for price points."

## The Silver Connection

### Parallel Movements in Precious Metals

Silver has moved in striking correlation with gold throughout this period. Starting from approximately $45 in late October, silver surged to an all-time record high above $120 per ounce by late January—a staggering 165% gain totaling $75-76 per ounce. Following gold's pattern, silver then experienced its own ABC correction.

"From $120, silver moves down to $63," Wagner noted, referencing the A-wave. "That's our A-wave. Our B-wave goes from 63 up. Now, this move here of the B-wave, if you recall with gold, it was much closer to the highs than silver. The C-wave completes a classic zigzag because what you're seeing is the C-wave being the lowest value of the correction."

Wagner expressed confidence that both metals will achieve new all-time highs: "I think both of them will achieve that. Both gold and silver are experiencing a correction right now. As I said, that's that ABC we talked about. Silver did the same thing, and it's also in its primary first wave—a new part of a motive phase."

## The Oil Picture: A Different Pattern Emerges

### Technical Analysis of Crude Oil

Oil's technical structure differs from precious metals. According to Wagner's analysis, oil's all-time record high came in "right above $119," which was labeled as a fifth wave. However, the subsequent B-wave was "almost at the all-time records high," indicating an extended B-wave pattern.

"The C-wave should conclude well below the A-wave, which it currently is," Wagner observed. "It tells me that what we witness with oil moving lower but gold and silver moving higher is not what we would expect in terms of direct correlations between those two asset classes."

The recent breakdown below the $87 support level, Wagner noted, was significant. The current low was "right above" the projected target area, suggesting potential for a bottom and subsequent rally.

### Oil as a Leading Indicator for Fed Policy

When examining longer-term charts, an interesting relationship emerges between oil prices and Federal Reserve policy. Historical data suggests oil has served as a leading indicator for Fed actions:

- Oil rose ahead of the 2022 Fed funds rate hikes, which caused markets to fall
- Fed funds rates began declining only after oil prices had already started falling

This correlation raises questions about the Fed's future policy direction if oil remains persistently elevated or continues declining.

## What This Volatility Signals About Market Sentiment

### Extreme Moves in Traditional Safe Havens

When an asset traditionally considered stable experiences 20% declines on two separate occasions within a short timeframe, it speaks volumes about market psychology and trading behavior.

"The trouble I had at the conclusion of the fifth wave was that this A-wave going from $5,600 down to $4,400 occurred over three—it's a daily chart—three trading days," Wagner observed, describing the March 30th session where gold opened at $5,450 and closed at $4,049. "That's a tremendous move in a day. It's one of the most sizable downside moves we've ever seen in gold. I believe that it holds the record for dollar moves."

This extreme volatility, Wagner suggested, reflects an environment where uncertainty dominates market movements: "We're in an environment in which the variables that gold is sensitive to—uncertainty being number one, of course crude oil and the dollar—but the fact that it went to an all-time record high in January and then, the best way to explain it technically as it fell down, was a classic ABC correction."

### Not a Double Top—But Something Significant

Regarding the potential double top pattern, Wagner clarified: "A double top would be if the next top was close to this one. You've got a low that comes in, then you have a lower high and then you get a lower low. So when you get an all-time record high, you get the first wave of the correction that was steep, but then when it went back up, you had a lower high. And therefore, I wouldn't—this is not a double top. It is a lower high than the previous high and then a lower low than the previous low."

This structure aligns with the Elliott Wave interpretation, confirming the correction pattern and the potential for new highs.

## Key Takeaways

1. **Gold is defying conventional correlations**: The metal has rallied alongside stocks while oil declined, suggesting market participants are responding to uncertainty rather than traditional safe-haven or inflation-hedge dynamics.

2. **The dollar weakness matters**: A 3% decline in the Dollar Index has contributed to gold's rally, and the metal remains highly sensitive to both currency movements and crude oil price swings.

3. **Elliott Wave analysis points to higher prices ahead**: Wagner's technical framework suggests gold is now in a new motive phase, with targets above $5,200 in the near term and above $5,600 (new highs) within 3-4 months.

4. **Silver is tracking gold's path**: Following a similar 165% rally and subsequent ABC correction, silver appears positioned to reach new highs alongside gold.

5. **The traditional oil-gold relationship remains inverted**: Until geopolitical tensions resolve and normal supply-demand dynamics reassert themselves, investors should expect unconventional correlations to persist, creating both risks and opportunities in precious metals trading.
