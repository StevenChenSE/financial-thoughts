## Rick Rule's Advice for Young Investors

In a recent interview at the 2026 Rule Symposium, legendary resource investor Rick Rule offered profound advice to Tiffany Djorgjiopolis of Natural Resource Stocks. While the symposium serves as a focal point for a year-long preparation process, the core message for aspiring investors remains timeless: before worrying about gold, silver, or any other asset, you must invest in yourself.

## The Primacy of Self-Education

Rule emphasizes that the most critical investment a young person can make is in their own education and character. He urges investors to look beyond traditional schooling, which often teaches conformity rather than independent thought. Instead, young investors should ask themselves difficult questions: What do I truly want to learn? What kind of life do I want to build? How curious am I? How hard am I willing to work? And crucially, how much risk am I willing to take?

By answering these questions, an investor builds the foundational knowledge and discipline required to succeed in the markets. Rule states clearly, "If you invest in yourself before you invest in gold or silver or anything else, you will do a better job as an investor."

## Essential Reading for Investors

To guide this self-education, Rule recommends three seminal books that challenge conventional wisdom and provide deep insights into economics and value investing:

1.  **Economics in One Lesson by Henry Hazlitt**: This book helps readers understand how the world actually works, as opposed to how academic professors might present it.
2.  **The Intelligent Investor by Benjamin Graham**: Described by Rule as the best investment book ever written relative to the effort required to read it.
3.  **Security Analysis by Benjamin Graham and David Dodd**: The book that shaped Warren Buffett's career. Rule notes that if one can endure the density of this text and apply its lessons, becoming wealthy is almost inevitable.

## Gold and Purchasing Power

Once an investor has educated themselves, they can begin to think about asset allocation. Rule suggests that gold should be viewed primarily as a tool for maintaining wealth and purchasing power over time. He notes that while the dollar loses purchasing power, gold maintains it. Having saved primarily in gold since 2000, Rule observes that goods and services appear surprisingly cheap when expressed in gold terms, whereas they seem expensive when measured in dollars. He advises young investors to consider gold for the preservation of their savings 25 years down the line.

## Building a Resource Portfolio from Scratch

When asked how he would allocate a natural resource portfolio if starting from scratch today, Rule provided a clear hierarchy based on valuation, certainty, and sector strength:

1.  **Oil and Gas**: Rule identifies this as the foundation of any resource portfolio. It is the largest sector, possesses the best data and talent, and is often underweighted by investors. He strongly disputes the notion that peak oil demand is imminent, stating it will not occur in his lifetime or likely in the lifetime of younger investors.
2.  **Gold Equities**: Currently fairly valued—a rare occurrence—gold stocks are Rule's second choice. He believes an increase in both gold prices and gold share prices is inevitable.
3.  **Copper**: While we may be early on copper, Rule sees zero doubt regarding production shortfalls over the next five years.
4.  **Uranium**: For younger investors, uranium offers "certain money" in the future, even if the "easy money" has already been made.

## The Power of Compounding

Rule concludes by highlighting the most important lesson in investing: compounding. Compounding requires time and consistent performance. For a young investor, securing certain performance over the long term sets the stage for lifelong financial success. As Rule jokingly notes, mastering these sectors requires years of knowledge, but the journey begins with the decision to learn.
