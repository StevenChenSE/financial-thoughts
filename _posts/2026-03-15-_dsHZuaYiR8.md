*At the Vancouver Resource Investment Conference, veteran resource investor and financial commentator Rick Rule shared his outlook on precious metals, market dynamics, and his firm preference for holding physical assets.*

## Gold’s Rally and the Current Consolidation

Gold has seen a significant run, but according to Rule, its reaction to geopolitical events like the tensions with Iran was muted because such events were already anticipated by the markets.

> “I love gold. I think gold is great… It already been priced in to a lot of the markets ahead of time.”

He views the current phase as a consolidation period, with gold and silver gathering strength for their next move higher. This bullish outlook is fundamentally tied to expanding global debt.

> “Obviously, the more massive the debt goes that we create, the higher the likelihood of silver and gold rallying.”

Rule expects gold to rise another **10-12% this year** and believes silver will climb back **over $100**. However, he cautions investors against the volatility that often follows sharp rallies, referencing a recent **$60 drop in gold** after a surge to $1,200 that “wiped out a lot of investors” who over-leveraged.

His advice is straightforward: **Invest only what you can afford.** Precious metals should be a part of your portfolio, but not at the expense of your mortgage or car payments.

## The Unshakeable Case for Physical Metal

When asked if he prefers physical gold or paper contracts, Rule’s answer was emphatic.

> “Always physical… There's not enough gold in the world to cover the amount of paper that's written on it.”

He draws a clear distinction between trading and investing. For trading, he uses futures and paper markets. For investing, he insists on taking delivery.

> “If I'm investing in them, I am buying them and I am storing them… I want to touch it, feel it, and hold it.”

He is less enthusiastic about mining stocks, citing “too many funny business going on there,” and prefers direct metal ownership.

## On Market Manipulation and Price Discovery

Addressing common skepticism about manipulation in the paper gold markets, Rule is unconvinced.

> “No, I don't think so. Listen, the markets still take price discovery.”

He argues that a true two-sided market is difficult to manipulate because it requires both a willing buyer and seller. Even overnight “smash” events in thin liquidity are part of trading—a zero-sum game where for every loser, there’s a winner. Prices, he says, ultimately revert to where buyers and sellers meet.

He saves his criticism for the **fiat currency system**, which he calls a “totally criminal system.”

## The Evolving Trading Landscape

Rule discussed changes in the marketplace, notably the move toward near-constant trading hours. He predicts **24/7 trading across all markets is less than a year away**, citing that many products are already at 24/5.

He also commented on the Chicago Mercantile Exchange (CME) reintroducing **single stock futures**, which failed in the past. He believes they have a better chance now due to higher market participation, more expiration dates, and the appeal to traders restricted by equity day-trading rules.

He notes that rising margin requirements often act as a tool that “helps the brokerage houses” more than it protects traders.

## Silver, Platinum, and Portfolio Strategy

**On the gold-to-silver ratio**, currently near 60:1, Rule sees room for it to tighten further, potentially into the **40s or 50s**. He highlights silver’s growing industrial demand as a new, powerful driver.

**On platinum**, trading around **$2,159** (well off its highs near $3,000), he sees value. Once twice the price of gold, platinum now sits at about **40% of gold’s price**.

> “I think it's a discounted item.”

His personal allocation for physical metal purchases is:
*   **50% Gold**
*   **30% Silver**
*   **20% Platinum**

## Final Word: A Long-Term Mindset

Rule’s overarching message is about intention and discipline. Physical metal is for **long-term holding**, not day-trading. The paper market carries a fundamental risk in his view: if a widespread demand for physical delivery occurred, the system might not hold.

> “You don't buy gold because you want to sell it tomorrow… If you buy paper markets for an investment for a long term… there's not enough to cover.”

In a world of expanding debt and financial uncertainty, Rick Rule’s strategy is clear: own tangible assets you can hold, size your position wisely, and prepare for the next leg up.
