In the calm after recent market storms, a critical long-term pattern for gold is emerging—one that even seasoned analysts have missed. According to Daniel Wahi of *Hot Trades of the Day*, this chart isn’t just important; it’s foundational for anyone bullish on gold and silver. “I’m flat out embarrassed that I didn’t notice it myself until today,” Wahi admits. “But once you see it, you cannot unsee it.”

## Short-Term Calm, Long-Term Clarity

After weeks of volatility, markets are taking a breath—a “small victory” in what Wahi calls “wild times.” While daily gold charts draw questions about another potential sell-off, Wahi shifts focus away from short-term noise. “In panic times,” he notes, “investors tend to forget the long-term developments.” His mission is clear: strip away emotion and focus on the facts.

Two immediate factors weigh on gold: the U.S. dollar and oil. But a third, underreported element has surfaced—one Wahi promises to detail soon. “The Russian central bank is selling gold reserves like there’s no tomorrow,” he reveals. This forced selling in Q1 2024 may explain recent downward pressure on gold, underscoring the economic strain from the Ukraine war. “The Russian economy is in deep, deep trouble,” Wahi says, a dynamic he believes will keep oil prices elevated.

## The Dollar’s Pivotal Role

For now, the dollar demands attention. On the daily chart, the DXY (U.S. Dollar Index) looks “really good,” respecting an uptrend line and testing a key resistance zone within a triangle formation. “It looks absolutely beautiful,” Wahi observes. Zooming out to the weekly chart reveals more: the dollar is bouncing at a long-term uptrend in place since 2008, following a “beautiful bottoming formation” after its 2025 correction.

A breakout above resistance would be bearish for gold in the short to medium term—consistent with their inverse relationship. That’s why Wahi isn’t rushing into new long gold positions or miners right now. “This resistance zone is a really big one,” he cautions, tracing it back to 2014.

## The Chart Nobody’s Talking About

Then comes the revelation. Switching to a monthly chart dating to 1986, Wahi plots the dollar’s long-term trajectory: extended periods of devaluation, followed by a strong uptrend since the financial crisis. But here’s what almost everyone has missed.

By connecting the dollar’s lower lows and lower highs, a clear **ultra-long-term downtrend channel** emerges. The dollar is now approaching the top of that channel—the final leg of its long-term rally. “We are closing in on the tip-top,” Wahi explains.

Crucially, the dollar’s uptrend since 2008 is nearing an end. A break below that trendline—which was briefly violated in late January, just before gold and silver flash crashes—would signal a major shift. “This is the end of the dollar rally,” Wahi states.

## What Happens Next?

Wahi outlines two potential scenarios:

1.  A fake-out breakout above current resistance, possibly toward 104–107 on the DXY, luring investors into the dollar before a rollover.
2.  A direct breakdown of the 2008 trendline.

Either way, the outcome is the same: “Once we break this uptrend, it’s Armageddon time for the dollar and it’s party time for gold and the gold bulls.”

This impending dollar devaluation is a key pillar supporting Wahi’s long-term gold target of **$10,000**, possibly higher if hype accelerates. “This chart is really important for the gold bull,” he emphasizes. “It should be important for anybody.”

## A Final Word for Investors

In a year poised for continued volatility, Wahi urges investors to remember the larger patterns at play. The dollar’ downtrend channel is a tectonic shift in plain sight—one that reaffirms the long-term case for gold.

“Maybe I should diversify a little bit out of the dollar,” he muses for U.S.-based viewers. For gold bulls, the message is clear: the dollar’s last gasp is approaching, and with it, the fuel for gold’s historic run.
