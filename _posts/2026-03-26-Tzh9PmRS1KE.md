**By Daniel Wahi**

The markets are finally calming down—and that gives us a chance to talk about one of the most important long-term chart patterns for gold that, to my embarrassment, I didn’t notice myself until today. This is information you need to know if you’re a gold or silver bull.

In times of panic, when emotions take over, investors tend to forget the long-term developments playing out behind the noise. My goal here is to strip away the emotion and focus on the facts.

Right now, the two most important charts for gold are the U.S. dollar (DXY) and oil. But there’s another factor that has recently come to light—one I’ll explore in depth soon—and it explains a lot about recent selling pressure: **the Russian central bank has been selling gold reserves aggressively in Q1 of this year.** As I’ve said on this channel, the Ukraine war is a race to the bottom, and the Russian economy is in deep trouble. That forced selling is likely contributing to gold’s recent pressure.

But today, let’s focus on the dollar.

---

## The Dollar’s Short-Term Setup Looks Strong

On the daily chart, the DXY has corrected after hitting a clear resistance zone, and it has bounced perfectly at an uptrend line that’s been in place since early this year. We’re now approaching the top of a triangle formation. It’s a clean, technical picture—and it suggests the dollar could break higher in the short to medium term.

Zooming out to the weekly chart reveals something even more compelling: the dollar is bouncing at a **long-term uptrend that dates back to 2008**. After a severe correction in 2025—which helped fuel gold’s rally—the dollar is now forming a beautiful bottoming pattern right at that historic trend support.

If the dollar breaks above its current resistance zone, which extends all the way back to 2014, that would typically be bearish for gold in the near term. That’s why I’m not aggressively bullish on gold in the short to medium term and have been cautious about adding new long positions or jumping into miners.

But here’s where it gets really interesting.

---

## The Chart Nobody Is Talking About

Let’s pull back even further—to the **monthly chart going back to 1986**.

What you see is a series of long-term cycles: extended devaluations, strong uptrends, and prolonged declines. Since the financial crisis, we’ve been in a long-term dollar uptrend. But if you connect the **lower highs and lower lows** on this multi-decade scale, a different story emerges:

**The dollar is inside a massive, ultra-long-term downtrend channel.**

We are now approaching the top of that channel—what could be the final leg of the dollar’s current uptrend. The last time we tested the lower boundary of this long-term uptrend was in late January. Remember what happened then?  
> “Tuesday, January 27th… When did the silver crash happen and the gold flash crash? Two days later.”

The market tried to break the dollar down—and failed. But the bigger picture is clear: **this dollar rally is running out of room.**

---

## What Happens Next?

In the near term, we could see a dollar breakout above resistance—possibly toward **104 or even 107** on the DXY—followed by what I believe will be a “fakeout for the ages.” Then, the real move begins.

Once the dollar breaks below its 2008 uptrend line, it’s what I call **“Armageddon time for the dollar—and party time for gold bulls.”** That breakdown will open the door to a major devaluation phase for the dollar, which will be one of the key drivers pushing gold toward my long-term target of **$10,000+** in this supercycle.

---

### Why This Matters Now

Markets in 2026 are going to remain volatile and emotional. That’s why it’s crucial to remember the long-term patterns unfolding beneath the daily noise. This dollar downtrend channel is one of those patterns—and once you see it, you can’t unsee it.

As I said at the start, I’m almost embarrassed I didn’t spot it earlier. But now that it’s clear, it shapes everything about how I see gold’s path forward.

If you’re in the U.S., this chart might also make you think about diversifying out of dollars. And if you’re a gold bull, it’s a reminder: the short-term pressures are real, but the long-term setup is still overwhelmingly in your favor.

Stay focused on the facts. The charts are telling us a story—we just have to listen.
