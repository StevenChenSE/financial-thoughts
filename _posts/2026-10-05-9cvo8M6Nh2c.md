## Precious Metals: The Shift Toward Physical Trusts

Precious metals have shown distinct divergence in institutional interest, particularly when comparing standard derivative-backed funds against physical allocation trusts and mining equities.

Meanwhile, the **Sprott Physical Gold Trust (PHYS)** has demonstrated a consistent, steady accumulation trend since early 2022, growing from under 100,000 shares to roughly 150,000 shares. Major firms backing PHYS include Royal Bank of Canada (RBC), Morgan Stanley, and Jupiter Asset Management.

### Gold Miners (GDX and GDXJ)
Interestingly, institutional holdings in the **VanEck Gold Miners ETF (GDX)** and **Junior Gold Miners ETF (GDXJ)** tell a different story. Smart money allocations in GDX peaked at 238,000 shares before dropping significantly to around 145,000 shares. A similar offloading trend appeared in junior miners (GDXJ), indicating institutional profit-taking or caution regarding mining equities despite underlying metal performance.

### Silver (SLV vs. PSLV)
The silver complex presents an even clearer divergence:
* **iShares Silver Trust (SLV):** After climbing to a peak of 190,000 institutional shares, allocations retreated down to approximately 144,000 shares.
* **Sprott Physical Silver Trust (PSLV):** Institutional ownership surged from around 60,000 shares in early 2022 to over 171,000 shares—nearly tripling. 

This pronounced rotation into PSLV points toward a growing institutional preference for fully allocated physical silver storage vehicles over synthetic or custodian-based alternatives. Silver mining ETFs like **SIL** and **SILJ** also enjoyed notable accumulation compared to prior years, though allocations have stabilized recently.
