## Dollar strength, geopolitical tensions, and Fed policy shifts pressure precious metals despite structural support

Silver is trading around $58 an ounce, down approximately 3.5% on the day, while gold has dipped close to 3%, testing the $4,000 level. The simultaneous decline points to a macroeconomic driver rather than metal-specific weakness. The US Dollar Index has surged to a six-month high, reinforcing the inverse relationship between the greenback and precious metals that has persisted throughout the year. This dollar strength mirrors late January levels, a period when gold and silver were simultaneously hitting their all-time highs.

### Geopolitical Escalation and Inflation Fears

Two primary forces are propelling the dollar higher, creating headwinds for precious metals. First, renewed conflict in the Middle East has escalated, with fresh strikes between US and Iranian forces and reports of a blockade affecting Iranian vessels transiting the Strait of Hormuz. While geopolitical conflict historically acts as a safe-haven catalyst for gold, the current market dynamic is pricing this tension as an inflationary risk. Higher oil prices resulting from the unrest are feeding directly into inflation expectations, which in turn are boosting the dollar.

Market commentator Peter Schiff has noted that traders may be conditioned to view war as bearish for gold in this context, arguing that short sellers could eventually be caught offside if the historical safe-haven relationship reasserts itself over time. However, the immediate market reaction favors the inflation narrative, pushing metals lower.

### Federal Reserve Rate Hike Expectations

The second major driver is the shifting probability of Federal Reserve policy. Markets are pricing in a meaningfully higher likelihood of a rate hike in the coming months, with several forecasters viewing a hike as more likely than not by the September meeting. Some projections even suggest the possibility of two hikes by year-end. Higher interest rates increase the opportunity cost of holding non-yielding assets like gold and silver, acting as a direct headwind. This chain reaction—oil prices rising, inflation expectations increasing, rate hike odds climbing, and the dollar strengthening—is compressing precious metals prices.

### Critical Data Calendar Ahead

The short-term trajectory for gold and silver will likely be dictated by this week's dense economic data releases. June CPI inflation data provides the first read on whether price pressures are cooling or re-accelerating, followed by the PPI producer inflation gauge. Retail sales data also rounds out the calendar. However, the most significant catalyst is expected to be Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Walsh's testimony before Congress, his first appearance since taking the chair. A hawkish tone or hotter-than-expected inflation prints could extend the pullback, while dovish nuances or cooling data may spark a relief bounce.

### Structural Support and Central Bank Demand

Despite the near-term volatility, the structural bull case for gold remains robust. China's central bank recently posted its largest monthly gold purchase in over two and a half years, marking roughly 20 consecutive months of official sector accumulation. This highlights a divergence between short-term trading dynamics and long-term accumulation trends, with central banks continuing to build reserves even as retail sentiment faces headwinds.

### Critical Minerals Policy Update

A separate development of potential significance for silver investors involves the critical minerals policy process. A presidential proclamation issued in January set an 180-day window for the Secretary of Commerce and the US Trade Representative to deliver an update, which expired recently. Discussions have included the possibility of a price floor and tariffs for critical minerals, a classification that includes silver. A price floor would provide price certainty to mining companies, potentially revitalizing domestic production capacity.

As of the deadline, no official proclamation or executive order regarding a silver-specific price floor or tariff structure has been announced. The administration did announce a $25 million investment with a private refining company to expand critical minerals refining capacity, though this focuses on rare earth elements rather than silver. While the door remains open for policy measures to materialize in the coming weeks or months, this remains a speculative watch item rather than a confirmed catalyst.

### Market Outlook

The current pullback appears to be a dollar-driven corrective move within a larger uptrend. In the immediate term, price action will remain highly reactive to inflation data and Fed commentary. Hotter CPI or hawkish testimony from Chair Walsh could push prices lower, while cooler data could trigger a reversal. Over longer horizons, the structural supports—including persistent central bank buying, supply tightness, and the historical tendency for gold to benefit from conflict once initial inflation selling exhausts itself—suggest a solid foundation. Any future confirmation of a silver price floor or tariff would represent a meaningful structural catalyst, potentially accelerating domestic investment and providing policy-backed support for prices.

## Summary

Gold and silver are experiencing a dollar-driven pullback amid escalating Middle East tensions and heightened expectations for Federal Reserve rate hikes. While short-term price action remains sensitive to upcoming CPI, PPI, and Fed Chair Kevin Walsh's testimony, the structural bull case remains intact, supported by persistent central bank accumulation and potential critical minerals policy developments.
