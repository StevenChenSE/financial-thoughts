In a recent social media analysis, financial commentator Andy of Finding Value dissected the energy sector’s explosive potential, arguing that the Energy Select Sector SPDR Fund (XLE) is poised for a historic bull run—with a price target of at least $180, and possibly as high as $215.

The discussion was sparked by a tweet highlighting that **short interest in the energy sector is at its highest level since 2008**, setting the stage for a potential short squeeze. But Andy emphasizes that the current breakout is not merely a reaction to recent geopolitical tensions.

## The Setup Was Already There

“A lot of people will attribute the move… to the war in Iran. I want to make something super clear. The war in Iran did not create this setup,” Andy states. Instead, he points to a long-term technical pattern: a multi-year consolidation followed by a false breakdown, mirroring the configuration seen before the last major energy bull market.

Zooming out on the XLE chart, he observes: “This is going to go way up probably, you know, up to here. **180 at least $180**… if it were to do the same move… roughly 215 or something like that.”

## The Long-Term Cycle Over Short-Term Noise

Andy stresses that profits are made by identifying long-term cycles, not chasing headlines. “Why was I bullish oil before the war? And was the war the catalyst or was the war… the outcome of what was going to be inevitable anyway? I think it’s the latter.”

He believes the fundamental backdrop is powerful: “I don't think oil is ever ever going to go back to $40, $50, $60 a barrel barring we don't have some ridiculous… depression.” The all-in sustaining cost for oil production, he argues, will be structurally higher going forward.

## A Lesson in Patience and Psychology

A significant portion of the analysis critiques the short-term mindset prevalent on social media. Andy compares it to “mites hockey,” where “everyone is just posting about crap that's going up… and then everyone just skates to where the action is.”

For long-term investors, he argues, precise entry points matter less than recognizing the cycle. Using a historical example, he notes that buying XLE at $16 during a previous trough—and enduring a pullback to $13—would have ultimately yielded a “10-bagger” if held through the bull market peak.

“If you're truly a long-term investor… you buy these undervalued assets… and then you don't worry about it. Don't let the short-term news get to you. Don't let the tweets from Trump, don't let Powell's decisions waver if it's not going to change the cycle.”

## The Macro Picture: Echoes of the 1970s

Andy connects the energy breakout to a broader macroeconomic regime change, drawing parallels to the 1970s. He highlights that the 10-year Treasury yield is testing the critical 5% level. “The other time that occurred when we passed 5% was 1970. Do you know what happened in 1970? A gigantic bull market in precious metals and commodities and energy.”

He envisions a scenario where money simultaneously flows out of overvalued stock markets and the bond market. “It's going to rotate into precious metals and energy and commodities just like 1970s. What makes you think this time is different?”

This thesis is supported by the ratio of gold to the S&P 500, which Andy believes is completing a massive bottoming pattern. “I think that there is a high probability that we go all the way to the top of this trend line… It means that gold will be priced eight, eight and a half times the price of the S&P 500.”

## The Dollar, The Fed, and the Inflationary Endgame

Citing analysis from Goldman Sachs, Andy outlines a perfect storm: an energy shock slows growth, the Fed is constrained from cutting rates due to inflation, and the dollar begins to weaken. “A softer dollar makes every commodity priced in dollars more expensive… Oil, copper, food inputs, all of it gets repriced higher.”

He predicts the Fed’s eventual response: “They're going to lower interest rates… to hold the stock market up, which they may be successful at doing at the expense of the dollar. And we are positioned for the dollar to go down… and precious metals and commodities will rip.”

## Final Thoughts: Positioning for the New Regime

Andy concludes by reiterating that we are at the beginning of a prolonged period where hard assets outperform financial assets. “This will be one of the largest changes in history because people are not ready for this. They're used to this big financial asset bubble that was created by the lowering of interest rates.”

For investors, the message is clear: look beyond the short-term volatility and geopolitical headlines. The charts, the debt backdrop, and the macroeconomic cycles are aligning for a sustained bull market in energy and commodities. The key is to understand the long-term cycle, position accordingly, and have the patience to see it through.
