## Beneath bullish FOMC sentiment lies record-low consumer confidence and a sharp contraction in AI capital expenditures.

While the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) maintains a uniformly optimistic view of the United States economy, underlying structural indicators suggest a far more fragile landscape. From depressed consumer sentiment to emerging disruptions in the labor market and a sharp slowdown in tech investment, the pillars supporting recent growth are beginning to show severe cracks.

---

### The Consumer Disconnect and AI Labor Anxiety

A critical challenge facing policymakers today is reviving consumer momentum in an environment dominated by persistent inflation and career uncertainty. Data from the University of Michigan’s consumer sentiment index remains near historic lows, reflecting widespread pessimism across American households. 

Much of this anxiety is compounded by rapid technological shifts. According to Pew Research surveys across developed nations, a significant majority of respondents report being more concerned that artificial intelligence (AI) will destroy jobs than optimistic that it will create new opportunities. Research by Bloomberg Chief Economist Anna Wong reinforces these concerns: industries with higher exposure to AI are already experiencing higher rates of unemployment. 

Until the private sector successfully demonstrates that AI serves as a net generator of industries and employment rather than merely a replacement for labor, moving the needle on consumer sentiment will remain an uphill battle.

---

### The Second Derivative: AI Capex Contraction

The disconnect between official policy stance and economic reality becomes even clearer when examining capital expenditure (capex). Former Federal Reserve Governor Kevin Warsh has frequently emphasized the importance of the "second derivative"—the rate of change in investment growth rather than static backward-looking levels.

Recent economic analyses highlight the vulnerability of GDP to tech capex:

* **GDP Concentration:** Research indicates that AI-related investments accounted for virtually all nominal GDP growth during recent expansionary quarters.
* **Capex Deceleration:** After running two to three standard deviations above historical norms, real AI investment growth plunged to -34.6% in the second quarter.
* **Growth Reversal:** Because AI investment previously provided almost the entire margin of economic expansion, a contraction in its growth rate directly jeopardizes forward GDP numbers.

Looking ahead, economic performance relies on the momentum of capital deployment. With AI capex shifting from rapid acceleration to contraction, the primary engine driving recent headline growth is rapidly cooling.

---

### Summary

The unanimous bullishness among Fed officials overlooks two key structural headwinds: historically weak consumer sentiment driven by inflation and labor fears, alongside a sharp contraction in the growth rate of AI capital expenditures. Because recent economic expansion has been disproportionately reliant on AI investment, the deceleration in this sector suggests that policymakers will soon have to confront an economic reality far softer than their current projections suggest.
