## A New Macro Regime: 1970s Inflation, Not 2008 Deflation
Tavi Costa, co-founder and CEO of Aurora Capital, joins *Metals and Miners* host Gary Bone to discuss the current market landscape. Costa argues that investors should stop comparing the current economic environment to the 2008 financial crisis and instead look to the 1970s. He believes we are entering an inflationary regime where hard assets, particularly gold, serve as safe havens, while the US dollar underperforms relative to other fiat currencies. This shift suggests that foreign bonds and commodities may offer better protection than US Treasuries, marking a significant departure from the deflationary correlation seen in 2008.

## The AI Bubble, Tech Valuations, and Capital Flows
The conversation begins with the rising competition between US and Chinese AI models. Costa notes that while Chinese models are capturing significant market share, the US tech sector faces a valuation problem with the CAPE ratio approaching 40. However, rather than viewing this solely as a bubble risk, Costa focuses on capital flows. He argues that tech companies are under existential pressure to innovate, forcing massive spending on infrastructure. As tech balance sheets leverage up and cash flows potentially deteriorate, capital will likely rotate into mining, infrastructure, and energy. Costa advises investors to focus on "earners" in these sectors rather than the "spenders" in tech, viewing the potential tech correction as a rotation opportunity similar to the early 2000s rather than a total market collapse.

## Fed Policy, Real Rates, and Dollar Dynamics
Costa highlights that the market has priced in multiple Federal Reserve rate hikes, but cooler inflation prints have shifted expectations. He points out that real rates are at two-year highs and the dollar is overbought, creating a tailwind for precious metals. He suggests that Fed Chair Jerome Powell has limited ability to hike rates further without severely impacting the economy. Costa views the current pullback in metals not as a bear market signal, but as a digestion phase before the next leg up. He cautions against expecting an immediate, straight-line rally like the NASDAQ has seen, predicting a choppy environment where miners establish credibility before a significant move.

## The Fiscal Pie and Supply Constraints
A core theme of the discussion is the "fiscal pie." Costa explains that a growing portion of government spending is allocated to debt service, crowding out pro-growth investments in infrastructure and energy. This dynamic forces policymakers to suppress rates to manage debt costs, fueling inflationary pressures. In the mining sector, supply has been stagnant for 15 years, with exploration budgets slashed since 2011. Despite record central bank buying and high profitability, miners are not reinvesting in exploration. Costa compares this to the energy sector, where ESG constraints limited supply, but notes that mining lacks the abundance of supply found in energy, making the supply-demand imbalance even more pronounced.

## Secular Bull Case for Gold, Silver, and Copper
Costa makes a strong case for a secular bull market in metals. Gold supply is stagnant, while central banks continue to accumulate reserves. He notes that mining companies are mining deposits discovered in the 1980s and 1990s, with ore grades deteriorating. For copper, Costa sees exceptional asymmetry. He compares the current copper market to silver at $50 or gold at $2,000 before their major breakouts, suggesting copper is in a price-discovery phase. With AI and robotics driving massive infrastructure needs, Costa believes copper could double in price over the next two to three years. He also discusses silver, noting that while primary silver producers face higher all-in sustaining costs (AISC) than byproduct miners, they offer valuable diversification and will perform well as silver approaches triple digits.

## Valuation, M&A, and the Future of Mining
Addressing the recent drawdown in miner stocks, Costa asserts that equity values have not breached intrinsic value. He argues that discounted cash flow models often assume backwardation (falling prices), whereas a contango (rising prices) scenario would make intrinsic values much higher. With cash piles growing and reserves declining, Costa predicts a massive wave of M&A consolidation in the sector. He encourages investors to buy during times of stress, noting that he made most of his wealth during periods of market fear. Looking long-term, Costa envisions an "AI buildup phase" where robotics will revolutionize mining operations, reducing labor costs and increasing efficiency, further enhancing the value of high-quality mining assets.

## Conclusion: Conviction and Letting Winners Run
In his closing thoughts, Tavi Costa emphasizes two key investing lessons: leaning on conviction during volatility and letting winners run. He advises against overthinking short-term overbought or oversold conditions, urging investors to understand the long-term fundamentals of their holdings. With a new fund in the making at Aurora Capital, Costa remains bullish on the mining sector, citing the unique convergence of supply constraints, fiscal pressures, and technological demand. He invites viewers to follow his work on Substack and X for deeper insights into macro trends and market opportunities.
