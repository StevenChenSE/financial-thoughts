The global order as we knew it has come to an end, ushering in an era of massive competition for critical minerals. While investors often focus on precious metals like gold and copper, a structural change is occurring in the base metals sector, specifically regarding iron ore. Frank Giustra, CEO of Fury Group, and Steven Dean, Chairman of the Oceanic Iron Ore Company, discuss why high-grade iron ore is becoming a strategic asset and how the Oceanic Iron Ore Company is positioned to capitalize on this shift.

## A Structural Change in Demand

This is not a typical bull cycle in metals. It is a structural change in how the world looks for metals. Steel production may remain flat in total volume, but the percentage of high-grade product required is increasing.

Several factors are driving this demand:
*   **China's Transition:** China is shifting from traditional blast furnace operations to electric arc furnaces and other methodologies that are environmentally more attractive. This requires higher quality, lower-impurity iron ore.
*   **Developing World Growth:** Developing nations, particularly India and parts of Africa, are increasing their steel needs for infrastructure.
*   **Western Reshoring:** The U.S. and Europe are competing to secure domestic supply chains, moving away from reliance on foreign sources.

## The Strategic Importance of Iron

Iron ore is a remarkably stable commodity, with 60 to 70% of global seaborne supply coming from four major companies: BHP, Rio Tinto, Vale, and Fortescue. However, it is critical to every major economic driver:
*   **AI and Hyperscalers:** The AI capex buildout, estimated at $1 trillion by the end of the year, requires massive physical infrastructure.
*   **Defense and Defense:** NATO rearmament and ongoing conflicts in the Middle East and Ukraine are depleting weaponry, creating a constant demand for steel in missiles, planes, tanks, and drones.
*   **Grid Infrastructure:** The U.S. electrical grid, designed in the 1960s and '70s, requires a complete refurbishment estimated at $700 billion to $1.4 trillion, necessitating vast amounts of copper and steel.

## The Case for High-Grade Ore

Steven Dean explains the economic advantages of high-grade iron ore. The premium over the 62% benchmark price is a function of two main factors:
1.  **Iron Content:** Higher grade means more iron per ton.
2.  **Low Impurities:** Lower levels of silica and other deleterious impurities make the furnace more efficient and reduce environmental emissions.

Test results from the Oceanic project's Hopes Advance project, dated January of this year, yielded an iron ore grade of 68% iron with 2% silicon dioxide. This is significantly higher than the 50% grade typically found in the Pilbara region of Western Australia.

## The Oceanic Iron Ore Company: A Unique Asset

Frank Giustra notes that Oceanic Iron Ore is his first and only iron ore investment. The key differentiator for the company is its location. The deposit sits on tidewater, meaning its transportation costs will be far below any other iron ore mine or deposit.

*   **No Rail Required:** Unlike the Simandou project in West Africa, which is spending $20 billion partly to build a 600 km railway to the coast, Oceanic does not face this logistical hurdle.
*   **Multigenerational Deposit:** The deposit is massive, with 1.44 billion tons of measured and indicated resources, and has a very long life.
*   **Low Operating Costs:** The company quotes operating costs of $30 per ton of concentrate.

## Government Support and Permitting

Recent developments in Canada signal a shift in government policy regarding critical minerals. Prime Minister Carney announced a package including up to $10 billion in federal funding to expand clean electricity generation and transmission in Labrador. While this specific package is further south than the Oceanic project, it is seen as the first step in a series of steps to support mineral development in the region.

The company is currently working on:
*   Updating engineering studies to 2026.

*   Finding strategic partners for offtake and investment.

## Conclusion

The world is facing a supply shock and a demand shock for metals, creating a "perfect storm" for the sector. Frank Giustra believes that both monetary debasement and physical underinvestment are driving prices higher. For Oceanic Iron Ore, the next 12 months will be crucial as they bring on new partners and fast-track the engineering and permitting processes. The goal is to move from theoretical value to cash flow, leveraging a unique, high-grade, tidewater asset in a rapidly changing global landscape.
