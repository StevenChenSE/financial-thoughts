## The Insurance Policy That's Outperforming Everything Else

When you look at the world today—geopolitical tensions, energy price shocks, supply chain disruptions, and economic uncertainty—something interesting is happening. Gold, often called the crisis hedge, is sitting at $4,800 and has been grinding its way back up after a March selloff. But here's what's even more fascinating: despite oil prices jumping over 20% in a single day and global instability reaching levels we haven't seen in years, gold hasn't reacted the way it traditionally has.

This tells us something important about where we are in this market cycle.

According to Raj Pralat, former Premier of the Yukon and current CEO of SA Mining, the disconnect between global instability and gold's measured response isn't a sign of weakness—it's a sign that something much bigger is building underneath the surface.

"Gold has always been the insurance policy," Pralat explains. "But it's not just an insurance policy anymore. It's creating wealth at a scale we've never seen before."

## The Macro Case for Gold

### Central Banks Are Buying Like There's No Tomorrow

The numbers are staggering. Global central banks, particularly BRICS+ nations, have been accumulating gold at an unprecedented pace. Current projections suggest approximately 850 tons of gold will be purchased by central banks over the next year alone.

This isn't a short-term trend. This is a fundamental shift in how nations view their reserve assets.

"When you look at what's happening with BRICS and the broader emerging market bloc, the proof is in the buying and the accumulation of gold," Pralat notes. "And it's not something that's just happened in this snapshot in time. We're looking at this consistently—they're buying, and the projections for the next year of buying are immense."

### The Geopolitical Disruption Factor

The current global situation has created strange dynamics:

- **Oil prices have surged above $90 and even touched $100** due to regional conflicts and supply disruptions
- **The US dollar is being pulled away from** by multiple nations seeking alternatives
- **Inflationary pressures are building** from energy costs

Yet gold's reaction has been muted compared to historical patterns. Pralat attributes this partly to "intervention by the US" and constant market manipulation, but he believes the long-term trend remains firmly bullish.

"When you look globally, people are pulling away from that US dollar, and when we look at BRICS—the proof is in the buying and the accumulation of gold."

### What's Not Priced In?

Here's the opportunity: **The junior mining sector hasn't fully priced in $4,800 gold.**

Many junior gold companies are still trading as if gold is $3,000 or lower. The delta between what the market is pricing and what gold is actually trading at represents significant upside potential for investors who can identify the right projects.

"For our company, we're really conservative on our projections. When you look at what you're getting with SA, the value per ounce—and really, there's double, triple folks inside our comparables that are there," Pralat says. "I think the juniors you're going to continue to see people look to capture value, and there's a lot of value to capture across the sector."

## The North American Mining Renaissance

### "We're Not Just Going to Purchase From Somewhere Else"

Perhaps the most significant shift Pralat sees is the awakening of both Canada and the US to a fundamental truth: **there's a huge opportunity in our own backyard.**

"We've seen countries waking up, both Canada and the US, and saying we're not just going to purchase from somewhere else," Pralat explains. "We're going to find it here on sovereign soil. We're also going to build out an industrial base that can process that."

This isn't just rhetoric. Federal policy in both countries is actively shifting toward supporting domestic mining, including:

- **Critical minerals development** for batteries, technology, and defense
- **Rare earth processing infrastructure** being built out in the southern United States
- **Indigenous partnership frameworks** that create sustainable community relationships

"The clock's ticking, and what I'm watching in the US from some meetings I've had with some of our companies is that there's a real sense of urgency there," Pralat notes. "The people working inside the US federal government are reacting with an incredible sense of urgency."

### Why This Matters for Investors

When Pralat served as Economic Development Minister for a decade, he learned something crucial: **economic diversification isn't easy.**

"It would be easy for a politician to say we're just going to diversify our economy. It's not an easy thing to do," he admits. "But there are certain sectors where you've done well in your country—and mining is one of them."

Canada's advantages in the mining sector include:

- **Deep institutional knowledge** spanning generations
- **World-class technical expertise** across exploration and development
- **A strong ecosystem of junior mining companies** ready to discover and develop assets
- **Favorable regulatory frameworks** increasingly supporting domestic production

## From Politics to Mining: A Personal Journey

### Three Generations in the Making

Pralat's transition from Yukon Premier to mining CEO wasn't a career pivot—it was a homecoming.

"This is third generation in our family that's been around mining," Pralat reveals. "My grandfather was a hard rock miner in Ontario and Quebec. I have a lot of family members, uncles that worked for some of the bigger mining companies in Canada."

His first foray into the sector came through a bootstrapped company that was eventually acquired in 2016. That company was located in the Yukon and had a project near a mine called Kamak, which was purchased by Goldcorp in the same year.

"You get excitement around it. You go out, you're searching, you find something that's exciting, you continue to move it along—and it doesn't always happen that you get a suitor and a good exit. But that was the experience, and it's something that gets in your blood."

### Why Mining Over Defense or Other Sectors?

After leaving politics, Pralat considered multiple sectors, including defense and Arctic security—areas where he had deep expertise. But he kept coming back to mining.

"I think it's a strong sector to be in. The people that work in the sector are people that are just great Canadians trying to build their country," he explains. "I also thought there was a long future in it. I think there's a lot of opportunity in the sector."

More importantly, Pralat was drawn to the people and approach at **Fiori**, the Vancouver-based mining group behind SA Mining.

### What Is the DSO Model?

**DSO stands for Direct Shipping Ore**—and it's a simplified approach to creating value.

Instead of building expensive processing infrastructure, SA Mining plans to ship ore from its current resources to other mills in the region. This dramatically reduces capital requirements and time to production.

The company currently has approximately **1.25 million ounces** in measured and inferred resources, and that number is still growing.

"When I think about all the juniors in the space, why do you look at this company to invest?" Pralat asks. "We've got this resource. If you look at measured and inferred, about 1.25 million ounces, still growing that out. Other juniors have bigger resources out there, but what we're looking to do is a DSO model—we want to ship that ore that we have to another mill, and we have some great options 80 kilometers away."
