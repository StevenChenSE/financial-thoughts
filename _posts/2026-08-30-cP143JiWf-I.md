## The Bond Market's Defiance and the TGA Dilemma

In a significant development for global markets, the US Treasury has signaled a potential move to tap its nearly $1 trillion Treasury General Account (TGA) to fund bond buybacks. This follows a previous announcement by Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent to increase buybacks of long-dated bonds by $2 billion to $4 billion. Despite these interventions, the bond market has shown little reaction, with the 10-year yield remaining above 4.7% and the 30-year yield exceeding 5.2%.

Gareth Soloway, a financial analyst and guest on the *Capital Cosm* podcast, argues that the government is "dipping its toe in the water" to gauge market tolerance. The primary goal is to drive yields down to make the US debt, which has now reached $40 trillion, more manageable. However, the lack of a significant yield drop suggests that the market is no longer responding to traditional Federal Reserve and Treasury interventions.

## The Loss of Monetary Control

Soloway highlights a critical shift in the financial landscape: the US government and the Federal Reserve may have lost the ability to artificially suppress interest rates through quantitative easing (QE) and bond interventions. For decades, these tools allowed the government to push rates down to stimulate growth. Now, with the "drugs" no longer working, the market is taking control.

This loss of control implies that interest rates will likely remain "higher for longer," potentially triggering a recession or a more severe economic correction. The government is caught in a difficult position: they need to lower yields to manage debt costs, but they also need a strong economy to win the upcoming midterm elections. This tension is driving the aggressive use of the TGA as a tool to influence the long end of the yield curve.

## Implications for Gold, Silver, and the Dollar

The breakdown in the fiat currency system is having a profound impact on precious metals. Gold has broken out of a long-term descending trend line, signaling a bullish shift. Soloway notes that while rising yields traditionally hurt gold, the current environment—characterized by unsustainable debt and a weakening dollar—makes gold a safe haven.

*   **Gold:** Expected to see short-term pullbacks, which Soloway views as buying opportunities. The long-term trend is strongly bullish due to the breakdown of the US dollar's credibility.
*   **Silver:** While silver has also rallied, it is lagging behind gold. Soloway expects silver to top out around $71.50 before pulling back to the $56 level, where he anticipates a buying opportunity.
*   **Dollar Index (DXY):** The DXY has broken below key support levels, suggesting a weaker dollar ahead. Soloway predicts the DXY could fall to 97 within a month and potentially 95 later this year. A weaker dollar typically benefits stocks and metals but can also import inflation.

## Stock Market and Crypto Outlook

The S&P 500 has remained resilient, holding near all-time highs despite the bond market turmoil. Soloway attributes this to strong corporate earnings and a lingering belief in a "V-shaped" recovery. However, he warns that the market is in a "limbo" state, with the potential for increased volatility if the dollar continues to weaken or if a recession materializes.

In the crypto space, Bitcoin has seen a significant rally following the bond buyback news, breaking out of a consolidation pattern. However, Soloway believes Bitcoin has topped out in the short term and may pull back to around $67,000. He notes a divergence between Bitcoin and the NASDAQ, suggesting that Bitcoin may be transitioning from a risk-on asset to a "digital gold" that reacts more to monetary policy and dollar weakness than to tech stock performance.

## The Endgame: A Sinking Ship?

Soloway concludes that the US government is on a "sinking ship" trajectory due to its inability to manage debt and inflation without extreme market intervention. While there are still levers to pull, such as Fed rate cuts or further TGA manipulation, the long-term outlook is grim. He advises investors to prepare for a potential reckoning by holding assets that offset currency collapse, such as gold and Bitcoin.

The key takeaway is that the era of easy money and artificially low rates may be over. Investors should remain cautious, watch for signs of a recession, and position themselves for a world where the US dollar's dominance is challenged by the sheer scale of US debt.
