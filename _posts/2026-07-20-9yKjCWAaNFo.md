## US Gold Reserves and the Shift in Global Monetary Power

The global conversation around gold is intensifying, with US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent recently placing America's gold reserves back in the spotlight. In an interview with Jesse Waters on Fox News, Bessent confirmed that the nation's gold stockpile at Fort Knox is "present and accounted for," emphasizing that the US holds the world's largest official gold reserves, valued at over a trillion dollars at current market prices. This statement comes amid growing geopolitical fragmentation, record central bank purchases, and renewed calls from lawmakers for a comprehensive audit of US gold holdings.

## Why Washington Is Talking Gold Now

The frequency of gold-related comments from the current administration is notable. President Trump has repeatedly raised the idea of physically inspecting or auditing Fort Knox, a notion previously floated by Elon Musk. Treasury Secretary Bessent, who has self-identified as a "gold bug," has pushed back against suggestions that US gold is missing. He highlighted the historical transition from silver and gold-backed currency to the fiat system adopted in the 1970s, noting that any outstanding old certificates still have their corresponding metal waiting at Fort Knox if needed.

Rich Checkan, President and COO of Asset Strategies International, suggests that this public emphasis on gold is an attempt to instill confidence in the US dollar. "The world is talking about gold," Checkan notes. "In fact, we're late to the conversation." With central banks globally buying gold at record levels for over four years, gold has surpassed both the euro and the dollar as the top tier-one reserve asset. Checkan argues that Washington is trying to reassure the world that despite the loss of faith in the dollar, the US still holds the "biggest pile of gold," which serves as a psychological backstop for the currency.

## The Audit Debate and Rehypothecation Concerns

Despite Bessent's assurances, skepticism remains regarding the actual state of Fort Knox. The last comprehensive physical audit of the facility occurred in 1953, with only partial inspections and vault seal verifications conducted since. A September 2024 Treasury report claimed all 147.3 million ounces were accounted for, but this was based on internal paper records rather than a modern, independent bar-by-bar inspection.

Congress is pushing for transparency with the Gold Reserve Transparency Act of 2025, introduced in both the House and Senate. These bills call for a complete inventory, physical assay, and independent audit of all US gold reserves, including a public accounting of transactions over the last 50 years. Checkan believes a credible audit would be a massive undertaking, taking weeks or months to count and verify every bar.

A key concern raised by Checkan is not necessarily whether the gold is missing, but how much of it has been "rehypothecated"—pledged or leased to other parties. Using the jewelry industry as an analogy, he explains that entities often borrow gold temporarily to meet production needs, paying a fee and returning the metal or its value later. At a national level, this could mean US gold is pledged to cover debts or geopolitical payments. Checkan admits he has no idea how much of the 8,133 metric tons are currently pledged, stating, "You don't have to take the gold out of the vault to pledge it to someone else."

## China's Gold Ambitions and the Dollar's Future

Speculation is also growing regarding China's gold reserves. While officially reported at around 2,300 metric tons, many analysts believe China holds significantly more, potentially between 5,500 and 20,000 tons. Checkan asserts that China is strategically building its gold holdings to support the credibility of its currency and economy, aiming to eventually surpass the US's 8,133 metric tons.

"If China did announce that it has more gold than the US, what do you think happens to the dollar?" Checkan asks. He believes that while China is working toward a seat at the global reserve currency table, it faces significant hurdles before it can supplant the US. However, the weaponization of the dollar and fiscal irresponsibility are driving central banks to de-dollarize and embrace gold as a neutral reserve asset.

Checkan also addressed Bessent's comment about Venezuela, clarifying that recent gold shipments from Venezuela to US refineries are part of a trade agreement to restore order to the resource market, not confiscation. This move benefits both nations, ensuring Venezuelan gold flows to the US rather than to geopolitical adversaries.

## Gold Price Targets: $7,000 to $9,000

Looking ahead, Checkan is highly bullish on gold, predicting it is "ready to blow." He argues that the current debt levels and money supply expansion have already baked in a much higher price for gold. Using the Dow-to-gold ratio, he suggests gold could reach between $7,000 and $9,000 per ounce in this run.

Although gold recently experienced a 25% pullback from its highs, Checkan views this as a healthy correction in a long-term bull market. He emphasizes that gold's upward trajectory is tied to government spending. "When you see a 25% drop back in gold's price with no sign of a fiscally responsible politician on the horizon, that tells me that gold is cheap."

Checkan dismisses the notion that rising interest rates will derail gold's rally, noting that the US cannot afford to raise rates significantly without crippling its $40 trillion debt load. He concludes that until politicians learn to balance the budget, gold must go higher as a hedge against inflation and currency debasement.

"Be your own central bank," Checkan advises. "The central banks... have been acting on it for four years. They've been buying gold." He urges investors to take advantage of current dips, viewing gold as essential wealth insurance in an era of fiscal uncertainty.
