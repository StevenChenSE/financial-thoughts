While headlines focus on AI stocks and cryptocurrency, a significant shift is quietly underway in the commodity markets. Multiple commodity charts have been breaking out for months, yet mainstream financial media remains largely silent. The Thomson Reuters CRB Index—a broad measure tracking energy, metals, gold, silver, and other raw materials—has broken free from a lengthy consolidation period that began in 2022. Even more compelling, when you adjust for the massive expansion of money supply since 2008, this breakout represents what may be one of the most asymmetric opportunities in markets today.

This article examines the technical breakout in commodities, the structural supply constraints driving this move, and specific commodity plays that deserve investor attention before the rest of the market catches on.

---

## The CRB Breakout: Reading the Charts

### Technical Confirmation on Multiple Timeframes

The CRB Index has achieved a decisive weekly candlestick breakout after an extended consolidation period. Each weekly candlestick represents a full trading week, and the sustained nature of this breakout—holding for multiple consecutive weeks—signals genuine momentum rather than a false break.

However, the real story emerges when examining the broader historical context. Zooming out reveals that current prices remain remarkably close to the 2008 highs. A casual observer might conclude that commodities are simply returning to previous peak levels and therefore overvalued. But this comparison ignores a critical variable: the extraordinary expansion of the monetary base that has occurred since then.

### Adjusting for Money Supply: The Real Picture

The M2 money supply provides essential context for commodity valuation. In 2008, M2 stood at $8.4 trillion. Today, that figure has ballooned to nearly $23 trillion—a tripling of the monetary base. When commodities are priced relative to this expanded money supply, the chart tells a dramatically different story.

Rather than sitting near historic highs, commodities priced against M2 show that we have barely gotten off the mat. This analysis reveals a clean, crisp monthly candlestick breakout with clear resistance and support levels. The current level sits roughly equivalent to July 2015 valuations. If commodities were simply to retrace to their 1980s levels relative to the monetary base, the implied move represents approximately 500% from current levels.

### Commodities Versus Equities: The Ratio Perspective

Another illuminating comparison examines the CRB Index relative to the S&P 500. This ratio reveals how commodities have performed against equities over time. The monthly chart shows another breakout in commodity outperformance relative to stocks.

From April 2020 through July 2022, commodities outperformed equities significantly. Since then, the S&P has reclaimed ground as commodities consolidated. However, the technical structure has shifted decisively. The S&P-to-commodities ratio has formed a falling wedge—a classic continuation pattern that typically resolves to the upside for commodities. Historically, returning to even a halfway retracement of the 1980s levels would imply a 10-12x move in commodities relative to the S&P 500.

This ratio analysis suggests three potential paths to reversion: the S&P could crash dramatically while commodities hold steady, commodities could surge while equities remain flat, or some combination of both scenarios. None of these outcomes would require returning to extreme historical highs.

## The Supply Crisis Nobody Is Planning For

### New Mine Production Has Collapsed

Technical analysis alone cannot explain why commodities may be poised for sustained outperformance. The fundamental story is equally compelling: new mine production across nearly all major commodities has effectively collapsed.

Consider the data compiled from multiple sources examining commodity-specific mine development:

- **Copper**: New mines coming online down 90% from peak levels
- **Iron ore**: New mine production down approximately 90%
- **Nickel**: New mine development down 80% from highs
- **Gold**: New mine production down 60% from highs

Yet despite this collapse in new supply, overall commodity production continues climbing. This creates an increasingly untenable situation: existing mines deplete over time, but replacement capacity simply does not exist to maintain current output levels, let alone meet growing demand.

### Why This Matters for Pricing

When new mine supply dries up but demand continues growing—whether from infrastructure development, industrial expansion, or the energy transition—the eventual supply shortfall becomes inevitable. Current prices do not reflect this coming constraint because markets focus on near-term supply from existing operations.

The discrepancy is particularly stark for iron ore. Global iron ore production has remained essentially flat for five to six years, yet no new meaningful capacity is under development. The industry has effectively stopped investing in future supply.

## Individual Commodity Analysis

### Iron Ore: The Forgotten Trade

Iron ore represents perhaps the most overlooked commodity opportunity available today. While attention focuses on gold, silver, and industrial metals like copper, iron ore operates largely under the radar. The Singapore Exchange iron ore futures contract shows a consolidation pattern similar to the broader CRB Index but more acute—a classic triangle formation that has now broken to the upside.

After breaking out, iron ore successfully retested the former resistance level as support and has resumed its upward trajectory. Comparing current prices to the January lows shows meaningful separation, though the July 2021 highs remain in sight. For a commodity that generates virtually no mainstream discussion, the technical setup is remarkably clean.

The fundamental backdrop reinforces the technical picture. Steel demand—both conventional and for emerging green steel initiatives—continues growing globally. The lack of new mine development suggests prices must eventually rise to incentivize future supply that the market currently refuses to fund.

### Copper: Dr. Copper Already Moving

Copper has earned the nickname "Dr. Copper" for its ability to diagnose economic health based on price movements. Industrial demand for copper spans electrical infrastructure, renewable energy systems, EVs, and construction. The metal appears to have already begun its move, trading near its second-highest closing price in history as of recent data.

What makes copper's move particularly significant is the absence of new supply to meet this demand. The 90% collapse in new mine development means that even if prices spike, additional supply cannot quickly materialize. Copper may be signaling what other commodities will follow.

### Nickel: Leading the Pack

Nickel has already broken decisively above its January 2026 highs—a remarkable development given that most commodities remain below their recent peaks. This early breakout positions nickel as a leading indicator for the broader commodity complex.

The electric vehicle battery demand has driven much of nickel's recent interest, but traditional industrial uses remain significant. Like copper, nickel's structural supply constraints create the conditions for continued price appreciation.

### Gold: The Canary in the Coal Mine

Gold operates differently from industrial metals—it serves as both a commodity and a monetary alternative asset. The daily chart shows gold resting on resistance, consolidating after the significant correction seen in late January. Current prices hover around $2,650 per troy ounce, with the market awaiting confirmation of the next major move.

However, gold's historical role as a leading indicator deserves attention. When commodities broadly begin a sustained move higher, gold typically participates. The current consolidation may represent the calm before significant upside. Geopolitical tensions—particularly developments in the Strait of Hormuz and broader Middle East instability—provide potential catalysts for renewed gold strength.

## Key Takeaways

1. **The CRB Index has broken out technically** after consolidating since 2022, but the true upside potential becomes clear only when adjusting for M2 money supply expansion—suggesting commodities remain dramatically undervalued relative to the monetary base.

2. **New mine production has collapsed across the commodity complex**—down 80-90% for copper, iron ore, and nickel—creating structural supply deficits that will eventually manifest in significantly higher prices.

3. **Individual commodities are showing leadership**: Nickel has already broken to new highs, copper is near all-time highs, and iron ore has broken free from its consolidation pattern despite receiving virtually no mainstream attention.

4. **The commodities-to-S&P ratio suggests massive potential**: Even a partial reversion to historical norms would imply 10-12x upside for commodities relative to equities, with full reversion representing 25x.

5. **Investors should consider allocations to lesser-covered commodities** like iron ore alongside traditional positions in gold and silver. The opportunity lies precisely where attention and interest remain minimal.
