### An analysis of energy markets, refining capacity, and geopolitical risks as global inventories tighten ahead of peak demand.

Crude oil and refined fuel markets are sending conflicting signals. While West Texas Intermediate (WTI) crude has retreated from $100 per barrel to trade around $90, refined products like diesel remain at historic highs despite recent dips. With the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) hitting its lowest levels since 1982 and the conflict in the Persian Gulf disrupting shipping routes, energy markets face an increasingly precarious supply-demand balance. 

Josh Young, Founder and Chief Investment Officer of Bison Interests, shared his detailed market outlook and addressed why current pricing may precede a significant surge heading into the winter.

## Fact vs. Fiction in the Strait of Hormuz

Recent mainstream media reports suggested tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz was returning to near-normal levels—some estimates citing 95% to 98% of pre-war volume. However, Young challenges these claims, noting that periodic spikes in traffic reflect coordinated military convoys rather than sustainable commercial flows.

The US Navy has periodically escorted clusters of tankers through the strait to conduct ship-to-ship transfers. While these operations produce temporary surges in reported flow, they mask the broader risks. Following official narratives of restored security, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) attacked several tankers, resulting in disabled vessels and casualties.

According to open-source vessel tracking and industry data, overall crude flows through the strait remain substantially depressed. High shipping premiums—reaching roughly $30 per barrel in transit fees alongside heavy US insurance backstops—continue to incentivize select high-risk runs, but genuine commercial normalization remains distant.

## Refined Products vs. Crude: The Crack Spread Dynamics

The core economic pressure on consumers has centered on distillate fuels such as diesel, gasoline, and jet fuel rather than crude oil alone. While $90 WTI crude is manageable for global growth, refined products trading at the equivalent of $200 to $250 per barrel have begun to risk demand destruction.

```
   Crude Oil ($90/bbl) ──► [ Refinery Operations ] ──► Refined Products ($200-$250/bbl eq.)
                                (95%+ Capacity)              (Historically Tight Inventory)
```

Several compounding factors have driven this extreme dislocation:
- **Refinery Disruptions:** Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refining facilities turned Russia into a net importer of refined products.
- **Export Reductions:** Chinese curbs and temporary bans on refined product exports constrained global distillate supply.
- **Capacity Bottlenecks:** Western refineries have struggled under structural capacity limits and unplanned outages.

In response to historic refining margins and regulatory scrutiny from the Department of Justice regarding maintenance scheduling, US and Canadian refiners have deferred seasonal maintenance. Refineries are operating at approximately 95% utilization—far above the typical 75% to 80% autumn seasonal baseline—in an effort to capture margins and rebuild depleted product inventories.

With the US SPR at roughly 284 million barrels and weekly emergency releases tapering significantly from earlier highs of 7 to 9 million barrels per week down to under 500,000 barrels, the buffer against further supply shocks is thin. 

As refineries pull heavily on domestic crude stocks, the 3:2:1 crack spread is poised to normalize from historic highs through a combination of softening product prices and substantially higher crude prices.

## Winter Projections and Market Catalysts

Speculative positioning in crude oil futures currently sits at multi-year lows relative to current price levels. Historically, extreme bearish sentiment combined with tight physical fundamentals sets the stage for an upward price breakout.

Key variables that will dictate the direction of the market into the winter include:
1. **Refinery Targeting in Eastern Europe:** If European diplomatic pressure leads Ukraine to shift targeting away from Russian refineries and toward crude export infrastructure, global distillate tightness could ease while crude supply tightens further.
2. **Red Sea and Chokepoint Security:** Continued volatility around the Bab el-Mandeb strait and Persian Gulf corridors will maintain elevated transit costs and insurance friction.
3. **Winter Distillate Draw:** The seasonal shift toward heating oil and winter blends will test the limits of refinery run-rates that have already deferred essential maintenance.

If structural supply constraints persist in the Middle East alongside sustained high refinery throughput, the drawdown on crude reserves could drive WTI crude well beyond current levels as winter demand peaks.
