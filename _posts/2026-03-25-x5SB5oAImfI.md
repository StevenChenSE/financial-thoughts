A shadowy financial mechanism first used by Russia to bypass Western sanctions is now being deployed by Iran, according to new analysis from Eric “King Kong” Young. The scheme—which converts oil sales into yuan and then into physical gold—creates a closed-loop system that allows Iran to continue trading under sanctions while China secures discounted energy.

This method isn’t new. In 2017, it was revealed that Russia was selling oil to China for yuan, with the option to convert that yuan into gold. By 2023, the pattern was clearer: Russia exercised that conversion option and stored the gold domestically on the Shanghai Gold Exchange. In 2025, analysts connected the dots further, noting that BRICS nations were assembling a network of vaults where gold could be stored to back currencies, deepening yuan integration into global trade.

Now, Eric Young has identified Iran using the same blueprint.

## The Mechanism: Oil → Yuan → Gold

In an exclusive interview, Young explained that Iran sells oil to China, settling the transactions in yuan—completely bypassing the U.S. dollar. The proceeds are held within China’s banking system, primarily through the **Bank of Kun**, which is located in a Muslim-majority border region conducive to trade with Iran.

The critical step comes next: Iran converts its yuan surplus into **physical gold** via the domestic Shanghai Gold Exchange. This creates what Young describes as a “closed-loop system of oil, yuan, and gold.”

> “This structure allows Iran to sustain trade under sanctions while China secures discounted energy,” Young noted, “establishing a scalable blueprint for broader yuan-gold settlement across global trade flows.”

In effect, Iran acts as what the analyst called a “bad cop” for China, testing and proving mechanisms to erode dollar hegemony. “Think about it,” he said. “Iran is being the bad cop for China’s yuan wishes.”

## Broader Implications: Another Brick in the Wall

The Iran case is seen as part of a longer-term shift away from dollar dominance. “It drives home just another brick in the wall of what we’ve been saying about U.S. dollar hegemony being taken out one brick at a time,” the host commented. “It’s a multi-polar world, and it’s happening now.”

While acknowledging the geopolitical discomfort of Iran’s role, he framed it as realpolitik: “Unfortunately, you need a bad cop like Iran to try and do that.”
