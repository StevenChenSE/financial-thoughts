## Danielle DiMartino Booth on Credit Market Distress, Strained Consumers, and Macro Fragility

The macroeconomic landscape is showing mounting signs of structural stress beneath the surface of resilient headline indicators. In an in-depth discussion, Danielle DiMartino Booth, CEO of QI Research, outlined the critical vulnerabilities facing the United States economy. From surging small business bankruptcies and widening credit spreads to deteriorating real consumer power, DiMartino Booth argues that recent Federal Reserve tightening represents a distinct policy mistake that misinterprets supply shocks for organic demand-driven inflation.

---

### Credit Markets Signal Deepening Distress

While equity indices hover near record levels, the corporate debt landscape tells a starkly different story. Danielle DiMartino Booth highlighted that credit spreads on lowest-rated high-yield debt—specifically Triple-C (CCC) bond spreads—have gapped out to more than 500 basis points over the federal funds rate, a trend steadily worsening since April.

This widening spread points to severe stress building across the financial system:
* **Small Business Failures:** Small business bankruptcies have surged by 64% year-over-year.
* **Aggressive Debt Issuance:** High-profile offerings, such as SoftBank’s junk debt yielding upwards of 10% to support artificial intelligence holdings, demonstrate that investors are demanding significantly higher compensation for credit risk rather than seeking traditional safety.
* **Distressed Exchanges:** According to Moody's and recent financial analyses, companies are increasingly relying on distressed debt exchanges rather than formal Chapter 11 restructurings, effectively delaying inevitable defaults rather than resolving core balance-sheet issues.

### The Reality of the K-Shaped Consumer

Headline figures often characterize domestic consumption as robust, but DiMartino Booth emphasizes that these aggregate numbers are heavily skewed by top earners—the upper tier of the "K-shaped" economy. 

Across middle- and lower-income households, real purchasing power is deteriorating:
* **Nominal vs. Real Sales:** While nominal retail sales show modest gains, sales volumes on an inflation-adjusted basis are falling.
* **Depleted Savings:** The aggregate personal savings rate has dropped to 3%. Concurrently, hardship withdrawals from 401(k) accounts have escalated.
* **Desperate Liquidity Moves:** Roughly 57% of homeowners who tapped equity through cash-out refinancing in the second quarter did so by accepting a higher mortgage rate, reflecting an urgent need for liquidity to cover everyday essentials.
* **Down-Trading in Retail:** Major discount retailers like Dollar General report that even higher-income consumers trading down are purchasing fewer items, while grocers like Kroger are rejecting price increases from major suppliers due to customer pushback.

### Supply Shocks vs. Monetary Policy Errors

A central issue raised by DiMartino Booth is the Federal Reserve's reaction to energy volatility. With diesel prices and transportation costs driving input expenses higher, companies are experiencing acute margin compression because they are unable to pass these expenses onto a tapped-out consumer.

Treating an energy supply shock—compounded by geopolitical conflicts in the Middle East and the Strait of Hormuz—as demand-driven overheating is a miscalculation. Core PCE inflation has steadily decelerated toward 3.3%, and key travel and hospitality sectors are actively cutting capacity due to slackening demand. Hiking interest rates in this environment does not resolve supply bottlenecks; instead, it accelerates corporate cost-cutting, layoffs, and demand destruction.

### Decelerating AI Capex and Labor Market Nuance

Although artificial intelligence has driven substantial market enthusiasm and nominal GDP contributions, real capital expenditure momentum is slowing. Deflated investment growth in AI contracted sharply in the second quarter, falling 34.6% as high borrowing costs forced Chief Financial Officers to evaluate capital deployment more critically.

Simultaneously, the labor market exhibits concealed weaknesses:
* **Low Claims vs. Gig Transition:** While headline initial jobless claims appear low, data reveals that only one in four unemployed individuals is actively collecting benefits, with many turning to gig platforms like Uber to stay afloat.
* **Declining Participation:** The overall labor force has contracted since late 2025, which mathematically holds down the headline unemployment rate despite slowing net hiring and subdued seasonal retail recruitment forecasts from firms like Challenger, Gray & Christmas.

### Housing Affordability and the Wealth Effect

Higher Treasury yields have pushed 30-year fixed mortgage rates back near the 7% threshold, putting homebuilders into distress and paralyzing existing transaction volume. While falling home prices could eventually restore affordability for younger buyers, DiMartino Booth notes that it presents a significant risk to high-net-worth consumption. If affluent households witness declines in residential and second-home equity alongside tightening credit conditions, the reverse wealth effect could further curb domestic spending.

---

### Summary and Macro Outlook

The Federal Reserve’s decision to tighten policy into a severe supply-side disruption risks exacerbating corporate default cycles, compressing corporate margins, and overburdening the real consumer. As bankruptcies accelerate and underlying economic metrics deteriorate, monetary policymakers will likely find themselves forced to reverse course and ease policy aggressively to stabilize a rapidly decelerating economy.
