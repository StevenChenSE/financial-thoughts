In a recent episode of *Capital Kosm* on October 13, 2026, host Danny welcomed financial analyst Peter Schiff for a deep dive into the shifting geopolitical landscape, the implications of US-Russia energy deals, and the looming threat of a sovereign debt crisis. Schiff offered a stark warning about the trajectory of the US economy, predicting that political maneuvering and unchecked spending will lead to significant financial instability.

## The Contradictory US-Russia Diesel Deal

The conversation opened with Schiff’s analysis of a recently announced diesel deal between the United States and Russia. Danny noted that while President Trump had previously signed bipartisan legislation authorizing up to 100% tariffs on countries buying Russian oil or gas, the administration had suddenly pivoted to purchasing large quantities of Russian diesel.

Schiff characterized this reversal as a politically motivated decision ahead of the midterm elections. He pointed out that truck driving is the most common occupation for men in America, and high diesel prices are a major pain point for voters. By framing the deal as a way to lower fuel costs for farmers and truck drivers, the administration aims to secure electoral support.

However, Schiff highlighted a critical contradiction:
*   **Sanctions vs. Purchases:** The US previously sanctioned third-party countries for buying Russian energy to prevent funding the war in Ukraine.
*   **Implicit Subsidies:** Much of the diesel the US is purchasing actually originates from Iran and is transacted through Russia. Schiff argued that this deal effectively subsidizes Iran, complicating the geopolitical strategy against both nations.

## The "Imaginary" Ukraine-Russia Energy Truce

Schiff also addressed President Trump’s claim that a truce had been reached between Russia and Ukraine regarding attacks on energy infrastructure. Trump announced the deal on social media, causing oil prices to drop initially. However, both Russia and Ukraine denied any such agreement existed.

When questioned by reporters, Trump insisted the deal was real simply because he said it was. Schiff views this as a reflection of the broader Trump presidency, where accomplishments are often defined by rhetoric rather than reality. He argued that this approach sheds light on how the administration handles economic claims, such as the assertion that the US has the "greatest economy in the world," a claim Schiff disputes.

## Midterm Elections and the Threat of Lame-Duck Presidency

With Polymarket odds showing Democrats at a 64% chance of taking the Senate and 91% for the House, Schiff predicts a significant shift in legislative power. He does not foresee gridlock but rather a scenario where a Democratic Congress passes legislation that President Trump signs, potentially leading to even larger deficits than under Republican control.

Schiff specifically mentioned the potential for the Democrats to pass the $5,000 stimulus checks that Trump had promised. While Trump linked the checks to a Republican victory, Schiff suggests Democrats could propose them independently, excluding the wealthy to appeal to their base. This would create a political dilemma for Trump, who would struggle to oppose his own proposal just because the opposition is implementing it.

Schiff warned that such "profligate spending" could trigger a currency crisis or a sovereign debt crisis. He also noted that Trump is already preemptively blaming a potential Democratic win for a possible 1929-style depression, setting the stage for future political excuses if economic downturns occur.

## Rising Bond Yields and the Bull Case for Gold

A significant portion of the discussion focused on the relationship between bond yields and precious metals. Schiff challenged the common market misconception that rising bond yields are bearish for gold. He argued that yields rise because:
1.  **Inflation Risk:** Inflation increases the risk of holding bonds.
2.  **Fiscal Concerns:** Lenders are losing confidence in the US fiscal position, fearing default or restructuring.
3.  **Supply and Demand:** There is not enough savings to fund the government's debt without higher interest rates to entice investors away from other assets like stocks and real estate.

Schiff contends that these factors are inherently bullish for gold. As bond prices fall and yields rise, investors lose confidence in treasuries. The natural substitute for treasuries, according to Schiff, is gold. He noted that central banks are already selling treasuries and buying gold, reinforcing this trend.

Furthermore, Schiff explained that higher interest rates increase budget deficits by raising the cost of servicing national debt. This creates a feedback loop that weakens the economy, potentially forcing the Federal Reserve to cut rates and engage in quantitative easing (QE) in the future. Markets, he argues, discount these future events into present prices, making current gold prices (around $4,160/oz) and silver prices (around $59/oz) attractive entry points before a potential surge.

## Bitcoin, Fannie Mae, and Asset Valuations

Schiff expressed skepticism about the sustainability of the "Trump trade," pointing to the sharp decline in Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac stocks, which have dropped about 80% from their peaks. He suggested that insiders likely sold at highs, leaving retail investors holding the bag.

Turning to cryptocurrency, Schiff advised Bitcoin holders to reduce their exposure. He believes the recent rally in Bitcoin was driven by government friendliness under the Trump administration. With Democrats likely to take control of Congress, Schiff predicts a hostile regulatory environment for crypto. He further argued that if Democrats win the White House in 2028, the political tailwinds supporting Bitcoin will vanish, potentially leading to a complete collapse.

Schiff concluded that for those seeking an inflation hedge, gold and silver remain the superior choices over Bitcoin, which he believes has seen its best days.

Peter Schiff’s analysis paints a picture of an economy on the brink of instability, driven by contradictory foreign policy, politically motivated fiscal spending, and rising debt. He urges investors to recognize that rising bond yields signal deeper fiscal problems rather than healthy growth, making gold and silver critical portfolio hedges. As the 2026 midterms approach, Schiff warns that the resulting political shifts could accelerate a currency crisis, making physical precious metals the most prudent safe haven.
