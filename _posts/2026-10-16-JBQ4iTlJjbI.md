In a recent episode of the *Capital Kosm* podcast, renowned gold bug and economist Peter Schiff outlined a grim economic forecast for the United States, specifically centering on the aftermath of the upcoming midterm elections. Speaking with host Danny on October 13, 2026, Schiff argued that the political landscape is poised to trigger significant fiscal instability, potentially leading to a sovereign debt or currency crisis.

## The Contradictory Russian Diesel Deal

Schiff began by dissecting the recent announcement of a diesel deal between the U.S. and Russia, noting the stark contradiction in recent policy. Just three weeks prior, President Trump signed a bipartisan bill granting him the authority to impose up to 100% tariffs on any country purchasing oil or gas from Russia. The stated goal was to cut off funding for Russia’s war effort against Ukraine.

However, Schiff pointed out that the administration has now pivoted to importing Russian diesel, ostensibly to lower energy costs for American farmers and truck drivers ahead of the midterms. Schiff characterized this as a purely "politically motivated decision," noting that trucking is the number one occupation for men in America, making it a key demographic for Republican votes.

Furthermore, Schiff highlighted the irony of the administration’s narrative regarding high gas prices. While Republicans often blame Iran for energy costs, Schiff noted that the war in Iran was initiated under Trump’s watch with unanimous Republican support. He argued that the administration’s implicit subsidization of Iran through these energy deals underscores the disconnect between political rhetoric and geopolitical reality.

## The "Imaginary Deal" and Political Reality

A significant portion of the discussion focused on Trump’s Truth Social post claiming a truce between Russia and Ukraine regarding energy infrastructure. When both Russia and Ukraine denied the existence of such a deal, Trump defended his claim by stating, "There was a deal because I said there was a deal."

Schiff used this anecdote to illustrate a broader critique of the Trump presidency: the idea that reality is defined by presidential decree rather than factual outcomes. He argued that claims of the "greatest economy in the world" or massive tariff revenues are similarly detached from actual economic metrics, existing primarily within the narrative constructed by the administration.

## Post-Midterm Fiscal Outlook

With Polymarket odds showing a 91% chance of Democrats winning the House and 64% for the Senate, Schiff anticipates a shift in legislative dynamics. Contrary to the fear of gridlock, Schiff predicts a "lame duck" Trump signing even more expansive legislation than under previous Republican control.

A central component of Schiff’s prediction involves the potential passage of $5,000 stimulus checks. While Trump originally promised these checks contingent on a Republican victory, Schiff suggests Democrats will pass a version excluding the wealthy to force Trump’s hand. If Trump opposes it, he risks appearing petty; if he supports it, it adds trillions to the deficit. Schiff warned that if such a program were made annual, it would become the second-largest entitlement in the U.S., surpassing Medicare in scope.

Schiff described the political messaging surrounding this issue as contradictory: Republicans promise stimulus if they win, but warn of a 1929-style depression if Democrats win. Schiff noted the logical flaw in this stance, as historical precedent shows that severe economic crashes typically lead to *more* government stimulus, not less. He interpreted the 1929 warning as a preemptive excuse for future economic downturns, blaming the opposition rather than current fiscal policies.

## The Bond Market and Gold’s Bullish Case

Schiff addressed the current stagnation in gold and silver prices, attributing it to a market misconception that rising bond yields are bearish for precious metals. He argued that yields are rising precisely because lenders are losing confidence in the U.S. fiscal position and the sheer volume of debt.

Key points from Schiff’s analysis on the bond market include:
*   **Debt Sustainability:** Higher interest rates increase the cost of servicing national debt, widening budget deficits further.
*   **Wealth Destruction:** As bond prices fall, investors holding bonds lose money. Schiff predicts capital will flee from bonds to gold as a safe haven.
*   **Central Bank Behavior:** Central banks are already selling treasuries and accumulating gold, signaling a loss of confidence in sovereign debt.

Schiff emphasized that today’s high rates are "sewing the seeds of tomorrow’s rate cuts." As the economy weakens under the weight of high debt costs, the Federal Reserve will likely be forced to cut rates and resume quantitative easing (QE). This pivot, he argues, will trigger a currency crisis and send gold prices soaring.

## Investment Advice: Exit Crypto, Enter Gold

Schiff urged listeners to view current gold prices (near $4,000/oz) and silver prices (under $60/oz) as a "window of opportunity" before the inevitable surge. He dismissed the notion that prices will return to previous lows, stating that the floor has shifted.

In contrast to his bullish stance on precious metals, Schiff issued a strong warning against Bitcoin and the broader crypto sector. He argued that the crypto rally was driven by the Trump administration’s friendly regulatory environment. With Democrats likely to control Congress, he predicts a hostile regulatory shift that could lead to a significant crash in Bitcoin.

"Bitcoin has seen its best days," Schiff stated. "What's left is the crash. We haven't had it yet... take some out, you know, sell." He recommended converting Bitcoin holdings into physical gold and silver to hedge against the coming inflation and potential hyperinflationary response to the debt crisis.

## Conclusion

Schiff’s outlook for the post-midterm era is one of escalating fiscal irresponsibility and eventual crisis. He believes politicians will inevitably choose inflation over default, leading to a devaluation of the dollar. For investors, he advocates for a defensive posture centered on physical precious metals, anticipating that the current calm in gold markets is merely the calm before the storm of monetary debasement.
