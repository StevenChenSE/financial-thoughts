## The Hidden Gem in AI Hardware

In the relentless buzz around AI stocks, one semiconductor giant sits largely overlooked by American investors—despite delivering the very chips that power the artificial intelligence revolution. Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC) represents what may be the most undervalued opportunity in the AI supply chain, trading at a discount simply because of geography and visibility rather than business quality.

John Woo, president of Ava Labs and a former tech investor who spent years on Wall Street managing his own fund at Kingdom Capital, sat down at Consensus Miami alongside Bonnie Chang—an avowed TSMC "whale"—to explain why this asymmetry exists and why it may be precisely the opportunity savvy investors are missing.

---

## Why TSMC Trades at a Discount

TSMC presents a paradox: it manufactures the chips that Nvidia, Apple, AMD, and virtually every major AI company depends on, yet it trades at a significant valuation discount compared to its customers. The reasons are both structural and psychological.

### Geographic Proximity Penalties

"There's a little discount for the location," Woo explained, noting TSMC's proximity to China as one factor keeping valuations suppressed. Beyond geopolitical risk, he argued that US companies simply receive more media attention and analyst coverage naturally. "If they were a US company, people would be more aware of this company."

### The Valuation Math

Bonnie Chang, who describes herself as a "huge portion" of her investment portfolio in TSMC, pointed out that even professional investors recognize TSMC's quality. "It's historically a low-vol, very steady high-quality company," she noted. "The valuation is very fair usually relative to itself."

Woo elaborated: "Relative to itself, it's probably more expensive because it sits in such an important place and they're the best at what they do. But relative to other AI stocks, it's still very cheap, especially considering the growth rates."

---

## Beyond TSMC: Asia's AI Hardware Advantage

TSMC isn't alone in representing Asian excellence in AI infrastructure. The broader Asian ecosystem offers multiple compelling investment opportunities.

### Key Players in the AI Supply Chain

- **TSMC** (Taiwan): Advanced semiconductor manufacturing
- **Memory companies in Korea**: Critical for AI model training and inference
- **Tokyo Electron** (Japan): Semiconductor equipment

"Asia is very good at the hardware and the electrical engineering layer of AI, which is really where a lot of the things that are in scarcity are," Woo observed. "There's a lot of good things to look at in Asian companies, and TSMC is probably at the very top of that list."

---

## The Work Ethic Factor: Can the West Catch Up?

President Trump has publicly stated a goal of seeing 50% of chips manufactured in the USA, raising the question of whether American companies can compete with Asian semiconductor manufacturing prowess.

### Cultural Differences in Manufacturing

Chang offered a revealing anecdote from her Taiwanese connections: "In Asia, I know a lot of engineers, they work three shifts. Whenever there's an earthquake, all the engineers—they don't go to their kids or their wives. They head straight back to their companies."

Woo, while acknowledging this work ethic, also pointed to American strengths: "Americans have a very entrepreneurial culture. They're a little bit more risk-taking." He described his own experience founding Ava Labs with Cornell engineers, where "we were all hard-working and very entrepreneurial at the same time."

The ideal scenario, Woo suggested, would be "blending those two things together"—hard work ethic combined with entrepreneurial risk-taking.

---

## How Long Can TSMC Sustain Its Edge?

The fundamental thesis for TSMC rests on one question: Can competitors replicate its technological leadership?

Woo's assessment was nuanced: "The reality of the world we live in in technology today in 2026 is that consistent and constant innovation can be an edge. And the only edge—even in quant trading firms—these quant models have a half-life that gets shorter and shorter."

The implication is clear: even Nvidia, AMD, and TSMC must perpetually innovate to maintain competitive advantages. There is no permanent moat—only sustained execution.

## Portfolio Construction in the AI Age

### For Individual Investors

Woo drew a striking parallel to the early internet era: "It reminds me of the internet days. If you can find a portfolio... you're going to get some of them wrong, but if you can find the Amazon, the Google, the Nvidia of back in the day today... you can just hold it for a while."

His advice: identify a portfolio of potentially best-in-class companies across various AI verticals and simply hold through volatility. "Imagine if you were 25 years ago—you probably got a few wrong, but if you identified Google, Netflix, and Amazon, just hold it and don't worry about the ups and downs."

### For Professional Investors: The Bottleneck Trade

Professional portfolio managers have become adept at identifying sequential bottlenecks in the AI supply chain:

1. **Nvidia GPUs** — The initial AI boom
2. **Networking** — Infrastructure to connect AI systems
3. **Photonics** — Optical components for data transmission
4. **Memory** — Companies like Micron and SK Hynix

"This is why you're seeing all of a sudden you're going from Nvidia alone to networking, then it becomes photonics, then it becomes memory," Woo explained. "They keep finding the next bottleneck and getting ahead of that. This is why you're getting these super spikes."

### The Micron Paradox

Micron presents a fascinating case study. "Micron was supposed to be a cyclical slow memory business," Woo noted. "On PE basis, it's still cheap, but people don't know how long it could last. The stock looks like it should be trading with no earnings, yet it's trading less than 10 times next year's earnings."

When Google announced Turbo Que (a more efficient AI model), Micron dropped 12% in a day on fears of reduced memory demand—yet the stock has since climbed roughly 50% higher. "Just because something is announced doesn't mean it's going to destroy that entire industry," Woo cautioned.

## The Reddit Effect and Valuation Discipline

The rise of momentum-driven retail investing has created a bifurcated market. Woo expressed being "torn" on this development:

**The Positive**: Individual investors now feel they can participate in markets, democratizing access.

**The Concern**: "I do worry that they over-speculate and there's less emphasis on valuation. In the long term—the power of compounding and buying relatively fairly valued companies will win out."

He referenced the internet era cautionary tale: "There are still people I remember who bought pets.com and have never recovered. And it's a shame because if they just held on to like the 10 or 15 really good ones or bought an index, they'd be very well off."

### The ETF Challenge

Active managers have been squeezed by quant-driven ETFs that create correlated movements across stocks regardless of individual company fundamentals. "Stocks more than ever are highly correlated and work up and down together," Woo observed, "sometimes nothing to do with the stock itself—just because of the weightings of the ETF."

## Bitcoin's New Chapter: Beyond the Old Guard

Bitcoin hovering around $81,000 has prompted discussions about whether the bear market has definitively ended. Fundstrat's Tom Lee famously stated that if Bitcoin closes above $76,000 for three consecutive months, the bear market is over.

Woo, while crediting Lee as "a very smart guy" who "knows statistics," offered his own framework:

### The Changing of the Guard

"Earlier this year, we were seeing a changing of the guard," Woo said. "The old guard that were the old school Bitcoin people were selling into MicroStrategy and the ETFs. I think that uncertainty has happened already."

At 18 years old, Bitcoin is maturing. "A lot of people, part of the reason I was attracted to investing in tech is we love innovation, the new thing," Woo reflected. "I think when you're 18 years old—and I think crypto is going into a new phase right now—it's no longer the new new thing."

This mirrors serial entrepreneurship: "You're like, why don't you just go from 0 to 10? Because some people like 0 to 1 and some people like 1 to 10."

### The RIA Education Pipeline

A second driver Woo identified is the gradual adoption by registered investment advisors (RIAs). "You need those individual RIAs to get educated. After they're educated on the product, then they recommend it to their clients. And that process takes four, five, six months."

With those channels having opened earlier in the year, Woo sees that education funnel now translating into actual client flows: "We're getting to the point where since the beginning of the year when these channels were allowed to start recommending it to their individual investors... the end user knows it enough. So I think you have that flow from those people."

## Building Real-World Asset Infrastructure: Avalanche's Vision

Ava Labs has been pursuing a dual strategy since its founding: serve cryptonative customers while becoming default infrastructure for institutional asset tokenization.

### The Broadridge Partnership

Broadridge Financial Solutions—a company that processes $8 trillion in tokenized assets monthly and handles critical backend functions like corporate actions, proxy votes, and securities processing—announced a partnership with Avalanche to put tokenized equities on-chain.

"This is fantastic and it's very important," Woo said. "They do all the corporate actions, proxy voting, and compliance stuff. This is a big part of traditional equities."

This partnership represents the "infrastructure layer" necessary for tokenized real-world assets (RWA) to function. "Everyone wants tokenized real world assets," Woo explained, "but they forget you need systems underneath that to make those things smooth."

### The Layers of Value Capture

Woo outlined a vision of value capture across multiple layers:

**Asset Layer** (built on Avalanche):
- Stablecoins (billions in circulation)
- Money market funds and treasuries
- Private credit and private equity
- Tokenized public equities

**Infrastructure Layer** (where partners like Broadridge fit):
- Corporate actions processing
- Proxy voting systems
- Compliance and regulation
- Custody and fiat on/off ramps

### Straits X: Asian Stablecoin Distribution

Straits X, a Singaporean company building Asia-oriented stablecoins, has chosen Avalanche as its blockchain—demonstrating what Woo called Asian companies' confidence in the platform. The company has deep distribution through GrabPay and Alipay connections.

### Custom Blockchain Networks

Avalanche operates as "a network of networks," allowing enterprises to spin up their own purpose-built, sovereign blockchains. "A lot of the traditional finance people have it in a closed private chain and not part of the permissionless," Woo noted, "but they're capturing value already just because they're in their own private chain."

The beauty of this architecture: companies can connect to hundreds of other layer-1 networks within the Avalanche ecosystem and eventually tap the main chain for liquidity—while maintaining EVM compatibility to bridge into the Ethereum world.

## Key Takeaways

1. **TSMC remains undervalued** relative to other AI stocks, trading at a discount due to geographic location (China proximity) and lower visibility compared to US companies—despite being the manufacturing backbone of the AI industry.

2. **Asia dominates the hardware layer** of AI infrastructure. Beyond TSMC, Korean memory companies and Japanese semiconductor equipment makers represent attractive opportunities in the AI supply chain.

3. **Professional investors should play the bottleneck game**, rotating through supply chain chokepoints (GPUs → networking → photonics → memory) as AI infrastructure needs evolve.

4. **Individual investors should focus on buying and holding** best-in-class AI companies through volatility, similar to the early internet era strategy that rewarded holders of Amazon, Google, and Netflix.

5. **Bitcoin's bear market may be over**, driven by a generational shift in ownership (old guard rotating out to ETFs and new institutional buyers) and the RIA education pipeline beginning to translate into actual client flows.

6. **Tokenized real-world assets are becoming infrastructure reality**, with partnerships like Avalanche and Broadridge addressing the backend systems (corporate actions, compliance, custody) necessary for institutional adoption.
