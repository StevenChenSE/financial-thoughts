## Insights from the Vancouver Resource Investment Conference

At the Vancouver Resource Investment Conference (VRIC), VRIC Media host Daryl Thomas sat down with Matthew Pipenberg of Von Greer to discuss the current state of the precious metals market, the psychological landscape of investors, and the macroeconomic forces driving gold's trajectory. Pipenberg emphasized that despite recent price fluctuations, the fundamental case for gold has never been stronger, driven by inevitable currency debasement, aggressive central bank accumulation, and a structural shift in global monetary power.

### Investor Sentiment and the Reality of Market Cycles

Pipenberg noted that the audience at VRIC is highly sophisticated, with investors traveling from Hungary, Serbia, the UK, Australia, and across the United States. The consensus among these attendees is clear: the primary trade is the devaluation of paper currencies. Investors are focused on wealth preservation through physical gold and silver, displaying patience and calm despite daily headlines and volatility.

Pipenberg highlighted that these investors understand market cycles well. He pointed out that in any secular bull market, significant shakeouts are inevitable. He referenced the 1970s gold bull market, where the metal rallied from $35 to $850 but experienced at least five corrections exceeding 20%, including a massive drawdown between 1974 and 1976 where gold was cut in half before surging eightfold. He argued that the current volatility is a standard feature of a bull market, not a sign of trend reversal. In fact, Pipenberg asserted that the fundamentals for gold at current levels of $4,000 to $5,500 are far more compelling than they were when gold traded at $35 or $200 in the 1970s.

### The Core Thesis: Debt, Debasement, and Real Money

The central theme of Pipenberg's analysis is that gold's direction is dictated not by its inherent properties, but by the trajectory of global debt and the consequent debasement of currencies required to monetize that debt. With U.S. public debt at $40 trillion and global debt approaching $360 trillion, Pipenberg described the debasement of money as "inevitable and openly obvious."

He characterized the shift toward gold as a move away from "paper currency which is confetti" toward "real money" and sound collateral. Trust in the U.S. 10-year Treasury, once the bedrock of global finance, is declining due to high debt levels and the weaponization of the dollar following the 2022 sanctions regime. Pipenberg stressed that gold is emerging as the new trusted collateral, a reality acknowledged by sovereign wealth funds, central banks, and increasingly by Wall Street institutions.

### Federal Reserve Liquidity and the "Worsh" Factor

Addressing monetary policy, Pipenberg discussed the actions of the Federal Reserve under the new leadership, referring to the incoming Chair as "Worsh." Despite hawkish rhetoric, Pipenberg argued that "Worsh" has been more dovish than former Chair Powell, particularly regarding liquidity provision. He cited a $24 to $30 billion increase in the M0 base money supply in "Worsh's" first few weeks.

Pipenberg revealed that "Worsh" is fast-tracking measures to circumvent Basel 3 compliance regulations, effectively releasing approximately $90 billion in cash reserves that banks were required to hold. He warned that this liquidity could be leveraged by "too big to fail" banks by 10 to 15 times, injecting over $1 trillion in backdoor liquidity into the system. This expansion of credit, facilitated through mechanisms like the supplemental leverage ratios and the Treasury General Account, accelerates money supply growth and fuels long-term inflation and currency debasement.

### Central Banks Are Stacking Gold at Record Paces

A major driver of gold's resilience is the unprecedented accumulation by central banks. Pipenberg noted that central bank gold stacking has increased fivefold since 2022. Over the last 11 fiscal quarters, central banks purchased over 200 tons of gold in 10 consecutive quarters. China has maintained a 19-month streak of buying, acquiring approximately 160 tons by May alone.

Pipenberg emphasized that this buying is not for jewelry or industrial use but represents a strategic shift. Central banks are replacing U.S. Treasuries with physical gold as the anchor of their reserves. He suggested that reported figures from the World Gold Council may understate the true volume, with some experts estimating actual buying could be ten times higher. This exodus of gold from Western exchanges like COMEX and LBMA to sovereign vaults underscores a "sea change" in the global monetary system.

### China's Exchange and the Shift in Pricing Power

Pipenberg highlighted a seismic shift occurring with the launch of a new gold exchange between Hong Kong and Shanghai, scheduled for July 24. This exchange will rely less on paper leverage and more on physical gold and silver, aiming for fairer price discovery compared to the "manipulated" markets of New York and London.

He recalled a statement from 2014 by the former head of the Shanghai Exchange predicting that China would eventually set the gold price, a notion once mocked by Western arrogance but now viewed as "deadly serious." Pipenberg sees this as part of a broader realignment where the flow of money and the understanding of gold are moving from West to East. The BRICS nations, while not fully unified, share a common distrust of the dollar and are building a trading system collateralized by gold rather than U.S. debt.

### Gold-Backed Treasuries and Wall Street's Awakening

Speculation mounted around July 4th regarding a potential announcement of gold-backed U.S. Treasuries, a proposal championed by former Fed nominee Judy Shelton. While Pipenberg acknowledged uncertainty about whether this would materialize, he argued that the mere discussion of such a policy in Washington and Wall Street is significant. Ten years ago, linking U.S. debt to gold would have been considered blasphemy in D.C.

The fact that policymakers and major institutions like Morgan Stanley, which recently recommended a 20% allocation to gold, are now engaging with the idea signals a crisis of confidence. Pipenberg noted that even major banks are projecting gold prices higher than his own estimates. This convergence indicates that the establishment recognizes gold is now trusted more than the "full faith and credit" of the U.S. government.

### Inflation, Shadow Stats, and Purchasing Power

Pipenberg was critical of official inflation metrics, comparing the Consumer Price Index (CPI) to a "fat camp scale" that is manipulated to show lower gains while the reality of rising costs is ignored. He endorsed the work of John Williams at Shadow Stats, which suggests that if measured by the standards of the Volcker era, inflation would be closer to 11% to 12%.

He argued that the compounding annual growth rate of U.S. debt, averaging 8% to 9%, is a more accurate proxy for real inflation. The Federal Reserve has consistently missed its 2% inflation target, with official projections often lagging reality. Pipenberg pointed out that while the S&P 500 is up 60% since the fourth quarter of 2021, it is down 40% when measured in gold terms. This erosion of purchasing power serves as an "invisible tax" on savers, making anti-inflationary assets like gold essential for wealth preservation.

### Conclusion: Be Your Own Central Bank

Pipenberg concluded with a message of pragmatic self-reliance. He described the political and monetary systems as broken, characterized by a revolving door of careerists and lobbyists who prioritize the system over the public. In this environment, individuals must "be their own central bank."

He advised investors to focus on protecting purchasing power through physical ownership of precious metals, warning against holding gold in ETFs or commercial bank vaults due to counterparty and operational risks. For free education and detailed analysis, Pipenberg directed viewers to voners.gold and vg.gold, where Von Greer publishes research on market narratives, cycles, and the importance of holding physical assets. As the monetary system transitions, Pipenberg urged investors to stay informed, remain calm through volatility, and recognize gold's role as the anchor of value in a debt-saturated world.
