## 2. The Copper Crunch: Supply vs. Demand

Turning to commodities, Andy analyzes the current scarcity of copper, noting a significant drop in production from the world's largest producer.

### Chile's Production Low
*   **Event:** *"Chile posted its lowest monthly copper output in almost nine years, highlighting the challenges facing the world's top producer as ore grades decline and key mines underperform."*
*   **Trend:** Copper is down 20% from its February highs, but the long-term outlook is tighter.

### Future Deficits and the "Never" Scenario
When asked about future supply, Andy projects massive deficits:
> *"Yeah, I was looking at the uh future deficits of copper and they're they just get massive the further you get into the future."*

He anticipates that supply will likely never meet demand for several commodities, including copper, silver, oil, natural gas, and potentially coal.
> *"Now, that's going to happen probably across a few different commodities. We could see that happen in silver. We could see that happen in uh oil and natural gas, potentially even coal."*

### The Inflationary Question
A major uncertainty lies in whether money printing (inflation) will outpace supply creation.
> *"Every time in history, what we had is we had demand go up from money printing and then supply would eventually catch back up. Now, the big question that I have is we're going to print a bunch of money. It's going to come into the system. It's going to be highly inflationary. What is the supply actually do? Are we going to meet that demand or is supply going to lag for the rest of my life?"*

Andy also questions technological substitution:
> *"Is is technology going to find new commodities or is technology going to replace copper and aluminum and all these different things with say graphine or something else. Is graphine even going to be viable?"*

Despite the uncertainty, his conclusion is clear:
> *"I just I just know things are going to get really tight uh for a bunch of different commodities and it's going to get tight starting like now forward."*

## 5. Warren Buffett's Lessons: Patience and Selection

Warren Buffett's approach offers further wisdom on market timing and asset selection.

### The Power of Patience
Buffett stated: *"If there is a big market decline, Berkshire will deploy cash. I'm still involved in making investments at Berkshire. We have north of 350 billion in cash on hand. Things don't look substantially deeper. Not in it to make five or 6%, which is what indices are down."*

Andy interprets this as:
> *"He's looking for a big decline. He's looking to deploy money. He just doesn't want to do it at overvalued prices. He's extremely patient and it's in my opinion people should learn from this. What should you learn? He's patient to purchase. He waits for good entry points that are massively undervalued and then he deploys capital."*

### Why Buffett Avoids Commodities
Buffett prefers "boring companies that compound over long periods of time" (e.g., candy, food, insurance, banks) because they offer stable, long-term compounding without the need to sell.
> *"That that's really what they are. But you can take some of his uh some of the things that he applies like patience and waiting for fear in the market and buying when things are very cheap. You can apply that to commodities."*

However, commodities cycle up and down viciously, lacking the long-term stability of Buffett's preferred assets.
> *"The reason he doesn't like commodities is because it cycles up and down quite viciously. you don't get that long compounding uh year afteryear return that lasts for 50 60 70 years that you never have to sell and and that's kind of what he deems to be the greatest efficiency of capital allocation."*

He also avoids most technology companies due to rapid obsolescence, except for those with massive moats like Apple or Google.
> *"Uh the they're not technology companies where technology companies cycle in cycle out. Uh the they get replaced with new technology. So he basically avoids technology... Uh he may take a a a a position in just something that's got a massive moat that's a technology company like Apple or Google or something like that."*

### The AI Bubble
Currently, the AI sector is trading at **41% concentration levels**, similar to the tech bubble of 2000.
> *"Uh bubbles tend to peak at 40% concentration levels. The current AI bubble is at 41%."*
