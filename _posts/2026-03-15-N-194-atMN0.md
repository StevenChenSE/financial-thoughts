In a striking critique of current military expenditure, defense analyst Elbridge Colby has highlighted what he calls a severe and foolish misallocation of advanced U.S. munitions in Ukraine. The core of his argument centers on a stark economic imbalance: using multimillion-dollar missiles to destroy cheap drones.

"We've run low, especially in all of the expensive exotic munitions—the missiles that are used for air and missile defense and the strike missiles," Colby stated. The reason for this depletion, he argues, is clear: "We've been shipping them in large numbers to Ukraine where they have been used, I think, very foolishly."

Colby pointed to the unsustainable math of such tactics. "When you start shooting missiles that cost you $2 million a pop to shoot down a $20,000 unmanned drone, you're wasting missiles. And the Ukrainians have done a lot of that."

The consequence, he warns, is a direct threat to American readiness. "We have, I think, unnecessarily reduced the sizes of our stockpiles." This drain specifically affects high-tech, precision-guided missiles, creating a potential vulnerability in U.S. air and missile defense capabilities.

He was careful to distinguish these "exotic" systems from more conventional firepower. "Now, if you're talking about regular artillery ammunition, no, we've got lots of that. In other words, the non-exotic straightforward conventional munitions, we have unlimited stockpiles."

The commentary underscores a growing strategic dilemma: how to support an ally effectively without eroding one's own military capital through cost-ineffective engagements. Colby’s analysis suggests that preserving America’s limited supply of advanced missiles for high-value threats is not just a matter of fiscal responsibility, but of national security.
