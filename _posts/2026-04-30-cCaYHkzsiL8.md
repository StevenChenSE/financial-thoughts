## The Coming Inflation Storm: Why This Time Is Different

Renowned market commentator and ADVFN founder Clem Chambers is issuing a stark warning to investors: brace yourself for sustained inflation of 7-9% annually over the next five to ten years. In a recent market analysis, Chambers explained why this inflationary period differs fundamentally from the COVID-era price spikes—and why it demands a fundamentally different investment approach.

"During COVID and early on in your show before inflation showed up, I said inflation's coming and then it arrived," Chambers recalled. "And they got it down now. It's coming again now. It's going to be at least as bad as the COVID inflation and it's going to go for a lot longer because it's not going to be inflation created to pave over or paper over something that lasted 6 months."

## The Drivers of Sustained Inflation

According to Chambers, the inflationary pressures now building aren't temporary phenomena to be managed—they're structural features of a massive economic transformation.

### The Great Industrial Build-Out

The United States is embarking on what Chambers describes as a "massive industrial build-out or an attempted build-out." This includes reshoring manufacturing, rebuilding domestic supply chains, and constructing new production facilities—initiatives that will require enormous capital expenditure.

### The AI Revolution's Hidden Cost

Then there's the "monster of AI," which Chambers characterizes as "another huge build-out." The energy demands alone are staggering:

- Data centers require massive power infrastructure
- AI model training consumes enormous electricity
- The semiconductor supply chain needs expansion
- Cooling systems and physical infrastructure demands are climbing

"You're talking about trillions of dollars of money, liquidity, cash that is going to be needed to fund all this," Chambers stated. "I'm not going to say, 'Oh, no, we haven't got the money to fund this. We're not going to print this.' They're just going to pull the lever and they're going to pump it out."

### The Fed's Implicit Stance

Chambers predicts that central bank messaging will deliberately mischaracterize economic conditions to justify accommodative policy. "When you're fed and your new Fed man says, 'Oh, we're going to get deflation,' I mean, that's not because he's on Planet Zorg high on drugs. It's because he's saying that so he can inflate, so he can lower it artificially interest rates, so he can print money."

The result? "It's going to be a printer-thon coming up to an economy near you."

## Jamie Dimon's Bond Warning: What It Means

J.P. Morgan CEO Jamie Dimon's recent warning about government debt levels triggering a bond market crisis has rattled some investors. Chambers considers it "very accurate" but emphasizes the government's ability to manage—or manipulate—the situation.

### The Government Can Always Buy Its Own Bonds

"One thing people are missing and need to think about is if a government owns owes money to its own people, is that a big deal?" Chambers asked. "Because it's a liability of the government, but an asset of the people in that country. So, it's the same both sides of the balance sheet."

This is precisely what quantitative easing represents. Governments can suppress interest rates indefinitely by purchasing their own debt, though the consequence is inflation rather than fiscal prudence.

### The Monetization Reality

"Government can always buy its bonds back and has been doing so even though technically it's illegal," Chambers noted. "They can publish, print their own bonds and then turn it into cash and then pay themselves."

This "monetization of government debt is really the shortcut to inflation," Chambers explained. "That's how you get hyper-inflation. That's what all the South Americans do. They don't bother taxing people. They just print the money and pay their people with it."

### The Real National Debt

Chambers offered a framework for understanding sovereign debt sustainability: the real national debt only counts obligations to foreign creditors.

"Americans rich, government poor, right? Money coming off your bonds yield is like a reverse taxation. That's not so bad. The trouble comes when you're Argentina and you owe that money to people that aren't in your country. In America, it's 30% or the last time I looked. The American national debt is only owed to people outside of America 30%."

The remaining 70% represents inter-generational transfer within the same economy—a different proposition entirely.

## Inflation: Types and Investment Implications

Not all inflation is created equal, according to Chambers. He outlined three distinct varieties:

### Stagflation: The Worst-Case Scenario

High inflation combined with economic stagnation. This "is really bad inflation" that erodes purchasing power without corresponding wage growth—essentially a tax on holding currency.

### Passive Inflation: Silent Erosion

Standard inflation that exists alongside economic normalcy. "Nothing much is going on apart from it's eroding the value of your money and eroding the liability of the government."

### Productive Inflation: The Best Bad Option

Inflation accompanying genuine economic growth, where wages and asset values rise faster than prices. "The inflation that creates economic activity, which you can put up with because your money goes up 10% even though the actual value of it goes down."

Chambers believes the coming period will be productive inflation: "Fortunately, it'll go into productive assets, which means that it won't be hyper-inflationary, but it will be high inflation."

## Gold, Silver, and the Geopolitical Premium

Precious metals have been "hovering around their respective ranges for about two months," and Chambers sees a specific catalyst that would trigger the next major move.

### "Gold Is for War"

Chambers articulated a nuanced thesis on gold pricing dynamics:

- **Before war**: Gold surges as investors seek hard assets
- **During war**: Gold comes under pressure as holders sell to fund activities
- **After war**: Gold rises again due to inflationary aftermath

"The gold is for war theme is all about Taiwan," Chambers explained. "Go look at the Taiwan stock exchange. It's just gone through the roof."

When geopolitical tensions surrounding Taiwan intensified in late January—reportedly involving tensions between President Xi and military leadership—the precious metals markets responded accordingly.

### Near-Term Outlook

For investors considering gold positions, Chambers recommended a patient approach: "If you want to know what's going to happen to gold, it will go on a trend. And you don't really have a trend until the volatility drops. Once it goes back to normal volatility, you just look at the trend and you can see where it's going."

His practical advice: "If you don't have exposure to gold, it's a good time to dollar cost average. Have a bit. Have a bit next month. Have a bit the month after that. You'll build up a position over a couple of years and you'll get a good average price."

## Where the Real Opportunities Lie

Chambers identified specific sectors poised to benefit from the coming economic environment.

### Nuclear Power: The Unsung Beneficiary

"There's Fluor Corp because they build clean sort out nuclear stuff and there ain't that many people out there that do that anymore. And boy, are they going to be building out nuclear like there's no tomorrow."

The uranium question matters, but more pressing is infrastructure capacity. "Where are they going to get energy?" Chambers asked. "Energy is the bottleneck. Energy distribution, energy making it, all of that stuff is the big choke point."

### Infrastructure Plays: Nokia and Beyond

Chambers expressed surprising enthusiasm for a company many had written off: "Nokia. Because Nokia's the biggest joke in town apart from the fact that Nvidia just put a billion dollars into them to build out 6G because the Chinese infrastructure people aren't trusted anymore."

This represents "AI in mobile infrastructure" combined with Western allied supply chains—a combination with growing strategic importance.

### The Copper Imperative

"Anything that's in that zone is going to go really well. People that make server center cabling, which is not your average cable. You know, copper. Full stop copper. There ain't enough copper before AI came along. And now it's come along."

### Energy Infrastructure: The 10x Challenge

Chambers offered a striking projection: "Energy that they're going to have to make 10x energy in the next 10 years. Right. I mean, that just changes everything."

## AI's Impact on Employment: The Brutal Truth

The wave of tech layoffs—from Meta, Microsoft, and others—has prompted speculation about AI eliminating knowledge worker jobs. Chambers offered a counterintuitive interpretation.

### Why Companies Are Really Cutting

"Those companies were hiring people for non-existent jobs. And they were doing it for decades. And now rather than hire a load of people that do nothing because it looks good on a spreadsheet, they are having to build out things that cost real money."

He revealed an uncomfortable industry terminology: "In Silicon Valley, they call employees—wait for it—in the HR department, they call them stock. So they're destocking. Those people are stock. They're not employees. They're stock."

Companies accumulated human "stock" to prevent competitors from hiring talent. Now, with capital constrained and physical infrastructure demands rising, that strategy has reversed.

### The Productivity Multiplier

Chambers described a new productivity paradigm: "I know programmers that are doing coding for 20 people. Now, they spend a quarter of a million pounds on AI doing it. But that's like 250,000 and two and a half million pounds for the programmers. And they're doing it single-handedly because of AI."

The result won't be mass unemployment but rather output expansion. "You'll get 10 times as much output, not a tenth of the programmers. Because the programmers will be there. They'll be able to wield AI, the ones that are employable anyway. And they'll just make infinitely more code."

## The Fed's Coming Pivot

With the FOMC meeting on the horizon, Chambers predicted aggressive policy accommodation.

"They're going to go on a complete printing bender. Even if inflation is going to be a reality. They won't be saying it. They'll be saying deflation. 'Oh, deflation, deflation. Why are you saying that? And then of course we want to print money.'"

The political dimension is impossible to ignore. Chambers noted that when Donald Trump wants lower interest rates, the Fed's "independence" faces tests.

## The Fundamental Division

Chambers concluded with a framework for understanding who will thrive and who will struggle:

"For the economically active, that's great news because you surf that huge wave of cash and you get loads of it yourself. And for the economically passive, you get crushed."

"The AI guys, they're cranking out a new model every 10 minutes. Why is that then? Because they're using AI to do it. They are just the little boy riding the elephant."

The prescription is clear: "The answer is to be on the offensive and looking for the opportunities and being active."
