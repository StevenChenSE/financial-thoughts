## Technical Breakdown of Key Support Levels, Moving Averages, and Risk-Reward Setups

In a recent episode of the KE Report, host Shad Marquitz sat down with Steve Penny, founder of Silver Chartist, to analyze the current technical landscape across the US Dollar, precious metals, and uranium markets. Following significant rallies in early 2024 and subsequent corrections, Penny provided a detailed chart-based assessment, emphasizing how technical analysis cuts through macroeconomic noise and identifies high-probability accumulation zones.

### The US Dollar: Technical Strength vs. Fundamental Weakness
The US Dollar has experienced a modest rebound this year, currently trading within a long-term uptrend channel. Penny notes that while he remains fundamentally bearish on the dollar due to well-known macroeconomic pressures, technical indicators dictate a cautious approach. The dollar index has recently confirmed a new uptrend after the 50-day moving average crossed above the 200-day moving average, with price action holding above both rising averages. This setup often triggers algorithmic "buy the dip" behavior among institutional traders. While a rising dollar acts as a headwind for commodities, historical precedent shows that precious metals can still advance alongside it. Penny expects the dollar to move slightly higher before a topping process begins, but traders must respect the current technical structure until it breaks.

### Silver: Drawing Parallels to 1974 and the $48–$54 Support Floor
Silver’s recent trajectory has drawn striking comparisons to the 1970s bull market. After peaking at $121.65 in January, silver has corrected by more than half. Penny likens the January peak to the 1974 top, suggesting that a prolonged base-building phase is likely underway. While the 1970s consolidation lasted nearly five years, Penny anticipates a shorter but still multi-quarter to multi-year consolidation period before the next major expansion. 

Technically, the $48 to $54 range represents what Penny calls the "mother of all support." The psychological and technical significance of the $50 level, which acted as a ceiling for decades, has now transformed it into a robust floor. With downside risk limited and upside potential substantial, Penny views the current price action as an asymmetric setup highly favorable for accumulation. He cautions against expecting an immediate V-shaped recovery to $121, emphasizing realistic timelines and disciplined position sizing.

### Gold: Working Off Extreme Overbought Conditions and the 200-Day Moving Average
Gold’s long-term chart displays a textbook cup and handle pattern that broke out above $2,089. The subsequent rally pushed the monthly RSI to the most overbought reading in its trading history. While the fundamental case for gold remains exceptionally strong, technical exhaustion necessitates a consolidation phase. 

Gold has recently dipped below its 200-day moving average, a level closely watched by institutions and algorithms. However, Penny points out that the 200-day is still sloping upward, which mitigates the bearish implications of the breakdown. He identifies $3,900 as the next key support level, with $3,500 representing a worst-case scenario that would align with a deeper silver pullback. A potential "death cross" (50-day crossing below the 200-day) is on the horizon, but Penny notes that such signals carry less weight when the 200-day remains bullish. He projects a compression pattern that will shake out weak hands over the next few quarters before gold resumes its long-term trajectory toward $10,000.

### Mining ETFs (GDX & SILJ): Undervaluation and Double Bottom Formations
The gold miners ETF (GDX) has experienced a steep 37% pullback from its highs. Despite the technical damage, the chart shows a potential double bottom forming around $73. A backtest to the $59 breakout level remains a lower-probability worst-case scenario. Like gold, GDX has lost its 200-day moving average, but the average continues to slope upward, keeping the long-term structure intact.

Silver junior miners, tracked by the SILJ ETF, present a similar technical picture but with a compelling valuation advantage. Penny highlights SILJ as the most undervalued component of the precious metals complex relative to gold miners, silver, and gold itself. SILJ recently printed a precise double bottom at $24.49. Reclaiming the 200-day moving average would quickly repair short-term technical damage and confirm a resumption of the broader uptrend.

### Uranium (SRUUF & URNM): Navigating Narrative Shifts and NAV Discounts
Uranium has faced headwinds as markets reassess the pace of AI data center construction, a narrative that previously fueled sector gains. Despite the near-term weakness, Penny remains highly constructive on uranium fundamentals. He points to the Sprott Physical Uranium Trust (SRUUF) as a key sentiment indicator. When SRUUF trades at an 11% to 12% discount to its net asset value, it has historically marked sector bottoms. Currently hovering around a 10% discount, Penny has been actively accumulating SRUUF.

For broader exposure, Penny recommends the Global X Uranium ETF (URNM), which offers a balanced mix of approximately 15% SRUUF, major producers like Cameco and Kazatomprom, and higher-torque small-cap names. URNM is currently trading below its 200-day moving average but has formed a double bottom near $51.29. The risk-reward profile remains attractive, with a quick recovery above the 200-day likely to accelerate the sector’s next leg higher.

### Trading Philosophy: Price Action, Position Sizing, and Discipline
Throughout the analysis, Penny emphasized a core trading principle: price and volume precede the news. Relying on headlines often leads to buying at peaks and selling at bottoms, as bullish media coverage tends to be loudest during market tops. Technical analysis provides an objective framework to remove emotion and identify high-probability setups. 

Penny also stressed the importance of a predefined trading plan. He shared his experience of scaling out of positions during the January peaks when risk-reward ratios became unfavorable, despite facing skepticism from followers. Taking profits at technical extremes preserves dry powder, allowing investors to scale back in during oversold conditions near major support. Proper position sizing ensures that volatility does not compromise long-term conviction, and adhering to a systematic approach ultimately leads to more consistent results.

### Summary
Steve Penny’s technical outlook suggests that while the US Dollar, gold, silver, and uranium are undergoing necessary consolidation phases, the long-term bullish structures remain intact. Key support levels around $48–$54 for silver, $3,900 for gold, and double bottom formations in GDX, SILJ, and URNM present favorable risk-reward accumulation opportunities. By respecting moving averages, monitoring discount metrics like SRUUF’s NAV gap, and adhering to disciplined position sizing and profit-taking strategies, investors can navigate the current volatility and position themselves for the next expansionary cycle in commodities and energy resources.
