## The Bull Market Is Back in Gear

After months of sideways consolidation, silver has broken out—and according to metals expert Bill Holtz, this move is just getting started. Speaking on Capital Kosm on May 12, 2026, Holtz laid out a compelling technical and fundamental case for significantly higher precious metals prices, predicting silver could reach $200-$250 by year end, potentially doubling gold's performance in the process.

"The bull market is back in gear," Holtz declared. "I think this move is going to make what we saw from November to January look like a tractor in first gear. I think this one's going to be a tractor in second gear."

## Technical Analysis: The Triangle Breakout

### Reading the Charts

Holtz identified a classic technical pattern that preceded silver's recent surge. After collapsing from January highs, silver had been "chopping sideways for a couple months," coiling into what analysts recognize as a triangle formation.

- Silver broke decisively above the $80 level, trading as high as $87—a two-month high
- The daily MACD (Moving Average Convergence Divergence) indicator has turned upward from very low levels, generating a buy signal
- Both silver and gold had become deeply oversold after the January collapse, with silver declining from $120-$125 down to $61-$70

"This sideways move that we've done for three months is basically a triangle," Holtz explained while reviewing the chart. "Silver had coiled up one, two, three, four times. Fifth time, boom, breakout."

### Why This Matters: The Launchpad Effect

Perhaps most critically, Holtz emphasized that the launchpad for this move is fundamentally different from the previous rally. In late 2024, silver surged from approximately $35. Now, the base is the $70-$80 level rather than the mid-$30s.

"The launch pad instead of being $35 is the $70 to $80 level," Holtz noted. "This is important to understand."

### Gold Lags Behind Silver

While silver has already broken out, gold is following a similar pattern with slightly delayed timing. Holtz observed that gold's triangle pattern is "not as defined as silver" but appears ready to confirm a breakout.

"Gold also has somewhat of a triangle that it seems to have broken out of as well," Holtz said. "It's just a little bit behind silver. I expect gold to break out, you know, next couple days."

**Historical Rule:** In a bull market, silver will outperform gold. In a bear market, silver will underperform gold. "It always happens," Holtz stated. "Go back in history—it's pretty much been always."

## Price Targets: Silver and Gold Forecasts

### Silver's Path Forward

Holtz isn't alone in seeing dramatically higher silver prices. Major financial institutions and respected technical analysts have published notably bullish targets:

| Analyst/Institution | Silver Price Target | Timeframe |
|---------------------|---------------------|-----------|
| Bank of America | $320 | 2026 |
| Michael Oliver | $300-$500 | Year end |
| Bill Holtz | $200-$250 | Year end |

While Holtz stops short of the most aggressive forecasts, he maintains confidence in substantial gains. "I don't know if I'd go to $300 bucks, but I certainly think we're going to run through $100, run through the $123 high. And I mean, I could see $200, $250 silver by the end of the year."

### The Silver-to-Gold Ratio: A Key Indicator

One of Holtz's most compelling arguments centers on the silver-to-gold ratio, which measures how many ounces of silver equal one ounce of gold.

- Current ratio: approximately 57-58
- Historical normal range: 15-30 during strong bull markets
- Holtz's target: 25-30

"If we get down to 25 from 25 to 30 from this call 55-60 level, that says that silver is going to double the performance of gold," Holtz explained. "Without a doubt, silver" will outperform gold in this environment.

### Copper's Quiet Strength

One often-overlooked confirmation of the metals thesis comes from copper, which recently reached $6.57 per pound—making new all-time highs without even consolidating.

"Check out the copper charts," Holtz urged. "Silver and copper move in the same direction. They always have."

## Supply-Demand Fundamentals: The Structural Case for Silver

### China's Silver Imports Surge

The fundamental backdrop for silver has tightened considerably. According to Holtz, China imported more silver in March than in any previous month on record. More significantly, as of January 1st, China is exporting zero silver.

"The supply-demand situation has not changed," Holtz emphasized. "If anything, it's gotten tighter."

### Mining Companies Face Chemical Shortages

One of the less-discussed factors affecting silver supply involves the industrial process of extracting metals from ore. Mining companies are experiencing severe difficulties sourcing essential chemicals, particularly sulfuric acid, needed for metal processing.

"You can dig up as much as you want, but if you can't separate the metal out through the chemical processing, you've got a bunch of ore on the pad, but you're not getting silver to market or gold to market," Holtz warned.

This creates a structural bottleneck that cannot be resolved simply by digging more ore—it's a processing and supply chain issue that compounds the already-tight supply situation.

### The AI Connection

Silver demand is also being driven by the technology sector that everyone is watching. "Without silver, there is no AI," Holtz pointed out. Silver is essential in:

- Semiconductor manufacturing
- Circuit board production
- Various electronic components

Additionally, next-generation EV battery technology—particularly solid-state lithium batteries—requires high silver concentrations. "These solid-state lithium batteries they're coming out with now... those are highly concentrated with silver."

## The Inflation Backdrop

### CPI Data Confirms Energy Pressures

At the time of recording, the Consumer Price Index (CPI) showed inflation at 3.8% year-over-year, with energy costs accounting for more than 40% of the headline gain. This confirms that inflationary pressures remain embedded in the economy.

"What you're showing there—if yields go higher, the cost of credit is going to go higher, and it means things can't get refinanced, they can't get rolled over," Holtz observed. "It starts to gum up the system, the real economy."

### Oil's Return to $100+

Oil has rallied back above $100 per barrel, and notably, the inverse correlation that had existed between metals and energy for the previous two and a half months appears to have broken down. "It seems like that correlation has been undone, and now they're moving in tandem again," Holtz noted.

### The 10-Year Yield Threat

The 10-year Treasury yield stands at 4.46% and appears ready to challenge the 4.5% level and potentially push toward 5%. Holtz views rising yields as inherently bullish for hard assets:

"What you're showing there—if yields go higher, the cost of credit is going to go higher and it means things can't get refinanced. It starts to gum up the system, the real economy."

When asked about commodities overall, Holtz pointed to the Thomson Reuters Commodity Index, which has already broken out to the upside. "Everything that we're fed is inflation," he stated. "You want to own real stuff instead of paper stuff."

### A Pattern From History

Holtz draws parallels to the 1970s, when three major waves of inflation reshaped markets. "We've already done one wave of inflation from 2021 up until 2022. And now it seems like we have our second wave of inflation coming down the pike."

## What This Means for Your Portfolio

### Choosing Between Silver and Gold

Holtz distinguishes between two types of investors when making allocation decisions:

**For those seeking price appreciation:** Silver is the clear choice. "If you're looking for the increase in purchasing power—another way of saying the price going higher—silver is going to outperform gold."

**For those storing significant wealth:** Gold becomes necessary due to logistics. "If you sold a business, sold a property, talking about larger sums of money that you want to store yourself, you've got to do it with gold. You can't do it with silver because it's too bulk."

**For beginners:** Start with silver. "When this system goes down, you're going to have to have some silver to borrow with. You'll be able to borrow with gold, but then you're going to end up giving someone credit because how do you get change back from your transaction?"

### The Dollar's Long Decline

Holtz offers historical context that illustrates the long-term case for precious metals. A bag of $1,000 worth of 90% silver dimes or quarters (coin base amounts) that cost $1,000 in 1964-65 now commands $60,000.

"During my lifetime, the dollar has lost 98, call it 98 and a half percent of its purchasing power since I was a little kid," Holtz reflected. "And you know how it happens. It happens slowly, then all at once."

## Looking Ahead: Fed Policy and Market Risks

### The New Fed Chair

With Kevin Warne expected to take over as Fed Chair within the next 10 days, Holtz offers predictions on monetary policy direction:

"I think he wants to lower interest rates. And I think he understands that the only way to keep the game going is to lower interest rates, push more liquidity into the system, and thus try to kick the can further down the road."

This expectation of continued accommodation would be inherently bullish for precious metals—though it risks creating "a bubble on top of the Uber bubble that we're living through now."

### Warning Signs Everywhere

Holtz sees systemic risks accumulating:

- Commercial real estate has "already blown up"
- Student loans represent "the biggest asset on the United States balance sheet"
- Private credit is "blowing up"
- Subprime auto loans continue to build

"The math, if you look at the way the system is set up, the way the fractional reserve system is set up, and the foundation to the financial system being credit—which can default—gold can't default," Holtz observed. "Credit will default because they've piled too much credit on top of too much credit."

Even Michael Burry—the investor famous for profiting from the 2008 housing crash—recently stated he believes the entire market is going to crash, noting things are "just too frothy."

## Key Takeaways

1. **Silver has broken out of a multi-month triangle consolidation** with strong technical indicators (MACD turning up, from oversold conditions) suggesting this move has significant room to continue.

2. **Silver is positioned to significantly outperform gold**, with the silver-to-gold ratio expected to decline from ~57-58 toward 25-30, historically indicating silver will double gold's performance.

3. **Price targets range from $200-$500 per ounce** depending on the analyst, with Bill Holtz conservatively projecting $200-$250 by year end—still representing substantial gains from current levels.

4. **Structural supply deficits support higher prices**: China's record silver imports and export halt, combined with mining companies' chemical processing challenges, create a supply bottleneck that cannot easily be resolved.

5. **The fundamental backdrop remains bullish**: Inflation data confirms energy-driven price pressures, the 10-year yield threatens higher levels, and the commodity index has broken out—all suggesting real assets will outperform paper in the period ahead.
