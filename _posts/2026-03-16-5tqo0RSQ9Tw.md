**March 16, 2026** – The mood in Dubai changed “within a second.” That’s how Florian Grooms, a financial analyst recently evacuated from the UAE, describes the onset of a conflict that has transformed the glittering metropolis into a ghost town. In an exclusive interview with Capital Cosm, Grooms detailed the on-the-ground reality of the crisis and its profound implications for global markets, particularly gold, which has just broken below the critical $5,000 support level.

### A City Brought to a Standstill

Grooms, who witnessed a drone being shot down over the Palm Jumeirah, described a scene of total disruption. "It's devastating. It's a total disaster... a nightmare," he stated. While praising the UAE's defense systems, he painted a bleak picture of the economic aftermath.

"The city is more or less dead," Grooms reported. "People work from home... No more people in the hotels, no more people in the restaurants, the shops don't have any business anymore." He emphasized the fragility of confidence: "It can take years to build trust and it can be destroyed within a moment."

The logistical breakdown is severe. With most international airlines canceling flights, only Emirati and Saudi carriers continue to operate. This paralysis has directly hit a crucial hub of global commerce: the gold market.

### Gold’s Precarious Plunge

Dubai handles an estimated 20-30% of the world's physical gold trade. The disruption there has created a unique and troubling scenario. Grooms explained that traders, often buying on margin, found themselves trapped.

"People were not able to ship the gold anymore. They needed to store it, to insure it, and that meant the costs for them were exploding," he said. This led to a fire-sale dynamic, with gold briefly trading $30-$50 below global spot prices as liquidity dried up.

Grooms has turned decisively bearish in the short term. "I've been warning... We are now in a risk-off scenario," he advised, urging investors to avoid leverage and prioritize capital preservation. He argues that gold, which surged from around $1,800 after the 2024 Gaza invasion to a high of $5,162, has already "priced in" the Middle East disaster. Now, a new leader has emerged: "The new king... is oil."

### The Liquidity Paradox and a Warning from History

His most critical advice is to maintain liquidity, but with a stark caveat. "I think most importantly is holding a very healthy liquidity position—with the disclaimer... that maybe big banks are going bust and then you were not even able to get your money out of the bank," Grooms warned, suggesting bank holidays or closures are a real possibility.

He pointed to the 2008 financial crisis as a historical parallel. "The day that Bear Stearns went bankrupt, gold marked a new all-time high at $1,038. And six months later, it corrected more than 30%... simply because people were running out of cash." In a looming recession, forced liquidation of physical holdings could create a similar downdraft, even amid bullish long-term fundamentals.

Technically, Grooms sees warning signs: a lower high at $5,419 after the $5,162 peak, and now a break below $5,000. He also notes an "open gap" on the charts near $4,350 from early January that the market may still need to fill.

### Risk-Off Across the Board

This caution extends to related assets. Grooms has reduced exposure to mining stocks, fearing they will underperform in a liquidity crunch despite being fundamentally undervalued with gold at $5,000 and silver near $80. "In a risk-off environment... if the liquidity disappears, the story can be as good as possible but people will sell," he reasoned.

The macro picture, he argues, is one of escalating global fracture. With China encircling Taiwan, strikes on Russian refineries, and a de facto energy war underway, the potential for a broader conflict is "extremely high." This sets the stage for an "energy crunch" and a stagflationary environment—ultimately bullish for gold, but only after a potentially severe crisis plays out.

"The best would be just to buy the physical barrels and hide them in your garden. But you can't do this with energy," Grooms noted. "You can do this with gold and silver."

### The American Anomaly and Oil’s Divergence

A curious divergence has emerged in equity markets. Since the conflict began, the S&P 500 is down only 2%, while global and emerging market indices have fallen significantly more. Grooms attributes this to an "American delusion" of energy independence and political messaging, but believes "the longer the war continues, the more painful the awakening probably will be."

Similarly, oil service stocks have sold off despite rising crude prices—a classic "sell the news" reaction after a long rally. Grooms believes a new oil bull market is intact, with physical prices in the Middle East already decoupling from futures, similar to the long-standing gold arbitrage between East and West.

### The Bottom Line: Safety First

For investors, Grooms's message is unambiguous: adopt a defensive posture.

*   **For new buyers:** "If you don't have any physical gold and silver, of course, I urge you to immediately run and buy something." He sees current prices—gold nearly 10% below its high, silver about a third below its $121 peak—as a good entry point.
*   **For existing holders:** "Hold on to your metals." Any major correction should be viewed as a buying opportunity to increase physical stacks.
*   **Overall strategy:** "Protect your capital. Make sure you lose as little as possible... I don't need to play the hero here."

The situation remains fluid and dangerous. "There is so many variables now and unknowns," Grooms concluded. "It's just a nightmare, honestly." In this nightmare, his prescription is clear: prioritize safety, liquidity, and physical possession above all else. The road to $5,000 gold has been shattered by crisis; the path forward is fraught with volatility.
