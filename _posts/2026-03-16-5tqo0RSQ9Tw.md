**March 16, 2026** – In an exclusive interview with *Capital Cosm*, financial analyst and Dubai resident Florian Grooms paints a devastating picture of a city brought to its knees and a global economy teetering on the brink. Having just evacuated from the Persian Gulf, Grooms describes a "total disaster" unfolding in Dubai, with profound implications for gold, oil, and markets worldwide.

### A City Frozen in Fear

The crisis began abruptly. "Two weeks and two days ago on a Saturday afternoon, they suddenly shot down a drone. I was even able to see it because I was hanging out on the palm and it was quite frightening," Grooms recounts.

While he praises the Emirates' defense systems for intercepting most attacks, the psychological and economic damage is irreparable. The mood shifted instantly. Tourists were stranded, residents fled, and the city's vibrant economy ground to a halt.

"The city is more or less dead," Grooms states. "People work from home... No more people in the hotels, no more people in the restaurants, the shops don't have any business anymore." He emphasizes the fragility of confidence: "It can take years to build... and it can be destroyed within a moment."

### Gold Market in Disarray

Dubai is a crucial hub for physical gold, facilitating an estimated 20-30% of global trade. The conflict has shattered this logistics network. With airports repeatedly shut down and most international flights canceled, moving bullion has become nearly impossible.

"We've seen something a little bit similar in the COVID crash in 2020 when oil went negative," Grooms explains. Traders, often buying on margin, were stuck with gold they couldn't ship. The costs for storage and insurance exploded, leading to a fire sale. "For a few days you were able to buy the ounce $30, $40, $50 below the current price in the markets."

This immediate pressure, combined with Grooms' view that gold had already "priced in" Middle East tensions over a two-year rally, has turned him bearish in the short term. He notes gold has broken below the key psychological level of $5,000 and warns of a potential correction toward an open chart gap near $4,350.

### The New Market King: Oil

While gold stumbles, Grooms identifies a new leader: "The new king... is oil who is dictating now the markets." He sees a major dislocation, similar to gold's past, between physical and paper markets. "There is a big gap between the physical price of oil that people have to pay in the Middle East and the futures market price."

He is unequivocal on the direction: "I would be surprised if oil is not moving up again above $100 and probably then also at some point above $120." This surge, however, may not directly benefit related equities. Grooms notes that oil service stocks have recently sold off despite rising crude prices—a classic "sell the news" reaction after a strong pre-war run-up.

### A Global "Risk-Off" Scenario

Grooms' overarching advice is stark: **Risk off.**

"Now we don't know how this will play out... no more leverage, no more short-term trading. Go on the sidelines," he urges. He has reduced exposure to mining stocks and advocates for capital preservation above all else. "The most important thing for the next 12 months: protect your capital."

His caution extends to traditional safe havens. While he advises holding physical gold and silver (calling current prices a "good deal"), he issues a severe warning on liquidity and banks.

"Most importantly is holding a very healthy liquidity position—with the disclaimer... that maybe big banks are going bust and then you were not even able to get your money out of the bank," Grooms says. "It could also be that we simply see bank holidays or bank closures... There's so many things now on the table."

### Connecting the Dots: Recession, Stagflation, and World War

The analyst connects the dots to a frightening big picture. Disrupted shipping in the Strait of Hormuz is causing immediate energy rationing in Asian nations like Thailand and South Korea. Combined with attacks on Russian refineries and the looming threat of conflict over Taiwan—which Grooms says would "end the AI boom"—the world is heading for an "energy crunch."

The probable outcome, in his view, is a global recession that forces leveraged investors to liquidate assets (as happened in 2008), followed by unprecedented money printing. This sets the stage for stagflation—rising prices in a shrinking economy.

"The potential for World War III is like extremely high now, unfortunately," Grooms adds soberly. "Over the last two weeks, more and more countries got drawn into this whole situation."

### The American Delusion

A curious divergence has emerged in markets. Since the war began, U.S. indices have fallen only 2%, compared to deeper drops in global and emerging market ETFs. Grooms attributes this to a dangerous "delusion."

"Americans are still completely delusional here," he argues, suggesting a prevailing belief in U.S. energy independence and political rhetoric downplaying the conflict. "The longer the war continues, the more painful the awakening probably will be."

### Final Advice: Safety First

In a world of extreme uncertainty, Grooms' strategy is defined by patience and prudence.
*   **For metals:** Hold physical gold and silver. Use any major price dip as a buying opportunity to increase your stack.
*   **For markets:** Adopt a risk-off stance. Reduce leverage and speculative positions.
*   **For capital:** Prioritize preservation. Ensure liquidity, but be aware of counterparty risk in the banking system.

"The whole world is such a mess right now," Grooms concludes. "I'm willing to give up some upside here maybe, but just to be safe."

As Dubai—a symbol of globalized commerce—lies paralyzed, its crisis is a stark warning of how quickly interconnected systems can unravel, leaving every investor exposed.
