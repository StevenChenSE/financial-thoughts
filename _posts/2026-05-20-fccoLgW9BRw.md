The China summit between President Trump and Xi Jinping wasn't just another diplomatic photo opportunity—it was a calculated display of shifting global power dynamics. In a wide-ranging interview with host Danny on Capital Cosm, financial analyst Simon Dixon argued that the May 2026 meeting in Shanghai revealed far more than surface-level trade negotiations. From symbolic gestures like Xi sitting in a higher chair to the absence of a red-carpet greeting, Dixon sees a carefully orchestrated repositioning where China presents itself as the rising superpower while America appears to be the declining one.

But beneath this theatrical performance lies a deeper transformation in the global financial architecture—one that may fundamentally reshape how power, capital, and energy flow across borders in the decades ahead.

---

## The Symbolic Politics of State Meetings

Dixon wasted no time identifying the visual messaging embedded in the summit arrangements. The imagery of Xi Jinping seated in a noticeably higher chair than Trump, combined with the absence of an official greeting upon Trump's arrival, spoke volumes about the relationship's new dynamic.

"Xi Jinping didn't meet Trump at the red carpet from the plane," Dixon observed. "I think that's symbolic as well. We had that kind of repositioning of Trump visiting China and China positioning itself as a rising superpower with America looking like the falling superpower."

Yet the summit also revealed something equally significant about America's internal power structure. The U.S. delegation was dominated not by elected officials but by executives from the corporate and financial establishment—representatives from BlackRock, State Street, Vanguard, and Larry Fink. This, Dixon argued, represents "the real power structure of America," which operates through the executives of major corporations serving the interests of the large shareholder class.

The inclusion of Tim Cook from Apple and executives from Boeing alongside financial titans painted a clear picture: the meeting was less about government-to-government diplomacy and more about aligning corporate interests in the AI and robotics transition.

## The Payment Systems Standoff

One element Dixon found particularly revealing was the presence of Visa and Mastercard executives at a summit ostensibly about trade and tariffs. Given China's strict capital controls and historical sensitivity to Western financial penetration, their attendance demands explanation.

"China will never open up its payment systems to America," Dixon stated flatly. "They have capital controls. They guard their financial markets because they suffered that century of humiliation and they know that the western tool for colonization is currency wars and capital market wars."

This historical memory runs deep. China watched how Western financial systems were weaponized against other nations and has deliberately built its own infrastructure—including the CIPS system as an alternative to SWIFT—to prevent dependency. Yet Dixon believes the financial industrial complex has been quietly preparing for a multipolar world regardless.

The strategic goal appears to be establishing interconnected financial centers across multiple regions: through the UAE, Hong Kong, Saudi Arabia (which recently opened its markets), and even Iranian and Venezuelan stock markets being brought back online. This creates hedging mechanisms against single-currency exposure.

"Integrating those payment rails into a new multipolar world is symbolically and strategically important," Dixon explained. "The deals have been negotiated over an extended period—more than a decade, I'd imagine—as we're progressing to this managed transition."

## The AI Infrastructure Arms Race

Perhaps the most technically substantive portion of the conversation concerned the divergent paths of American and Chinese AI development. The numbers tell a striking story: America is building approximately 5,000 data centers while China operates around 500 managing its surveillance infrastructure.

Dixon cautioned that raw numbers can be misleading. "It's more around how much energy do they have," he noted. "China has multiple energy forms—whether it be fossil, coal, LNG, hydro, solar, wind, nuclear—and China is also building out mega-scale infrastructure."

The real game-changer came with DeepSeek, a Chinese AI system that achieved approximately 90% of the capability of the best American AI systems at roughly one-tenth the cost. Even more significantly, DeepSeek now operates with zero U.S. dependency—fully integrated across Chinese chips, hardware, energy infrastructure, and software.

Meanwhile, American valuations for AI infrastructure have reached extraordinary levels. Dixon referenced the upcoming $2 trillion SpaceX IPO and $1 trillion OpenAI valuation alongside an economy requiring massive capital inflows simply to build out AI data centers.

"There's this real valuation mismatch between what's happening in America and what's happening in China," Dixon argued. "The dependency from American elites on China remains profound."

## The Straight of Hormuz: Engineered Crisis and Strategic Benefits

The closure of the Strait of Hormuz emerged as a central thread throughout the analysis. Dixon articulated how this manufactured crisis serves multiple constituencies simultaneously:

- **Russia and Iran** benefit from elevated energy prices and sanction relief
- **American energy exporters** can command premium pricing for oil and LNG
- **European economies** are pushed toward American LNG contracts at higher prices
- **Bond markets** experience pressure that may justify Federal Reserve intervention

"This confirms where I think this is going," Dixon said. "I think everything's taking longer, and I can see how now everyone's benefiting from the strait being closed."

The timing appears deliberately coordinated with major financial events. Dixon suggested that SpaceX and OpenAI IPOs could be timed to coincide with announcements of the strait's reopening, creating a powerful market pump for these massive valuations. "If I'm Goldman Sachs, I don't want the strait open," he quipped. "I want to time the announcement of the opening of the strait with probably post-IPO or with the IPO."

NATO's announced willingness to deploy to the strait by July adds another dimension—a potential deadline that could accelerate deal-making while positioning Europe as a willing participant in American energy export strategies.

## Bond Markets and the Road to the "Big Print"

Dixon walked through the mathematical pressure building in sovereign debt markets. The 10-year Treasury yield was approaching 4.7%, territory he called "capitulation level" for tariff policy. The 30-year yield stood at 5.19%—a level that became "very symbolic throughout the whole Iran war."

Commodity prices reinforced the pressure. Year-to-date, the Thompson Reuters commodity CRB index had surged 33% within just five months, combining with structurally overvalued capital expenditures for AI buildouts.

"We're getting the 1999 dot-com boom and bust with the 2007 financial crisis yields with the 1973 energy crisis," Dixon summarized. "And the stock market is taking all the the value."

The engineering appears designed to create justification for what Dixon calls "the biggest print we've ever seen"—a massive expansion of Federal Reserve bond purchases. The logic follows a clear path:

1. Strait closure strengthens the dollar in DXY terms
2. Higher yields make government debt "unsustainable"
3. "Manufactured crisis" requires emergency spending
4. Federal Reserve purchases bonds to stabilize markets
5. Bond vigilantes are "satisfied" through central bank intervention

This dynamic, Dixon argues, represents the pathway to "fiscal dominance"—where monetary policy becomes subordinate to fiscal needs.

## The Surveillance State and Domestic Threat Assessment

Perhaps the most provocative portion of the interview addressed domestic security concerns. Dixon pushed back against conventional narratives about external military threats, arguing that "the bigger risk to America is not external war but it's internal war."

He connected the ongoing construction projects—including references to the "ballroom" facility with underground military infrastructure—to a broader agenda of population control rather than national defense.

"We're moving to that pre-crime social media scraping arrest based upon predicted behavior, volunteer social credit scores, enforcement through drones, robotics, Terminator-type environment," Dixon described. "That is what the end result of the AI data center buildout is. There is not enough energy in the world to power that. So they're building capacity to power it."

The pattern he identifies follows consistent logic: manufactured domestic crises create justification for expanded surveillance infrastructure, digital ID systems, central bank digital currencies, and algorithmic social control. These domestic measures mirror strategies previously deployed abroad through intelligence agency operations.

"Elites know that the next enemy is the domestic population," Dixon stated bluntly. "And they also want it to be because that's how you get the end game of Bank Secrecy Act, Patriot Act, Genius Act, and now Clarity Act, which provides everything you need."

## Iran's Alternative Financial Infrastructure

The conversation concluded with an examination of how Iran has developed parallel financial systems that circumvent traditional Western dominance. According to Dixon, Iran has built infrastructure that:

- **Circumvents Lloyd's of London** through alternative insurance structures
- **Bypasses SWIFT** using Bitcoin-based transaction systems
- **Eliminates maritime protection dependencies** through multi-signature Bitcoin wallet technology and new insurance contracts

"They're stealing the stable coins because they could call Tether and say freeze them," Dixon explained, referencing how Iran shifted away from dollar-denominated digital assets after experiencing freezes. "They still have their Bitcoin, and they've been mining Bitcoin with nuclear energy to build an internal legal system."

Dixon emphasized that this isn't sanction circumvention advice—"there are serious consequences to violating sanctions law"—but rather an observation of infrastructure being built that represents a genuine alternative to Western financial rails.

China's strategic intent, Dixon believes, is not to elevate Iran as a regional hegemon but to distribute power among UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Iran while maintaining Chinese control through financial and energy corridors. The goal is a weaker yuan that still leverages dollar pricing rails while shifting gold reserves eastward—gold is "migrating from London to the East" as the Chinese system accumulates precious metals backing.

"We're in currency wars," Dixon concluded. "And those currency wars are being determined, and they're not quite the same as the theatrical kinetic wars that make the headlines."

## Key Takeaways

1. **The China summit was fundamentally symbolic.** China used the meeting to visually position itself as a rising superpower while signaling that Taiwan is an inviolable red line. The real power structure—corporate executives from BlackRock, Vanguard, and major tech firms—attended alongside elected officials.

2. **A multipolar financial system is actively emerging.** Visa and Mastercard's presence suggests Western financial institutions are positioning themselves within China's CIPS system and new payment rails across the UAE, Middle East, and beyond—not to open China to Western access, but to gain footholds in an interconnected alternative system.

3. **DeepSeek represents a genuine AI paradigm shift.** China's AI achievement—90% capability at one-tenth the cost with zero U.S. dependency—is fundamentally altering the competitive dynamics. American valuations for AI infrastructure now rest on assumptions that may not survive this competition.

4. **The Strait of Hormuz closure serves multiple coordinated interests.** Higher energy prices benefit Russia, Iran, and American exporters simultaneously while pressuring European and Asian bond markets. The eventual reopening will likely be timed with major IPOs to maximize market pumps.

5. **Currency wars are being waged quietly through financial infrastructure, not military conflict.** The transformation from petrodollar to BRICS energy corridors, the eastward migration of gold, and Iran's Bitcoin-based financial system represent the real battlefield—far removed from the "theatrical kinetic wars" dominating headlines.
