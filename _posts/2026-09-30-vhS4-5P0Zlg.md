## Renowned Economist Steve Hanke Breaks Down Money Supply Acceleration, the Affordability Crisis, and Risks Facing the Bond Market

Long-term U.S. Treasury yields have reached multi-decade highs, with the 10-year yield climbing past 5.28% and the 30-year yield breaching 5.6%. At the same time, consumer sentiment has plummeted to levels not seen in a decade, driven by high mortgage rates, elevated energy costs, and persistent price pressures. 

In a wide-ranging discussion, Steve Hanke, Professor of Applied Economics at Johns Hopkins University, examines the root causes behind persistent inflation, the mechanics driving bond yields higher, the generational shift in housing affordability, and why the secular bull market in gold remains firmly in place.

---

### The U.S. Affordability Crisis and Housing Market Strain

According to Professor Hanke, the primary concern facing the average American household is the worsening affordability squeeze. With median household income hovering around $87,500, a vast segment of the population earning below that threshold faces severe financial strain from basic living costs, housing, and fuel.

The housing market presents one of the clearest examples of this divide:
* **Generational Barriers:** While prior generations could afford real estate purchases early in adulthood, the median age of first-time homebuyers has climbed to a record 40 years old, according to the National Association of Realtors.
* **Shift to Rental Markets:** As high home prices and elevated mortgage rates price buyers out of single-family homes, demand shifts heavily into rental housing, keeping upward pressure on rents.
* **Socioeconomic Repercussions:** Hanke notes that high living and education costs directly affect family planning decisions, contributing to declining fertility rates across OECD economies.

### The Mechanics of Money Supply and Inflation

A central misconception in mainstream financial commentary is that the Federal Reserve tightens monetary policy simply by raising the federal funds rate. Hanke emphasizes that monetary policy is determined by changes in the money supply, not interest rates alone.

```
Monetary Acceleration Breakdown:
------------------------------------------------------
Divisia M4 Growth Rate:     7.4% (vs. 5.6% a year ago)
Hanke's Golden Growth Rate: 6.0% (Target for 2% inflation)
CPI (6-Month Annualized):   5.1%
Producer Price Index (PPI): 5.4%
```

Despite elevated benchmark interest rates, broad money supply measures are accelerating. Hanke explains that private credit creation by commercial banks is expanding rapidly. When banks issue new loans—frequently driven by massive capital expenditure in sectors like artificial intelligence (AI)—they credit borrower checking accounts, thereby generating new broad money ($M2$ and Divisia aggregates). 

Because the Divisia M4 rate is well above Hanke's 6% "golden growth rate," underlying inflation remains elevated across consumer prices (CPI), producer prices (PPI), and personal consumption expenditures (PCE).

### Six Factors Driving Long-Term Bond Yields

Rising yields reflect several compounding structural pressures rather than a single catalyst. Hanke outlines six key drivers behind the upward momentum in Treasury yields:

1. **Persistent Inflationary Pressures:** Elevated money supply growth continues to undermine the purchasing power of fixed cash flows.
2. **Surging Credit Demand from AI:** Capital spending on AI infrastructure and data centers has generated over a trillion dollars in combined debt and equity issuance, competing directly for capital.
3. **Ad-Hoc Economic Policies:** Tariffs and trade restrictions act as direct consumer taxes and distort price structures.
4. **Geopolitical Shocks:** Geopolitical tensions involving Iran and energy refining disruptions in Russia keep energy and diesel costs elevated.
5. **Trade Volatility:** Shifting trade postures toward China create friction and policy reversals.
6. **Erosion of Economic Confidence & Regime Uncertainty:** Constant policy shifts create hesitation, prompting investors to demand higher risk premiums.

When asked about the possibility of a "run on the bond market," Hanke clarifies that rather than a traditional bank run, markets experience a **bond buyer strike**. In this scenario, investors refuse to purchase new Treasury issuance at prevailing rates, forcing the government to offer higher yields to clear debt auctions.

### Asset Allocation: Nominal Bonds vs. TIPS vs. Gold

Given the macroeconomic backdrop, Hanke outlines his perspective on major asset classes:

* **Nominal Treasuries:** Unattractive at current levels due to the six unresolved structural drivers keeping yields elevated and the lack of disinflationary momentum.
* **Treasury Inflation-Protected Securities (TIPS):** A more attractive fixed-income alternative, providing a guaranteed real yield insulated from rising inflation.
* **Gold:** Hanke remains firmly bullish on the yellow metal, viewing short-term pullbacks as standard corrections within a broader secular bull market, with a long-term target between **$6,000 and $7,000 per ounce**.

### Summary

The surge in long-term Treasury yields and persistent inflation are directly linked to accelerating broad money growth and immense credit demand from corporate spending. With nominal bonds facing headwind pressures and consumers squeezed by high living costs, investors must navigate elevated regime uncertainty by focusing on real returns and sound monetary fundamentals.
