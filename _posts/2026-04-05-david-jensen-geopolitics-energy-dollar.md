In a recent conversation on *Monaco 64*, economic analyst David Jensen—author of the *Jensen’s Economic Precious Metals and Markets* newsletter—offered a sobering assessment of the current state of global energy, the weaponization of trade, and the potential for a seismic shift in the role of the U.S. dollar.

According to Jensen, the ongoing conflict in the Middle East is not merely a regional skirmish; it is a systematic dismantling of energy and fertilizer production capacity that is forcing a radical realignment of the global economy.

### The "Burn It Down" Strategy
Jensen posits that the current instability in the Persian Gulf is designed to create a "Petrodollar 2.0." By disrupting energy flows from the Middle East, the United States is effectively forcing Europe into a deeper dependency on American exports.

"America has become 60% of the LNG supply to Europe," Jensen noted. "This further destruction—it’s not just a logistical issue, it’s actual destruction of capacity—is making Europe even further reliant on the United States."

This dependency is being exacerbated by a deliberate "bifurcation" in the insurance market. Jensen points to the work of energy analyst Anas Alhajji, who observed that while oil shipments remained relatively stable, the cost of insuring Liquefied Natural Gas (LNG) tankers through the Suez Canal became prohibitively expensive—rising from 0.25% of the cargo value to as high as 7–12%. This effectively forced LNG traffic to bypass the Suez, routing around Africa and cementing a new, more expensive supply chain controlled by Western interests.

### The Looming Energy Crisis in Asia
While the West navigates this transition, the impact on Asia and Oceania could be catastrophic. Jensen estimates that roughly 10 million barrels of oil per day—nearly 10% of global supply—are currently at risk. 

"If you look at Bangladesh, I think it’s 98% of their oil comes from the Persian Gulf," Jensen explained. "They’ve got under a week of strategic reserves. You’re talking about a country of over 100 million people."

As these nations face a potential collapse in supply, we are already seeing the early signs of a desperate "bidding war" for energy. Jensen described tracking a large crude tanker that, upon approaching India, suddenly changed course to head toward China—presumably because the Chinese were willing to pay a higher premium. 

Jensen warns that as these tankers stop arriving, we should expect the price of oil to skyrocket. "I would expect that you’ll see some prices—like the Murban price—rapidly go through $150 and then $200 a barrel," he predicted.

### The Return of Neo-Feudalism
Jensen views these geopolitical maneuvers through the lens of the World Economic Forum’s (WEF) broader policy objectives. He argues that the systematic destruction of market-based energy independence is a precursor to a "neo-feudal" model.

"I see a push towards digital ID, digital control—the Chinese model—and ultimately, I see coming out of WEF policy is neo-feudalism," Jensen stated. "Where you end up with this underclass... where the asset holders own everything and 80 to 90% of the people hold almost nothing."

Regarding the political landscape, Jensen remains skeptical of populist rhetoric. When asked about Donald Trump’s role in these events, Jensen suggested that regardless of political posturing, the actions being taken—specifically the escalation of conflict in the Middle East—align with a broader agenda of economic breakdown. "This guy is burning it all down," Jensen said. "It feeds right into the WEF implementation of social and economic breakdown and the introduction of universal basic income, digital currency, and social credit scoring."

### Gold, Silver, and the End of the Dollar
As the trust in the U.S. dollar as a medium of exchange wavers, Jensen believes we are witnessing a return to "hard" money. He notes that barter trade is already emerging in the energy sector, with countries swapping crude for refined products. He expects this to expand into the precious metals market.

"I still see the silver market as being the most stressed market that there is out there," Jensen said. With combined vault holdings in London, Shanghai, and New York totaling only about 160 million ounces available to the market, the supply-demand imbalance is reaching a breaking point.

"I wouldn’t be surprised to see settlement—payment in terms of silver—in some cases," Jensen added. "Gold bars have an incredible wealth density in a very small place... it’s a very short step to taking gold bars in payment."

As the world watches the 20-to-40-day window for tanker transit close, Jensen suggests that the coming weeks will be a turning point. For those watching the markets, the message is clear: the era of cheap, reliable energy and dollar-denominated stability is being replaced by a volatile, fragmented, and increasingly physical-asset-based global economy.
