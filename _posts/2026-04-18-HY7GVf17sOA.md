## The Illusion of Peace: Inside the Islamabad Negotiations

Two days after diplomatic talks in Islamabad and roughly a day after the US Navy established its blockade of the Strait of Hormuz, geopolitical analyst Simon Hunt offers a sobering assessment from his base in Dubai. The markets may be celebrating the ceasefire, but Hunt remains unconvinced—and he's not alone.

"This is only a pause," Hunt told Danny on Capital Cosm, April 14th, 2026. "We are heading into a much bigger escalation that will not peak for several weeks, if not a few months, when both sides will realize that the costs of escalation outweigh the costs of defusing the situation."

According to sources within the Iranian delegation, what transpired in Islamabad was less a genuine peace negotiation and more a tactical time-buying exercise. Vice President JD Vance was reportedly on the telephone constantly to Washington, with moments when he appeared close to reaching some form of agreement—only for those discussions to be "squashed" following a subsequent conversation.

The most revealing detail from Hunt's sources: President Trump proposed sharing control of the Strait of Hormuz with Iran. The offer was never going to be accepted. The negotiations ended abruptly, with the American delegation boarding their plane back to Washington without warning the Iranian side. Sources suggest Jared Kushner and Richard Grenell (referred to as "Wikoff" in the transcript) remained behind.

"The discussions were purely an opportunity to buy time," Hunt explained. "Before the discussions took place in Islamabad, both sides wanted a pause in order to replenish and locate their forces in places where they wanted them to be."

## The Real War: Control of Energy and the Future of Global Order

What appears to be happening extends far beyond the current conflict. Hunt identifies two overarching objectives driving Washington's approach.

"Trump wants control of Iran's energy," Hunt stated. "If he got control of Iran's energy, then he's got control of the Gulf."

The second objective is perhaps even more significant. "If he achieved that objective, it probably dismantles the BRICS group of nations because Iran is a pivotal hub—a pivotal logistical hub—for the BRICS group of countries."

This represents nothing less than a contest over the future world order: a unipolar world led by America, or a multipolar world increasingly shaped by China, Russia, and their partners. "What's happening in Iran is a precursor for the bigger blowoff later on," Hunt observed.

## China's Strategic Hand

Hunt believes China played a crucial role in bringing Iran to the negotiating table, but not for reasons of peace. Days before the Islamabad talks, Beijing flew in three massive cargo planes carrying sophisticated equipment.

"They wanted time to install that before the war continued," Hunt explained. The irony is sharp: America and Israel subsequently bombed the North-South railway corridor that China financed and built—part of the infrastructure Iran uses to export oil to Beijing.

Despite this provocation, China needed time to position its assets. The ceasefire served everyone's immediate interests while everyone reloaded for the next phase.

## The Uranium Question: 5 Years vs. 20 Years

The latest reports from diplomatic circles reveal the enormous gap between the two sides' positions. Iran offered to cease uranium production for five years. President Trump demanded twenty years. The chasm between these positions is not bridgeable.

"I think it's posturing," Hunt said bluntly. "Both sides want time."

For Iran, that time allows for the integration of new military equipment from Russia and China and the repositioning of forces. For America, it means continuing the massive troop movements into the region—a deployment Hunt describes as "not a cheap exercise."

Hunt expects the next major assault to target Bandar Abbas, Iran's key port city. "If I know it, Iran knows it," he noted, suggesting Tehran is already taking precautions.

## The Gulf States' Impossible Choice

Perhaps the most underreported dimension of this conflict involves the Gulf States, particularly the UAE, which is Iran's second-largest trading partner after China.

"If America attacks Iran with Israel, we are probably going to see Iran attacking the Gulf States, especially American assets," Hunt warned. "There are some major American assets here in the UAE such as data centers."

Should Gulf states continue supporting American operations while simultaneously trading with Iran, the response will be swift and devastating. "If the attacks on Iran continue and the Gulf states continue to support America, then Iran will attack the oil installations with hypersonic missiles. That's the final endgame."

The rulers of the Gulf face an existential calculation. "At some point, the rulers have a choice to make. Do we still bow to America, or do we start kneeling towards Iran?"

Hunt believes Iran doesn't want this outcome. "They would much rather have peace with the Gulf States, but they will go down that line if America continues to attack their country."

In Dubai, the immediate reaction to the ceasefire told its own story. "The UAE flags were flown everywhere. There were parties and people celebrating," Hunt recounted. "The word was going around about opening restaurants. I kept my mouth shut. But I'll be surprised if we don't see an escalation."

## The Economic Aftershock: How War Reaches Your Wallet

With the conflict now in its fortieth day, the economic consequences are only beginning to materialize. The latest Producer Price Index data shows wholesale prices up 0.5% for March alone, and 4% year-over-year—the largest twelve-month gain since February 2023.

"This is just the beginning," Hunt cautioned. "The Fed preparing for a possible $2 trillion credit market collapse centered on opaque private credit ecosystem where pensions, insurers, and funds are parked."

Energy prices ripple through everything. "Whether it be diesel, whether it be helium, whether it be sulfuric acid—all these things are embedded into global society. It's not just oil."

Sulfuric acid connects directly to fertilizers and agricultural production. With approximately 30% of global fertilizer supplies affected by Gulf disruptions, food prices are poised to surge. "Food prices are going to soar. Everything is going to rise if this war continues beyond a few weeks."

The ceasefire offers little relief. "Only 15% of normal [traffic] has gone through the straits," Hunt noted. Maritime traffic is rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope to the Gulf of Mexico, with over 120 ships arriving there yesterday compared to the usual twenty. The longer routes translate directly into higher prices for consumers.

Oil has already moved from the pre-war level of $65 to $94 per barrel—a $30 premium baked into every barrel. Hunt expects this to climb much higher. "I would be buying oil at these prices believing it's probably going to go to $150 or higher."

The benefits flow not to ordinary Americans but to energy companies. "With inflation rising as it already is, and you're approaching the big driving season in America, the American people won't benefit."

## The Financial Reckoning: $38,000 Gold and the Collapse of the Western Monetary System

A gold expert Hunt respects has published projections of gold reaching $38,000 per ounce—likely in the 2030-2032 period. This isn't merely a price forecast; it's an indictment of the current monetary order.

"We're talking about the complete collapse of the western monetary system," Hunt explained. "It's not gold itself. It's what gold reflects as real money. At $38,000 gold, the currency is worthless. It's the end of the fiat monetary system."

China and Russia, Hunt noted, know what's coming. "They will link their currencies to gold. They have the gold to do it."

The timing of military escalation may be tied to financial events. "The big serious money are sellers into this market and have been. That's probably when they'll be compelled to launch the greater war—once that happens."

## Europe Prepares for What Comes Next

While attention focuses on the Middle East, Europe is quietly transforming. Hungary's recent election saw Viktor Orbán's fifteen-year dominance end, with a new young government ascending. Hunt sees limited change in Hungary's fundamental orientation.

"Relations with Russia will probably not change much," he predicted. "The relationship with Brussels will be better because Hungary wants EU money, which is basically debt money anyway."

More significant is the broader European pivot. "It seems the EU is getting ready for war because you've had the UK government explain they need to transition into wartime footing. The Canadian government said something similar as well."

The pattern is clear: "The Anglo sphere as well as the Europeans have been prepping themselves for a big war. They need a war to distract attention from their own problems—debt, immigration, and the electorate's hatred of Brussels. War is needed."

## A Practical Prescription for Uncertain Times

Hunt's advice is direct: focus on the essentials.

"If you have a garden, make some of the garden into a kitchen garden. Keep your deep freeze filled up. If you're investing, invest in companies involved in the production and distribution of food."

This isn't paranoia. It's pattern recognition. From Hunt's vantage point in Dubai, watching troops move and alliances shift, the trajectory seems clear.

"We are going to have an escalation," he concluded. "The gaps between what Iran wants in its ten-point plan and Washington's fifteen-point plan are just too large to bridge. This is all part of the buildup to a much bigger war—whether it's 2028 or 2030, who knows—which will decide the future of the world."

For now, the celebration in Dubai may have been premature. The ceasefire that looked like peace may simply be the eye of a storm that hasn't yet finished building.
