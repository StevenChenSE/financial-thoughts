Two days after Islamabad negotiations between Iran and the United States, and roughly a day after the US Navy blockade of the Strait of Hormuz began, geopolitical analyst Simon Hunt offers a sobering assessment: the ceasefire is temporary, and a much larger escalation is inevitable.

"I'm sticking to my guns, which have been for weeks, if not months, that we are heading into a much bigger escalation," Hunt said during an appearance on Capital Cosm. "That will not peak for several weeks, if not a few months, when both sides will realize that the costs of escalation outweigh being outweighed by defusing the situation."

## Inside the Failed Islamabad Talks

According to intelligence sources within the Iranian delegation, what transpired during the Islamabad negotiations reveals both sides were playing a calculated game of stalling.

"Vance was on the telephone constantly to Washington," Hunt explained. "There were moments when he was close to coming to some form of deal, but then following a conversation, it got squashed."

The most striking revelation: President Trump proposed sharing control of the Strait of Hormuz with Iran—a proposition Hunt says Iran would never accept. The discussions ended abruptly, with the American delegation boarding planes back to Washington without notifying their Iranian counterparts. Unverified reports suggest Kushner and Wikoff remained behind, adding to the air of mystery surrounding the collapsed talks.

Despite rumors circulating about imminent deals and a return to negotiations, Hunt dismisses such speculation. "I don't think that's at all likely because it's not just America supporting Israel."

## The Strategic Endgame: Iran's Energy and BRICS

According to Hunt, two fundamental issues drive Washington's approach. First, following American moves in Venezuela and increased presence in Nigeria, Trump wants control of Iran's energy resources. "If he got control of Iran's energy, then he's got control of the Gulf."

Second, and perhaps more significantly, controlling Iran would likely dismantle the BRICS grouping of nations. "Iran is a pivotal hub, a pivotal logistical hub for the BRICS group of countries," Hunt noted.

This explains the massive troop movements into the region and increased military aircraft presence—actions Hunt describes as anything but cheap. "That's not a cheap exercise."

## China and the North-South Corridor

Hunt also shed light on China's role in the escalating tensions. Days before Iran agreed to the ceasefire, China flew in three massive cargo planes carrying sophisticated equipment, likely seeking time to install the systems before resuming hostilities.

The irony, Hunt pointed out, is that America and Israel subsequently bombed the massive railway China built and financed as part of the North-South Corridor—an oil railway Iran uses to export oil to China. "That in itself tells you a big story."

## The Ceasefire: Pure Posturing

Both Tehran and Washington claim legitimate negotiating positions, but Hunt sees through the rhetoric. Reports emerged that Iran offered to cease uranium production for five years, while Trump demanded twenty—a deal-breaker on both sides.

"I think it's posturing," Hunt stated flatly. "Both sides want time."

Why? Iran needs time to integrate new equipment from Russia and China and reposition forces. Washington, meanwhile, is positioning for what Hunt believes will be the main assault: an attack on Bandar Abbas.

"If I know it, Iran knows it," Hunt noted. "So they're positioning themselves or repositioning themselves."

The unusual rhythm of this conflict—fighting for twelve days, then pausing for extended periods—follows historical war cycle patterns, according to Hunt.

## The Gulf States' Impossible Choice

Perhaps most concerning is the position of regional allies. Many observers noted the curious response in Dubai when the ceasefire was announced: UAE flags flew everywhere, and people celebrated as if normalcy had returned.

But Hunt remained skeptical. "I kept my mouth shut, but I think I'll be surprised if we don't see an escalation."

The situation presents an impossible dilemma for Gulf States. While the UAE and other GCC countries represent Iran's second-largest trading partner after China, American military assets saturate the region, including major data centers.

"If America attacks Iran with Israel, then we are probably going to see Iran attacking the Gulf States, especially the American assets," Hunt warned. "And if the attacks on Iran continue and the Gulf states continue to support America, then Iran will attack the oil installations with hypersonic missiles. That's the final endgame."

At some point, regional rulers must choose: bow to America or kneel toward Iran. Hunt believes Tehran prefers peace with Gulf States, but will follow that path if attacks on Iranian territory continue.

## Economic Shockwaves: The Real Crisis Is Coming

The economic ramifications extend far beyond stock market reactions. The Producer Price Index (PPI) for March showed a 0.5% monthly increase and 4% annual gain—the largest twelve-month increase since February 2023. With PPI being a leading indicator of consumer prices, the worst is yet to come.

Hunt draws parallels to the 1973 oil shock. "It took one year before markets fully assimilated and recognized the shocks that the change in energy prices will have on everything. And that's when the market started falling."

The ceasefire has provided minimal relief. "With the ceasefire, how much has gone through the straits? Very little. Fifteen percent of norm," Hunt noted. Maritime traffic is rerouting around Cape Horn, with over 120 ships entering the Gulf of America yesterday compared to the usual twenty, dramatically increasing shipping costs and final product prices.

"It's not just energy," Hunt emphasized. "It's the products of oil. Whether it be diesel, whether it be helium, whether it be sulfuric acid—all these things are embedded into global society." Sulfuric acid affects fertilizers and agricultural production, with roughly 30% of such output coming from Gulf-region refineries. "Food prices are going to soar. Everything is going to rise if this war continues beyond a few weeks."

Who benefits from the conflict? Hunt is direct: "They won't be the American people because inflation will, as it is already happening, will be rising—and you're approaching the big driving season in America."

## Oil at $94: A $30 War Premium

At the time of recording, WTI crude traded at $94 per barrel. Just two months earlier, before hostilities began in late February, oil sat at $65. "Got a $30 premium attached to it," Hunt observed. "Would you invest in oil at this price?"

While declining to offer formal financial advice, Hunt made his position clear: "If I was putting money on the table, whether it's today or in a week's time or a fortnight's time, I would be buying oil at these prices believing it's probably going to go to $150 or higher."

His investment thesis extends to food production and distribution companies. "I would certainly be buying anything to do with food. That would be my primary purchase."

## The $2 Trillion Credit Collapse

Beyond oil and commodities, Hunt sees structural fragility in global finance. Reports indicate the Federal Reserve is privately preparing for a possible $2 trillion credit market collapse centered on the opaque private credit ecosystem, where pensions, insurers, and funds are parked.

"It's all coming at a time when the G7 monetary system is imperiled," Hunt noted. "It's just the beginning of what? The beginning of a financial eventual collapse."

The Fed and G7 central banks will attempt to prop up markets, Hunt believes, but serious institutional money sees through such maneuvers. "The big money, big serious money are sellers into this market and have been. And that's probably when they'll be compelled to launch the greater war is once that happens."

The timing, Hunt suggests, points to 2028.

## $38,000 Gold and the Death of Fiat Currency

Gold continues its upward march, trading at approximately $4,800 per ounce at time of recording. Hunt referenced a forthcoming report from a gold expert projecting the yellow metal will reach $38,000—"probably in the 2030-2032 period."

Such a price "in itself is telling you about the demise of the western monetary system," Hunt stated. "We're not talking about gold itself. It's what gold reflects as real money."

At $38,000 gold, currencies would effectively become worthless—a complete collapse of the fiat monetary system. Hunt noted that China, Russia, and Iran understand this trajectory, which explains their moves to link currencies to gold. "They have the gold to do it."

## Hungary's Election: A European Microcosm

Shifting to European politics, Hunt offered thoughts on Hungary's recent election, where Prime Minister Viktor Orbán's party lost its majority for the first time in sixteen years.

"Any party that's been in office for a long period of time, corruption becomes a big issue," Hunt observed. "The guys who supported that party are sixteen years older. And you have a new young group who have backed the new prime minister."

Despite expectations of improved relations with Brussels, Hunt predicts minimal substantive change. "Relations with Russia will not change much. Relations with Brussels will be better because Hungary wants the EU's money, which is basically debt money anyway."

## Everything Points to War

Hunt closed with a stark assessment of Western preparations. "It seems like the EU is getting ready for war because you've had the UK government explained that they need to transition into a wartime footing. The Canadian government said something similar as well. It seems like the Anglo sphere as well as the Europeans have been prepping themselves for a big war."

The reasoning is cynical but pragmatic: "They need a war to distract attention from their own problems—debt, immigration, and the electorate's hatred of Brussels. War is needed. So really everything is pointing to war. I'm sorry to say."

## Hunt's Final Word: Prepare

For those seeking actionable advice, Hunt keeps returning to fundamentals: "If you have a garden, make some of the garden into a kitchen garden. Keep your deep freeze filled up. And if you're investing, invest in companies involved in the production and distribution of food."

The Islamabad ceasefire, the celebrations in Dubai, the optimism in markets—Hunt sees it all as temporary illusion. This conflict, he believes, represents merely a precursor to the larger confrontation that will determine whether the world remains unipolar under American leadership or becomes multipolar, led by China, Russia, and Iran.

That defining moment, in Hunt's analysis, arrives by 2028.
