---

Two days after the Islamabad negotiations between Iran and the United States, with the US Navy blockade of the Strait of Hormuz now in its second day, the world watches with bated breath. Markets are surging on ceasefire optimism. But is this really the beginning of peace—or merely a strategic pause in an escalating conflict?

Simon Hunt, geopolitical analyst based in Dubai, isn't buying it.

"I'm sticking to my guns, which have been for weeks, if not months, that we are heading into a much bigger escalation that will not peak for several weeks, if not a few months, when both sides will realize that the costs of escalation outweigh being outweighed by defusing the situation," Hunt told Capital Cosm host Danny on April 14, 2026.

---

## What Really Happened in Islamabad

According to intelligence sourced from a member of the Iranian delegation, the Islamabad talks were never genuine peace negotiations. They were a tactical timeout.

"Before the discussions took place in Islamabad, both sides wanted a pause in order to replenish and locate their forces in places where they wanted them to be," Hunt explained. "Therefore, the discussions were purely an opportunity to buy time."

The details are revealing. Vice President Vance was on the telephone constantly to Washington throughout the proceedings. There were moments when negotiators appeared close to a deal—but each time, following a conversation with someone back in the US, the breakthrough collapsed.

The most striking revelation: President Trump reportedly wanted to cut a deal over the Strait of Hormuz with Iran—essentially sharing the spoils of one of the world's most critical chokepoints. Iran, predictably, would never accept such terms.

The discussions ended abruptly, with the Iranian delegation given no warning. Vance boarded his plane back to Washington, though unverified reports suggest Jared Kushner and Richard Grenell (Wikoff) remained behind.

## The Real Objectives: Energy and BRICS

So what is Washington really after? Hunt sees two interconnected goals that explain the escalating military buildup.

"Having taken Venezuela, having made America's presence known in Nigeria, Trump wants control of Iran's energy," Hunt stated. "And if he got control of Iran's energy, then he's got control of the Gulf."

But this isn't just about oil. It's about geopolitics on a grand scale.

"If he achieved that objective, then it probably dismantles the BRICS group of nations because Iran is a pivotable hub, a pivotable logistical hub for the BRICS group of countries."

This explains why the ceasefire feels hollow. Reports of massive US troop deployments and increased air assets flooding into the region aren't cheap posturing.

"That's not a cheap exercise," Hunt noted dryly. "So I think that we are going to have an escalation."

The disconnect between the diplomatic theater and military reality wasn't lost on observers in Dubai. Hunt described the scene when the ceasefire was announced:

"The UAE flags were flown everywhere. There were parties and people celebrating. Here in this hotel, the word was going around—'We're opening restaurant X and restaurant Y.' I kept my mouth shut, but I think I'll be surprised if we don't see an escalation."

## China's Calculated Move

Iran had been in the driver's seat until agreeing to the ceasefire. The question: why would they concede ground when they held the advantage?

"The irony is that America and Israel bombed the huge railway which China built and financed, which is part of the north-south corridor," Hunt revealed. "That after oil railway—that's the railway that Iran uses to send its oil to China."

China had flown in three massive cargo planes with sophisticated equipment just days before the negotiations. They wanted time to install it before the conflict resumed.

"I think it was buying time because China had flown in three massive cargo planes with sophisticated equipment and they wanted time to install that before the war continued."

Meanwhile, the latest reports indicate Iran offered to cease uranium production for five years—but Trump demanded twenty, making agreement impossible.

"I think it's posturing," Hunt assessed. "Both sides want time."

For Iran, that means integrating Russian and Chinese equipment, repositioning forces, and preparing for what Hunt believes will be the main assault: an attack on Bandar Abbas.

"If I know it, Iran knows it," Hunt said. "They're positioning themselves or repositioning themselves. It won't be K Island or any of the other islands which have been reputed to have been the center of attack."

## The Gulf States' Impossible Choice

Most observers miss a critical dimension of this conflict: the UAE and other GCC countries are Iran's second-largest trading partner after China.

This creates a geopolitical paradox. While supporting American operations, these nations face an existential threat.

"If America attacks Iran with Israel, then we are probably going to see Iran attacking the Gulf States, especially the American assets," Hunt warned. "And there are some major American assets here in the UAE such as data centers."

And if attacks on Iran continue while the Gulf states support America?

"Then Iran will attack the oil installations with hypersonic missiles. That's the final endgame."

At some point, Hunt believes, the rulers face a binary choice: "Do we still bow to America, or do we start kneeling towards Iran?"

He doesn't think Iran wants this outcome. "They would much rather have peace with the Gulf States, but they will go down that line if America continues to attack their country."

## The Economic Aftershock

The conflict's economic consequences are already unfolding—and they're just beginning.

The March 2026 Producer Price Index showed wholesale prices up 0.5% monthly, with a 4% annual gain—the largest twelve-month increase since February 2023. Energy ripples through everything: diesel, helium, sulfuric acid, fertilizers.

"Energy goes into everything," Hunt emphasized. "It's not just oil. Sulfuric acid—what does that mean for fertilizers, urea, etc. About 30% comes out of the Gulf. It's going to impact food prices. Food prices are going to soar. Everything is going to rise if this war continues beyond a few weeks."

The ceasefire hasn't provided relief because oil transit through the Strait remains at only 15% of normal. With tankers just beginning to arrive at refineries, the full impact hasn't yet hit consumers.

"The norm is $95," Hunt noted. "Got a $30 premium attached to it. Oil was at $65 before the war started in late February."

Shipping is already rerouting around the Cape of Good Hope to the Gulf of America, with 120+ ships arriving there yesterday versus the usual 20. Longer routes mean higher costs that will eventually flow through to every consumer good.

"With the ceasefire, how much has gone through the straits? Very little. 15% of norm. We're starting to see some rerouting of maritime traffic into the Gulf of America. As a result, 120-some odd ships were coming into the Gulf of America yesterday as opposed to the usual which is around 20."

"Who benefits from all this? Is it the American people or the oil companies?" Danny asked.

"They won't be the American people because inflation will, as it is already happening, will be rising," Hunt replied. "And you're approaching the big driving season in America."

## The Financial System Under Siege

Beyond commodity prices, Hunt sees structural fragility in the western financial system.

"We're also getting reports of the Fed now privately preparing for a possible $2 trillion credit market collapse centered on opaque private credit ecosystem where pensions, insurers, and funds are parked."

This comes at a time when the G7 monetary system is already imperiled.

"It's all coming at a time when the G7 monetary system is imperiled. It's just the beginning."

"The beginning of what?" Danny pressed.

"Eventual financial collapse," Hunt said matter-of-factly. "The Fed will do its best and other G7 central banks would do their best to prop the markets up, but the big money will see through it. The big money—big serious money—are sellers into this market and have been. And that's probably when they'll be compelled to launch the greater war is once that happens."

The timeline: 2028.

## $38,000 Gold and the Death of the Monetary System

Gold has been on a remarkable run. But according to Hunt, we've seen nothing yet.

"We're sending out a report tomorrow based on what a real gold expert is saying that gold will reach a peak of $38,000."

The timeframe: likely 2030-2032.

This isn't merely a bullish commodity call. It's a statement about the future of money itself.

"What we're talking about is the complete collapse of the western monetary system," Hunt explained. "It's not gold itself. It's what gold reflects as real money. That pretty much means the currency's worthless at $38,000 gold."

"It's the end of the fiat monetary system, which is what China and Russia know is coming and why they will link their currencies to gold. They have the gold to do it."

For Hunt, this is the real story beneath the headlines. "That in itself is telling you about the demise of the western monetary system."

## Hungary and the European Calculus

Away from the Middle East, political shifts in Hungary offer a window into broader European calculations.

The opposition party swept to power, ending Viktor Orbán's sixteen-year dominance. Hunt sees this as generational change more than ideological revolution.

"It's like any party that's been in office for a long period of time—corruption becomes a big issue. And the guys who supported that party are sixteen years older. And you have a new young group who have backed the new prime minister."

Elite families had been quietly shifting their support. The new government will improve relations with Brussels—for one simple reason: "Hungary wants the EU's money, which is basically debt money anyway."

But broader European preparations for conflict are underway. "It seems like the EU is getting ready for war because you've had the UK government explained that they need to transition into a wartime footing. The Canadian government said something similar as well."

"War is needed," Hunt concluded. "They need a war to distract attention from their own problems—debt, immigration, and the electorate's hatred of Brussels. War is needed. So really everything is pointing to war. I'm sorry to say."

## The Real Endgame

Hunt's framework is clear: what's happening between America and Iran isn't a contained regional conflict. It's a proxy battle in a larger struggle that will define the coming decades.

"This is all part of the buildup to a much bigger war that will happen—whether it's 2028 or 2030, who knows—which will then decide the future of the world: whether it's a unipolar world led by America, or whether it's the multipolar world led by China, Russia, and probably Iran."

"What's happening in Iran is a precursor for the bigger blowoff later on."

The pattern of this war—start, stop, pause, resume—follows historical cycles of great power competition. "It's a very odd war. You fight for twelve days, take a six-month break. Read history."

The peaks and valleys serve strategic purposes. Both sides use pauses to reposition, rearm, and reassess before the next escalation.

"I think the war will reach such a peak that both sides will want a period of calm before you get to the next big pivotable moment," Hunt explained. "Whether it's in a few weeks or a few months, I have no idea. But I think we'll reach that peak and then we will have a period of defused momentum, and then you hit the next big peak—whether 2028 or sometime between 28 and 2030."

## Preparing for What's Coming

For those watching these developments, Hunt offers practical guidance.

"I come back to my constant theme: food. If you have a garden, make some of the garden into a kitchen garden. Keep your deep freeze filled up. And if you're investing, invest in companies involved in the production and distribution of food."

The investment thesis follows: oil to $150 or higher. Food producers and distributors. Gold and silver as real money when the system finally breaks.

And as for today's prices, which might seem expensive to some?

"If you're buying at $4,800 gold, it may not seem that cheap today, but if we get $38,000 gold, I mean, you're going to be kicking yourself at these prices."
