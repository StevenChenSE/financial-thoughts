The global financial landscape is shifting dramatically as the Chinese technology sector experiences a severe downturn, while US investors remain heavily invested in the "AI trade." Macro strategist David Woo joins *David Woo and Bound* to dissect the fragile fundamentals behind major AI firms like Anthropic, the geopolitical risks surrounding Iran, and the political headwinds facing the Federal Reserve. This analysis explores why the most crowded trade in history may be approaching a breaking point.

## The Chinese Canary in the Coal Mine

A significant divergence has emerged between US and Chinese tech markets. While the NASDAQ continues to hold steady or rise, China’s **STAR 50 index**, which tracks the 50 largest science and technology companies in China, has plummeted by approximately 40% in recent months. This crash occurred despite reports that China has narrowed the gap with the US in artificial intelligence capabilities.

David Woo argues that this divergence stems from fundamentally different strategies. The US approach, heavily promoted by President Trump, aims to control the "choke points" of the AI supply chain to ensure a winner-takes-all outcome where a few dominant players capture all future profits. In contrast, China’s strategy relies on intense internal competition among roughly 20 major AI model developers and numerous chip makers. China believes this fierce competition will drive innovation regardless of who ultimately wins.

Woo posits that the Chinese crash is a leading indicator for the US market. The NASDAQ valuation currently assumes that AI will produce a winner-takes-all scenario, but Woo bets on **commoditization**. As the number of competitors increases and technology becomes more accessible, margins will compress, undermining the valuations of companies that rely on monopoly-like profits.

## Anthropic’s IPO and Financial Reality Check

Amidst this backdrop, AI giant **Anthropic** filed for an Initial Public Offering (IPO). Woo scrutinized the company's financial statistics to reveal a stark reality behind the hype:

*   **Revenue:** $16.29 billion in the first half of 2026.
*   **Operating Loss:** $693 million (an improvement from the $2.1 billion loss in H1 2025).
*   **Net Loss:** $18.7 billion.
*   **Operating Cash Flow:** Negative $3.9 billion.

The most alarming statistic cited is the slowing growth rate. While Anthropic reported a ~300% quarter-on-quarter growth rate in Q2, leaked data suggests this dropped to around 45% in Q3. Furthermore, data from **RAMP**, an AI spending management company, indicates that total token spending across all models had zero growth in August and September.

Woo challenges the proposed $2 trillion valuation for Anthropic. To justify such a valuation compared to profitable giants like **TSMC** or **Meta**, Anthropic would need to reach approximately **$1.2 trillion in revenue by 2030**. Given that total worldwide software spending in 2025 was only $1.2 trillion, this projection is highly unrealistic. The core issue is a "margin squeeze": while demand for AI tokens grows, prices are falling due to commoditization, while compute costs (GPU rental rates) are rising due to supply constraints and data center construction delays.

## Hyperscalers: Fear Over Economics

Major hyperscalers like **Amazon**, **Microsoft**, **Google**, and **Meta** have transitioned from capital-light social media or cloud businesses to heavy infrastructure development models, resulting in negative free cash flow. Woo argues these companies are driven by the "fear of missing out" rather than pure economics. They cannot afford to be left behind if AI proves transformative.

However, the debt market is becoming increasingly cautious. **CoreWeave** bonds recently traded at 15%, signaling investor skepticism. With an estimated $500 billion in new AI-related bonds expected in 2027, and reports from Elon Musk that ~200 "neoclouds" no longer have access to debt markets, the financing environment for AI infrastructure is tightening. This capital constraint could force a slowdown in data center construction, further impacting chip demand and valuations.

## Geopolitical Risk: Iran, Oil, and Elections

The second major pillar of Woo’s analysis concerns oil and geopolitical instability. He asserts that Trump is highly likely to authorize military action against **Iran** before the US midterm elections. Key factors driving this probability include:

1.  **Israeli Election Dynamics:** The Israeli Chief of Staff recently blocked a proposed strike on Iran due to timing concerns relative to Israel's election. With the Israeli election concluded, this obstacle is removed. Woo predicts that regardless of whether **Netanyahu** wins or loses, the probability of a US/Israel strike on Iran increases significantly.
2.  **Trump’s Political Calculus:** With polls suggesting Democrats will dominate the midterms, Trump has little political capital to lose. A pre-election strike could serve as a legacy-defining act. Additionally, Woo notes that Trump’s recent diesel deal with Putin indicates a willingness to sacrifice Ukraine interests to focus on Iran.
3.  **Oil Market Impact:** Even without closing the Strait of Hormuz, oil prices remain high due to elevated freight costs (up to $40/barrel for risk premiums) and low global inventories. A direct strike on Iran could spike oil prices further, triggering inflationary pressures that force the Federal Reserve to maintain tighter monetary policy, which is detrimental to tech stocks.

## Market Sentiment and Technical Warning Signs

Despite deteriorating fundamentals, the AI trade remains the most crowded position in global markets. A Goldman Sachs report highlighted that long-short systematic, fundamental, and multi-strategy hedge funds all suffered their worst days since the early pandemic era during a recent NASDAQ dip, indicating high correlation and lack of diversification.

Technically, Woo points to warning signs:
*   The NASDAQ 100 has broken through key trend line support.
*   It has fallen below both the 50-day and 100-day moving averages.

Woo suggests that while sentiment currently holds the market up, any significant negative catalyst—such as a spike in oil prices or further evidence of AI revenue deceleration—could trigger a rapid unwind. Interestingly, he notes a potential inverse relationship: if high oil prices cause an AI selloff, bond yields might fall (rallying bonds) as investors seek safety, though this dynamic is complex given the correlation between real yields and tech valuations.

## Global Implications: Brazil and France

Woo also touched on other global markets that serve as diversification plays or potential future crises:
*   **Brazil:** Following President Lula’s re-election and Bolsonaro’s concession, Brazilian assets sold off. Woo views this correction as a buying opportunity for an idiosyncratic trade uncorrelated with AI or oil.
*   **France:** Woo expresses deep concern about France’s social unrest, predicting it will become a major story in 2027 leading up to the next presidential election. He describes the situation as having "no parallel in modern history," suggesting Europe faces significant political instability ahead.

## Conclusion

The convergence of slowing AI revenue growth, rising compute costs, and tightening debt markets suggests the US AI trade is vulnerable. With China’s tech sector already crashing 40% under similar competitive pressures, Woo warns that Wall Street may not be far behind. Coupled with high geopolitical risk regarding Iran and oil prices, the balanced risk for investors is starting to turn significantly to the downside.
