The financial world is buzzing with anxiety as the Nasdaq has dropped approximately 19% from its October peak. By most traders' definitions, a 20% drop constitutes a bear market. Are we there yet? In an interview at Ben Cowen’s "Into the Cryptoverse" conference, Jason Pizzino, founder of TIAinvesting.com, offers a nuanced perspective on where we stand in the broader economic cycle and what signals investors should watch for.

## The 18-Year Economic Cycle Framework

Pizzino bases his analysis on a long-term economic cycle that has repeated consistently for approximately 220 years. This cycle spans roughly **18 years**, characterized by about 14 years of upward momentum followed by four years of decline.

The mechanics of this cycle are heavily influenced by credit expansion and interest rates. A critical component is the value of US land, which often pumps up asset prices generally. Pizzino notes that until a "land-led recession" occurs, stock market prices tend to continue rising. However, he points out that US land prices have already topped in real terms—meaning when adjusted for inflation, they have stopped increasing. This is a significant leading indicator suggesting that the stock market may be nearing its own top.

## Stock Market Topping Process and Retail Behavior

Currently, Pizzino believes the broader market (S&P 500 and Nasdaq) is in a "topping process." While recent lower lows have caused worry, he suggests we might see a bounce where retail investors rush in, believing they are buying at a discount. This behavior often leads to a temporary rally that creates a "higher high" or a "lower high," confusing the narrative before the market ultimately rolls over.

Pizzino emphasizes that technical analysis is crucial here. He looks for specific levels where sentiment shifts from buyers to sellers on a larger scale, not just day-to-day movements. Currently, selling volume remains very high, and there is no clear floor yet. He advises against trying to "buy the bottom dollar" and instead recommends waiting for signs of accumulation, such as reduced media noise, smaller price ranges, and buying volume outweighing selling volume.

## Global Real Estate Divergence: Australia vs. the US

The conversation highlights a stark contrast between global real estate markets. Pizzino, who is from Australia, points out that Australia is currently experiencing its fastest real estate crash in at least 40 years, with seven consecutive months of declines across capital cities. This decline was triggered by a May budget change altering tax benefits for property investors, combined with cooling demand.

In contrast, the US real estate market behaves differently due to structural differences:
*   **Mortgage Structure:** The US has prevalent 30-year fixed-rate mortgages, insulating many homeowners from rising interest rates. Australia and Canada typically have shorter fixed periods (e.g., five years), leading to higher refinancing costs and more fluidity in the market.
*   **Demand Dynamics:** In the US, mortgage applications are at historic lows, yet prices remain resilient partly because owners with low-rate mortgages are reluctant to sell, keeping supply off the market.

Pizzino predicts that while the US real estate correction may not be as severe as Australia’s current crash or the 2008 crisis, it is still part of a broader topping phase. He notes a divergence in US cities: some have corrected for four years since their 2022 peaks, while others are only now beginning to decline, leading to a balancing out of national averages.

## Commodities and Bitcoin: The Late-Cycle Run

Pizzino views gold and silver as assets that typically run late in the economic cycle. He believes they have peaked for now, with gold reaching highs in early 2026 before falling significantly (from $5,500 to around $3,500). Historically, metals can take years or even decades to bottom, often range-trading during that period. Consequently, Pizzino is currently staying away from precious metals for the short term.

Regarding Bitcoin, Pizzino rejects the notion that it trades identically to gold ("digital gold"). He views Bitcoin as a higher-risk asset that struggles in "risk-off" environments with expensive money and high interest rates. If a larger recession occurs, he doubts Bitcoin will break its previous bull market top of $126,000. While he might consider short-term trades if Bitcoin drops to the $30,000–$40,000 range (offering better risk-reward), he remains cautious at higher valuations like $59,000, citing insufficient upside potential relative to downside risk.

## Investment Strategy: Patience and Accumulation Signals

So, what should investors do? Pizzino suggests that cash is currently performing well, outperforming many assets when accounting for inflation and opportunity costs. For those looking for opportunities, he points to specific real estate markets where prices have been sideways or down for several years, particularly in areas like Melbourne where properties are trading below replacement costs.

The key to timing the bottom is watching for **accumulation**. Signs include:
1.  **Less Media Noise:** The asset falls out of the news cycle.
2.  **Smaller Price Ranges:** Volatility decreases compared to peak levels.
3.  **Volume Shifts:** Buying volume begins to exceed selling volume, indicating that investors are absorbing supply.

Pizzino warns against the psychological trap of assuming a 50% drop means the bottom is in; drops can easily extend to 70% or 80%. He advises using the 18-year cycle as a roadmap rather than a precise timing tool, especially for those considering leveraged investments near the end of a cycle.

## Conclusion

We are likely in the final stages of a long economic upswing. While the Nasdaq’s 19% drop is alarming, Pizzino argues that the true top may not yet be confirmed by technical accumulation signals. Investors should remain patient, watch for shifts in volume and sentiment, and be wary of retail-driven bounces that often precede deeper corrections. Whether in stocks, real estate, or crypto, understanding where we are in the 18-year cycle is essential for navigating the current market environment.
