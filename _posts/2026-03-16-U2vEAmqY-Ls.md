In an era defined by regional instability and economic uncertainty, investors are increasingly seeking safe havens and strategic plays to protect and grow their wealth. Daryl Thomas, host of the VR Media YouTube channel and an active investor, recently shared his insights on navigating these volatile times. His top picks? **Gold** and a select group of **oil royalty and land-leasing companies**.

## The Golden Thesis: More Than a 2020 Story

Thomas began accumulating physical gold in 2020, spurred by the economic shutdowns and a growing understanding of monetary history. While that initial investment has appreciated significantly—gold is up roughly **80% over the past 12 months**, dramatically outperforming the S&P 500—he believes the core reasons for holding gold have only intensified.

“The biggest thing to understand is that the global monetary order is changing significantly,” Thomas says, pointing to sustained central bank buying as a critical signal. “They are doing this for a reason.”

For Thomas, gold is not a trade but a permanent form of savings—"real money" outside a financial system he views as controlled by political elites. He advocates viewing “gold as money itself, and fiat currencies as currencies.”

*   **When would he sell?** Not based on a specific dollar price. Instead, he looks at relative value: “If the Dow to gold ratio goes to one, I may sell some gold for the Dow.” His commitment is long-term, driven by a belief that massive government debt and deficits make fiat currency devaluation inevitable.

*   **Addressing volatility:** When gold plunged 20% after briefly touching $5,500 earlier this year, Thomas wasn’t shaken. He saw the preceding $1,000 monthly surge as an anomaly. “That’s not common for gold… I just kind of wrote it off as like a anomaly.”

## Oil Plays: Owning the "Toll Booths," Not the "Cars"

With oil prices sensitive to geopolitical conflict, Thomas avoids chasing major producers that have already seen big run-ups. Instead, he employs a “toll booth” strategy inspired by investor Benjamin Demazi.

“Think about it as owning the roads and the toll booths, rather than owning the cars that are driving on the roads,” he explains.

His oil exposure comes through:
1.  **Royalty Companies:** Like **Viper Energy**, which earn royalties from oil production without bearing extraction costs.
2.  **Land-Leasing Companies:** Such as **Texas Pacific Land** and **Landbridge**, which own land and infrastructure leased to drillers. “You’re pretty much paying the landlord to operate as a business.”
3.  **Diversified Royalties:** He holds **Franco-Nevada**, primarily a gold/silver royalty company, for its oil royalty segment.

This approach offers exposure to oil prices with less direct operational risk.

## Inside the Mining Ecosystem

Thomas also invests in gold mining equities, with a focus on the royalty/streaming sector for lower risk. For junior explorers—the riskiest part of the market—he has a three-year horizon and a meticulous selection process:
*   **Follow the "Big Money":** Tracking investments by well-known financiers like Rick Rule.
*   **Assess Management:** “What’s the track record of the CEO?”
*   **Evaluate Jurisdiction:** Prioritizing projects in safe mining regions.

## The Economic Reality: A "K-Shaped" Divide

Beyond charts and tickers, Thomas points to tangible economic strain. He describes a “K-shaped economy” where wealth disparity is widening.

“I definitely see the pain on the streets… higher cost of living, wages not keeping up with inflation,” he observes, contrasting today’s environment with the lower debt and stronger dollar that benefited previous generations.

This underlying fragility reinforces his hard asset philosophy. While some analysts, noting gold’ historical patterns of sharp corrections after double-tops, advocate waiting for a pullback, Thomas cautions against trying to time the market.

“People said, ‘Oh, it’s going to pull back’ at $3,000 and $4,000… At what point does someone think, okay, you can’t time tops or bottoms?”

## The Bottom Line

Daryl Thomas’s strategy is a blend of conservative principle and calculated risk. He anchors his portfolio in permanent, non-sovereign money (gold) and uses strategic equity investments (royalties, select miners) to compound wealth. His outlook is built on a macro view of enduring debt, currency devaluation, and geopolitical instability—factors he believes make traditional financial assets increasingly vulnerable. For investors navigating today’s volatility, his approach underscores a classic adage: own the essential infrastructure of value, not just its fleeting representations.
