In an era defined by regional instability and economic uncertainty, investors are increasingly seeking safe havens and strategic plays to protect and grow their wealth. Daryl Thomas, host of the VR Media YouTube channel and an active investor, recently shared his insights on navigating these volatile times. His top picks? **Gold** and a select group of **oil royalty and land-leasing companies**.

## The Golden Thesis: More Than a 2020 Story

Thomas began accumulating physical gold in 2020, spurred by the pandemic-era shutdowns and a deepening understanding of monetary history. Influenced by figures like Robert Kiyosaki and authors detailing the "power of gold," his thesis was straightforward: own "hard money" outside a financial system controlled by elites and prone to bailouts.

But what about today, with gold having surged approximately **80% in the past 12 months** (dramatically outperforming the S&P 500's 16%)? For Thomas, the reasons for holding have only intensified.

"The biggest thing to understand is that the global monetary order is changing significantly," he says, pointing to sustained central bank buying as a critical signal. "They are doing this for a reason... I've been following central banks buying gold since 2022."

He views gold not as a trade, but as **permanent savings**—a way to trade fiat currency for "real money." His approach is strategic:
*   **Physical Gold:** He has no price target for selling his core physical holdings. He may only consider swapping gold for other assets if key ratios, like the Dow-to-gold ratio, reach specific extremes (e.g., a ratio of one).
*   **Gold Equities:** This is where he seeks to "maximize fiat savings." He focuses on miners and the royalty/streaming sector, which he finds less risky than pure exploration juniors.

When challenged on gold's volatility—noting its **20% single-day drop after briefly touching $5,500**—Thomas remained unfazed. He attributed the January spike, where gold rallied nearly "$1,000 in a month," to unusual froth. "That's not common for gold... I just kind of wrote it off as an anomaly."

## The Oil Play: Owning the "Toll Booths," Not the "Cars"

With oil prices sensitive to geopolitical strife, Thomas avoids chasing major producers that have already seen significant run-ups. Instead, he applies a "toll booth" philosophy inspired by investor Benjamin Demazi.

"I'm looking at some of the oil royalties and such," he explains. His strategy involves investing in companies that own the infrastructure and land, leasing it to drillers. This model generates revenue with less direct operational risk.
*   **Franco-Nevada:** Provides exposure through its oil royalties.
*   **Viper Energy:** A pure-play oil royalty company.
*   **Landbridge & Texas Pacific Land:** Companies that lease land to oil operators.

"How do they make money? Well... the oil companies that do the drilling... have to lease the land. So, you're pretty much paying the landlord," Thomas clarifies.

## On the Ground: Observing a "K-Shaped Economy"

Beyond charts and tickers, Thomas sees economic strain firsthand. He describes a **"K-shaped economy"** where generational disparities are stark.

"I have some friends who... are struggling to make ends meet," he shares, noting wages lagging behind inflation. He contrasts this with the boomer generation, which "grew up in an America that had way less inflation... Home values were significantly cheaper. Government debt and deficits were a lot lower."

This reality reinforces his macro outlook. He believes relentless deficit spending is politically unavoidable. "Politicians always want to print. They always want to overpromise... To try to... balance the budget, to cut entitlements... That's political suicide."

## Investor Profile & Final Thoughts

Thomas characterizes his style as "conservative/risky." He balances a desire to compound wealth over time with strategic allocations to higher-risk junior exploration companies—where he follows "the big money" and management track records—and a three-year time horizon.

His core message is one of **monetary sovereignty**. "It's all about becoming my own central bank," he states, advocating for assets governments can't print. While he continues to buy gold even at higher prices, believing the long-term debt trajectory is unsustainable, he acknowledges the tactical wisdom of dollar-cost averaging. "You could even buy a little bit and still hold some liquidity and then if we do get a sell off... load up the boat even more."

In a world of regional instability and currency debasement, Daryl Thomas’s portfolio is built not on speculation, but on the foundational principles of owning real assets and collecting rents from essential industries.
