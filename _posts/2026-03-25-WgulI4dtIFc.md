In a world of escalating conflict, soaring gold prices, and financial instability, geopolitical forecaster Martin Armstrong offers a sobering long-term view: the United States and its allies are on an unsustainable path toward a sovereign debt crisis, fueled by endless wars and political short-sightedness.

In a recent interview on Palisades Gold Radio, Armstrong, founder of Armstrong Economics, argued that the dominant trends of the next decade will be defined by this looming fiscal reckoning and the geopolitical miscalculations driving it.

### The Neocon Legacy and the Debt Spiral

Armstrong traces the root of the crisis to post-World War II borrowing and the foreign policy of the neoconservative movement. "The vast majority of the national debt has been largely to fund all these neocon endless wars," he states, listing Vietnam, Korea, and Iraq. This borrowing, he asserts, has been done with "no intention of paying anything back."

He illustrates the point with a historical anecdote about the Iraq War. "Cheney back then said in an interview that going into Iraq, 'oh no, it's only going to be a matter of weeks, not months.' Turned out to be eight years." This pattern of underestimation, he warns, is repeating with Iran.

The consequence is a Ponzi-like rolling of national debt. "Our interest expenditures this year have exceeded military [spending]. That's not going to stop. All right. The end of this is a sovereign default. And it's happened many, many times throughout history."

### Gold: The Barometer of Uncertainty, Not Inflation

This environment, Armstrong explains, is why gold is rising. "Gold goes up because of uncertainty." He recalls 1979-80, when gold spiked from $400 to $875 in six weeks following the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan. "I've learned over the years... somebody knows when war is going to start. There's always insiders... and they start to move their money in advance and that's what the computer picks up."

His proprietary models, which analyze capital flows, have repeatedly flagged major events. He cites the Lebanese Civil War, the start of the Iran-Iraq tanker war, the 1998 Russian collapse, and more recently, the Hamas attack on Israel. "You saw defense stocks moving in advance," he notes, drawing a parallel to post-9/11 investigations where "they were looking for people who bought puts on airlines, you know, a few days before."

### The Iran Miscalculation: Igniting a Religious War

Armstrong warns that the current confrontation with Iran is fundamentally misunderstood by policymakers. He distinguishes between Sunni and Shia Islam, noting that about 40% of Saudi Arabia's population is Shia. "My warning has been that this can easily turn into... a religious war and that makes it much more serious."

He draws a direct parallel to historical tactics of creating external enemies to consolidate power. "The Ayatollah [Khomeini] brilliantly took the embassy, called it 'the great Satan'... And then anybody that opposed him, he could then say, 'Oh, you're an agent of the great Satan.'... This is how you silence opposition."

By attempting to decapitate Iran's leadership, Armstrong argues the West is falling into this very trap. "You deliberately went to kill the Ayatollah [and] proves the very thing that they used to retain power." He reveals that after a 2025 attack, Iran reorganized its government with "four layers of redundancy," making regime change a protracted affair. "The computer is definitely showing this is not something like Venezuela... This is going to be a long, dragged-out affair... for two to three years."

### Oil, Strategic Reserves, and Political Myopia

On oil markets, Armstrong sees sustained upward pressure. While the U.S. gets only about 3% of its oil from the Middle East, Europe is highly vulnerable. He criticizes the depletion of the U.S. Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR) for political gain. "Biden unleashes the strategic reserve just for the election... That's, you know, you're personally using national assets for your own benefit."

With the SPR at historic lows and U.S. imports at roughly 6 million barrels per day, he dismisses the idea that reserves can suppress prices. "Any idea that the strategic reserves will be able to suppress prices is a pipe dream."

### The Weaponization of Finance and the Flight to Gold

A key theme is the dangerous weaponization of the global financial system. Armstrong details how the SWIFT payment network was coerced into becoming a tool of sanctions. "Obama first tried it back in 2014... They refused. They said, 'You're not going to turn the system into a weapon.' So what did they do? They replaced the head of SWIFT in 2019 and he does whatever he's told."

This move, he argues, backfired spectacularly. By threatening Russia and, by extension, China, it accelerated the move toward alternative financial systems like BRICS. "China said, 'Oh, okay, fine. Thank you.' And they create BRICS. It's geopolitical to prevent somebody like Blinken and these neocons from trying to use the world monetary system to hurt them."

This is a primary driver behind central bank gold buying. "China has to know... If we're going to go to war, you never own the debt of your adversary... So, they've been stockpiling gold. And so, it's neutral." He adds that gold's appeal is also rooted in the stability of the U.S. dollar, which, unlike European currencies, is never canceled. "70% of our paper dollars are outside the country is because we've never canceled our currency."

### The Inevitable Revision and the Dollar's Fate

Looking ahead, Armstrong foresees a complete revision of the monetary system around 2032. He doubts a return to a fixed gold standard, which he calls inherently flawed. "You can't fix the price of gold and not the quantity of dollars. Obviously, it's going to go bust."

The dollar's reserve status, he concludes, is less about government policy and more about America's role as the world's premier consumer economy. "Everybody's got to sell their products here. So when you do that, you still have to price it in dollars."

Ultimately, Armstrong presents a stark forecast: driven by debt and conflict, the world is marching toward a sovereign default crisis. The rising price of gold is not a speculative bubble but a cold, logical signal from the global capital markets—a warning that the era of endless war and endless debt is reaching its mathematical and historical limit.
