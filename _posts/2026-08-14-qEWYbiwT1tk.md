## How Liquidity, Geopolitics, and AI Are Shaping Markets

Markets have surged to new all-time highs, with gold climbing above $4,400 and major indices like the S&P 500, Dow Jones, and NASDAQ rallying strongly. In a recent interview, Clem Chambers, founder of FM Returns, explained that these moves are driven by aggressive liquidity injections from the US Treasury and Federal Reserve, which are designed to stabilize markets and fund massive re-industrialization and AI buildout efforts.

According to Chambers, while gold and silver remain excellent long-term inflation hedges, speculative opportunities lie elsewhere—particularly in copper and the broader AI value chain. He also highlighted the geopolitical importance of an upcoming meeting between President Xi Jinping and US leaders, the strategic situation around Iran and the Strait of Hormuz, and why passive investing or excessive hedging may leave wealth behind.

## Liquidity Injections and Market Behavior

Chambers emphasized that recent market recoveries are not organic but engineered through liquidity. “Every time something crops up that’s unpleasant, out comes somebody, pulls a lever, pumps in the liquidity, gets it back up again, and sets it off on that straight line again,” he said.

He pointed to historical patterns: during both of Trump’s terms, market dips triggered by tariffs or geopolitical tensions were followed by V-shaped recoveries once liquidity was reintroduced. This pattern has continued today, with bailouts and interventions aimed at preventing systemic stress—most recently involving Japan and Asian markets.

Chambers warned that while these injections temporarily stabilize markets, they inevitably fuel inflation. “They’re going to print a lot of money going forward. So much money and that’s going to cause elevated inflation. That’s all good for gold,” he noted, estimating inflation could reach 5–7% as the US attempts to re-industrialize and compete with China.

## Gold and Silver: Hedges, Not Speculative Plays

With gold recently spiking above $4,400, many investors wonder if it will reach $8,000. Chambers remains skeptical in the short term. “When I look at it, I just see a pause… I’m on the record saying $3,500, I’ll start to be interested,” he said, noting that gold is better suited as a long-term store of value than a speculative instrument.

He praised gold and silver as ideal for “stackers” and those seeking protection against inflation but cautioned against overhyping them as get-rich-quick assets. “No one ever got rich hedging. And in fact, it’s expensive,” he said, pointing to the high cost of options-based hedges compared to potential portfolio returns.

Instead, Chambers suggested that speculative investors focus on equities and commodities directly tied to the AI and energy buildout, where liquidity is flowing most aggressively.

## Copper: The Next Vertical Moonshot

Chambers’ standout prediction is for copper. He expects a “vertical moonshot,” potentially doubling in price within a year. His reasoning is straightforward: electrification demands are outstripping supply.

“Lots and lots of electrification… And AI is huge amounts of electrification,” Chambers explained. To catch up with China’s energy capacity, the West needs a 250% increase in electricity infrastructure—requiring vast amounts of copper for pylons, transformers, and cables.

New copper mines take 10–20 years to develop, creating a structural supply deficit. “It’s going to go ape, isn’t it?” he said, comparing copper’s chart pattern to previous commodity spikes that saw rapid vertical moves followed by corrections.

He also noted that commodities tend to rotate through “hot” phases—cocoa, nickel, potatoes—and copper is next in line. “We’ve had silver spike, we’ve had gold spike, we’re going to get copper vertical moonshot,” he concluded.
