In a recent interview with Investing News Network, Alex Ecarian, co-founder of Allegiance Gold, made a compelling case that gold’s current bull market is far from over. Drawing on his background in finance and personal experience with currency instability, Ecarian outlined the structural and cyclical forces that he believes will drive gold—and silver—significantly higher in the coming years.

### From Wall Street to Physical Gold
Ecarian’s journey into the gold industry is rooted in firsthand experience with financial fragility. “I was born and raised in Beirut, Lebanon, and at a very young age, I experienced a civil war and what currency fluctuation meant,” he shared. He recalled his parents exchanging currency for physical gold, a lesson that resonated deeply during the 2008 financial crisis. “That was a stark reminder that you can't just depend on the system.”

After a 12-year career in banking, including a stint at Smith Barney (later Morgan Stanley), Ecarian co-founded Allegiance Gold, a full-service physical precious metals dealer focused on education and transparency. “Wall Street doesn't want you to know a lot about it… governments don't want you to own a lot of it,” he noted, explaining his shift to the physical metals space.

### Gold’s Bull Cycle: Still in the Early Innings
Ecarian situates the current gold cycle as beginning in 2020, when governments worldwide unleashed unprecedented monetary stimulus. “We sacrificed the currency to save the economy,” he said. He views gold bull markets historically as 10- to 12-year cycles, suggesting we are only in the “fourth or fifth inning.”

Key to his thesis is the behavior of central banks, which he calls “the insiders of the money game.” He highlighted that central bank gold buying has surged from an average of 400–600 tons annually to over 1,000 tons in recent years. A World Gold Council survey indicated that 79% of central banks plan to increase their gold reserves further. “Why? Because it's a neutral asset… it's not something that could be seized,” Ecarian explained.

With gold rising from roughly $1,500 in 2020 to about $5,000 today, he believes the second half of the cycle accelerates faster. The fundamental drivers—massive deficit spending, growing debt, and persistent central bank buying—remain firmly in place.

### Geopolitics, Inflation, and the Fed’s Dilemma
Addressing why gold hasn’t spiked higher amid Middle East conflict, Ecarian argued that gold is currently responding more to monetary policy expectations than geopolitics. “Gold is simply responding to the fact that the Federal Reserve might not lower interest rates as anticipated,” he said.

He pointed to a “trifecta” of inflationary pressures:
1.  **Higher oil prices**, with crude rising from about $50 to over $100 a barrel.
2.  **Increased food costs**, as one-third of global fertilizers transit the Strait of Hormuz.
3.  **Sustained military spending** for a war originally forecast to cost $50–255 billion.

“We are essentially printing money to pay the deficit,” Ecarian stated, noting that U.S. interest payments alone approach $1 trillion annually. These factors, he believes, will force the Fed to delay rate cuts, creating a headwind for gold in the very near term but setting the stage for much higher inflation.

For 2026, Ecarian estimates that the $50 oil price surge could add 0.5% to 2% to inflation figures, though he cautions that official metrics are often misleading. “The average consumer knows by now that inflation is much, much higher than that.”

### Cracks in the System and the Stagflation Risk
Ecarian sees mounting stress beneath the surface of the economy. He highlighted downward revisions to GDP and jobs data, cracks in the private credit market, and weakening corporate balance sheets. “The bond market is also signaling some weakness,” he said, particularly in BBB-rated debt.

When asked about stagflation, he replied, “Yes and no.” While government military spending could artificially boost GDP, underlying consumer weakness and high inflation could create a stagflationary environment. He is closely watching Q1 GDP data and consumer spending trends for confirmation.

Regarding the upcoming Fed meeting, Ecarian expects no policy change but will scrutinize the commentary for concerns about trade, jobs, and banking sector stability. “The banking sector for me is where the cracks will start showing,” he said.

### Price Targets: $10,000 Gold and $100 Silver
Ecarian encourages investors to look beyond short-term volatility. “Gold is still up year-to-date about 17%… in the last year, gold is still up about 65%.” His long-term forecast is ambitious: “By 2030–2032, we could very well see price between $10,000 to $12,000.”

He is equally bullish on silver, forecasting it could “challenge the three-digit level” and reach around $100 within the next year. He noted silver’s recent volatility—from $96 before the Middle East conflict to around $80—is a healthy consolidation. Fundamentals remain strong, with structural deficits, robust industrial demand, and declining visible inventories.

Ecarian also sees potential for a decoupling between the physical and paper silver markets, driven by a squeeze on physical supply. “It's not a matter of paying attention to the short-term price volatility; it's more about positioning.”

### Final Word: Positioning Outside the System
Ecarian’s overarching message is one of prudence and long-term preparation. With recession risks rising and systemic vulnerabilities growing, he advocates holding physical precious metals as a form of financial insurance. “How do I position myself that in the event something happens, at least I have something that is really outside of the system.”

For investors, his analysis suggests that the current pullback may be a buying opportunity within a much larger, multi-year bull market—one that he believes is just getting started.
