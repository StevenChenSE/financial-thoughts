## A Remarkable Juncture for the Precious Metals Sector

The precious metals sector is currently at a historic turning point, driven by record-breaking gold prices and a structural supply deficit in silver. Gold briefly touched US $5,400 per ounce in January 2026, nearly doubling from the previous year, fueled by record central bank buying exceeding 850 tons and a global flight to safe-haven assets amid geopolitical uncertainty and dollarization trends. Although prices have pulled back modestly to the $4,800–$4,900 range, gold continues to outperform major asset classes. Meanwhile, silver is entering its sixth consecutive year of supply deficit, with industrial demand from EVs and photovoltaics competing with monetary buyers, prompting City to set a $110 target for the second half of the year.

This macro environment has reshaped conversations across the industry, from financing and investor sentiment to exploration priorities. In a recent ITV webinar, industry leaders shared their perspectives on navigating this landscape, highlighting key developments in British Columbia, Quebec, and New Zealand.

The discussion featured a heavyweight panel of mining executives and investment professionals:

*   **Robert Eford**, Chief Executive Officer of **Ruer Gold**, leading exploration and development in New Zealand’s historic gold fields.

## Macro Dynamics: Gold Stability and Silver’s Industrial Surge

Peter Espig noted that while expectations have shifted dramatically—with $4,000 gold once seen as ecstatic, $5,600 is now met with caution—Nicola Mining focuses on profitability at a $2,000 gold price, treating everything above as "gravy." This disciplined approach keeps them in a low-cost tier, maximizing benefits from current highs.

Sean Kungan highlighted the compelling story of silver, noting that average silver producers are now making $40–$50 million on every million ounces produced. With industrial demand accounting for 50% of silver consumption (up from 10% decades ago) and supply dropping from 1.2 billion ounces to 800 million annually, a significant deficit is driving prices. Kungan emphasized that mining companies are generating record cash flows, creating incentives to explore and develop rather than just produce.

## Project Updates and Strategic Developments

### Nicola Mining: Diversifying Revenue Streams
Peter Espig reported that Nicola commenced mining operations at the Dominion project in early July, processing high-grade gold material through the Merit Mill. Assays from surface veins exceed one ounce per ton. Additionally, Nicola is reopening the Treasure Mountain Silver Mine, with mining expected within 12 months. The company also completed its first drill hole at the Craigmont copper project, revealing visible native copper at 840 meters. Espig outlined a strategy of generating cash flow from gold and silver to fund copper exploration, avoiding dilution and leveraging permitted assets for rapid development.

### Laflura Minerals: Turning Orphans into Assets
Cal Mali discussed Laflura’s acquisition of assets from Monarch Mining, including the Beacon Gold Mill and the Swanson Gold project. Initially considered too small, Swanson has been transformed through aggressive land acquisition and drilling. Recent assays returned 2.95 g/t over 80 meters and 2.37 g/t over 88 meters, with the system wide open at depth. Mali aims to expand the Swanson resource to over one million ounces this year. Furthermore, Laflura is negotiating a $30 million off-take agreement with Trafigura, a major commodity trading firm, to support the development of the Beacon Mill and Swanson project.

### Ruer Gold: Critical Minerals and Fast-Track Permitting
Robert Eford highlighted Ruer Gold’s fast-track decision process in New Zealand, which could permit the Reefton project in just six months. The project is not only a gold story but also a significant antimony play, with 9,000 tons of antimony expected over the mine life. Eford noted that antimony, designated a critical mineral by the US, provides political leverage and project certainty, even if its price volatility makes gold the primary economic driver. The company expects to submit a mine permit by October, starting the six-month permitting clock for a Q2 2027 target.

### Contango Silver and Gold: M&A and Cash Flow Generation
Sean Kungan detailed Contango’s merger with Dolly Varden, combining a cash-flowing gold mine with a high-grade silver resource. The merged entity holds over $100 million in cash and generates $100 million annually in free cash flow. Kungan’s strategy is to leverage this cash flow to develop the Kitsault Valley flagship asset and the Lucky Shot mine, aiming for 5 million ounces of annual silver production and 200,000 ounces of gold. The company is also exploring other high-grade projects in the Northwest to expand its footprint.

## Investment Strategies and Market Outlook

Chris Kurlo of CG Wealth Management emphasized a "barbell approach," allocating 80% of assets to stable major producers and 20% to emerging opportunities. He stressed that geology and jurisdiction are paramount, though high-quality discoveries can be found in higher-risk jurisdictions with proper due diligence. Kurlo noted that institutional inflows are returning to the sector, with mining generating 30% margins, outperforming tech. He advised retail investors to avoid behavioral biases like fear and greed, accumulating positions during weakness and focusing on companies with strong management teams and geological potential.

Robert Eford added that competition for quality projects has intensified, with institutions sitting on surplus cash but remaining selective. He warned that if a company’s stock is flat despite a bullish commodity cycle, investors should look for red flags. Quality projects that create value through exploration and permitting tend to attract financing and outperform peers.
