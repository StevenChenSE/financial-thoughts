### The Global Diesel and Refining Bottleneck

The distress among freight operators stems directly from the ongoing energy and diesel crisis. While crude oil prices remain elevated, the primary pressure point lies in refining capacity and the widening crack spread. 

Refineries are under extreme pressure to optimize yields:
1. **Product Switching:** Shifting capacity toward diesel production reduces gasoline and jet fuel output, driving up consumer prices at the pump and forcing airlines to trim flight schedules.
2. **Winter Competition:** In colder regions, diesel competes directly with home heating oil, which shares an almost identical chemical profile, setting the stage for elevated heating costs in the fourth quarter.
3. **Infrastructure Limits:** Strategic Petroleum Reserves (SPR) globally and within the US are operating near historic lows. Drawing reserves below critical levels creates operational risks due to required pipeline and storage pressurization thresholds.

Geopolitical friction—including attacks on energy infrastructure in the Middle East and shifting export policies among major producers such as Russia and Saudi Arabia—continues to restrict the availability of distillates globally.
