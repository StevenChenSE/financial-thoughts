While the Fed claims economic activity is expanding and inflation is under control, market indicators tell a different story. ## The Math of Debt: An Unsolvable Equation

Bill Halter emphasizes that the Fed’s decisions are largely irrelevant at this stage. "The math says the credit markets are headed for a crackup," Halter states. The core issue is the sheer volume of debt, which has become unserviceable in the long term. In the past, adding $1 of debt to the system generated $3 to $4 in GDP. Today, that same dollar of debt yields less than 50 cents in GDP growth. This diminishing return means that borrowing more money no longer fuels economic expansion but instead masks a contracting economy.

David Morgan agrees, noting that the Fed is trapped in a corner. Raising rates makes debt servicing impossible, while lowering rates accelerates the decline of the dollar and triggers a rush into gold. "There is no way out at this point," Morgan says. The system is relying on "extend and pretend" tactics, but the underlying mathematics point to an inevitable train wreck.

## The Rotation into Real Assets

As confidence in paper assets wanes, a significant rotation of capital is underway. David Morgan highlights that central banks have been aggressively accumulating gold, a "stealth move" that has preceded the current retail and institutional interest. This rotation is largely coming from US Treasuries and other debt instruments as investors seek tangible assets that cannot default.

Morgan notes that gold has already outperformed the S&P 500 and real estate significantly over the last 25 years. "If you know what the ending is, prepare for it ahead of time," he advises. The ending, according to both experts, is the collapse of fiat currency and the credit bubble. In such a scenario, gold and silver represent the purest and safest forms of money.

The relationship between oil, gold, and silver remains critical. Halter points out that oil prices are destined to rise in the long term, driven by geopolitical tensions in the Middle East and the high cost of energy for mining operations. Higher oil prices signal higher inflation expectations, which further erode the purchasing power of fiat currencies.

Halder also addresses the misconception of deflation versus inflation. He argues that we will likely experience hyperinflation and hyperdeflation simultaneously. Assets like gold and silver will skyrocket in price due to currency debasement, while credit availability will dry up, causing a deflationary spiral in paper assets.

As the M2 money supply accelerates and debt levels become unsustainable, the transition to real money is not just an option but a necessity for preserving wealth.
