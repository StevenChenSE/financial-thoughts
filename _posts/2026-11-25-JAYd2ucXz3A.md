In a recent discussion on the *Risk Takers* podcast, Michael Howell of CrossBorder Capital delivered a sobering message to investors navigating current market volatility. As equities experience sharp swings—highlighted by Nvidia’s massive post-earnings rally and subsequent sell-offs—Howell argues that we are firmly in a bear market driven by a tightening global liquidity cycle. His central thesis is clear: do not mistake temporary rebounds for a recovery. The true bottom will only arrive when there is a definitive upward inflection in global liquidity, signaled first by assets like gold, silver, and Bitcoin.

## The Liquidity Cycle Dictates Market Direction

Howell emphasizes that "it is very difficult to make money when liquidity is going against you." This tightening has been evident for some time, with the growth rate of global liquidity peaking around late 2025 or early 2026. Because markets price at the margin, the flow of new capital is the crucial leading indicator.

The sequence of asset performance follows a predictable pattern based on sensitivity to liquidity:
1.  **Bitcoin:** As the most liquidity-sensitive asset, Bitcoin peaked first and has been in a clear bear market throughout the year.
2.  **Gold and Silver:** Precious metals peaked around January, confirming the cycle's progression.
3.  **Bond Markets:** Yield curves (US, Japanese, German Bunds) have been flattening since Q1, signaling a shift from speculation to turbulence.
4.  **Equities:** Stocks are the last to feel the "cold blast" of tightening liquidity. The recent choppiness and sell-offs represent this delayed impact.

Howell notes that in bear markets, there are often more up days than down days, but the down days are vicious enough to drag indices lower. This creates a psychological trap for investors who jump in during rallies, only to be caught by further declines.

## Bond Markets Tell the Truth: It’s Not About Fiscal Fears

A common narrative among commentators is that rising bond yields are driven by fears of US fiscal irresponsibility, "Trumpisms," or debt default risks. Howell refutes this entirely. By dissecting the 10-year US Treasury yield into two components—policy rate expectations and the term premium—he shows that:
*   **Term Premiums are Falling:** Across major markets (US, UK, Japan), investors are demanding *less* compensation for holding long-term debt, not more. This indicates a flight to safety, not a crisis of confidence in government credit.
*   **Policy Expectations are Rising:** The primary driver of higher yields is the strength of the real economy. US nominal GDP growth is at its fastest rate since the mid-1980s. Strong economic activity drives up expectations for the terminal policy rate (the peak interest rate), which in turn pushes bond yields higher.

"Equity markets have got a lot of hype in them but bond markets are pretty much the truth," Howell states. The flattening yield curve is consistent with an economy transitioning from boom to slowing growth, not a fiscal collapse.

## The Fed’s Hidden Strategy: Treasury QE and Debt Monetization

Howell argues that interest rates matter less than they used to because the Federal Reserve is not actually tightening aggressively. Instead, the US government has shifted from "Fed QE" (central bank money printing) to "Treasury QE." This involves issuing a massive volume of short-term Treasury bills to fund government spending and debt refinancing.

Key insights on this strategy include:
*   **Scarcity at the Long End:** By funding primarily through bills rather than long-dated coupons, the Treasury creates scarcity in long-term bonds, artificially suppressing yields by an estimated 70 basis points.
*   **Monetization of Debt:** When banks and foreign entities buy these bills, it effectively functions as money creation. For every 5% shift toward shorter debt issuance, approximately $3 trillion in liquidity is generated.
*   **Accommodating the AI Boom:** This strategy keeps funding conditions stable for hyperscalers and tech giants who need to issue hundreds of billions in equity and debt to fund capital expenditures (capex) for data centers and AI infrastructure.

The Fed’s primary goal is not fighting inflation but maintaining funding stability in the repo and collateral markets to prevent a financial crisis while accommodating the massive supply of new debt.

## The Path to Recovery: Watch Gold, Silver, and Bitcoin

Howell predicts that equities will likely make new lows before finding a durable bottom. He expects equities to have a mixed year, with the second half stronger than the first, but the true turning point lies in the global liquidity cycle.

To identify when the bear market is ending, investors should monitor:
1.  **Liquidity Sensitive Assets:** Gold, silver, and Bitcoin must form a floor and begin to rise consistently. These assets will lead the recovery because they are the first to benefit from renewed liquidity.
2.  **Yield Curve Steepening:** A bull steepening of the yield curve (long-term yields falling faster than short-term rates) typically occurs 8–9 months after the bottom in liquidity. This signals the onset of recession or slowing growth, which prompts central banks to ease policy.
3.  **Economic Data:** Confirmation that the real economy is slowing down, allowing policymakers to step back from hawkish rhetoric and release liquidity back into financial markets.

Howell estimates this transition will occur sometime in 2027, with the turbulence phase lasting through the end of Q1 or Q2 of next year.

## Strategic Advice: Patience and Inflation Hedges

For investors, the current environment demands patience. "You don't get pulled back into markets too quickly," Howell warns. While short-term trading opportunities exist, they come with significant risk ("sleepless nights").

The long-term strategic imperative is to protect wealth against **monetary inflation**. While high street (consumer) inflation may fluctuate due to cost factors like oil and productivity, monetary inflation—the devaluation of paper money through debt monetization—is structural. Governments cannot cut spending or raise taxes sufficiently to pay down their debts; they must inflate them away.

**Recommended Actions:**
*   **Stay in Cash/Mid-Duration Bonds:** Short-term safety is preferred during the turbulence phase.
*   **Accumulate Inflation Hedges Strategically:** Buy gold, silver, and Bitcoin progressively when they are 1–2 standard deviations below their trend lines. Do not try to time the exact bottom; instead, build positions over a 2–5 year horizon.
*   **Avoid Long-Term Bonds:** As debt is monetized, long-duration bonds will suffer from devaluation.

The era of low-cost deflation (cheap Chinese goods, low oil prices) is reversing, while monetary inflation is accelerating. This creates a wealth transfer away from cash holders and toward those holding hard assets and productive equities, but only after the current liquidity trap resolves.
