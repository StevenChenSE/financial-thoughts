## Introduction

In a recent episode of *The Real Story* with host Michelle Makori, Lobo Tiggre, founder and CEO of The Independent Speculator, discussed his successful navigation of the recent gold market volatility. Tiggre, who spent 14 years at Casey Research working alongside legendary investor Doug Casey, revealed that he liquidated all his gold and silver equities earlier in the year when prices peaked. Now holding the largest cash position of his investing career, Tiggre outlined his specific price targets for re-entering the market, ranging from a base case bottom near $3,400 to a "generational buying opportunity" if gold crashes to $2,800.

## The Victory Lap: Timing the Peak

Tiggre began by reflecting on his decision to sell precious metals stocks in late January, just as gold approached its all-time high of $5,589 per ounce. While he acknowledged that many long-term bull arguments—such as central bank buying and fiat debasement—remain valid, he emphasized the importance of price action over macro narratives for active speculators.

"I didn't see Jensen Huang's fidelity issues blowing up the stock market," Tiggre admitted regarding the recent Nvidia turmoil. "But in January, I saw gold and silver going vertical... I had a large amount of unrealized gains that I'd been building for years, and I didn't want them to slip through my fingers."

He stressed that this was not about genius prediction but standard speculative discipline: when markets go vertical, take profits. By selling his equities (while retaining physical bullion as insurance), Tiggre positioned himself with significant cash reserves ahead of the correction that saw gold fall nearly 37% from its highs to around $3,500.

## Price Targets: Where Is the Bottom?

Tiggre provided specific technical levels for where he intends to deploy his capital. He identified **$3,300 to $3,400** as a critical support zone, describing it as a "shoulder" on a potential head-and-shoulders chart pattern that has held for nearly half a year.

*   **Base Case:** If gold stabilizes around $3,300–$3,400, Tiggre will begin buying back into precious metals stocks, anticipating bargains during tax-loss season.
*   **Bear Case:** If that level breaks and gold falls below $3,200 or even $3,000, he views it as a catastrophic market error. "That's when you're selling your kids, selling your blood, selling your kidney... back up the truck big time," Tiggre joked.
*   **Worst Case Scenario:** He calculated that if gold repeats the post-2011 or 1980 blow-off top retreats (a 50%+ drop from peak), it could reach approximately **$2,800**. While he considers this unlikely given current fundamentals, he stated, "I think that would be a generational buying opportunity... the foundation of fortune is built on."

## The Fed and the "Dove in Hawk's Clothing"

A significant portion of the discussion focused on Federal Reserve policy. Tiggre described Fed Chair Jerome Powell as a "dove in hawk's clothing," suggesting that the Fed is using hawkish rhetoric to jawbeat markets rather than actually intending to hike rates aggressively.

Tiggre argued that the market's fear of rate hikes driving gold down is based on flawed logic, particularly regarding the Middle East conflict. He noted that while oil prices rising due to geopolitical tension (such as threats to the Strait of Hormuz) are often interpreted as inflationary pressure requiring higher rates, the true inflationary driver is fiscal dominance and money printing.

"I think you'll see a strong market reaction to have an era of easing on top of where we already are," Tiggre predicted. He warned that placing large bets ahead of the December 9th Fed meeting is dangerous, as the outcome could swing gold prices dramatically in either direction depending on whether Powell delivers a surprise hike or signals continued easing.

## Nvidia, Political Intervention, and Market Fragility

The conversation also touched on the recent volatility surrounding Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang. Tiggre expressed concern over President Trump’s public intervention to ensure Huang’s reinstatement, calling it a departure from free-market principles. "This is not how free markets work... The government shouldn't be appointing CEOs or telling the board what to do," Tiggre stated.

He raised two critical points regarding this event:
1.  **Institutional Credibility:** Political interference in corporate governance undermines the rule of law, a key factor that historically attracted global capital to US assets. This erosion of institutional trust is a long-term structural argument in favor of gold.
2.  **Key Person Risk:** If Nvidia is so critical to national security and the AI race that one CEO’s absence threatens the company, it exposes a massive vulnerability. Tiggre argued that such dependence on a single individual is a weakness that needs addressing through better succession planning.

## Central Bank Buying as a Floor

Despite the recent selloff, Tiggre highlighted record central bank buying as a fundamental support for gold prices. According to World Gold Council data, central banks purchased an estimated 321 tons in Q3, with significant unreported accumulation likely occurring in China and other sovereign nations.

Tiggre noted that this buying persists even as some sovereigns face acute cash needs due to war and economic stress. He views this not as a guarantee against all downside but as evidence of a paradigm shift toward de-dollarization. "They have a mandate... they're diversifying their political risk," he explained. This structural demand makes the $2,800 worst-case scenario less likely, though Tiggre remains cautious about short-term volatility.
