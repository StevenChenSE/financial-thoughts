## Insights on Fed dynamics, labor market realities, and mining sector opportunities

**Introduction**

Adrian Day, founder of Adrian Day Asset Management, shared his latest market perspectives during an interview at the Rick Rule Symposium. Discussing the Federal Reserve's direction, the true state of the U.S. labor market, and the outlook for gold and mining equities, Day offered a nuanced view of the current economic landscape. While he maintains a critical stance toward the Federal Reserve as an institution, he expressed genuine admiration for new Fed Chair Walsh, highlighted significant distortions in official employment data, and reaffirmed a strong long-term thesis for gold and mining stocks despite near-term volatility.

## Fed Chair Walsh and FOMC Dynamics

Adrian Day began by acknowledging his longstanding skepticism of the Federal Reserve, citing its role as a "price fixer" of money and its historical association with the Great Depression and modern inflationary episodes. However, he noted a distinct shift in tone regarding Fed Chair Walsh.

"I'm pleasantly surprised by Walsh," Day stated. "He's very professional. He's got deep knowledge and insight into markets." Day highlighted Walsh's recognition of the Fed's over-reliance on backward-looking government data, particularly jobs numbers that are often revised multiple times and lag significantly behind real-time economic conditions. Day praised Walsh for understanding that decisions based on outdated data can cause distortions.

Despite this positive assessment, Day warned of the structural limitations Walsh faces. The Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) has traditionally been a collegiate body, but Day noted that collegiality has eroded in recent years, leading to more dissents under Jerome Powell. While Walsh received a unanimous vote in his first meeting, Day anticipates fractures will emerge as the committee is divided between dovish members favoring easier policy and hawkish members pushing for tighter conditions.

## Labor Market Realities vs. Headlines

A significant portion of the discussion focused on the discrepancies between headline labor statistics and the underlying economic reality. Day argued that the jobs market is far weaker than official Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) figures suggest.

Day pointed to several factors distorting the data:
*   **Data Lag and Revisions:** Jobs data is often revised downward in subsequent months, yet media narratives frequently focus only on the headline number of the current month.
*   **World Cup Distortion:** Day noted that the World Cup is likely inflating current employment figures through temporary part-time work and gig economy activity, such as Uber driving, which may not persist once the event concludes.
*   **Sector Composition:** Recent job growth has been heavily concentrated in government and healthcare sectors. Day questioned the health of a labor market where half the jobs are effectively government-related.
*   **Hidden Unemployment:** Day highlighted that 5.2 million people are actively looking for work but are excluded from unemployment counts because they did not visit an employment exchange in the last four weeks. Additionally, 4.8 million people are underemployed, seeking full-time work but holding part-time positions. This suggests approximately 11 million people are not fully employed yet remain outside official unemployment metrics.

Day also touched on inflation dynamics, noting that a drop in oil prices typically takes six to eight weeks to feed through to gasoline prices and the Consumer Price Index (CPI). This lag creates a complex environment where better jobs data and lower inflation could theoretically support rate hikes, even as the underlying economy struggles.

## Gold Outlook: Short-Term Risks and Long-Term Bull Case

The market sentiment regarding interest rates has shifted dramatically, with Fed funds futures now pricing in over an 80% probability of a rate hike, a reversal from earlier expectations of cuts. Day believes a rate hike immediately prior to the midterm elections is unlikely, suggesting July as a more plausible window if hikes occur.

For gold, Day outlined a bifurcated outlook:
*   **Short-Term Risks:** Day acknowledged a meaningful probability of gold retesting recent lows in the near term. He mentioned the possibility of a dip toward $3,600, driven by hawkish rhetoric or data surprises. Gold stocks could see meaningful declines in such a scenario.
*   **Long-Term Thesis:** Despite short-term risks, Day remains highly bullish on gold. He noted that a rate hike is already priced in, meaning the market is positioned for the wrong move; any surprise would likely be on the downside (i.e., no hike), which would be positive for gold.
*   **Price Targets:** Day affirmed a target of $5,000 for gold within the current year. He expects the bull run to resume, particularly as the temporary distortions from the World Cup fade and the true weakness in the labor market becomes apparent in the latter half of the year.

## Mining Holdings and M&A Prospects

Day provided insights into Adrian Day Asset Management's portfolio and the broader mining sector. The firm holds significant positions in **Altius Minerals**, **Lara Exploration**, and **Origin**, each representing over 10% of assets. Day emphasized that these are core holdings the firm has no intention of selling. Other major holdings include **Agnico Eagle** and **Franco-Nevada**.

Regarding **Agnico Eagle**, Day addressed recent operational issues, dismissing the "rock problem" as a minor setback in the context of a manageable production loss of 40,000 to 60,000 ounces annually. He viewed the stock dip as a buying opportunity.

On Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A), Day predicted continued activity, particularly among mid-tier companies looking to expand pipelines, such as **Fortuna Silver** in West Africa. He cautioned that small companies should not assume acquisition is guaranteed; competitive tension is essential for shareholders to receive premiums. Day highlighted the importance of multiple interested parties, as seen in the **Aleris** and **Rupert Resources** deals, to drive up valuations.

## Barrick Gold and Strategic Shifts

The conversation turned to **Barrick Gold's** ongoing restructuring, including plans for a North American listing and the potential disposition of African assets. Day discussed speculation regarding a spin-off or sale of African operations, noting rumors of interest from **Endeavour Mining**.

Day also analyzed the complex relationship between Barrick and **Newmont**, particularly concerning joint venture assets. Newmont has raised objections regarding Barrick's use of JV assets, and Day suggested Newmont may view the restructuring as an opportunity to acquire valuable assets, such as those in Nevada. He praised former Barrick CEO Mark Bristow as a "maverick" who delivered significant value, citing the **Four Mile** PEA as a crowning achievement, though he noted the new management appears intent on undoing some of Bristow's strategies.

## Cash Strategy and Market Timing

Concluding with portfolio strategy, Day revealed the fund maintains approximately 8% cash. He described the current environment as a "great time to be buying" for new accounts and mid-risk global portfolios, though he advised against deploying all capital at once due to the risk of a retest of lows.

Day emphasized the utility of cash reserves to capitalize on volatility. He highlighted that market overreactions to news, such as the dip in Agnico, provide entry points for well-positioned investors. With a "calm" prevailing in the market, Day compared the current setup to previous periods preceding significant rallies, suggesting investors are "teed up" for the next leg of the bull market in gold and mining equities.
