## Introduction
In a recent interview on Capital Cosm, host Danny and guest David Hunter, a well-known contrarian investor, discussed the current state of the global economy. The conversation centered on a controversial but detailed thesis: a coming "global bust" will force central banks to print an unprecedented amount of money, ultimately leading to hyperinflationary conditions. Hunter argues that the current 3-5% inflation rate is a minor issue compared to the 25% year-over-year inflation he predicts will follow a massive liquidity injection.

## Market Reaction to PPI and Market Reaction
The discussion began with the release of the Producer Price Index (PPI) data. The PPI rose 0.4% in August, beating expectations slightly, while the core PPI was up 0.2%. On an annual basis, the PPI stood at 5.4%. This data, combined with oil prices pushing toward $100, contributed to a sell-off in the stock market. The Dow Jones, S&P 500, and NASDAQ all declined, while the 10-year US Treasury yield gapped up to 4.92%.

Hunter noted that while the PPI was higher than desired, it was driven largely by commodity prices. He emphasized that the market is nervous about several factors:
*   Geopolitical tensions in Iran.
*   Rising oil prices.
*   The likelihood of a Federal Reserve rate hike.
*   Concerns over the upcoming midterm elections.

Despite the "wall of worry," Hunter remained bullish on the stock market, arguing that the market is absorbing these issues well. He raised his price targets significantly, setting a target of 10,000 for the S&P 500, 70,000 for the Dow, and 36,000 for the NASDAQ. He believes these targets are achievable within a 3 to 6-month timeframe, driven by a "parabolic final run" into a secular top.

## The Global Bust and the $20 Trillion Print
Hunter’s core thesis revolves around a "global bust," a scenario where the global banking system begins to fail and unwind. He argues that with over $330 trillion in global debt and unprecedented levels of derivative leverage, a trigger event—whether a bank failure in Europe, Asia, or Japan—will cause a rapid deleveraging.

To prevent a total collapse, central banks will be forced to intervene. Hunter predicts that the Federal Reserve will print $20 trillion in new money through Quantitative Easing (QE), with other central banks doing the same proportionally. This could result in a total of $50 trillion in new money entering the global system.

He contrasts this with the $5 trillion printed in 2020, noting that current Fed leadership, including Kevin Warsh, has expressed reluctance to repeat such aggressive easing. However, Hunter believes that in a crisis, they will have no choice but to "turn on the spigots." The delay in this intervention will allow the market to hit an "air pocket" and drop significantly—potentially 80% from top to bottom over 8 to 12 months—before the money printing stabilizes prices.

## Inflation to 25% and Commodity Prices
Once the $20-50 trillion in new money enters the system, Hunter predicts a surge in demand for commodities, particularly as the US reshores manufacturing and builds infrastructure for AI and semiconductors. Since supply cannot be built quickly enough to meet this demand, prices will skyrocket.

*   **Oil:** Hunter forecasts oil could drop to $30 during the bust but eventually reach $500 per barrel in the next cycle due to supply constraints and money printing.
*   **Gold and Silver:** While he remains a long-term bull, he acknowledges that gold and silver could fall 50-80% during the deflationary phase of the bust. He has raised his gold target to $7,000 and silver to $200.
*   **Inflation:** The combination of money printing and commodity price surges will push inflation to 25% year-over-year, similar to the early 1980s but more severe.

## Political Implications and the 2026 Midterms
Hunter also discussed the political landscape, arguing that the 2026 midterm elections are crucial for the future of the US as a constitutional republic. He believes that if Democrats gain control of both chambers of Congress, they will pursue policies that undermine the current political order, such as packing the Supreme Court and eliminating the filibuster.

He suggested that some political actors may be intentionally prolonging geopolitical tensions, like the Iran situation, to make the current administration look weak ahead of the midterms. However, he believes the US is not losing the "war" with Iran and that the political games will eventually give way to the larger economic crisis.

## Conclusion
David Hunter’s outlook is one of high risk and high reward. He sees the current market rally as a final push before a significant correction triggered by a global bust. The subsequent central bank response, he argues, will lead to a new era of high inflation and commodity-driven growth. Investors are advised to be aware of these risks and to be aware of the "last inning" of the current secular bull market.
