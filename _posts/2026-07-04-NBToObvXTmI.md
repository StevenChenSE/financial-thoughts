Gold to $17,000? Ryan King Explains Why the Debt Crisis Reckoning Is Inevitable

The veteran gold executive breaks down the structural bull case for gold and Equinox Gold's path to becoming a million-ounce producer

In late 2025 and early 2026, gold surged to levels that shocked even the most bullish analysts, reaching $5,500 per ounce before pulling back to around $4,000. While retail sentiment has turned bearish and nervous buyers who purchased near the top have exited, a growing chorus of industry veterans argues this correction is merely a pause in an inevitable march toward much higher prices. Ryan King, Executive Vice President of Capital Markets at Equinox Gold, recently returned to the show to explain why he believes the sovereign debt crisis reckoning is coming, and why gold could eventually reach $17,000 per ounce.

King was a guest on the show two years ago in 2024, right before the major bull rally in gold and silver began. At that time, he noted a structural bull market was underway, but nothing of the magnitude seen in late 2025 and early 2026. Now, with gold having experienced its sharpest correction in years, King sat down again to discuss what happened, why it matters, and what investors should watch for going forward.

The Perfect Storm for Hard Assets

According to King, the rise in gold to all-time highs against nearly every currency wasn't accidental—it was the culmination of a series of events that stacked up against hard assets. "In my opinion, we had a series of events that finally really stacked up against hard assets," King explained. "We're seeing that across the board. It isn't just gold. We're seeing the devaluation of fiat currencies against all hard major assets—silver, copper, real estate, you name it."

The root cause, in King's view, stems from quantitative easing policies that created loose monetary conditions, leading to "significant amounts of debt, the largest debts we've seen in the history of the world." This has created a trap for central banks: they face pressure to raise interest rates to combat inflation, but they're largely unable to do so because of the massive debt burdens they carry. "They do not want to reduce GDP outlook. They really largely can't afford to pay higher rates on the amounts of debts they have," King noted.

This dynamic—where governments continue printing money to keep economies running rather than allowing necessary corrections—has systematically devalued fiat currencies against hard assets. King believes this trend is not temporary but structural, driven by what he calls "the sins of our past and the policies that have been put in place."

A Historical Parallel: The 1970s Are Coming Again

To understand where gold might be headed, King draws a parallel to the 1970s, a period that featured similar dynamics: policy uncertainty, an oil embargo, and geopolitical turmoil. During that era, gold rose from approximately $180 per ounce to around $850—an almost 500% increase—but not in a straight line. "Through the 1970s, this scenario that is playing out today happened then," King explained. "Gold pulled back from $180 to about $110 to $120 an ounce. And then because of inflationary pressures, the oil embargo, and things happening geopolitically, gold rose to $850 an ounce."

King acknowledges he doesn't know exactly where the current cycle will end, but the historical precedent suggests the path will involve volatility before substantially higher prices. In that context, a correction from $5,500 back toward $4,000 isn't surprising—it's the nature of cyclical assets that rise dramatically. The key question is whether the structural case remains intact. King believes it does: "Structurally, we are very much intact."

Pierre Lassonde's $17,000 Prediction: More Than Just Speculation?

One of the most striking predictions King referenced came from well-known gold investor Pierre Lassonde, who stated: "Just on the M2 money supply alone, we're talking about $17,000 plus dollars per ounce." That's Lassonde's prediction over the next five years.

To put this in context, if the Federal Reserve's M2 money supply continues expanding at rates consistent with recent decades, gold would need to appreciate substantially simply to maintain its purchasing power relative to the growing money supply. This isn't hyperbole—it's a mathematical consequence of the monetary policies that governments around the world have adopted. As King noted, "We're heading towards a major, major sovereign debt crisis again."

The evidence for this crisis is already visible. In the United States, the Congressional Budget Office projects the debt-to-GDP ratio will exceed 120% by 2036—higher than World War II levels. Japan faces similar challenges, with fiscal deficits expected to widen and military spending potentially doubling, putting severe pressure on the yen. In the UK, recent data showed the government spent more on social welfare than tax receipts in a single quarter. Europe faces its own debt pressures, while China and other emerging markets deal with the ripple effects of global monetary expansion.

The Sovereign Debt Crisis Reckoning

King doesn't mince words about what this means: "Every government continues to push the can down the road. As Ray Dalio says, every empire will go through this situation and eventually fall." While no one knows exactly what the reckoning will look like or when it will arrive, the trajectory seems clear.

The uncomfortable truth, King suggests, is that politicians prioritize short-term stability and re-election over long-term fiscal responsibility. "As a politician, you want to continuously get reelected. So, you want to keep the party going as long as you can. You want to get votes. You want to keep the party going as much as possible in order to sustain some level of reasonable GDP and ultimately growth. And that means more debt and that means printing more capital, printing more money."

This dynamic has played out across administrations and political systems, making it a structural rather than partisan issue. The consequences, however, will eventually come due.

Why Investor Sentiment Has Turned Cold—and What It Means

Despite gold still trading near record highs from a five-year perspective, sentiment has dried up dramatically. King attributes this to several factors. First, gold "rose tremendously fast," creating the need for technical backfilling. "Nothing goes up in a straight line," he noted. "Yes, we've corrected this year from $5,500 down to $4,000."

Second, psychological factors have taken over. "Psychologically, the buyers that were in later are now nervous and have sold out," King explained. "They're going into this situation where we've seen these cycles before." Many investors fear a repeat of 2011, when gold hit $1,900 before correcting all the way down to $1,100.

King acknowledges these concerns are understandable, but he urges investors to consider the long-term picture: "If you look at the long-term picture, this is a great asset. If you're going to be an investor and look at the long-term picture, as many macro investors say, buy a 5 to 15% allocation in gold."

The shift in sentiment has also affected the mining sector, which saw tremendous activity—more exploration, drilling, and financing—during the euphoria phase. Now, with investor attention turned elsewhere, mining companies face a tougher environment for capital raising. However, King emphasizes that companies like Equinox Gold must take a long-term view: "You have to build your business and manage what you can control."

Equinox Gold's Transformation: From Calibre to Orla

King's current company has been actively transforming itself through strategic mergers. In 2025, Equinox Gold completed its merger with Calibre Mining—a deal that significantly strengthened the balance sheet. "We ended up divesting of some smaller assets that were lower quality within our portfolio where we had to focus management's time to unlock more value," King explained. "That allowed us to significantly transform our balance sheet. We went from about a net $1.6 to $1.7 billion debt position to today we're sitting at net debt of about $400 million prior to this most recent deal with Orla Mining."

The company also optimized its portfolio of operating assets and guided the market that it would be producing 700,000 to 800,000 ounces of gold annually, with a significant portion coming from Canada.

Now, Equinox has announced an even more transformative merger with Orla Mining. As King described, "This isn't a situation where one company is solving another company's problems. This is both companies coming from a position of strength to unlock more value for our shareholders, and we actually see annual production growth from the combined entity."

The combined company will be a major force in the gold mining industry. The midpoint of the merged entity's guidance will be 1.1 million ounces of gold annually—"north of a million ounces a year," as King put it. This positions the company in the intermediate-to-senior gold producer category, which typically commands premium valuations. Importantly, 70% to 75% of the company's net asset value will come from Canada and the United States—safe jurisdictions that institutional investors actively seek.

Strong Leadership and Shareholder Backing

The management team leading the combined company brings decades of experience. Darren Hall, current CEO of Equinox, will lead the merged entity as CEO. Jason Simpson, president and CEO of Orla Mining, will become president. The company will also be chaired by Chuck Jeannes, described by King as "a very seasoned veteran" who was CEO of Goldcorp—the world's most valuable and largest gold mining company at the time—through the last cycle.

The shareholder backing is equally impressive. Ross Beaty, founder and significant backer of Equinox, will remain as a special advisor. Pierre Lassonde, one of the most successful mining entrepreneurs in the sector and founder of Franco-Nevada, will maintain his significant stake. Prem Watsa of Fairfax Financial is also a major supporter. Combined, these legendary mining entrepreneurs represent approximately 11% of the pro forma company.

King is particularly enthusiastic about Lassonde's involvement: "Pierre has been very vocal about this opportunity. He's been in the Globe and Mail and a number of other well-known publications regarding this opportunity and creating what he would call a 'baby Agnico Eagle' because of the significant amount of gold production coming out of Canada."

Geographic Diversification: A Strategic Advantage

While some mining companies consolidate assets in single regions to capture synergies, Equinox operates across multiple jurisdictions—Mexico, the United States, and Canada. King acknowledges this has advantages and trade-offs: "It's a diversified portfolio. You like to have diversification in your financial portfolio. We like to have diversification in our portfolio that is gold-focused, but you're operating in different jurisdictions at different assets."

The Orla merger actually enhances this geographic balance. Orla operates Musselwhite Mine, Greenstone Mine, and Valentine Mine—all in Canada—creating three Canadian operations on a pro forma basis. This allows for supply chain and personnel benefits while maintaining the diversification that protects against jurisdiction-specific risks. "From time to time, there could be challenges at one operation or within one jurisdiction vis-à-vis royalties or government taxes or different social issues that can arise at different businesses. So, it's nice to have that diversified portfolio to help deliver a consolidated result," King explained.

Why Equinox Could Be a Compelling Opportunity

Despite the transformational potential of the Orla merger, Equinox Gold's stock has been beaten down along with the broader sentiment toward gold miners. The company trades at approximately 0.5 times NAV, while peers trade at 7 to 8 times NAV. "Always when you announce these big deals, it's change. And change brings question marks and uncertainty. Change brings bigger shareholders selling out and watching the position to see what happens or watching the transformation to see what happens and then looking at another entry point maybe down the road," King acknowledged.

However, this discount may present an opportunity for investors who believe in the long-term gold thesis. As King put it: "I just believe it presents a compelling valuation opportunity. I believe it presents a compelling opportunity for the leadership that will be leading this business. And again, if your view is that over time we will probably see higher gold prices because of devaluation of currencies—which has happened over the last 30 plus years now—then I think you're probably wanting to look at the space."

The company's strategy is to focus on delivering reliable results. "The number one thing investors want to see is reliability. Delivering into expectations. That's critical for getting comfort, getting peace of mind for an investment product like Equinox Gold," King emphasized.

Looking ahead, Equinox has significant milestones coming in the second half of 2026, including permitting decisions and integration progress. The company also has a 70% annual production growth rate over the next five years from new assets, though this will require disciplined capital allocation.

The Three Criteria for Acquisitions

King, who has participated in multiple mining deals over the past several years, offered his three criteria for evaluating acquisition targets:

**Number one: Jurisdiction.** "If you can be picky, be picky," King advised. "Picky about where you're going to operate. I think that's just critically important." Safe jurisdictions like Canada and the United States offer political stability, established mining regulations, and access to institutional capital.

**Number two: Economics.** "You have to look at the economics of the deposit, of course," King continued. "Grade is important, but if you have good grade but you have a huge stripping ratio to get at that ore, it could be very challenging economically." The company uses a conservative gold price assumption of $2,200 to $2,500 per ounce when evaluating whether a project can deliver solid returns.

**Number three: Growth potential.** "Is there potential to grow beyond what you know?" King asked. "Every investor wants something to grow beyond what currently exists. I mean, Equinox will spend over a hundred million dollars a year on exploration, discovering new zones and expanding resources for the sustainability and longevity of the assets."

These criteria reflect a disciplined approach to M&A that prioritizes quality over quantity—a philosophy that has guided the Calibre and Orla mergers.

The Long-Term Outlook

King remains constructive on gold over the long term, drawing on historical patterns and current dynamics. While the correction has been painful for recent buyers, he believes cycles are shortening in this environment. "With geopolitical environment, debt, sovereign debt crisis, devaluation of currencies, I think cycles are shortening myself," he said. "That then therefore leads to potential revaluation of these hard assets, and it's not just gold. You're seeing it in copper. You're seeing it in silver. You're seeing it in many of these hard assets that are difficult to come by."

For investors willing to look beyond short-term sentiment, the structural case for gold—and gold miners—remains compelling. Whether gold reaches $17,000 as Lassonde predicts, or settles somewhere in between, the forces of monetary expansion, sovereign debt accumulation, and currency debasement point in one direction. As King summarized: "It's an unfortunate situation that we face ourselves in here. But it is a problem, and at some point in time, reckoning's going to come."
