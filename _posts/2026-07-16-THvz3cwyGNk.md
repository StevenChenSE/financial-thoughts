**Subtitle:** Francis Hunt analyzes inflation data, technical breakouts in yields, the "B2B landlording" data center boom, and the long-term trajectory for precious metals.

## Introduction

In a July 15, 2026, episode of *Capital Account*, host Danny sits down with guest Francis Hunt, known as The Market Sniper, to dissect the latest macroeconomic developments. The discussion covers the newly released Producer Price Index (PPI) and Consumer Price Index (CPI) reports, the technical outlook for the US 10-year yield, the risks posed by corporate debt in the tech sector, and the strategic outlook for gold and silver. Hunt emphasizes a "hyperstagflation" model where stagnation meets rising costs, warning of credit crowding as capital floods into the build-out of a "surveillance grid" via data centers.

## Inflation Reports and the Hyperstagflation Model

Francis Hunt begins by analyzing the PPI report, which showed an annualized rate of 5.5%, revised down from over 6%. Hunt contextualizes this alongside a recent CPI undershoot, suggesting the data must remain consistent with official narratives. He notes that Federal Reserve Chair Powell is keen to avoid a repeat of the "transitory inflation" error made in 2020. Hunt recalls calling the "debt turn" in 2020, predicting an escalation in rates as the ability to fund debt on decreasing interest rates ended. He highlights the severity of the shift by citing Austria's 100-year bond issued at a fraction of 1%, which has since lost 73% of its value. Hunt maintains a macroeconomic model of "hyperstagflation," expecting growth and employment numbers to underwhelm while inflationary pressures persist.

## Technical Analysis: 10-Year Yield Breakout

Hunt presents a technical analysis of the US 10-year yield, indicating a bullish breakout. He identifies a "bull flag" pattern and a "W bottom" structure, suggesting that borrowing costs on the long end are poised to rise further. Despite recent volatility, the yield remains north of the key 4.5% level. Hunt points out a higher volatility floor (HVF) trigger that has been reclaimed. His technical targets include an immediate move toward 4.7%, with a broader call for the 10-year yield to reach the 5.75% to 6% range by the middle of next year. This breakout signals increasing costs to finance debt and a devaluation of existing fixed-income assets.

## Corporate Debt and the "Surveillance Grid" Boom

The conversation shifts to corporate debt, noting a lackluster $25 billion capital raise by Amazon and Oracle's debt facing a downgrade to the lowest investment grade level. Hunt warns that a further slip would force institutional divestment. He draws parallels between the current debt buildup and the subprime crisis, labeling the massive borrowing for data centers as "B2B landlording." These data centers require specific infrastructure, including cooling and water, and house "tenants" (chips) with short lifespans. Hunt argues that this "surveillance grid" build-out is crowding out credit for small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs). He posits that "unicorns" deemed too big to fail are co-opting banks into irresponsible lending, ultimately socializing the costs of this infrastructure expansion onto the public.

## Market Distortion and ETF Dynamics

Hunt discusses how passive investing via Exchange-Traded Funds (ETFs) has distorted market dynamics. He describes a "two-tier" stock market where mega-cap unicorns benefit from continuous passive buying, sustaining higher valuations, while smaller caps and cyclical industries like mining are starved of capital. He highlights the disparity in market capitalization, noting that a single tech company can be worth more than the entire primary silver mining sector. Hunt also touches on the tokenization agenda, suggesting that major asset managers are more interested in the surveillance capabilities of blockchain for tracking asset ownership than in the underlying assets like Bitcoin.

## Gold and Silver: Historical Suppression and Outlook

Turning to precious metals, Hunt reviews the historical suppression of gold. He outlines cycles of suppression: a 20-year period from the 1980s to the 2000s, a 5-year period from 2011 to 2016, and a 2-year suppression leading to the 2021 peak. With gold hovering around $4,000 and silver near $58, Hunt characterizes the current price action as a "falling wedge" continuation pattern rather than a bear market. He advises that summer months are typically weak for breakout traders but expects a resumption of the uptrend later in the year. Hunt interprets geopolitical events, such as tensions involving Iran and tariff threats, as tactical maneuvers to suppress gold prices and protect the fiat debt system. He also questions narratives regarding US gold sales, noting massive purchases by China and other central banks.

## Strategic Takeaways

Francis Hunt concludes with strategic advice for investors. He emphasizes the importance of timing, warning that while short-term technical structures may allow for further moves in risk assets, the risk of a sudden correction is high. He describes the current environment as a risky game where the "music could stop" abruptly. For precious metals, Hunt identifies a "blue pot accumulation" opportunity, particularly as the market exits the summer seasonality. He advises maintaining a long-term perspective, accumulating real assets, and avoiding excessive screen time. Hunt encourages viewers to focus on health and enjoyment of life, noting that wealth built slowly is more likely to endure. He reiterates that gold will lead the next move, with silver offering higher beta exposure, and predicts that the current correction will eventually resolve into further upside.
