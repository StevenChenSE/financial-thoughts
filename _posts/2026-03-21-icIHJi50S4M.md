In a striking interview on the *What's Capital?* show, chartist Andy from the *Finding Value Finance* channel laid out a sobering case for an impending energy crisis—one that could see oil prices reach between $500 and $1,000 per barrel by the 2030s.

Host Danny introduced Andy on March 19, 2026, noting, “You’re one of my favorite chartists out there.” The conversation quickly turned to energy, with Danny observing that while prices were already high, “we would anticipate energy blowing out even higher given the circumstances.”

Andy didn’t mince words. “Short term, it looks pretty ugly for the economy and the world with energy probably going to spike quite dramatically here,” he began. The core issue, in his view, isn’t inventories but **flow rates**—the actual daily supply of oil reaching the market. He estimates a severe disruption: “Flow rates have been impacted, I’d say somewhere between 10 and 20 million barrels per day. That’s 10 to 20% worldwide.”

## The Charts Point to a Historic Spike

Using Elliott Wave theory and fractal analysis, Andy explained that oil is entering a powerful “wave three of wave three”—typically the largest and longest move in a market cycle. “When I go and I look and people are going to tell me, you know, that’s not possible. The economy can’t handle it. I don’t know, guys. When you lose 10 to 20% of flow rate, you’re going to get ridiculous pricing. $100 is nothing.”

His long-term projection is staggering: “I get values that extend into like 2030s-ish that are almost $1,000, like between $500 and $1,000 is what I see at the top of the bull market.” In the nearer term, he sees oil easily hitting $150-$200, consolidating, and then surging again.

## A Fundamental Shift: From Resource Abundance to Scarcity

Andy presented several ratio charts to illustrate a paradigm shift. One key chart showed the ratio of oil to the S&P 500 squeezing into a long-term “falling wedge” pattern before breaking out. “I think this side over here is resource abundance,” he said, pointing to the left side of the chart. “And I think at the bottom of 2026… this is the beginning of resource scarcity.”

He believes this signals a multi-decade period where **hard assets like energy and metals will dramatically outperform financial assets**. “What we’re at the beginning of is this big shift where energy is going to pretty much outperform everything. And I think this is just the starting point of it.”

This has dire implications for the broader economy, especially sectors like AI and tech that are energy-intensive. “If these data centers are going to have trouble finding an energy resource… that weakens the entire markets,” Danny noted. Andy agreed, highlighting the near-perfect correlation between oil and GDP. “If you lose 10 to 20% of flow rate, what you’re losing is GDP. It will be a recession.”

## The War Cycle and "Insider" Positioning

The discussion turned to how charts seemed to foreshadow geopolitical conflict. Danny recalled that Andy had spotted unusual strength in oil services stocks while oil was still at $60, suggesting “insider positioning” ahead of the Iran war.

Andy connected it to market cycles. “The charts are a reflection of the market conditions… And then war is also a response that they have during these market conditions.” He pointed to historical patterns where major energy breakouts were preceded by consolidation and then catalyzed by war—from 9/11 to the present conflict. “It’s almost like a clock.”

## The Endgame: Inflation, Currency Pressure, and Physical Assets

Looking at a long-term chart of the Producer Price Index (PPI) divided by the Consumer Price Index (CPI), Andy sees a potential historic reversal. This ratio bottomed in 2002 and again in 2020. If it breaks its decades-long downtrend, it would signal a world where input costs (driven by energy) are rising much faster than consumer prices—a highly inflationary environment.

“That world is not recognizable to most people,” he warned. In such a scenario, “the S&P 500 is not the spot to be… This is a world of hard assets.”

His final forecast ties everything together: energy scarcity cuts GDP growth just as governments are forced to print money to respond, leading to massive inflation. “That massive inflation is going to put pressure on currencies and bond markets around the world and that pressure is going to mount in physical metals. That will be the endgame I think. So oil, fertilizers, and physical metals I think are the ultimate endgame.”

When Danny asked if fertilizer shortages implied problems with global food production, Andy confirmed the link. “Fertilizers are made from natural gas… If you have a disruption in energy, fertilizers are the last thing you’re going to cut because that’s what you have for food production.”

## A Lifetime of Tight Supply?

Andy ended on a sober note about the future of supply. He contrasted past crises—like the 1970s peak of conventional oil in America or the 2000s global conventional peak—with today. Each was mitigated by new sources: global expansion, then shale fracking.

“This time… I’m not sure that we can replace in terms of flow rates what we’re losing,” he said. “We’re drilling in the middle of oceans and we are getting this stuff out of sand. That’s called desperation. That doesn’t mean you have abundance.”

His conclusion is stark: the coming energy crisis may not be a temporary shock but the beginning of a prolonged era of scarcity, reshaping the global economy and investment landscape for decades to come.
