At Consensus Miami, Scott Melker—the host of the Wolf of All Streets podcast and one of crypto's most recognizable voices—sat down to discuss the state of Bitcoin, the death of traditional altcoin seasons, and why he believes Bitcoin is a million-dollar asset. His answers cut through the noise of daily price action to address some of the most pressing questions facing crypto investors today.

---

## What's Driving Bitcoin to $82,000?

Bitcoin's surge from $75,000 to $82,000 in mere days has caught attention across financial markets. According to Melker, the move isn't being driven by retail investors—which actually signals *more* upside potential ahead.

"I think sentiment is changing slightly or certainly on the institutional side. I don't think it's retail that's driving it at all, actually, which is why I believe there's more fuel to the fire," Melker explained. "Nothing's better marketing for Bitcoin than higher Bitcoin prices."

The institutional thesis rests on several pillars:

- **ETF flows** continuing to pour into Bitcoin products
- **Anticipation around the Strategic Bitcoin Reserve (SBC)** and expectations that the U.S. government will eventually trade above par value for its holdings
- **The inevitability thesis**: Traders are front-running Michael Sailor's inevitable billions in future Bitcoin purchases

"I think renewed optimism around clarity is probably partially responsible for it," Melker added. "ETF flows and then, of course, I think we're now at a point—for better or for worse—where when people anticipate that SBC is going to start trading above par, they're trying to front run Michael Sailor inevitably buying billions of dollars of Bitcoin in the coming days. They know it's going to happen. They know what should happen to price. Seems like an easy trade."

---

## Why Retail Sentiment Remains Fragile

Despite Bitcoin's relative strength, Melker acknowledges that retail investor confidence remains battered. The disconnect between Bitcoin's performance and broader crypto sentiment explains the weak hands.

"Retail has just been beaten down by the crypto market for years," Melker said. "This time, even though Bitcoin has performed relatively well—not as well as previous cycles—I think that doesn't really feel very comforting for people who have been holding altcoins this entire cycle."

The psychological weight of peak prices compounds the problem:

"In the past, if you had Bitcoin go to an all-time high like up to 126, you would have expected that all coins would have been high beta to that. They would have had an altcoin season, and you would have seen 50% at least 100% gains on those. And they just really haven't. And I think those people are disillusioned and just haven't really come back."

There's also a benchmarking problem. Even with Bitcoin up 35-40% from its recent lows near $60,000, investors are still comparing current prices to last year's $126 peak—making the current levels feel disappointing rather than celebratory.

## The Death of Traditional Altcoin Season: A New Paradigm

Perhaps the most provocative claim Melker made was that altcoin season isn't coming back—at least not in any form crypto veterans would recognize.

"I think altcoin season comes but in a totally different iteration of what we've seen in the past," Melker explained. "So I think now you have sort of a have and have not situation for the crypto market."

### The Institutional "Haves"

The winners going forward, according to Melker, are assets with institutional backing:

- **Bitcoin** — the flagship institutional asset
- **Ethereum** — ETF presence and enterprise adoption
- **Solana** — growing institutional narrative
- **XRP** and select others depending on regulatory developments

### The Retail "Have-Nots"

"Everything else" is now relying on crypto-native liquidity that has dried up significantly. Several forces have converged to drain this liquidity:

1. **The October 10th liquidation events** — "a huge liquidation event for a lot of the crypto natives. Millions of people lost all of their capital."

2. **Retail rotation into precious metals trading on crypto rails** — "I think gold and silver and oil trading on crypto rails where you could trade with perpetual swaps and heavy leverage—silver trading more with more volatility than altcoin—I think a lot of the retail interest went there."

3. **Prediction markets eating altcoin lunch** — "The really untold story about altcoins is that prediction markets have really eaten their lunch. I don't think you need to speculate on a meme coin that you know is a rigged game when you can speculate on the weather tomorrow."

Melker sees this as a structural shift. The days of retail-driven altcoin explosions that mirror Bitcoin's moves are likely over. The path forward for altcoin investors requires being "very selective."

## Bitcoin to $200K—And Why That's "Exceptionally Low"

Melker didn't hedge when asked about his Bitcoin price target. While Patrick White coined the "rocket ship moment" phrase in reference to the Clarity Act's passage, Melker sees it differently:

"200,000 is an exceptionally low target, right? I think it's a bare market sort of target where people don't have the optimism that they once did. I just don't know when."

His actual view? "I think Bitcoin is a million-dollar asset."

The key caveat: nobody should expect precise timing. "Everybody wants to know it'll be at this price by this time, and that's just not the way that you invest."

## The Clarity Act: Framework for the Next 100 Years

On the Clarity Act, Melker offered a nuanced take—potentially important for price, but transformative for the industry structure.

"This was Patrick Witz's take, and actually to me, I don't think it is particularly a rocket ship for price because I think that there's been so much anticipation for it. We've been talking about it for so long. You've got to imagine that it's kind of a buy the rumor situation."

But the long-term implications are massive:

"What it does change is the altcoin market to some degree because we'll have a lot of clarity—no pun intended—on what's a security and what's a commodity and what activities companies can participate in."

More importantly, "the real honest answer is that it gives a framework for what the next hundred years of this industry looks like in the United States. It gives builders the confidence that they will not go back to the Gary Gensler era."

The humor wasn't lost on Melker: "I love you said 100 years because people in crypto do not look long-term." His response captured the industry's irony perfectly: "We can't do 100 minutes."

He added: "Think about the fact that we're talking about the Howie test for how we should look at crypto. It's from the 1930s. So imagine in the year 3000, we're looking back and saying that Clarity Act, man, that's really old."

## Bitcoin vs. Traditional Hedges: Why Melker Prefers Digital Gold

When asked whether $10,000 gold or $10,000 Bitcoin is more likely, Melker didn't hesitate.

"$10,000 Bitcoin," he said immediately. Then, acknowledging his friend Mike McGlone's contrary view: "You got like the angel on your shoulder and the devil."

Melker's reasoning is rooted in macroeconomic concerns:

- "Stocks are overvalued"
- "Debt to GDP is terrifying"
- "Stock market value to GDP is terrifying"
- Even traditional hedges like "silver or gold—the volatility that they've traded with gives me less confidence even in them than a Bitcoin"

His personal strategy is brutally simple: "I simply just buy Bitcoin."

Notably, Melker views Bitcoin through a specific lens that diverges from crypto's tech narrative. "I think there's Bitcoin and everything else. And everything else is tech and VC," he explained. "I don't like the Bitcoin trades like a risk asset tech narrative because I don't believe it to be true. I don't think the data actually shows that it's true."

His approach: dollar-cost averaging Bitcoin as a "long-term savings account." The irony, he notes, is that most people do the opposite. "You have the people lining up at Bitcoin 50,000 and the line's empty, and Bitcoin 125 and there's this long line. Because that's how people do it."

## Tech Stocks and AI: Why Melker Is Cautious

Despite covering crypto, Melker was asked about the AI stock boom. His answer reflects caution about current valuations.

"I'm very concerned about all the huge IPOs that are coming and those draining liquidity from tech," he said. "Like, if SpaceX and OpenAI all come on the market at multi-trillion dollar valuations this year, are multi-trillions of dollars sitting on the sidelines waiting to buy those? Or is somebody going to have to sell something to buy that?"

His conclusion: "Who's your buyer of SpaceX? Who's your buyer of OpenAI? It's someone who's going to sell their Nvidia or their Google or their Alphabet or Meta or whatever it is to buy that. There's just not enough capital to support those IPOs in my opinion."

That said, Melker is bullish on one specific IPO: "I think SpaceX. It's just so hard to bet against Elon Musk. He just never loses."

## Michael Sailor's Evolving Bitcoin Strategy

Melker shared his perspective on MicroStrategy's latest earnings call, where Sailor for the first time acknowledged potentially selling Bitcoin.

"I asked him pretty candidly about it because you know, he said to me in the beginning of the conversation, 'I've been the never sell Bitcoin guy,'" Melker recalled. "I said, 'You mentioned selling Bitcoin for the first time on the call yesterday.'"

Melker believes the unstated reason involves regulatory obligations: "When you have regulators and the SEC and such and you're offering a security like Saylor—retail needs to be protected, which means they need to know that if the yields come down or if it comes off par, that the actual asset that you're holding is on the table to protect them. And that means selling Bitcoin."

But Sailor's actual statement reveals the deeper commitment: "'I will never be a net seller. I will continue to be a net buyer.' He said, 'I guarantee you, if I sell Bitcoin for some mechanical reason at the beginning of the month, I've bought more by the end of the month. I will always be adding to my Bitcoin stack, even when I'm selling.'"

Melker sees this as a case study in "strong opinions loosely held"—the idea that conviction should evolve when presented with new information. "Otherwise, you're just a religious zealot, right? And you just believe in something, politics, religion, whatever it is."

He pointed to Sailor's own evolution: "Go back to his old tweets where he said Bitcoin would fail and it was like a scam basically, right? So he's on the evolving path of many, and he realizes that there's going to be a lot of ways to put that Bitcoin to work, and some of that's going to be on other crypto rails."

## Key Takeaways

1. **Institutional demand is the primary driver of Bitcoin's current price action**, not retail enthusiasm—which suggests more upside remains as institutions continue accumulating.

2. **Traditional altcoin season is effectively dead**. The crypto market has bifurcated into "institutional haves" (Bitcoin, Ethereum, Solana) and retail-dependent assets facing structural liquidity challenges.

3. **Melker's price target is $1 million for Bitcoin**, viewing $200K as a conservative floor rather than a ceiling. Timing remains uncertain, but the direction is clear.

4. **The Clarity Act matters more for regulatory certainty than for immediate price action**, establishing a framework for the next century of crypto development in the United States.

5. **Bitcoin should be viewed as a long-term savings account**, not a trading vehicle—and certainly not as a risk-on tech asset. Dollar-cost averaging during dips, not buying breakouts, remains the optimal strategy.
