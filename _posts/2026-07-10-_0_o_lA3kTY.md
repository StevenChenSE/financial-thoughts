## Market indicators, geopolitical shifts, and the future of AI

In a July 9, 2026 interview on *Capital Cosm*, host Danny sits down with geopolitical analyst Doomberg to dissect the intricate relationship between financial markets, military conflict, and global strategy. The discussion centers on the counterintuitive signals provided by bond yields regarding the ongoing tensions with Iran, the vulnerability of Ukraine following the recent NATO summit, and the accelerating trajectory of artificial intelligence.

### The 10-Year Yield as a War Counter-Signal

The conversation opens with a critical mental model for assessing conflict escalation: the 10-year Treasury yield. Doomberg argues that a yield above 4.5% acts as a reliable counter-signal to war escalation. At the time of the interview, the yield stood at 4.56%.

Doomberg explains that when bond markets sell off and yields spike into "dangerous territory," it indicates the market does not approve of aggressive policy. Historically, this financial pressure forces a policy reversal. Following recent incidents in the Strait of Hormuz where tankers were fired upon, the US and SECOM reacted with heightened force, and President Trump engaged in tough rhetoric. However, the subsequent spike in bond yields, coupled with drops in gold and oil, precipitated a classic deescalation. Doomberg notes that Iran's strong desire for a deal aligns with these market signals, suggesting the conflict is backing off rather than intensifying.

### Gold, Oil, and Gulf State Economics

A significant portion of the analysis focuses on the behavior of precious metals during conflict. Doomberg challenges the traditional view that gold always rises during war. He proposes that in the current geopolitical context, gold movements are tied to the liquidity of Gulf States.

These nations primarily export oil and gold. When war disrupts oil flows, Gulf States are forced to sell gold reserves to maintain liquidity. Therefore, a drop in gold prices can signal active conflict and oil disruption, while a rise in gold indicates that peace is imminent and oil markets are stabilizing. Doomberg views gold as a long-term store of value, advocating for dollar-cost averaging regardless of short-term price fluctuations.

### NATO Summit and Ukraine's Vulnerability

The discussion shifts to the recent NATO summit in Turkey, where Trump made supportive statements for President Zelensky, including openness to licensing Patriot missile production to Ukraine. Doomberg expresses skepticism about the tangible benefits for Kiev, highlighting a severe strategic vulnerability.

According to Doomberg, the US military is dangerously short on air defense missiles due to massive expenditures during the Iran conflict, where approximately 2,000 Patriot missiles were reportedly expended. With the US unable to rapidly restock munitions due to rare earth supply constraints, Ukraine is left "naked" with minimal air defense capabilities. Doomberg frames the broader conflict as a continuation of World War II, which he argues began in 2014. In this war of attrition against the US military-industrial complex, Russia is strategically waiting for Ukraine's defenses to collapse.

### Strategic Petroleum Reserve and Energy Independence

Addressing concerns over the Strategic Petroleum Reserve (SPR), Doomberg dismisses alarmist narratives surrounding its depletion. The SPR has dropped to levels last seen in 1983, currently holding around 319 million barrels. However, Doomberg argues the SPR is largely irrelevant to US national security today.

The US is now a hydrocarbon superpower, heavily integrated with Canadian energy resources. The SPR's primary function is political price management rather than supply security. Furthermore, the oil in the SPR was not sold but loaned to major corporations like Exxon and Chevron. These entities are contractually obligated to return the oil plus interest, ensuring the reserve will naturally refill over the coming years.

### Japanese Yields and Financial Warfare

Doomberg also highlights the significance of the Japanese 10-year yield hitting all-time highs. The spread between Japanese and US 10-year yields has narrowed to 1.7%, reflecting a broader financial battle. Japan faces a critical choice between defending its currency or its domestic bond market. This dynamic, alongside China's actions in the gold market and sanctions evasion, represents the financial front of the ongoing global conflict.

### AI Singularity and Technological Competition

The interview concludes with a look at the rapid advancement of artificial intelligence. Doomberg references OpenAI's latest model, which is 54% more token-efficient on agentic coding, and notes similar developments from Claude. He cites Ray Kurzweil's *The Singularity is Near*, observing that the doubling time for AI improvements is shrinking from years to months, and potentially days, signaling the approach of a technical singularity.

Additionally, Doomberg discusses China's strategy of releasing cheap AI models to undercut Western companies. This move is part of a broader financial war aimed at eroding the profitability of the US stock market, which currently underpins the US fiscal situation.

### Media Literacy and Upcoming Book

Finally, Doomberg touches on the importance of media literacy, distinguishing between lies of commission, omission, and paltering. He emphasizes the need to critically evaluate all information sources, including Western outlets like the *Financial Times* and alternative media. He is currently writing a book on "Sources and Methods," aimed at teaching readers how to systematically cut through propaganda, algorithmic noise, and AI deepfakes to find the truth.

Doomberg provides a comprehensive analysis of current geopolitical and market dynamics, asserting that a 10-year yield above 4.5% signals a deescalation in the Iran conflict due to market pressure on policy. He reinterprets gold price movements through the lens of Gulf State liquidity, highlights Ukraine's critical air defense shortages amidst the broader war of attrition, and dismisses concerns over the Strategic Petroleum Reserve given US energy dominance. The discussion extends to the financial implications of rising Japanese yields and the rapid progression of AI toward singularity, concluding with a call for rigorous media literacy to navigate an era of sophisticated information warfare.
