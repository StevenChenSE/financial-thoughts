The conversation about artificial intelligence replacing human workers has reached a fever pitch. Tech giants are cutting thousands of jobs while promising a future of abundance. Elon Musk proposes "universal high income" as the solution to mass unemployment. Meanwhile, the robots are getting faster, the AI agents are getting smarter, and workers everywhere are wondering: is my job next?

We sat down with Alexandra Shagalinska, Associate Research Associate at Harvard Law School and AI futurist, to cut through the hype and examine what's really happening—and what's actually coming next.

---

## The UBI Obsession: Silicon Valley's Favorite Answer to Automation

Elon Musk recently made waves on X with a bold proposal: "Universal high income via checks issued by the federal government is the best way to deal with unemployment caused by AI. AI robotics will produce goods and services far in excess of the increase in the money supply, so there would not be inflation."

It's a compelling vision—but Shagalinska sees significant problems with it.

**The history nobody mentions:** Universal Basic Income has been discussed for at least 300 years and has never been successfully implemented. The closest experiments—a two-year pilot in Finland, for instance—provided supplemental income, not primary support. These pilots tell us almost nothing about long-term behavioral effects.

**UBI isn't enough—even if it worked.** Sam Altman proposed $3,000 per month for every American. "That's a great bonus," Shagalinska notes, "but that's definitely not something that can support you on a monthly basis." Musk's response: UBI isn't ambitious enough. We need *high* income.

**The socialist irony.** "He's proposing the government tax AI companies heavily and distribute that income to citizens, which frankly is a bit of a socialist idea—unlikely for a libertarian," Shagalinska observes.

**The inflation paradox.** Musk claims AI will produce so much abundance that分发更多钱不会导致通货膨胀. Shagalinska's response: "I could only assume that this is a world without money, really, where money is just symbolic."

## Reality Check: Robots Aren't There Yet

Despite the breathless predictions, Shagalinska sees a significant gap between the vision and the current state.

**Robotics isn't where Musk wants it to be.** "Robots are not as skilled as I think Elon Musk would wish them to be," she says. "They're definitely not replacing the majority of work that humans are performing today."

**Productivity gains are real but modest.** While generative AI has delivered measurable improvements, the productivity spike has been around 16-20%—not the transformative leap that would justify mass unemployment or universal high income.

**The timeline problem.** Musk frequently predicts rapid, imminent change. But Shagalinska isn't convinced: "I just don't feel that this is a realistic timeline."

Her interpretation? "He's throwing in another concept for other people to worry about. It's just a way to mediate the fears surrounding massive unemployment."

## The Tech Sector Is Already Feeling It

Recent announcements confirm that something is shifting in the job market:

- **Meta:** Cutting 10% of its workforce
- **Microsoft:** Voluntary retirement program for up to 7% of employees, primarily senior directors
- **Amazon:** Most widespread layoffs in company history
- **Total:** Approximately 92,000 tech employees laid off in 2026 so far

Shagalinska sees two factors at play:

1. **COVID overhiring correction.** Companies expanded aggressively during the pandemic and are now trimming. The Meta Metaverse project is a prime example—a bet that didn't pay off, resulting in complete team dissolution.

2. **Agentic AI's emergence.** For the past four months, a new category of AI has been transforming the industry.

---

## Agentic AI: The Real Productivity Revolution

This is where Shagalinska sounds the loudest alarm—and the most compelling detail.

Agentic AI represents a qualitative leap beyond chatbots. These systems can:

- Make payments autonomously
- Run different operational loops
- Query databases and compile results
- Send emails and write reports
- Fulfill end-to-end tasks

**The tribal structure.** In software development, these agents are forming their own hierarchies: "You have an agent that is an orchestrator, and that orchestrator is orchestrating the work of other smaller agents that are doing other things, reporting back. It resembles a big organization—or even a society."

**The productivity implications are staggering.** In IT specifically, one skilled developer using agentic AI tools can now accomplish what previously required 50 people. "I would say that there are jobs where the spike in productivity is incredible," Shagalinska notes.

This explains the layoffs. This is why five people can now do what 50 did. And this is just the beginning.

## What Jobs Survive? The Human Orchestrator

If hand-coding becomes obsolete, what remains?

**"We need orchestrators."** The future worker supervises AI agents, reviews their outputs, and maintains accountability. "Somebody has to really review what those agents have done. They're obviously not flawless—they do make mistakes still—and there has to be some human quality assurance."

**Coding for understanding, not employment.** Students at Harvard and MIT are already questioning why they need to learn to code. Shagalinska's answer: you'll still need it to understand what AI is doing, but fewer people will do it professionally. The curriculum is actively being reconsidered.

**Beyond coding: everything with "tech plus creativity."** UX design, app development, PowerPoint creation, Excel work—"All of it can be done with AI very effectively," she says.

## The AI Arms Race: Anthropic's B2B Gambit

While OpenAI dominates headlines, Anthropic is making a quieter, potentially more disruptive move.

**The revenue gap:** OpenAI led Anthropic $6 billion to $1 billion at the start of 2025. By early 2026, that's widened to $20 billion versus $9 billion. But Anthropic is growing faster in percentage terms.

**Anthropic's strategy:** Specialized models for specific industries—financial services, legal services, research. They're not building a generic encyclopedia. They're building surgical tools.

**OpenAI's Achilles heel:** Despite launching new products and image generation capabilities aimed at replacing Canva or Figma, "they have not been doing a great job at B2B." Anthropic has.

The differentiator? Trust. "After the whole situation with the Pentagon, some users decided to follow Anthropic because of certain values or principles. They seem to be a company with a particular trajectory, mission, vision—and to some businesses, that's persuasive."

## Claude Mythos: The Security Wild West

Anthropic has released something called **Claude Mythos**—a frontier model positioned above Claude Opus, specifically designed to spot software vulnerabilities.

**What we know:**

- It sits above Claude Opus in capability
- It's incredibly effective at identifying bugs in core systems
- Access is deliberately restricted to vetted organizations and governments
- Microsoft and "a couple of other giant players" have received access

**The dual-use problem.** The same capabilities that protect banking systems from attacks can be used to exploit them. "Getting something like Mythos is obviously for a malicious player... a great gift."

**The timing matters.** Cyber attacks on cryptocurrency platforms and OpenAI itself have "intensified over the past couple of months." Mythos exposes vulnerabilities—but also creates temptation.

"It can be a protective shield, but it can also be a very, very massive weapon."

## China Bets on Physical AI

In a striking contrast to Western focus on language models, China is making a massive bet on **physical AI and robotics**.

**The marathon that made headlines.** A humanoid robot recently competed in—though "won" might be generous—a human marathon. More impressive than the result is what it represents: battery technology, autonomous systems, cooling systems all advancing simultaneously.

**China's philosophy differs.** They're not prioritizing generative AI. Instead, they want AI that changes physical infrastructure—builds bridges, constructs cities, improves daily life.

**The physical intuition breakthrough.** Language models "have no physical intuitions. They don't know real life. They don't have experiences of our world. That has its own limitations." China believes that robots navigating the physical world can push AI to the next threshold of capability.

**The cultural gap.** In China, robotics generates optimism. "Everybody wants to build them. Everybody wants to own a robot at home." In Western civilization, Shagalinska observes, physical robots often feel "scary." There's a narrative component to technological adoption—and China is winning that narrative.

## The Endgame: AI Running Entire Companies?

Consider this scenario: A company in the UAE used AI to design a jet engine and manufacture it in two weeks. Could AI eventually:

- Design products autonomously?
- Manufacture them in automated factories?
- Sell via optimized e-commerce distribution?
- Run the entire operation from A to Z?

**"It's not unlikely for me," Shagalinska says.** Where processes are formalized, clear, and systematic, AI can step in.

**The pieces already exist:**

- Autonomous factories ("dark factories" in China, where robots work without light)
- Agentic AI for sales and operations
- Generative systems for design
- Robotics for physical labor

"When that is actually colliding, you might see incredible change. It's not implausible for something like that to happen in the next years."

## Key Takeaways

1. **Elon Musk's "Universal High Income" faces serious obstacles:** The technology isn't mature enough to produce the abundance he describes, and the economic mechanisms for funding it remain undefined.

2. **Agentic AI is the real revolution, not chatbots:** While generative AI has delivered modest productivity gains (16-20%), agentic AI is enabling small teams to accomplish what once required massive workforces—especially in software development.

3. **Tech sector layoffs have dual causes:** COVID overhiring is being corrected, but agentic AI is genuinely reducing headcount needs in ways that will accelerate.

4. **Human coders may become obsolete, but human supervisors won't:** The future requires fewer, more skilled workers who can orchestrate and quality-control AI agents. Coding itself may become primarily educational rather than vocational.

5. **China and the West are playing different games:** While OpenAI vs. Anthropic dominate Western AI news, China is making a major bet on physical AI and robotics that could define the next era of automation.

The question isn't whether AI will transform work—it's whether our institutions, policies, and preparations are moving fast enough to adapt.
