## Interest Rates Headed Higher Despite Political Pressure

Despite pressure from the White House for lower interest rates, Horitz believes the Federal Reserve will be forced to continue its tightening campaign. The PCE inflation measure—the Fed's preferred gauge—recently hit a three-year high, with core PCE rising to 3.4-4% and headline inflation at 4.1%.

"If you look at the bigger picture, credit card debt is so deep, which is a big high number of interest rate over 14% of the people are 90 days past due," Horitz noted. "You've got a situation that the peer-to-peer lenders, Prosper, the Cabbage, their average rate is in the twelves and 13s, which tells you that the free market can handle higher rates."

Horitz predicted that 10-year Treasury notes, currently just under 5%, could reach 6% by year end, arguing that higher rates are actually healthier for the economy.

"The days of the low interest rate, the printing more money, the bailing out the banks—all that stuff really has been," he said. "Although the stock market has performed well, you have to look at the average consumer, the consumer is struggling. You have the K-shaped economy."

## Why Gold at $4,000 Makes Sense

Despite higher interest rates traditionally being bearish for gold, Horitz recently initiated a long position in the precious metal around $4,000 per ounce. His reasoning centers on technical analysis and market timing.

"I thought $4,000 was a real solid level to get in. If you look at a chart, it's a solid level. Big whole numbers are solid," Horitz explained. "And again, we were down, you know, over 25% since the highs. At some point, I'm going to draw a line in the sand and say, 'Okay, I'm going to trade this.'"

Horitz believes the gold market has already priced in the negative effects of higher interest rates, while the stock market has not. He expects gold to rally toward $4,500 before reassessing his position.

"The gold markets have already accounted for the higher interest rates and the other problems that would be typically a problem in the gold market," he said. "Those I think have been priced in based on the 30% haircut. So for the equities to catch up, we would need a 30 to 40% haircut there as well, which is what I'm expecting."

## Stock Market "Delusional" and Overvalued

When asked to characterize the current state of the stock market, Horitz didn't hesitate: "I think the market is well overvalued. I think it is delusional."

The S&P 500 was trading near 7,300 points at the time of the interview, with the Nasdaq showing particular weakness despite some intraday recovery. Horitz highlighted concerning technical patterns.

"If you look at the chart, you see that we're making lower highs on a regular basis and lower lows as we head down," he observed. "The NASDAQ was down 400 and then Micron's earnings came out right at the bell and the NASDAQ turned up and rallied 700 points in less than two minutes. To me, that's telling you that the tops of these markets are in."

The trader remains short the S&P and specifically recommends selling high-flying tech names like Nvidia, which he views as extremely overvalued. He noted that smaller, lesser-known names that experienced big rallies have already been cut in half, and this typically signals the beginning of broader market weakness.

"Palantir got down to $106 before coming back. Look at what happened to SpaceX—went to $240 and now it's back to $155," Horitz pointed out. "So once again, these are signs that are telling you that markets are much overvalued and they're certainly going to first, the first sectors they picked on were some of the Dow sector."

## Key Takeaways for Investors

Horitz's analysis suggests several concerning trends that investors should monitor closely. The combination of returning risky lending practices, elevated inflation, rising interest rates, and extreme market valuations creates an environment reminiscent of pre-2008 conditions. His recommendation to avoid tech overvaluation and consider hard assets like gold reflects a defensive positioning strategy.

While acknowledging that "you can't fight the markets," Horitz maintains that the current price action suggests the equity markets are significantly overvalued and overdue for a correction. Whether that correction comes today, tomorrow, or over the coming months, the underlying fundamentals point to mounting pressure on both the housing market and stock market.
