## Navigating Wealth in an Era of Fragile Trust

As we move into the second half of 2026, the long-held belief that governments can borrow without consequence and central banks can create money without destroying confidence appears to be breaking down. In this insightful discussion, Clive Thompson, a former Swiss banker and retired wealth manager with nearly 50 years of experience, joins to share his perspective on the current financial landscape. From the economic realities of China to the looming crisis in U.S. debt, Thompson offers a roadmap for protecting wealth in a potentially unforgiving financial system.

### China’s Economic Reality: Luxury Malls and Automation

Thompson recently returned from an extensive trip to China, where he observed a stark contrast between the country's technological advancements and its consumer sentiment. While luxury shopping malls have replaced street markets, they are largely empty, serving more as social hubs than retail destinations. "The shops are empty," Thompson noted, highlighting that consumers are hesitant to spend due to job insecurity, despite rising ownership of high-tech electric vehicles like Teslas.

Simultaneously, automation and AI are permeating daily life. From robot waitresses to QR code ordering, the integration of technology is accelerating. Thompson also encountered unique cultural practices, such as restaurants collecting phone numbers to track potential food poisoning incidents, underscoring the rigorous, albeit sometimes unsettling, standards of safety and accountability. Despite the visible economic slowdown, the presence of advanced AI tools like DeepSeek and Quen indicates a robust technological shift, even as Western access is restricted within the country.

### The Fragility of Government Debt and Sentiment

A central theme of the discussion is the sustainability of government debt. Thompson argues that the market is no longer driven by fundamental numbers like CPI or GDP, which are often revised or manipulated, but by sentiment. This sentiment can swing rapidly, as seen in the UK’s gilt crisis a few years ago, where a poorly received budget triggered a market panic that required emergency intervention by the Bank of England.

Thompson points out that there is no magic debt-to-GDP ratio that guarantees stability; Japan’s debt exceeds 300% of its GDP, yet it remains stable for now. However, the U.S. is facing a critical juncture. With a $2 trillion deficit and an interest burden nearing $870 billion, the government is essentially borrowing from Peter to pay Paul. Thompson suggests that if 30-year Treasury yields approach 6%, the Federal Reserve may be forced to intervene, buying treasuries to cap yields. This "buyer of last resort" scenario would signal a one-way bet for investors, potentially driving capital away from bonds and into hard assets.

### Gold: The Anchor for a Monetary Reset

Thompson remains bullish on gold, not just as an inflation hedge, but as a tool to survive a potential monetary reset. "Gold is what will get me to the other side of what's coming," he stated. He believes that when the system restarts—whether through higher inflation, capital controls, or the implementation of Central Bank Digital Currencies (CBDCs)—holders of cash and government bonds are likely to lose value.

He highlights a historical precedent for how the U.S. Treasury might address its debt crisis: revaluing its gold holdings. In 1934, the U.S. government raised the price of gold from $20.67 to $35 per ounce, effectively creating money out of thin air by selling gold to the Federal Reserve and receiving irredeemable gold notes in return. Thompson suggests that revaluing gold to $15,000 or higher today could provide the Treasury with the liquidity needed to reduce debt or fund spending without issuing new treasuries, thereby lowering yields. This maneuver, while drastic, remains a viable contingency plan that has not yet been utilized.

### Silver: A Compelling Bull Case

While gold captures headlines, Thompson sees a strong case for silver. Physical silver inventories at the CME have been declining over the past three years, driven by industrial demand that exceeds mine supply. Although there is some rotation to copper in solar panels, the electronics industry’s appetite for silver continues to grow. Thompson notes that above-ground stocks are diminishing, and as they do, sellers become more reluctant to part with their holdings at current prices. He recently purchased a kilo of silver coins, emphasizing that silver’s dual role as a monetary and industrial metal makes it a compelling asset.

### Lessons from the 1970s and Investor Psychology

Thompson’s personal journey with precious metals began in the UK during the high-inflation 1970s. Influenced by his grandfather, who taught him the value of gold sovereigns through simple tricks involving chocolate bars and pound notes, Thompson started collecting silver coins and later bought his first Krugerrand. He has never sold an ounce of gold, viewing it as a permanent store of value to be passed down to heirs.

He warns against the biggest mistake investors make: putting too much money into their favorite investment. This leads to emotional decision-making, causing investors to take profits too early or hold onto losses too long. Instead, he advocates for a disciplined asset allocation strategy, keeping positions small enough to avoid emotional distress. He shares his own allocation, which has recently shifted to reduce equity exposure from 55% to 50%, while increasing holdings in gold, silver, and a small percentage of Bitcoin to prepare for various scenarios.

### Financial Education for the Next Generation

Finally, Thompson discusses his initiative to make financial literacy accessible through his "Little Trot" book series. Inspired by historical financial frauds and the complexity of the 2008 crisis, he created illustrated stories for children that simplify concepts like inflation, savings, and banking risks. These books are available for free download on his website, aiming to equip the next generation with the knowledge to navigate financial pitfalls.

### Conclusion

As confidence in government debt wavers and the financial system faces unprecedented pressures, Clive Thompson’s advice is clear: diversify, hold tangible assets, and prepare for the unexpected. Whether through a gradual rise in inflation or a sudden monetary reset, gold and silver remain critical components of a resilient portfolio. By understanding the mechanics of money and maintaining emotional discipline, investors can protect their wealth in an increasingly uncertain world.
