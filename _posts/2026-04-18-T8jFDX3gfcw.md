## The Iran Ceasefire: A Strategic Reset

The most pressing question on everyone's mind is what happened to the Iran war. After weeks of escalating tension, the conflict appears to have been put on ice. According to Professor Jen, this isn't defeat—it's recalculation.

"I think the ceasefire is a reset, because Americans were clearly losing the war. They were on the back foot, and this ceasefire gives a chance for the Americans to reposition themselves, rethink their strategy, and go into phase two."

Behind the scenes, the pieces are already moving. The Indonesian defense minister recently visited Washington D.C. and signed an agreement with Pete Hegseth that will allow Americans to use Indonesian airspace—a move toward full military cooperation that would give America control over the Strait of Malacca.

"The Strait of Malacca is the biggest choke point in the world," Professor Jen noted. "China gets about 80% of its oil from the Strait of Malacca. If America were to choke it off, it would collapse the East Asian economies."

Simultaneously, the USS George H.W. Bush—the third aircraft carrier—has been moving toward the theater, bringing approximately 10,000 Marines into the region and bringing total troop levels to around 60,000. With those numbers, Professor Jen expects a ground incursion of some sort.

On the home front, Trump has requested $1.5 trillion for next year's Pentagon budget and will likely get it. There's now automatic draft registration for young American men starting in December, and the Pentagon has asked General Motors and Ford to begin war munitions manufacturing.

"Trump has announced a naval blockade against Iran," Professor Jen explained. "The American Navy won't go near the Strait of Hormuz because then they're within range of Iranian ballistic missiles and drone strikes. But what they can do is embargo ships in the Indian Ocean."

By announcing a naval blockade against the Strait of Hormuz but operating within the Indian Ocean, America is effectively imposing a worldwide blockade against any enemy ships breaking sanctions—including Russian and Chinese tankers.
