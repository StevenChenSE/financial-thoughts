In a recent interview, veteran market strategist David Hunter laid out a strikingly bullish near-term outlook for equities and precious metals, paired with a stark warning about a looming global bust in 2027. His forecasts are not for the faint of heart: he sees the S&P 500 soaring to **9,500**, the NASDAQ hitting **32,000**, and gold reaching **$6,800**—all within a matter of months.

## The Bullish Backdrop: Geopolitics and Sentiment

Hunter believes the current market correction—driven by fears over the Iran conflict and inflation—is short-lived. He argues that geopolitical developments under the current administration are being executed with a focus on swift resolution, not prolonged entanglement.

> "Yes, in the very short run there's uncertainty... but there's a lot to be bullish about in terms of what he's done and what he's doing," Hunter stated. "That as a backdrop is another reason why I think this market short-term is correcting, but I don't think it's going to last very long. And I think there's big upside in the second quarter."

He sees a path to resolving tensions in the Middle East and the Western Hemisphere, which would remove major overhangs. Combined with overwhelmingly negative Wall Street sentiment—where many believe the market has already topped—this sets the stage for a powerful contrarian rally.

## Staggering Price Targets and Timelines

Hunter has aggressively raised his targets in recent months. His current forecasts represent gains of **40-50%** from recent levels:

*   **S&P 500:** 9,500
*   **NASDAQ:** 32,000
*   **Dow Jones:** 65,000
*   **Russell 2000:** 3,800

He believes these moves could happen with stunning speed. "The last one was those kind of gains in six months. I think this one could be that kind of gain in **three or four months**," he said.

His outlook for precious metals is equally explosive. After a parabolic move and a sharp correction earlier this year, he sees a powerful resumption of the bull market.

> "I raised my target in January and then raised it again during that first sell-off... I'm at **$180 on silver**. So, and I think we could be there this summer... and I'm at **$6,800 on gold**."

He views current weakness in the metals as a buying opportunity, noting that related mining stocks could see even more dramatic gains, with some potential "triples from here."

## The Oil Paradox and the 2027 Cliff

While bullish on almost everything else in the near term, Hunter holds a bearish view on oil. He believes the spike from the Iran conflict will fade quickly.

> "As soon as this thing gets resolved... you will see oil go back into certainly back into the $60s." Looking further ahead, he added, "I have a pretty negative view about next year in terms of a global bust. I think ultimately could see oil down to **$30**."

This sets up his most critical long-term warning. Hunter believes the massive rally he forecasts for 2024 will represent a historic, generational top—akin to Japan's 1989 peak. A devastating global bust will follow in **2027**.

His reasoning centers on extreme systemic leverage, which now far exceeds 2008 levels. "On the downside it really exacerbates the downside. And I think we're going to see a banking crisis that could exceed the banking crisis of 2008-09," he warned, highlighting vulnerabilities in Canadian, Australian, European, and Asian banks, with China as a "wild card."

## Investor Takeaways: Focus on Risk/Reward

For investors navigating the current volatility, Hunter's advice is to focus on the asymmetric risk/reward, not on trying to pinpoint the exact bottom.

> "Everybody's focused on the weakness because it's right in front of you. But they're not paying attention to the risk/reward, which is... the upside is 40 or 50%. And in the miners and the metals, it's much more than that."

He suggests the market may have another week or two of choppiness, but the stage is set for a major upward move as we move into the second quarter. The fuel for this move, in his view, is a combination of resilient fundamentals, strong earnings, and deeply negative sentiment that has kept investors "with one leg out the door" throughout this entire bull run.
