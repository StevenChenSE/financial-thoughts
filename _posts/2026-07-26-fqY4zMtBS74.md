## The End of the Petrodollar and the Rise of a New Monetary Order

The global financial landscape is undergoing a seismic shift, characterized by the decline of US imperial influence and the erosion of the petrodollar system. In a recent interview, William Mitchell, founder of the Commodity Discovery Fund and author of *The Big Reset*, outlines how geopolitical tensions, specifically in the Middle East, are accelerating this transition. He argues that the United States’ strategy in the region has failed, damaging Gulf infrastructure and undermining the security guarantees that once underpinned the dollar’s dominance. As Gulf nations realize that US bases are liabilities rather than assets, they are increasingly looking toward bilateral trade in currencies other than the dollar, with China emerging as the primary beneficiary of this realignment.

## Central Banks Drive Gold’s New Bull Market

A critical component of this monetary reset is the unprecedented accumulation of physical gold by central banks. For five consecutive years, central banks have purchased approximately 1,000 tons of gold annually, representing one-third of world production. This trend is not speculative but strategic; central bankers are hedging against the decline of the US dollar-centered system. Recent data indicates that total gold holdings by central banks now exceed their US Treasury holdings.

Major institutions are taking note of this shift. A report by Deutsche Bank, echoing Mitchell’s thesis, suggests that gold is returning to its role within the financial system. The bank projects that gold prices could reach between $12,000 and $14,000, driven by the potential for central banks to redirect trillions in Treasury assets toward gold. This institutional demand, rather than retail speculation, is the primary engine behind the current precious metals rally.

## Sovereign Debt Crisis and the Risk of Financial Collapse

The path to this new monetary regime is fraught with risk, particularly regarding sovereign debt. Mitchell warns that rising interest rates, which have been in a long-term downtrend since 1980, are now entering an uptrend that poses a severe threat to global financial stability. With US national debt approaching $40 trillion and similar burdens in China, Japan, and Europe, the market is approaching a point where countries may struggle to finance their budgets.

Mitchell identifies the risk of a full-blown sovereign debt crisis as one of the most significant dangers to the current system. Unlike previous crises, this one could stem from the core of the dollar-based system itself. The US has historically avoided such crises due to its ability to print dollars, but as demand for Treasuries wanes, this insulation is disappearing. The weaponization of the dollar has further accelerated the de-dollarization process, with countries like Russia and China actively reducing their Treasury holdings.

## Copper: The Next Critical Commodity

While gold and silver remain central to the monetary reset, copper is emerging as a critical commodity for the industrial future. Driven by the AI revolution and the transition to electric vehicles, copper faces a severe supply deficit. Older mines are depleting, and new discoveries are insufficient to meet growing physical demand. Major producers like BHP, Rio Tinto, and Codelco have announced production declines, creating a structural shortage.

Mitchell highlights copper as a potential safe haven in its own right, noting its resilience during recent market corrections. The physical demand for copper exceeds production, and increasing supply is difficult due to geological and political constraints. This supply-demand imbalance positions copper for a significant bull market, potentially rivaling the status of oil in terms of strategic importance.
