## Introduction: The Market’s Halloween Scare

The stock market behaved like a horror movie this week, particularly for investors holding technology stocks. In a dramatic display of volatility, major tech names suffered significant losses, dragging down the broader indices. However, according to Gareth Soloway, President of Verified Investing, the situation is more nuanced than a simple broad-market collapse. While tech was "killed" by disappointing earnings and sector rotation, defensive sectors held their ground. The critical question now is whether this dip represents a buying opportunity or the beginning of a deeper correction driven by macroeconomic pressures.

## A Rotation, Not a Broad Sell-Off

A key distinction highlighted in Soloway’s analysis is that the recent market decline was not a universal sell-off. While the S&P 500 dropped approximately 2.5%, this figure masks the underlying reality: most stocks within the index actually rose on Friday. The decline was driven almost exclusively by the tech and communications sectors, which pulled the index down due to their heavy weighting.

The **S&P 500 heat map** revealed a clear rotation of capital:
*   **Defensive Gains:** Consumer staples, energy, basic materials, utilities, and healthcare all posted gains.
*   **Tech Losses:** Communications and technology were the primary drag on performance.

This suggests that money is not leaving the market entirely but rather rotating from high-growth tech stocks into defensive plays. Soloway notes that hedge fund managers, who typically charge "2 and 20" fees (2% management fee, 20% of profits), are incentivized to keep capital deployed. They prefer rotating between sectors rather than moving to cash, which generates no return for them.

## The Tech Earnings Crisis: CapEx and EPS Misses

The immediate catalyst for the tech sell-off was a series of disappointing earnings reports from major players like **Google**, **Intel**, **Nvidia**, **AMD**, and **Meta**. A recurring theme emerged across these companies: massive Capital Expenditure (CapEx) spending on AI infrastructure combined with significant misses on Earnings Per Share (EPS).

For example, Google’s reported EPS was $2.12, missing the consensus estimate of $3.01 by nearly 30%. While individual companies often miss expectations, a sector-wide pattern of high spending and low returns raises concerns about the sustainability of the AI trade. Investors are questioning whether the massive investments in data centers and chips will translate into near-term profitability.

### Key Tech Stock Performance
*   **Google:** Lost $500 billion in market cap over 48 hours; down 12% after-hours on Thursday.
*   **Intel:** Down 30%, marking its worst single-day performance since 1974.
*   **Nvidia:** Down 5%.
*   **AMD:** Down 10%.

## Intel: A Cautionary Tale for Short-Term Traders

Intel’s plunge was particularly notable given its status as a blue-chip stock rather than a speculative meme stock. Soloway identifies two main factors behind Intel's decline:
1.  **Overhyped Expectations:** The stock had been buoyed by the AI narrative and assumptions of special treatment from the US government.
2.  **Fundamental Weakness:** Recent earnings indicated that despite potential government backing, Intel is not currently benefiting enough to justify its valuation.

From a technical perspective, Soloway sees Intel approaching a long-term trend line dating back to August 2025. While he believes Intel will likely go lower over a 1-3 year horizon due to structural issues, he suggests short-term traders might look for a bounce off the **$60 level**.

## The Macro Driver: Oil and Geopolitics

According to Soloway, the single most important factor determining the market's next move is not tech earnings, but **oil prices**. With oil trading above $100 per barrel due to ongoing tensions involving Iran and the US, inflationary pressures are mounting. This "straw that breaks the camel's back" threatens both the consumer and corporate CapEx budgets.

Soloway argues that for the market to sustain its rally, oil needs to fall back to **$70–$80 per barrel**. If oil remains high:
*   Inflation will remain sticky, forcing the Federal Reserve to maintain higher rates.
*   Corporate spending (CapEx) will weaken as debt financing becomes more expensive.
*   The risk of a recession increases, which could ultimately force oil prices down through destroyed demand rather than a geopolitical deal.

The upcoming **midterm elections** on November 3rd add another layer of uncertainty. While Soloway views the election itself as a likely non-event for markets (favoring gridlock), he warns that post-election military escalation in Iran could spike oil prices to $120+, which would be detrimental to the economy.

## Bitcoin and Gold: Panic Selling vs. Safe Havens

Despite their status as alternative assets, **Bitcoin** and **Gold** were not spared in the recent sell-off. Soloway attributes this to panic selling, where investors liquidate all risk assets indiscriminately during times of fear.

### Bitcoin
Bitcoin broke below the $81,000 support level following news of a **Binance hack**, which shattered its perception as a safe haven. Key technical levels for Bitcoin include:
*   **Support:** $75,500. If this breaks, there is little support until **$66,000–$67,000**.
*   **Long-Term Outlook:** Soloway remains bullish on a 1-2 year horizon, targeting **$160,000** by January 2028 based on trend lines from the 2017 and 2021 bull markets.

### Gold
Gold fell because it had been bought primarily as a speculative "tech play" rather than a defensive hedge during its run-up to $5,600. As fear took over, those speculative buyers exited. Soloway expects gold to potentially retest **$3,500** in the short term but remains a long-term bull due to global debt and money supply expansion.

Given the heightened volatility (VIX in the mid-20s), Soloway advises investors to focus on "best of breed" companies. Rather than chasing high-risk, debt-heavy names like **CoreWeave** or **IonQ**, he recommends accumulating high-quality tech stocks that are currently beaten down but have strong fundamentals and diversified business models.

*   **Accumulating:** Google (due to valuation and diversification via Waymo), Oracle (technical support holds).
*   **Avoiding:** CoreWeave (high debt load, riskier profile), Micron (supply glut concerns with new factories coming online in 12-18 months).

## Conclusion: Bullish Structure, Fragile Foundation

Soloway maintains that the S&P 500 remains technically bullish as long as it holds key trend lines dating back to 2022 and 2023. The potential upside target is just above **8,000**. However, this bullish case is entirely dependent on oil prices stabilizing or falling. If oil remains high, the Fed may be forced to hike rates, crushing economic growth and invalidating the bull thesis. For now, patience, defensive positioning, and selective accumulation of high-quality tech stocks are the prudent strategies.
