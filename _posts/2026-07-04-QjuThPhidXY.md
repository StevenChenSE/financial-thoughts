Economist David Woo delivers stark warning about tech sector and what happens when the AI bubble finally bursts

David Woo, CEO and founder of David Woo Unbound, recently appeared on a financial show to discuss the unwinding of the tech trade, the fragile state of the AI boom, and what it all means for markets going forward. With semiconductors falling dramatically—especially overseas in the Korea Cosby index—and softer than expected jobs numbers, Woo argues that the foundation supporting the AI rally is crumbling. His prediction: NASDAQ could be down 20% "like in no time" once the AI story unwinds.

"Once the AI bubble has burst, I'm telling you the US economy is going to hit a break and go into a recession," Woo stated unequivocally. The economist, who has built a reputation for making accurate macro calls, pointed to the upcoming Q2 earnings from Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta as the potential catalyst. "Even a little tiny hint that the capex story is over, I'm telling you NASDAQ will be down 20% like in no time."

## The AI Bubble: What's Really Going On

Woo's thesis centers on a critical misunderstanding in the market about what has been propelling the AI trade. While most investors attribute the rally to genuine AI advances, Woo argues it's actually been driven by pent-up demand from policy uncertainty that subsided last year, combined with R&D tax provisions that made AI investments suddenly attractive.

The economist is particularly bearish on the two companies he says underpin the entire AI ecosystem: OpenAI and Anthropic. "80% of all AI revenue goes to these two companies," Woo explained. "They're the ones who then pay the hyperscalers for computing power, and then the hyperscalers turn around to buy chips from Nvidia, and then Nvidia goes out and basically buys chips from TSMC."

Woo sees serious monetization problems ahead. He pointed to Meta's recent push into cloud computing as evidence that even major players are struggling with AI returns. "When Meta says they're going to start renting out their excess computing power, they're basically admitting they bought too many chips, they overbuilt their AI infrastructure, and now they cannot pay for it because their models are no good."

## The Mythos Problem and Regulatory Risk

The narrative around Anthropic's Mythos model has been central to AI enthusiasm, but Woo sees it as a double-edged sword. While Mythos initially re-energized the AI trade by suggesting a breakthrough toward artificial general intelligence, Woo argues it has actually dramatically increased regulatory risk.

"The problem is that Mythos is so good that it's been held back for national security reasons," Woo explained. "We now know that any hackers upon accessing Mythos will be 100,000 times more dangerous and more destabilizing. This is why the Five Eyes intelligence alliance issued an unprecedented warning."

With only 150 users having access to Mythos nearly three months after its release, Woo questions how Anthropic will ever monetize its research and development. Compounding the issue, Chinese startups have already developed AI models with comparable capabilities to Mythos, undermining the competitive advantage of American AI leaders.

## Why Gold Struggles Until AI Bursts

One of Woo's most counterintuitive insights is that gold's biggest problem is AI itself. "Gold is going to continue to struggle until the AI bubble has burst," Woo said. "Real yields have been the biggest headache for gold bulls."

The connection works through the economy: AI-driven stock gains have created a positive wealth effect, encouraging consumer spending and supporting economic growth. Higher real yields—driven by a stronger economy—make inflation-hedge assets like gold less attractive. Woo expects real yields to "keep going higher until the AI bubble bursts, and that's when gold is going to get real help."

He drew a parallel to the dotcom era: "Going into the final phase of the dotcom bubble from 1999 to 2000, real yields were going up, the dollar was going up, NASDAQ was going through the roof, and gold did poorly. It was not until the dotcom bubble burst, the dollar started to go down, and the Fed started to cut interest rates, that gold got this huge boost. That lasted a whole decade."

## Semiconductor Sector Faces Reckoning

The recent semiconductor selloff, which saw the Korea Cosby index fall 8% and Japan's most valuable company tumble more than 13%, reflects growing concerns Woo has been articulating for months. The shift from training chips—which Nvidia dominates—to inference chips changes the competitive landscape significantly.

"Training chips are very difficult to design, so Nvidia doesn't have much competition there," Woo noted. "But for applications, you need inference chips, and anyone can make those. This is why even OpenAI has now designed their own inference chips."

Memory chip makers like Samsung, Micron, and SK Hynix have been the bright spot, but Woo sees that rally fading. "These high bandwidth memory chips are not that sophisticated. It just happened that there's a massive shortage because these companies were on their knees two years ago after COVID. New capacity is coming online by the end of this year, and the Chinese are pretty good at making these chips too."

## Iran War Reveals US Military Limitations

In a separate but related discussion, Woo expressed alarm at what he called "the first proxy war between the United States and China," referring to the Iran conflict. He cited reporting from major outlets including the New York Times and Washington Post confirming that Iran still retains around 70% of its missiles and mobile launchers despite the US launching more than 1,000 Tomahawk cruise missiles—roughly ten times the annual procurement rate.

"There's no doubt in my mind the US got its ass kicked in Iran," Woo said bluntly. "The US spends a trillion dollars a year on defense. Where does that money go? The country that spends more money on defense than the next ten countries combined cannot basically blow away Iran that represents 1% of US GDP."

Woo connected this to broader concerns about US hegemony: "The Chinese are probably looking at this laughing. I think the US military capabilities have become a joke in Europe, in China, in Russia. Everybody's saying, 'What? You spend a trillion dollars?' It doesn't show."

## Oil and the Geopolitical Outlook

Despite recent weakness in oil prices—back down to around $70 from highs of $120—Woo remains bullish on the energy sector. He pointed to the Lebanon ceasefire agreement brokered by Secretary of State Marco Rubio as a potential game-changer.

"For the first time, Lebanon essentially acknowledged Israel's right to exist," Woo explained. "The Christians and Sunni Arabs in Lebanon don't want Iran to get more involved in their domestic affairs, so they joined forces with Israel to keep Iran out."

Woo expects oil to start heading higher within a week after July 4th and the funeral for Iran's supreme leader. He also believes Iran is in a stronger negotiating position than the US acknowledges. "The Iranians believe they can take pain more than Trump can. Trump cannot tolerate any pain. Stock market goes down 5%, he's already freaking out."

## Bitcoin and Stable Coins: Different Stories

On cryptocurrency, Woo distinguishes between Bitcoin and stable coins. "Trump is not interested in Bitcoin. Trump if there's anything Trump is interested in is stable coins because Trump understands one thing very well: the dollar needs to remain strong if the US is to continue its hegemony."

Woo sees stable coins as "the dollar's projection into the digital space" and potential big buyers of US government bonds. Bitcoin, meanwhile, has limited upside in his view. "If the AI bubble bursts, a lot of retail investors are going to be sitting on a lot of losses and they're going to be liquidating everything, probably Bitcoin as well. At the end of the day, Bitcoin is held mainly by retail investors."

## Woo's Current Positions and Market Outlook

Woo shared his current trading positions, which reflect his bearish AI thesis:

- Long gold
- Long oil
- Short NASDAQ 100
- Long steepeners (long 5-year treasuries, short 30-year treasuries)

"These two things—oil and NASDAQ—are going to control all other markets," Woo explained. "If you tell me where NASDAQ is going to be, where oil is going to be, I'll tell you exactly what's going to happen to all other markets."

The economist acknowledged that his AI bearish call was early—the market rallied significantly in April before pulling back again—but he remains confident in the thesis. "I would be the first one to admit I did not see the massive rally in Micron, Samsung, and SK Hynix. That has been the biggest story of 2026. But that's going to unwind later this year."

Woo concluded with a sobering assessment for investors: "There's no asymmetric trades right now. The market is not pricing in the scenario we're discussing. Prediction markets show only a 9% chance of recession this year. It may still be an early trade, but when the AI bubble bursts, there will be tears in the streets."
