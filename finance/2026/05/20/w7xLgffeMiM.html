Alex Crraer: Iran Ceasefire Fragile, Oil Disruption Could Reach 32% | Capital Cosm

May 20, 2026 2402 words
Watch Source Video

The numbers are staggering: up to 32% of global oil supply could vanish from markets if the Iran ceasefire collapses. We’re watching the largest disruption in oil market history unfold in real-time, and yet most people haven’t grasped what’s coming. Alex Crraer, geopolitical strategist and former financial industry professional, joined Capital Cosm’s Danny on May 11, 2026, to break down a situation that could reshape global economics for years.

“The oil disruption may go supernova,” Crraer warned. “What we’ve had so far as a result of this war is about 8% of the global oil supply has been taken off the market—maybe up to 12%. And this has been the largest disruption in the history of oil markets. And I don’t think it’s even reflected in the current price.”

The Iran Ceasefire: Fragile by Design

The ceasefire between the United States and Iran is hanging by threads. Both sides continue exchanging limited strikes—swapping jabs without crossing into full-scale resumption. Yet the situation remains dangerously unstable.

Why Neither Side Wants Full Escalation

  • Iran faces catastrophic consequences from large-scale escalation. The regime understands that provoking a full military response could devastate their infrastructure and leadership.
  • Trump has a critical upcoming trip to China scheduled for the following week. Sources indicate the Chinese administration has signaled that any escalation from the U.S.—particularly a return to bombing Iran—would jeopardize Trump’s Beijing visit entirely.
  • Netanyahu must also account for this diplomatic timeline. Israeli actions will likely remain constrained until after the China meetings conclude.

“Both sides will be careful not to escalate the conflict too much,” Crraer explained. “Trump has painted himself into a corner. He has no way to militarily prevail. He can escalate and wreak a whole lot of damage on Iran. But that’s going to come at a very, very steep price.”

The Nuclear Question

Trump has consistently framed the conflict around preventing Iran from acquiring nuclear weapons. Yet U.S. intelligence assessments tell a different story entirely:

Since 2007, the complete collective of U.S. intelligence agencies has maintained a high-confidence assessment that Iran stopped working on nuclear weapons in 2003. This assessment has been reiterated multiple times since—including during the current Trump administration.

  • Tulsi Gabbert testified in recent months that Iranians are not working on a nuclear weapon, stopped in 2003, and show no indication of restarting.
  • Iran’s spiritual leadership issued fatwas against nuclear weapons on two separate occasions.

“People in intelligence agencies know all this,” Crraer noted. “It’s just that the media is poisoning the well because there’s a segment of the political class in the United States who are very powerful and who dominate over Western media who desperately wanted this war.”

The current leadership situation remains unclear after U.S. and Israeli actions eliminated previous Iranian spiritual leaders. The son of assassinated leader Ali Khamenei—Mustafa Khamenei—now occupies the position. Notably, fatwas against nuclear weapons are not random Islamic rulings. The logic is straightforward: nuclear weapons are inherently indiscriminate, killing non-combatants, which contradicts Islamic law prohibiting the killing of innocent civilians.

The 32% Oil Disruption Scenario

Current oil prices hover around $98 per barrel for WTI, with Brent crude slightly over $104. While this seems high compared to prices just months ago at $60, Crraer contextualizes this as relatively modest:

  • In 2022, oil reached $120 per barrel
  • In 2008, prices hit $140 per barrel

What Escalation Would Mean

Iranian officials have communicated their response plan if full-scale war resumes (though they state they would not initiate such escalation):

  • Destruction of Saudi Arabia’s oil facilities and pipelines transporting crude to the Red Sea
  • Devastation of UAE offshore oil installations
  • Broad destruction of energy facilities across the entire region

This scenario would push disruption from 8-12% to approximately 32% of global oil supply—a figure without modern precedent.

“Oil price could go anywhere,” Crraer said. “At this point, there’s no telling where the oil price might go. And I think the markets haven’t digested what has happened properly. Large-scale price events always span a period of time. It takes months, sometimes years, for price to catch up with the new reality.”

Hidden Consequences: Fertilizer and Food

Beyond immediate energy impacts, the Persian Gulf region serves as a critical hub for global fertilizer production. Disruptions to this supply chain will cascade through agricultural systems worldwide:

  • Reduced food production in coming months
  • Food shortages likely emerging
  • Potential breakdown of trade networks
  • Semiconductor shortages (due to regional manufacturing dependencies)
  • Metal and energy supply constraints

“We cannot be complacent,” Crraer emphasized. “Even though a lot of people want to see Trump humiliated and punished, wishing ill on Trump at the moment is too high a price to pay for everybody who would be collateral damage—collateral victims of this whole dumpster fire.”

China’s Path to Resolution

Crraer believes the Beijing trip represents a pivotal moment. China could potentially engineer a face-saving arrangement allowing Trump to declare victory domestically while wrapping up hostilities.

The Proposed Framework

Based on Crraer’s analysis of plausible diplomatic scenarios:

  1. Nuclear commitment: China guarantees Iran makes an irrevocable commitment to never develop nuclear weapons
  2. Inspection regime: A mutually agreed monitoring system—potentially using Chinese or Russian inspectors rather than the IAEA—verifies compliance
  3. Face-saving declaration: Trump announces he secured binding guarantees, backed by China and Russia, that Iran will never obtain nuclear weapons

“Iranians don’t want a nuclear weapon,” Crraer explained. “They don’t want to develop one. They don’t want to use one. So they could say, ‘Okay, since you’re pressing us so hard, we’ll give you this concession.’ And then Trump can come out and say, ‘Here’s what I got. I got a commitment guaranteed by China and Russia.’”

Strategic Calculations

For Trump, the calculus is clear: midterm elections loom, and economic performance will determine Republican success. A cooperative relationship with China—open trade rather than tariff escalations—would significantly boost economic prospects.

“Improving relationship with China and solving the Iran problem could be a boost for Trump,” Crraer noted. “That would be in everybody’s interest. Even though Trump crossed the line, even though he blundered tragically, allowing the conflict to reach its bitter end would hurt everyone—China, the United States, Iran, the entire world.”

Russia-Ukraine: The Endgame Approaches

Separately from the Iran situation, Crraer shared alarming intelligence about developments in Ukraine:

“I have a friend in Kiev,” Crraer revealed. “He has considerable experience in diplomacy and foreign policy. Today’s conversation was very different from our usual check-ins. What he told me is that the situation in Kiev is extremely tense, and he said something is going to erupt very imminently.”

Civil War on the Horizon

Crraer’s source, who has remained in Kyiv throughout the conflict, painted a grim picture:

  • People are walking around with AK-47s on the streets
  • The population is absolutely traumatized, pissed off, desperate, fatigued, tired, exhausted, and demoralized
  • His assessment: civil war will break out within four weeks maximum

“I asked him, ‘Do you mean the civil war is going to break out?’ And he said to me, ‘100%. You cannot imagine how bad the situation is.’”

Russia’s Readiness to Conclude

Independent information from another source suggests U.S.-Russia relations have soured significantly. The Russians now appear ready to wrap up the Ukraine conflict relatively soon—possibly within summer months.

“Whether that’s a major arrow advance against Ukrainian positions or some other scenario, I don’t know,” Crraer said. “But I think something is about to go down, and I think it’s going to be in Russia’s favor. Project Ukraine is about to sink.”

The American Betrayal Factor

The situation has been complicated by U.S. actions regarding Iran:

  • Trump launched strikes on Iran while negotiations were ongoing
  • 40-50 Iranian officials and the spiritual leader were killed mid-diplomatic process
  • The Russians observed this and lost confidence in the Trump administration entirely

“If they were ready to stab the Iranians in the back while negotiating with them, how can anybody trust them?” Crraer noted. “How can you build rapport with other leaders? How can you build a relationship of trust?”

Russian confidence in U.S. diplomacy has evaporated. Sources indicate the recent Victory Day celebration at the Russian embassy in Washington was notably frosty, with the ambassador declaring the process would continue to conclusion without regard for American preferences.

Europe’s Coming Collapse

Crraer’s outlook for Europe is stark: economic catastrophe looms as the Ukraine conflict winds down.

“I think we’re going to hit a Weimar Republic moment,” Crraer warned. “There’s going to be an economic collapse. Interest rates are going to spike. Bond prices are going to collapse. I think the euro and the British pound are going to collapse.”

The Trade of the Century

For investors, Crraer identifies a clear opportunity: short European bonds, particularly British gilts. French, German, and especially British bonds face existential pressure as the post-Ukraine reality sets in.

“This system is ready to be toppled,” Crraer stated. “Over the next two to three years, the trade of the century is going to be going short European bonds.”

European Preparations—or Lack Thereof

Reports indicate European governments are making unusual preparations:

  • Germany restricted men aged 18-45 from traveling abroad for more than three months without approval
  • Hospitals across France, Germany, and elsewhere have received notices to prepare for mass casualties
  • Conscription discussions are accelerating

Yet Crraer doubts Europe could mount a kinetic response:

“Europe has made itself completely dependent on the United States for their security. They have so many gaps in defense architecture, logistics, weapons systems, satellite systems—everything. They are so far behind that they are not ready for war at this time.”

European populations show zero appetite for conflict. Recent protests across major European cities demonstrate strong opposition to war with Russia. The narrative-engineering required to manufacture consent would take years—and Europeans haven’t forgotten that even Ukrainians weren’t interested in fighting on the war’s eve.

The British Alternative

As NATO appears increasingly dysfunctional, Britain is organizing a separate alliance:

  • Joint Expeditionary Force: 10 northern European navies operating under British command
  • Naval chiefs from the Netherlands, Norway, Sweden, Denmark, Baltic states, and Belgium signed agreements in late April
  • The alliance aims to be “ready for war by 2029”
  • Strategy involves harassing Russian maritime traffic through the North Sea and Arctic

“The Brits are already engineering a new alliance aimed at Russia,” Crraer noted. “They just announced this last month at Whitehall in London. As NATO implodes, the British are engineering alternatives.”

The longer-term objective appears to be:

  1. Establish kinetic conflict between the JEF coalition and Russia
  2. Use this to draw remaining European allies (France, Poland, Germany) into hostilities
  3. Eventually lobby the United States to join
  4. Leverage immigrant communities in America with heritage connections to these European nations

“They’re never going to stop trying,” Crraer warned. “We saw this with WWI and WWII—America didn’t want to get involved, but they lobbied and schemed until they got us in.”

Fed’s Response and Market Implications

Central bank actions are already visible in the markets. The NASDAQ has climbed nearly vertically from all-time highs—a phenomenon Crraer attributes entirely to quantitative easing.

“There’s only one explanation: it’s the QE,” Crraer said. “The Fed has been pumping liquidity into the system.”

The M2-Stock Correlation

Historical data spanning over a century demonstrates that stock prices correlate most strongly with central bank actions:

  • Monetary inflation (expanding money supply) → stock prices rise
  • Slowing monetary inflation or quantitative tightening → stock prices fall

“We’ve seen the correlation clearly,” Crraer explained. “When you look at charts from 1999 to present, you see the NASDAQ tracking M2 money supply almost exactly. There’s sometimes a slight delay, but the correlation is unmistakable.”

The Inflationary Reckoning

The Fed’s liquidity flooding will inevitably translate to consumer price inflation:

  • Food prices are already rising and will accelerate
  • Energy costs will continue climbing
  • Real estate and rental prices will increase
  • The delay between monetary expansion and price inflation is predictable—but the inflation itself is inevitable

“We can’t predict exactly when, but we can predict that it’s definitely coming,” Crraer said.

The Dollar’s Reserve Currency Privilege

One factor sustaining U.S. markets over the long term is the dollar’s global reserve currency status.

“The United States having the global reserve currency means that over longer stretches, U.S. stock markets outperform other markets by quite a bit,” Crraer explained.

How Reserve Status Works

When America runs trade deficits (necessary with a reserve currency):

  1. Americans effectively pay for imports with printed money
  2. Most of this money stays in accounts at Federal Reserve banks for exporting nations
  3. These nations must invest this cash somewhere—some goes to U.S. Treasuries, some flows into equity markets
  4. This systematically supports American markets and asset prices

“European stocks, emerging market stocks, and U.S. stocks over longer stretches—U.S. markets outperform by far,” Crraer noted.

Key Takeaways

  1. Oil market disruption could reach 32% if Iran hostilities escalate, pushing prices to unprecedented levels with cascading effects on food production and global trade.

  2. China holds the key to resolving the Iran conflict. Trump’s Beijing visit offers a potential diplomatic off-ramp where China brokers a face-saving nuclear commitment for Iran—allowing Trump to claim victory while avoiding catastrophic escalation.

  3. Ukraine is approaching collapse on two fronts: civil war within Kyiv within weeks, combined with Russian readiness to conclude military operations during summer 2026.

  4. Europe faces an economic Weimar-style crisis as the Ukraine conflict ends. Shorting European bonds—particularly British gilts—represents Crraer’s “trade of the century” over the next 2-3 years.

  5. The Fed’s liquidity injection is creating predictable inflation while artificially supporting stock markets. The NASDAQ’s vertical climb mirrors M2 money supply expansion almost exactly, and consumer price inflation will follow with certainty.

  6. Trust in American diplomacy has collapsed among Russian and Chinese leadership, complicated by perceived betrayals during Iran negotiations and continued weapons support for Ukraine.
