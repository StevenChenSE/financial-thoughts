🔴 A DIRE WARNING To Gold & Silver Buyers As Oil Prices Surge | @NorthstarBadcharts · CapitalCosm

Technical signals point toward a major macro rotation into energy as precious metals consolidate.

Financial markets frequently test investor patience when long-term cycles shift. While gold and silver have experienced notable bull market expansions, market structure indicates that the energy sector—specifically crude oil—may be preparing for a significant phase of outperformance.

In a recent market discussion on Capital Cosm, technical analysts Kevin Wadsworth and Patrick Karim of Northstar & Badcharts presented evidence detailing why oil appears ready for a major multi-year breakout, how Treasury yields fit into this historical macro cycle, and what precious metals investors need to know about navigating current market conditions.

1. Crude Oil: Building Toward a Multi-Year Breakout

Crude oil (WTI) has faced strong horizontal resistance across multiple time frames in the $100 to $112 zone. According to Wadsworth, multi-year consolidations that repeatedly test overhead resistance eventually tend to resolve to the upside.

  • Historical Range Expansions: Looking back over a 150-year horizon, oil trades within distinct multi-decade ranges before stepping up sharply.
  • Long-Term Targets: A multi-year move toward $200 to $350 per barrel would represent a roughly 200% advance from current levels—a move that fits historical commodity bull cycles (such as the 1970s and 2000s) rather than an unprecedented anomaly.
  • Futures Curve Clues: Karim noted that 12-month forward contracts (tracked via vehicles like the United States 12 Month Oil Fund, USL) and longer-dated futures curves show underlying structural strength and flattening, suggesting institutional expectations of persistent long-term price support.

2. Pricing Assets in Gold and the Oil-to-Gold Ratio

When assessing asset performance during a broader commodity bull cycle, measuring assets against fiat currency can obscure relative strength.

Evaluating the Oil/Gold ratio reveals a falling expanding wedge pattern that has repeatedly bounced from key multi-decade support. While gold remains in a secular bull market, its current consolidation phase allows oil to outperform on a relative basis. For the Oil/Gold ratio to achieve its projected targets, crude oil will require substantial upside participation rather than relying solely on precious metals weakness.

3. The Oil vs. Silver Dynamic

On a long-term yearly close basis, crude oil has historically closed above the nominal price of silver for the vast majority of the past two centuries.

Recent market cycles saw silver temporarily close higher than oil on an annualized basis—an occurrence Karim highlighted as an anomaly. Historically, mean reversion restores a substantial premium (often 180% to 300%) of crude oil over silver. If silver consolidates in its current price zone, restoring historical relationship norms points to crude oil trading significantly higher over the next few years.

4. Rising 10-Year Treasury Yields

The macroeconomic backdrop closely mirrors inflationary, yield-rising regimes of the past:

  • Breakout from a 40-Year Downtrend: The US 10-Year Treasury yield broke free from its multi-decade declining channel post-2020.
  • Key Levels to Watch: Sustained closes above 5.3% open the technical path toward 6.0% to 7.0%.
  • Commodity Correlation: Historically, persistent upward pressure on bond yields aligns with broad commodity strength and energy cycles rather than deflationary asset expansion.

5. Mining Stocks vs. Energy: Navigating the Rotation

Many investors expected gold and silver mining equities (GDX, SILJ, ASA) to lead markets higher. However, ratio charts comparing mining indexes against energy leaders like ExxonMobil (XOM) show that miners have broken below key momentum support.

6. Strategy: Traders vs. Long-Term Stackers

Wadsworth emphasized that optimal decision-making depends entirely on an individual’s timeframe and approach:

Market Role Technical Guidance
Short-Term Traders Exercise Patience: Avoid attempting to catch exact bottoms in gold and silver. Wait for clear base breakouts with confirmed support retests to maintain high profit factors (e.g., 3:1 to 6:1 return-to-risk ratios).
Long-Term Investors / Stackers Accumulate on Weakness: Precious metals have already absorbed major percentage corrections. For multi-year accumulators, broad consolidation phases provide favorable dollar-cost averaging opportunities.

Summary

Technical analysis across commodities, yields, and ratio pairs indicates that crude oil and the broader energy sector are structurally positioned for a powerful, multi-year move. While gold and silver remain in secular bull markets, their intermediate corrective phase creates a window where energy assets and oil-linked equities are poised to lead the next leg of the commodity cycle.
