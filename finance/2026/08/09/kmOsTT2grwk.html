Markets Surge: Where's The Top? Trader On Next Levels For Stocks, Gold | Kevin Steur · David Lin

Trader Kevin Steur Explains the Rebound and What’s Next for Risk Assets

Markets are rebounding aggressively. The S&P 500 and Nasdaq have reached new all-time highs, and gold has surged from $4,000 to above $4,400 in just days. Kevin Steur, managing partner of StockT8, joins the show to break down what’s driving the rally, where gold could head by year-end, which sectors offer the best risk/reward, and what’s really limiting the next phase of the AI boom.

Why Risk Assets Are Rebounding So Fast

Steur is candid: there isn’t one single catalyst. Instead, several factors converged:

  • A pivot in sentiment after the recent Fed meeting, with Jerome Powell’s tone surprising many participants.
  • A selling-off of the dollar, which has been supportive for gold.
  • A shift in rate expectations: where markets were pricing cuts two months ago, some major banks now see potential rate hikes this year.
  • A sharp resurgence of flows into tech: hedge funds have been buying tech at the fastest pace since the COVID-era rally.

Steur’s own algorithm caught some of the early swing lows, and his personal book has performed well as risk appetite returned. He notes that while the move is fast, it’s not purely parabolic across the board; some assets and sectors are leading more cleanly than others.

Best Sectors and Trades Right Now

Steur shares a few concrete ideas from his current playbook:

  • Software and tech: He’s long IGV (an iShares software ETF), citing strong technical support, limited overhead resistance, and algorithmic signals that favor software over semis for now. He’s also selective within the “Magnificent Seven,” watching names like Microsoft for strength and Netflix for potential bottoming.
  • Oil refiners: He highlights CRK (Cracker Barrel Old Country Store? No—CRK is the ticker he references for oil refiners), which his system flagged for a breakout in late June. He prefers CRK as a smoother way to play oil volatility: refiners’ margins have been healthy, and the ETF has been less wild than direct oil trades like USO or XLE. However, with Iran-related uncertainty, he’s monitoring signal strength and could reduce exposure.
  • Broad market exposure: He remains long SPY in retirement accounts, likes setups where price breaks above key moving averages (5, 13, 20, 50) with improving signal scores, and watches support levels closely. He’s willing to trim into strength when events like potential Iran actions loom over the weekend.

He emphasizes avoiding highly concentrated, single-theme portfolios. His approach is diversified across tech, energy, commodities, and precious metals so that not everything moves in the same direction.

How Steur Uses His Algorithm and Market Signals

Steur walks through his dashboard and process:

  • High-level overview: He checks trend and signal scores for major indexes (Dow, S&P 500 via SPY, Nasdaq via QQQ, small caps via IWM) and the dollar.
  • Confluence system: For a stock or ETF like SPY, the system flags support and resistance levels. Anything with a “heavy” support score above 15 is significant; SPY showed heavy support around 751.32.
  • Moving averages and breakouts: He likes aggressive breaks above the 5, 13, 20, and 50-day moving averages. When those align with rising signal scores and healthy RSI, he’s more comfortable adding.
  • RSI and risk management: With RSI around 63 on the S&P 500—below the typical 70 overbought threshold—he isn’t rushed to sell but will reduce risk ahead of binary events like potential Iran strikes.
  • Macro gauges: He tracks the MOVE index (bond volatility), VIX, Treasuries, and the dollar to understand broader risk conditions.

His philosophy: avoid large drawdowns, use stops, keep dry powder, and focus on asymmetric setups—where downside is defined and upside has room.

Gold Breakout: Is $4,600 Realistic by Year-End?

Gold’s move has been eye-catching:

  • From roughly $4,000 to above $4,400 in a week, including multi-day gains that are unusual for gold.
  • GDX (gold miners) is showing a fresh breakout after a prolonged bearish-to-neutral phase, with strong support around 75.32 and limited overhead resistance.
  • Key names like Newmont, Agnico Eagle, and Pan American Silver were weak earlier in the summer but are now improving.

Steur sees this as a “textbook asymmetric setup” short-term:

  • He identifies $4,572 as the next major resistance level for gold, which aligns with areas that previously acted as both support and resistance.
  • If gold clears that level, ending the year above $4,600 is plausible.
  • He’s bullish but cautious: gold markets are relatively small, so prices can move quickly on modest flows. He wants to see consistent volume rather than a “hockey stick” spike before declaring a new sustained uptrend.

Prediction markets on Koshi reflect rising confidence:

  • Over 53% of traders see gold above $4,300 by year-end.
  • About 75% see gold at or above $4,600.

Steur’s view is consistent with that: cautious optimism, with $4,572–$4,600 as the key near-term zone.

Oil, Inflation, and Geopolitical Risk

Oil and geopolitics remain wildcards:

  • WTI is around $78 at the time of recording, with volatility tied to Iran.
  • Steur doesn’t want to bet on a single geopolitical outcome. Instead, he prefers structured exposure via ETFs like CRK to capture refined margin upside without the full volatility of crude.
  • He believes the stock market will care if oil spikes significantly higher, especially if a more prolonged boots-on-the-ground scenario emerges in Iran.
  • Oil volatility has been a drag on the 10-year yield and inflation expectations, complicating the macro backdrop.

On inflation vs. deflation:

  • Steur previously favored a deflation trade based on falling agricultural commodities and oil. That thesis didn’t play out.
  • He exited with a small profit, using tight stops and preserving capital.
  • He now views the environment as mixed: energy inputs point one way, commodities another, and geopolitics adds noise. He adjusts positions instead of clinging to a single macro view.

Bonds, the Fed, and Medium-Term Risks

Fixed income and monetary policy are central to Steur’s medium-term outlook:

  • The MOVE index has been elevated but not alarming; he watches it alongside VIX and the 10-year yield.
  • He expects the bond market to hold up short-term but sees potential trouble if yields rise further or volatility spikes.
  • He’s focused on the Fed’s path:
    • Earlier rhetoric suggested a strong anti-inflation stance (“inflation is a choice”).
    • Now some banks are pricing in possible rate hikes, after markets had expected cuts.
    • Steur worries about the tension between rising rates, high borrowing costs, and ongoing capex for AI and data centers.
  • He flags volatility potential into early next year if conditions don’t normalize, particularly if the Fed hikes multiple times while inflation remains sticky.

SpaceX, AI, and the Risk of Concentration

On SpaceX:

  • Recent revenue beat but heavy capex caused the stock to drop.
  • Steur is not a buyer at current levels due to share structure and release uncertainty, plus declining signal strength in his system.
  • Long-term, he sees Starlink as the core revenue engine and wouldn’t bet against Elon Musk, but he wants a better technical setup before committing.

On the AI bubble:

  • He’s watched memory and chip ETFs like DRAM surge and then crash fast, with severe knock-on effects in markets like South Korea.
  • He doesn’t think we necessarily need Apple or Nvidia to drop 40% for an “AI burst”; concentrated sub-sectors can already cause outsized damage.
  • He’s wary of upcoming IPOs such as OpenAI’s rumored fall listing and potential government stakes. To him, heavy government involvement can be a warning sign: if a company is truly unstoppable, why seek such arrangements?
  • He hopes AI leaders like Dell, Microsoft, and others continue to perform, but he remains cautious on valuations and concentration.

The Real Constraint: America’s AI Power Problem

Steur raises a critical, underappreciated bottleneck:

  • If all planned data centers are built, they could consume roughly half the U.S. power grid by 2030—and we’re already in 2026, nearing 2027.
  • The U.S. faces shortages in:
    • Copper
    • Critical minerals (with limited exports from China)
    • Skilled labor (electricians, construction workers)
  • Costs are spiking: some electricians working on data centers are reportedly earning as much as $400,000 a year.
  • Meanwhile, China is building nuclear capacity and appears structurally better positioned in the short term to support AI energy demand.

His concern:

  • U.S. AI companies face rising tokenization and compute costs, while Chinese alternatives could be 50% cheaper with similar accuracy.
  • If major firms consider shifting workloads to China’s AI infrastructure, it creates a policy and competitiveness dilemma for the U.S. government.

How Steur Outperforms in a Volatile Market

The S&P 500 is up roughly 60% from the May 2025 low. Steur’s edge isn’t about being right all the time:

  • He targets 50% accuracy but focuses on risk management:
    • Uses stops to avoid large drawdowns.
    • Prefers “singles and doubles” over chasing every home run.
    • Keeps dry capital to exploit asymmetric opportunities.
  • He rotates between sectors with strong momentum and solid support:
    • Exited gold before a major sell-off; re-entered on a fresh breakout.
    • Avoided software during its worst decline; now long as conditions improved.
    • Swing-traded crypto (e.g., ETH) when signals aligned, without holding through chop.
  • He uses ETFs to reduce single-stock risk and improve liquidity during volatile periods.
  • He maintains discipline: journals trades, respects when macro assumptions fail, and pivots instead of doubling down on losing theses.

Summary and Key Takeaways

  • Markets are rebounding on a mix of Fed sentiment shifts, dollar weakness, and heavy tech buying; the move is strong but not uniform.
  • Steur favors software (IGV), selective tech names, and structured oil plays (CRK) while staying diversified across asset classes.
  • Gold’s breakout is compelling; $4,572–$4,600 is the key near-term resistance zone for a potential year-end push.
  • Oil and geopolitics remain risky; he prefers ETF exposure over direct bets on uncertain events.
  • The bond market and Fed policy are critical: rising yields, potential hikes, and sticky inflation add medium-term risk.
  • AI faces real physical constraints: power, copper, minerals, and labor may slow U.S. data center buildout versus China.
  • Outperformance comes from risk management, sector rotation, using ETFs wisely, and staying flexible when macro assumptions change.
