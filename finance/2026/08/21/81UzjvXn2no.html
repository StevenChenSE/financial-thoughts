Interest Rates Explode: 'Everything Blows Up' Once This Level Hits | Chris Vermeulen · David Lin

A Cautionary Tale of Rising Rates and Volatile Assets

Markets are experiencing significant whipsawing action as the U.S. Treasury steps in to manage surging long-term interest rates. The 30-year Treasury yield has broken out of a massive multi-year pattern, a move that has not been seen since 2007, just before the global financial crisis. This sudden spike has triggered a violent reaction in asset classes ranging from gold and Bitcoin to broad equity indices.

Christopher Vermeulen, Chief Market Strategist at The Technical Traders, joins to dissect these moves. He argues that while short-term traders may be excited by the volatility, the long-term picture for precious metals and cryptocurrency remains bearish until specific technical conditions are met. Meanwhile, he identifies a bullish structure in the S&P 500 that suggests further upside is possible, provided that momentum holds.

The 30-Year Yield and the Treasury’s Response

The primary driver of current market anxiety is the 30-year U.S. Treasury yield. Vermeulen notes that the last time this asset traded with such frequency and volatility was in 2007. The monthly chart for the 30-year yield is pointing toward an 8% move, a trajectory that suggests a potential “blowup” in the financial system if unchecked.

In response to the rising yields, the Treasury announced it would double its bond buybacks to $4 billion per operation. This news caused a sharp drop in yields and a rally in stocks, gold, and Bitcoin the following day. However, Vermeulen points out that this move was short-lived. Yields quickly rebounded, with the 30-year yield rising back above 5.23%. He views the Treasury’s intervention as an attempt to “cap” the long end of the curve and prevent a full-blown crisis, but he believes the underlying momentum in yields is still upward.

Gold and Bitcoin: News-Driven Bounces vs. True Trends

The recent surge in gold and Bitcoin has been fueled by the same news cycle that affected the bond market. Gold rallied to nearly $4,580, while Bitcoin surged over 15% to break above $72,000. Despite these impressive gains, Vermeulen remains skeptical of the sustainability of this rally.

He emphasizes that both assets are currently in a long-term downtrend, characterized by a series of lower highs and lower lows. The 150-day moving average, which serves as his long-term cycle indicator, is sloping down, with price trading below it. For Vermeulen, this is the definition of a correction phase. He warns that news-driven moves create “FOMO” (Fear Of Missing Out), leading to parabolic spikes that often unwind quickly.

To confirm a true bullish shift, Vermeulen looks for two key factors:

  1. Higher Pricing: Price must break above previous resistance levels.
  2. More Time: The move must be sustained over a longer period, ideally following a “staircase” pattern rather than a sharp spike.

He notes that the 20-day and 50-day moving averages need to cross above the 150-day moving average to signal a change in the major cycle. Until then, he remains on the sidelines, viewing the current rally as a “relief rally” or a bounce within a downtrend.

Equity Outlook: Bull Flags and AI Momentum

In contrast to his cautious stance on gold and Bitcoin, Vermeulen is bullish on the S&P 500 and the NASDAQ. He identifies a large bull flag pattern on the weekly chart, with the S&P 500 pointing toward a target of 8,500. This represents approximately a 10% upside move from current levels.

The NASDAQ shows a similar pattern, with potential for an 18-20% move higher. However, Vermeulen cautions that this depends on the AI sector, particularly semiconductors (SMH), continuing to lead the market. High interest rates are a headwind for the AI trade, as the sector relies heavily on borrowing for data center expansion. He warns that if the AI bubble pops or if long-term yields continue to creep higher, the equity rally could stall.

Trading Strategy: Asset Revesting and Risk Management

Vermeulen’s trading philosophy is centered on “Asset Revesting,” a strategy that involves holding assets only when they are rising in value. He does not believe in holding assets that are in a downtrend, as this defies the purpose of investing. His approach involves:

  • Trend Following: Entering trades only when the long-term trend is up.
  • Statistical Targets: Using historical data to determine where an asset is likely to run before hitting resistance.
  • Scaling Out: Trimming profits as price targets are hit to reduce exposure.
  • Capital Preservation: Prioritizing the protection of capital over maximizing returns in a bull market.

He also discusses his book, After the Rally, which explores the risks of traditional investing strategies like buy-and-hold and dividend reinvestment. He argues that these strategies carry significant risk in the later years of an investor’s life, as a single major correction can wipe out decades of growth. The book aims to educate investors on the “other side” of the market, highlighting the importance of adapting to changing market conditions.

Conclusion

The current market environment is defined by a conflict between rising long-term yields and a strong equity rally. While the Treasury’s intervention has provided temporary relief, the underlying momentum in the 30-year yield suggests a potential for further stress. Vermeulen advises investors to be cautious with news-driven spikes in gold and Bitcoin, waiting for confirmed trend changes before entering. For equities, he sees upside potential but emphasizes the need to manage risk and stay disciplined in the face of volatility.
