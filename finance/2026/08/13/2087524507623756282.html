AI Bubble Risks: Why Gold, Silver, and Quality Stocks May Win Next

How Nvidia and AI Hype Could Drive Precious Metals Higher

Introduction

Felix Prehn argues that the next major move in gold and silver may not be driven by inflation, Fed policy, or geopolitical shocks—but by the structure and fragility of today’s AI boom. Drawing on recent data, he outlines how Nvidia’s financing, shifting corporate buybacks, drained oil reserves, and extreme U.S. valuation levels create a scenario where precious metals and quality stocks are positioned to benefit. He also stresses that being right on direction is not enough—timing exits is critical.

Four Signals That Converged in a Single Week

Prehn highlights four developments reported separately but potentially linked:

  • Nvidia arranged roughly $500 billion in financing so its own customers could buy Nvidia chips, with the stock rising on the news.
  • At the same time, the cost of insuring Nvidia’s debt against default via credit default swaps doubled. Bond traders were quietly demanding more protection even as equities hit all-time highs.
  • U.S. mega-cap tech companies shifted from buying about $190 billion in their own shares annually to selling around $147 billion in 2026, turning the long-standing net buyer of equities into a net seller.
  • America’s Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to its lowest level since 1983, leaving little energy buffer for a supply shock.

Taken together, these signals suggest a system running hot on leverage, with diminished cushions and a changing source of equity demand.

U.S. Equities at Record Valuations

Prehn shows a composite of eight long-run valuation metrics—price-to-earnings, price-to-book, price-to-sales, market cap to GDP, and others—indicating that U.S. equities are more expensive now than during:

  • The 2000 dot-com bubble
  • The 1929 peak

He emphasizes that anyone invested in index funds, 401(k)s, or major tech names is directly exposed to this valuation environment, now heavily concentrated around a handful of AI-driven companies.

The Core Lesson: Being Right Is Not Enough

A central theme is that investors often lose money even when their thesis proves correct, because they fail to manage exits. Prehn says Wall Street uses a simple, repeatable three-step system for deciding when to sell—when to take profits, when to cut losses, and when to leave a deteriorating trade—but this framework is rarely shared with retail investors. He invites viewers to a free live session at whentosell.org to learn this process.

The Opportunity: Gold and Silver Miners at 50-Year Lows

Prehn argues that gold and silver mining stocks are cheap by historical standards:

  • The sector generates around 10% free cash flow yield.
  • Many miners trade below the value of the gold in the ground and on their balance sheets.
  • Despite being unloved and considered “risky,” gold miners remain one of the most profitable segments of the S&P 500.

He notes a potential technical breakout in mining indices but remains cautious, preferring to confirm a move above key moving averages before committing. For exposure without picking individual miners, he mentions the GDX ETF as a common reference point, while reminding viewers he is not a financial advisor and that the sector is volatile.

For stock selection, he points to his Winston app, where users can filter gold and silver miners by country, quality score, and other criteria.

The Trigger: AI, Circular Financing, and Pension Fund Risk

Prehn’s central concern is how the AI boom is funded:

  • Nvidia partnered with large asset managers—Apollo, Blackstone, BlackRock, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs, and KKR—to help customers finance GPU purchases.
  • The capital ultimately traces back to pension funds and insurance companies.
  • The narrative is that GPUs are long-lived assets like toll roads, but AI chips rapidly depreciate as newer, faster models arrive.
  • The asset managers collect fees upfront and transfer risk to the lenders—retirement and insurance funds.

He compares this structure to late-1990s telecom financing used by companies like Lucent and Nortel, which lent customers money to buy their own equipment, creating inflated revenue and eventual massive write-downs and bankruptcies.

Meanwhile, the doubling of Nvidia’s CDS spreads signals that bond markets see rising leverage and risk in this model, even while equity markets celebrate AI growth.

Drained Buffers and the Inflation-Gold Link

With the Strategic Petroleum Reserve at multi-decade lows, the system has minimal shock absorption. Prehn argues:

  • A financial accident or AI-related unwind could trigger stress.
  • Governments have repeatedly responded to crises over the last 20 years by printing money.
  • Large-scale money creation tends to weaken the dollar and boost inflation expectations.
  • Gold and silver historically perform well when fiat currencies are debased and real assets become more attractive.

He presents a chart showing that the biggest structural buyer propping up U.S. equities for the last decade—tech buybacks—has now flipped to net selling as companies issue shares to fund AI spending. This removes a key support for stock prices.

Prehn’s Own Moves: Miners and Quality Stocks

Prehn shares what he is personally doing:

  • He is closely watching gold miners, waiting for confirmation of a breakout, with GDX as a benchmark, and considering individual miners using quality filters in the Winston app.
  • Separately, he recently increased his position in a high-quality, non-U.S.-biased fund (IQLT), which holds strong companies across Europe, Japan, Canada, Switzerland, and others, with minimal exposure to U.S. mega-cap tech.

His rationale:

  • Value and quality stocks have underperformed growth for over a decade.
  • Historically, after tech bubbles peak, capital rotates back into quality businesses.
  • He wants exposure outside the “American AI casino,” in companies with durable earnings and limited dependence on AI hype.

He stresses he is not calling for a market collapse tomorrow, but expects the current party to end at some point, leaving junk stocks impaired while resilient businesses survive.

Practical Steps and Risk Management

Key takeaways from Prehn:

  • Understand your exposure: index funds and tech-heavy portfolios are long the most expensive market in history.
  • Consider diversification into gold, silver, and miners at historically low valuations.
  • Look at high-quality, globally diversified stocks with limited concentration in U.S. AI names.
  • Develop a clear plan for selling: define profit targets, stop-loss levels, and exit triggers before you buy.
  • Avoid treating investing like gambling; discipline in exits matters more than picking winners.

Summary

Felix Prehn argues that the AI boom—anchored by Nvidia—is built on circular financing, elevated leverage, and record valuations, while systemic buffers like oil reserves have been drained and the long-standing tech buyback support has reversed. In this environment, he sees gold and silver miners as cheap, cash-generative, and historically positioned to benefit if a correction triggers money printing and dollar weakness. He also favors high-quality, globally diversified stocks outside U.S. mega-cap tech. Crucially, he stresses that investors must master exit strategies—not just entry—to protect gains when the current cycle ends.
