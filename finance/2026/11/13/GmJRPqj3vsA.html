🔴 BUCKLE UP! The AI Bubble Has Just BURST | Ed Dowd · CapitalCosm

Introduction

In a recent episode of Capital Calls, host Danny welcomed back macro strategist Ed Dowd to discuss the current state of the financial markets. The conversation centered on a stark conclusion: the AI bubble has already burst. Dowd points to semiconductor stocks as the leading indicator for this downturn, suggesting that while the broader S&P 500 may linger, the tech sector’s correction is underway. Beyond market mechanics, Dowd analyzed the political landscape following recent elections, the implications of potential Fed rate hikes, and geopolitical tensions that could further destabilize the global economy.

The Semiconductor Leading Indicator

Dowd’s primary thesis rests on the performance of semiconductor stocks (semis). He asserts that semis are the leading indicator for all things tech-related. According to Dowd, the technical picture for these assets deteriorated significantly in early October.

  • Technical Breakdown: Semiconductors attempted to rally to new highs but failed. Subsequently, they broke below their July lows with authority, entering a bear market technically.
  • The AI Bubble Status: Dowd declares that the AI bubble has officially burst. While he acknowledges uncertainty about how quickly the Nasdaq will follow suit, he believes the trend is irreversible. The S&P 500 may go sideways or even hit new highs temporarily, but it will eventually “give up the ghost.”
  • Historical Parallel: Dowd draws a parallel to the dot-com bust in March 2000. At that time, the S&P 500 went sideways for six months before collapsing in September 2000. He suggests we are currently in a similar phase where investors are rotating out of AI stocks into other sectors like industrials and healthcare, but this rotation will not last long.

Nvidia, China, and Political Timing

A significant portion of the discussion focused on Nvidia and recent news regarding its CEO, Jensen Huang. Dowd highlighted the timing of allegations concerning an affair with a Chinese spy and potential violations of export controls involving $50 billion in chips sent to China.

  • Strategic Timing: Dowd suspects the release of this information is strategically timed following political shifts (referred to as “the power switch to blue”). He argues that forces within the government are leaking this information to shift the narrative toward national security concerns.
  • Regulatory Agenda: The goal, according to Dowd, is to regulate or ban open-source AI models, which are predominantly Chinese. This move would cement a duopoly for OpenAI and Anthropic. Dowd argues these companies’ business models are currently broken because they cannot cover their CAPEX costs due to commodity-like pricing pressure from open-source competitors.
  • Nvidia’s Outlook: Dowd predicts Nvidia will miss earnings in the next six months. He cites negative free cash flow among hyperscalers, pausing CAPEX, and the inability of neo-cloud providers to finance themselves as key reasons for this decline.

The Anthropic IPO Warning Signs

Dowd also scrutinized the upcoming Initial Public Offering (IPO) for Anthropic, noting several red flags that suggest weak demand and valuation concerns.

  • Valuation Haircut: Initial chatter placed Anthropic’s valuation at $2 trillion. This has been walked back to $1–$1.5 trillion.
  • Reduced Raise: The target raise amount dropped from $100 billion to $69 billion.
  • Filing Amendments: The company had to file amendments to its S-1 registration statement, which Dowd interprets as a sign of internal errors or AI-generated documentation failures.
  • Retail Access: Brokerage firm Schwab lowered the minimum account size required for clients to request IPO shares from $100,000 to $50,000. Dowd argues that in truly hot, oversubscribed IPOs, access is restricted to ultra-high-net-worth individuals. Opening it up to retail investors suggests a lack of institutional demand.
  • Prediction: Dowd warns that investors buying and holding Anthropic stock could lose at least 50% of their investment over the next 12 months.

The Fed’s Impossible Choice

The discussion turned to monetary policy, with Dowd expressing deep concern about the Federal Reserve’s current position.

  • Inflation Data: Recent CPI data was described as “super hot,” with headline inflation estimated at 4.0–4.1%. Dowd believes this will force the Fed to hike rates in December, despite his view that they should pause.
  • Credit Market Fragility: The AI build-out is predicated on credit bonds, not equity. When the Fed signaled rate hikes in September, the credit markets imploded, with triple-C bond spreads widening and yields in Italy and France spiking. The Fed had to walk back its hawkish stance temporarily.
  • Recession Induction: Dowd cites a statistic from Dennis Gartman (former global macro head at BlackRock) stating that 18 of the last 20 recessions were Fed-induced. He argues that hiking rates into a supply shock is a blunt instrument that will accelerate a deflationary collapse.
  • Yield Curve Dynamics: Dowd expects the yield curve to flatten as long-end yields begin pricing in growth and deflation scares, even if short-term rates remain high.

Political Rejection and Economic Reality

Dowd analyzed the recent election results, describing them not as a mandate for the Democratic party (“the blue team”) but as a rejection of the current administration’s economic management.

  • Real Wage Growth: He pointed out that real wage growth has been negative (around -2%) during periods of political change, citing Reagan vs. Carter in 1980 and Bush vs. Clinton in 1992. The same dynamic applied to Trump’s victory in 2024.
  • Consumer Weakness: Retailers like Costco have indicated that the US is already in a recession. Consumer stocks (McDonald’s, Nike) are struggling, mirroring the pre-2008 financial crisis environment where consumer-facing equities collapsed before the broader market.

Geopolitical Risks and Global Preparedness

Finally, Dowd addressed rising global tensions, particularly regarding Iran and potential conflicts in Ukraine and Taiwan.

  • Iran Conflict: Despite talk of peace deals, Dowd notes that US military hardware continues to move toward the Middle East. He views this as preparation for escalation rather than de-escalation.
  • Global Stockpiling: Dowd highlighted reports of 33 governments worldwide advising citizens to stockpile food and supplies. He interprets this not as conspiracy, but as intelligence agencies seeing logistical preparations for war.
  • Preparation Advice: Dowd advises individuals to prepare for the worst (stockpiling essentials) without living in fear, noting that supply chain breakdowns are a standard consequence of global conflict.

Summary

Ed Dowd’s analysis presents a bearish outlook driven by technical breakdowns in semiconductor stocks, indicating the AI bubble has burst. He warns of a Fed-induced recession due to necessary rate hikes amid high inflation and fragile credit markets. Additionally, he highlights political shifts aimed at regulating open-source AI to protect corporate duopolies and points to global military preparations as signs of impending geopolitical instability. Investors are advised to be cautious with tech valuations, particularly around upcoming IPOs like Anthropic, and to prepare for economic and geopolitical turbulence.
