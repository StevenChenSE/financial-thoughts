Nvidia Crushes Earnings: Will Tech Stocks Rebound Or Crash More? | Jay Singh · David Lin

Introduction

On November 17th, following after-hours trading, Nvidia released its highly anticipated Q3 earnings report, delivering a robust performance that sent stock futures up approximately 3.5%. Despite beating both earnings per share (EPS) and revenue estimates, the market’s reaction was tempered by significant leadership turmoil: the departure of founder and CEO Jensen Huang. With interim CEO Colette Kress stepping in, questions about Nvidia’s future direction, its role as the “bank of AI,” and the implications of new export restrictions dominate the conversation. This article explores the earnings details, the mystery surrounding Jensen Huang’s exit, the upcoming Anthropic IPO, and broader market signals indicating a potential shift toward defensive stocks.

Nvidia’s Q3 Performance: A Double Beat Amidst Leadership Chaos

Nvidia reported strong financial results for the quarter, with revenue hitting $115 billion, beating expectations by 6.2%. Adjusted EPS came in at $2.68, surpassing estimates by 8.5%. The company also increased its buyback authorization by $150 billion, bringing the total to $377 billion.

However, the numbers were overshadowed by the news that Jensen Huang is not returning to the CEO role. The board chairman explicitly stated during the call that they are not considering Huang’s comeback. This announcement caused the stock to briefly dip before recovering on positive outlook discussions. Analysts note that while the fundamental business remains strong—with data center revenue expected to grow 75% in 2028—the “Jensen Huang premium” has been priced out of the market. Since the scandal and subsequent ouster were disclosed, Nvidia’s stock value has dropped by over 10%, representing a loss of nearly half a trillion dollars in market capitalization.

The “Bank of AI”: Nvidia’s Financial Engineering

A critical aspect of Nvidia’s current strategy is its role as a financial backstop for the AI ecosystem. With $62 billion in equity investments and approximately $118 billion in guarantees, Nvidia is effectively financing its own customers, particularly NeoClouds (companies like CoreWeave) and frontier labs.

Interim CEO Colette Kress emphasized a more disciplined approach to these guarantees. She stated that Nvidia would be selective, ensuring that customers have secured land, power, and shell for data centers before extending credit. This shift aims to mitigate risk as NeoClouds grow faster than their balance sheets can support. Despite the growing credit exposure—with accounts receivable reaching a record $81 billion—Nvidia’s balance sheet remains robust. With $99 billion in cash and only $38 billion in debt, the company has an incredibly low loan-to-value (LTV) ratio of roughly 18%, even when accounting for off-balance-sheet guarantees. This financial strength allows Nvidia to continue supporting the infrastructure buildout without significant liquidity concerns.

The Jensen Huang Mystery: Scandal or Strategy?

The reasons behind Jensen Huang’s departure remain unclear, with various theories circulating. While reports cited extramarital affairs involving employees and potential conflicts of interest with Singapore-based Mega Speed, many analysts suspect deeper geopolitical and corporate motives.

Some theories suggest that US intelligence agencies and hardline lawmakers pushed for Huang’s removal to enforce stricter chip export bans, such as the Remote Access Security Act (RASA). Others point to competition from closed-source AI labs like Anthropic and OpenAI, which may view Huang’s support for open-source models as a threat to their margins. Additionally, some commentators speculate that Beijing engineered the scandal to destabilize America’s leading AI hardware company. Regardless of the cause, the timing—coinciding with Trump’s visit to China for the APEC summit and new export restrictions on six countries including Singapore and Malaysia—has fueled speculation about a coordinated effort to tighten control over advanced AI chip exports.

Anthropic IPO: Growth, Losses, and Investor Caution

Amidst Nvidia’s turmoil, Anthropic is preparing for its initial public offering (IPO), targeting a valuation of approximately $1.4 trillion. The company aims to raise around $90 billion through a combination of public listing and private placement, with a price range of $70–$78 per share.

Anthropic’s financials show parabolic growth, with revenue jumping from $2.5 billion in the first nine months of 2025 to $33 billion in the same period of 2026. However, this growth comes with staggering net losses, reported at $104 billion for the nine-month period. Analysts caution that much of this loss is non-cash, driven by fair value remeasurements of convertible notes due to rising valuations. The actual cash burn is significantly lower, estimated around $10 billion when excluding these adjustments.

Despite the hype, investors are wary of a “pop and drop” pattern seen in recent mega-IPOs like SpaceX and Cerebras, where stocks surged initially only to plummet during lock-up expirations. With Anthropic’s first unlock scheduled for November 19th, market participants are watching closely to see if the stock can sustain its valuation or if it will follow the trend of recent tech listings that have retreated sharply post-debut.

Broader Market Trends: The Shift to Defensive

The broader tech sector is showing signs of fatigue. A Bank of America fund manager survey revealed a 25 percentage point drop in overweight positions in tech, the largest single-month decline since 2006. Institutional investors are rotating into defensive sectors like consumer staples, healthcare, and utilities, which have outperformed the Nasdaq by 26% since October 1st.

This defensive rotation is occurring alongside a wave of cyber-related disruptions, including outages at Microsoft, major banks, and Apple services. While not all incidents were confirmed as cyberattacks, the frequency of these events has heightened concerns about infrastructure resilience. As the market digests high interest rates and geopolitical uncertainties, the focus is shifting from pure growth to quality and value. Investors are increasingly looking for beaten-down mid-cap names and high-yield preferred stocks rather than chasing high-multiple tech giants.

Summary

Nvidia’s strong Q3 earnings were overshadowed by the permanent departure of Jensen Huang and new export restrictions on AI chips to key Asian markets. While Nvidia remains financially robust, acting as a critical credit provider for the AI infrastructure buildout, its leadership vacuum creates uncertainty. Meanwhile, Anthropic’s massive IPO highlights the sector’s explosive growth but also raises questions about sustainability and lock-up risks. With institutional investors rotating into defensive stocks and cyber disruptions adding volatility, the tech sector may face continued pressure before finding a new equilibrium.
