🔴 The Military Is Stocking Up For An Iran War LAND INVASION?! | Simon hunt & Alasdair Macleod · CapitalCosm

In a recent episode of Capitalism, host Danny interviews financial analysts Simon Hunt and Alasdair Macleod regarding escalating tensions in the Middle East. The discussion centers on concerning logistical preparations by the US military, the geopolitical implications of an upcoming Putin-Trump summit, and the broader economic fallout that could signal the end of the fiat currency era.

Signs of a Land Invasion

Simon Hunt raises alarming observations about recent procurement activities within the Pentagon. He points to specific stockpiling efforts that suggest preparations for a ground war rather than just aerial strikes:

  • Hot Weather Boots: The quartermaster is building up stocks of boots required for operations in warm climates, typical of Iran and Iraq.
  • Blood Supplies and Food Reserves: There is a notable increase in reserves of bloodlines and food specifically tailored for Muslim eaters, not just American troops.

Hunt suggests that while an immediate air attack might be postponed due to the upcoming summit between Donald Trump and Vladimir Putin, it could be delayed until the second half of next year. This delay would provide sufficient time to assemble land forces, potentially launching an invasion route through Syria into Iraq and subsequently into Iran.

The trigger for renewed conflict may not come directly from Tehran but rather from Iraq. Hunt notes that the Iraqi Prime Minister, under US influence, is taking legal action against Iranian-controlled militias. Given the large Shia population in Iraq, this could spark internal explosions or events that propel the US to support its government there, dragging it back into conflict with Iran.

The Geopolitical Stalemate

Both Hunt and Macleod are skeptical about the outcome of the upcoming Putin-Trump summit. They argue that Trump is entering negotiations from a position of weakness against a unified front of Russia, China, and Iran. Their ultimate objective, according to Macleod, is to remove American influence from the Middle East entirely.

Key points include:

  • No Negotiation Possible: Macleod views this not as a meeting of equals but as an adversary (Trump) facing partners (Putin and Xi). Trump’s negotiating style may even threaten to attack if his demands are not met, which Hunt argues would have no impact on Moscow or Beijing.
  • Israel’s Vulnerability: The US is determined to protect Israel, with most of Congress heavily influenced by pro-Israel lobbying. However, if Asian hegemonic powers succeed in pushing the US out of the region, Israel becomes significantly exposed.
  • Military Readiness: Three aircraft carriers are currently positioned with the capacity to launch 375 sorties per day, surging up to 600. B-2 bombers and fuel supplies are already staged in Europe and Israel.

Economic Fallout and Oil Infrastructure

The analysts warn that the economic consequences of any renewed attack on Iran will be profound, regardless of whether the war ends quickly. The damage inflicted on regional oil wells, pump stations, and pipelines means it could take two to four years for oil flows to return to pre-war levels.

  • Shipping Disruptions: Data from the IMF indicates that shipping through the Strait of Hormuz has dropped to an average of just three ships per day.
  • Saudi Arabia’s Silence: Saudi Arabia has banned information about its own condition, but reports suggest their oil exports are severely cut off, with nothing going to Yanbu and minimal transit through Hormuz.
  • Market Denial: Hunt and Macleod argue that financial markets are ignoring these realities, believing in “fairy tales” of stability while the underlying infrastructure collapses.

The Collapse of the Fiat System

A significant portion of the discussion focuses on the monetary implications of these geopolitical shifts. Alasdair Macleod argues that we are witnessing the end of the fiat currency era.

  • Bond Market Crisis: US 10-year yields have surpassed 5%, creating a dangerous gap between bond yields and equity valuations. With massive amounts of leverage in the system (broker loans and repo markets), rising yields threaten to trigger a liquidity crisis similar to March 2020 but on a larger scale.
  • Government Debt Traps: Governments, not just private entities, are trapped by debt. With debt-to-GDP ratios exceeding 125-130%, central banks cannot raise rates to fight inflation without collapsing the system. They will likely suppress interest rates, leading to currency devaluation.
  • Gold and Silver as Safe Havens: Macleod predicts that gold has bottomed out and silver will follow. As confidence in the dollar evaporates, investors will flee to assets with no counterparty risk. He forecasts silver prices could surge dramatically, potentially requiring $500 to buy an ounce of silver as the dollar loses purchasing power.
  • China’s Gold Accumulation: Chinese banks are accumulating gold directly from refiners, bypassing London and New York markets. With a high household savings rate, Chinese citizens are shifting wealth into gold accumulation accounts, signaling a move toward a gold-backed currency system for China and Russia.

Cyber Threats and Global Instability

The conversation also touched on recent cyber attacks against major US banks and global organizations like Zoom, CNN, and HSBC. While attribution is difficult, Macleod suggests that Russian provocations are deliberate responses to NATO’s military posturing. He warns that Europe has lost touch with reality, making dangerous promises about defeating Russia while facing internal energy crises.

Germany, for instance, faces a power emergency due to slow grid expansion and the shutdown of nuclear plants, exacerbated by data center demands. This domestic fragility makes European nations vulnerable to both external cyber threats and internal resource shortages.

Conclusion

Hunt and Macleod paint a grim picture of the near future. With military preparations for a potential land invasion in Iran, diplomatic stalemates with Russia and China, and severe structural weaknesses in the global financial system, the risk of a major geopolitical and economic crisis is high. The analysts advise investors to prepare for significant volatility, food shortages, and the eventual collapse of the current fiat currency model, recommending gold and silver as essential hedges against systemic failure.
