Introduction

In the aftermath of the US midterm elections on November 5, 2026, financial markets experienced a dramatic reversal. While the initial post-election day saw excitement, Day Two brought a sharp correction in US equities, with the NASDAQ down over 5% and semiconductor indices plunging more than 11%. However, amidst this volatility, precious metals stood out. Gold futures for December delivery climbed back to $4,100 per ounce, while silver held steady at $52.

In a recent discussion on Capital Cosm, host Danny joined guests Eric Yeung and Francis Hunt to analyze these developments. The consensus among the experts is clear: the traditional capital rotation into US Treasury bonds during equity sell-offs has failed. Instead, gold and silver are emerging as the primary safe havens, signaling a fundamental shift in the global monetary landscape driven by a “fiat and debt-based collapse.”

The Failure of the Bond Bid

For decades, the standard playbook for market stress involved a flight to safety where capital would rotate from equities into US Treasury bonds. However, Yeung and Hunt argue that this mechanism is broken. Despite significant equity sell-offs, US Treasuries did not see the massive bid they historically attract.

“We’re not seeing that right now,” Yeung noted. “The 10-year yield dropped slightly, but it wasn’t a humongous bid.”

Hunt provided a compelling analogy for this shift. He described gold as a “smart friend” who had been demonetized and ignored by the market, which was partying on the “fuel of debt.” Now that the “toxic drug taking friends” (bonds and equities) are suffering from the hangover of hyper-debt, investors are suddenly realizing that gold has been sitting at the table all along, healthy and strong.

The key indicator to watch, according to Hunt, is when bonds fail to strengthen significantly while equities correct. If yields remain elevated or creep higher during an equity crash, it confirms that trust in the debt market has eroded permanently. The 40-year bond bull market ended in 2020, and Hunt asserts that “bonds will never be what they were.”

The AI Bubble Deflates

A major driver of the current equity correction is the popping of the Artificial Intelligence (AI) bubble. Yeung pointed out that many AI companies are now turning out negative cash flows. The massive infrastructure investments required for AI have not yet generated sufficient returns, leading to a reality check in valuations.

Hunt highlighted the fragility of the semiconductor sector, represented by the SOXX ETF, which saw brutal sell-offs. He referenced concerns raised by institutions like the Bank for International Settlements (BIS) regarding “indigenous revenue” or circular revenue models used by tech giants. These accounting practices, reminiscent of past corporate scandals like Enron, are coming under scrutiny as the market reprices these assets.

“The true fact is it’s hypervalued, excessively valued, and needs to correct,” Hunt stated. The narrative surrounding AI debt financing and circular revenues suddenly matters because the underlying fundamentals no longer support the inflated valuations.

Geopolitical Instability and the Lame Duck Presidency

The political landscape following the midterms adds another layer of volatility. Yeung predicts that with Democrats gaining control of Congress, there will be intense investigations into President Donald Trump’s administration, potentially leading to attempted impeachments. He described this scenario as “Watergate times 10,” suggesting a loss of presidential control similar to the Nixon era.

Historical data supports Yeung’s bullish view on gold during periods of political instability:

  • 1973-1974 (Nixon/Watergate): Gold went up 5x.
  • 1977-1980 (Carter/Inflation): Gold went up 8x.

Yeung believes that if Trump becomes a “lame duck” president, losing control of the government machinery, gold and silver could see similar multiples. Hunt added that while he views both parties as part of a “uni-party,” the resulting gridlock allows behind-the-scenes handlers to advance agendas that may include controlled demolition of financial structures, further benefiting hard assets.

The Shift in Global Collateral Standards

A critical development discussed was the potential replacement of US Treasuries as the world’s pristine collateral. Yeung explained that under Basel III regulations, US Treasuries carry only a 2% haircut when used as collateral, whereas physical gold carries a 20% haircut. This has historically discouraged banks from using gold for liquidity.

However, new systems in Hong Kong, such as the HKPMCC OTC market and “Project Ensemble,” are tokenizing physical gold to make it more usable as collateral. Yeung suggests that Russia may already be using these facilities to bypass US sanctions. If gold becomes a viable alternative to Treasuries for global liquidity, its demand could surge dramatically.

Investment Strategy: Stacking Metals vs. Chasing Bonds

Given the current environment, both experts advise caution regarding traditional asset classes.

Francis Hunt’s Recommendations:

  • Do not go long bonds: He has a fundamental bias against going long any bond or debt instrument since 2020.
  • Stack Gold and Silver: Buy monthly and via lump sums without leverage. He views current dips as “discount windows.”
  • Prepare for Supply Chain Disruptions: Hunt warns of potential grid outs, food supply issues, and cyberattacks on infrastructure (fridges, blood banks). He suggests having prepper essentials like wind-up radios.
  • Look Global: Consider non-US markets like the CSI 300 (China) or Singapore Straits Index for better value.

Eric Yeung’s Recommendations:

  • Hold Physical Metals: He is holding all his physical gold, silver, and miners, including uranium miners despite recent underperformance.
  • Watch the Miners: While mining costs (energy) are rising, many miners have low original cost bases (under $2,000/oz for gold), keeping them profitable. If oil prices fall, miner margins could improve.
  • Monitor Sovereign Debt Trends: Watch countries like Saudi Arabia, which may be selling assets to maintain dollar pegs rather than buying Treasuries, further weakening the bond market.

Conclusion

The events of November 2026 mark a pivotal moment in financial history. The failure of US Treasury bonds to act as a safe haven during an equity crash signals that the post-1980 binary world of “bonds or equities” is ending. With the AI bubble deflating, political instability rising, and global trust in fiat debt eroding, gold and silver are reasserting their role as the ultimate stores of value. As Hunt concluded, “There is no bad gold investment right now… you just might have a little longer to wait before you do your 3x.”
