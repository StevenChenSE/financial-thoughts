Gold's Violent Reversal: How Far Does Rally Go? | Gary Wagner · David Lin

Introduction

Gold markets have recently experienced what Gary Wagner, editor of The Gold Forecast, describes as “whipsaw action” characterized by extended rallies and deep corrections. After hitting a low near $3,800 in early November, gold has rebounded significantly to the $4,100 range. This surge marks a potential shift back into bullish market sentiment, although Wagner cautions that investors are not yet “out of the water.” With the Federal Reserve facing an 80% probability of raising rates at the next meeting and complex macroeconomic factors at play, understanding the technical levels and inverted correlations is crucial for navigating the remainder of the year and beyond.

The Nature of the Recent Correction

Wagner notes that while the current volatility is intense, it does not compare to the multi-year correction seen between 2011 and 2015, where gold prices were halved from approximately $1,900 to below $1,000. The recent decline saw about one-third of the price come off the top, which technical studies confirm as a deep but manageable correction rather than a catastrophic collapse.

Key timeline events include:

  • Late January: Gold hit record highs.
  • February 28th: Initiation of military action in Iran, influencing oil and market sentiment.
  • July: Consolidation followed by the strongest rally of the year, moving from roughly $4,000 to $4,750.
  • September/October: A significant fall from grace, finding support near $3,800 before rebounding.

Key Technical Levels and Targets

Wagner’s technical analysis highlights specific support and resistance zones that dictate gold’s immediate trajectory.

Support Zones

The recent low of approximately $3,800 served as a critical test. Wagner had previously identified $4,000 as a key level matching the “original kickoff point” for previous rallies. When gold broke below $4,000, many expected further downside, but the market found support near $3,800. A subsequent green candle and a major $132 move on Wednesday signaled a potential bottoming pattern.

Resistance and Upside Targets

Currently trading around $4,155, gold faces immediate resistance. Wagner identifies two primary levels:

  1. R1 (Major Resistance): The current area above recent candles. Breaking this on a closing basis is essential for sustained momentum.
  2. 38.2% Fibonacci Retracement: A significant resistance zone derived from the drop from the second all-time high of $4,748 down to $4,000.

If gold breaks above current resistance, Wagner projects the next solid target at approximately $4,400. He emphasizes that even reaching this level would constitute an exceptional year for gold investors.

Inverted Market Correlations: The “Quagmire”

A central theme of the discussion is the inversion of traditional market relationships, which Wagner calls a “quagmire.” Historically, higher inflation expectations acted as a bullish tailwind for gold. However, in the current environment, this relationship has flipped 180 degrees.

Why Gold Sold Off During High Inflation

When oil prices spiked (reaching above $100/barrel due to geopolitical tensions with Iran), inflation expectations rose. Traditionally, this would boost gold. Instead, gold declined. Wagner explains this paradox through the lens of liquidity and Federal Reserve policy:

  • Fed Reaction: Higher inflation signals led markets to anticipate aggressive rate hikes by the Fed. Rising rates are poison for non-yielding assets like gold.
  • Margin Calls: Institutional traders with leveraged positions faced margin calls as equities sold off. To cover these calls, they liquidated liquid assets, including gold, rather than holding it as a safe haven.

The Oil-Gold-Dollar Dynamic

Recently, oil prices have fallen (dropping from over $100 to the 80s), and long-term yields (10-year and 30-year) have also declined. Wagner observes that gold is rising in tandem with falling oil and yields, suggesting a return to more traditional correlations where lower inflationary pressure or easing financial conditions support gold prices.

Political Landscape and Macroeconomic Uncertainty

The midterm elections have introduced further complexity. With Democrats controlling both chambers of Congress, the legislative environment has become a “lame duck” scenario for both Congress and the President until January.

  • Spending vs. Policy Gridlock: While Democrats may push for increased spending programs (potentially inflationary), they are also positioned to block specific policies from the current administration. This creates uncertainty regarding fiscal policy direction.
  • Inflation Persistence: Wagner notes that inflation remains well above 3%, having not returned to the low levels of the Clinton era. The key driver remains inflation expectations and how they influence the Federal Reserve’s monetary policy decisions.

Investment Strategy: Dollar Cost Averaging

For investors who purchased gold near recent highs and are currently underwater, Wagner advises a long-term perspective over short-term trading. He recommends dollar cost averaging (DCA)—buying small incremental amounts over time.

  • Rationale: In a market that is ultimately bullish but prone to deep peaks and valleys, DCA reduces the probability of loss and maximizes the chance of acquiring positions at better average prices.
  • Personal Approach: Wagner describes his own strategy as “set it and forget it,” buying small amounts consistently. This ensures that high-priced purchases constitute only a small percentage (6-8%) of the total portfolio, mitigating risk.

Long-Term Outlook: The $5,000 Benchmark

Wagner provides specific price targets for various time horizons, distinguishing between achievable levels and “ridiculous” extremes.

  • Downside Risk: A “ridiculous” low where even Wagner might throw in the towel is estimated around $3,500.
  • Near-Term Upside: The first achievable target is $4,750, potentially taking up to two years.
  • Long-Term Benchmark: The previous all-time high of $4,975 has been surpassed, with the new psychological benchmark being $5,000.
  • “Ridiculous” Upside: Wagner defines a “ridiculous” price as anything above $6,000. While he acknowledges that numbers like $10,000 were once laughed at, he admits the rapid rise to $5,500 made such forecasts seem less absurd. However, he believes reaching $6,000+ requires a specific confluence of economic brutality, military conflict, or severe global instability.

Conclusion: Will 2027 Be Positive?

When asked about gold’s performance in 2027, Wagner offers a cautious but constructive outlook. He notes that gold is down approximately 5% for the year (calculated from January 1st). The trajectory into 2027 will depend on:

  1. Global economic resilience and cooperation between nations.
  2. The severity of military conflicts (particularly regarding Iran and oil supplies).
  3. The Federal Reserve’s ability to manage inflation without triggering a recession.

Wagner concludes that while the tops seen earlier in the year “will get hit again,” the current reversal suggests a gradual return to bullish sentiment. Investors should remain vigilant, utilize technical levels for entry points, and maintain a long-term perspective on gold’s intrinsic value as a hedge against currency debasement and geopolitical uncertainty.
