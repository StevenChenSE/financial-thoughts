The Great Demand Depression

The United States housing market is currently experiencing what Nick Gerli, CEO of Reventure Consulting, describes as the “worst demand depression ever.” While many observers believe the market has not crashed because national price indices remain high, underlying data suggests a severe contraction in transaction volume. October 2026 saw existing home sales plummet to a 16-year low, while pending home sales hit their lowest level in recorded history.

This phenomenon is distinct from the 2008 financial crisis. In 2008, high demand was fueled by subprime lending and speculative buying, leading to a bubble that burst due to credit failures. Today, the issue is a fundamental disconnect between home prices and affordability, driven by pandemic-era price surges that outstripped local income growth. With mortgage rates hovering above 7%, buyers are simply opting out of the market.

The Supply-Demand Imbalance

A common misconception among sellers is that low demand is caused by a lack of inventory due to the “lock-in effect” of low mortgage rates. However, data from realtor.com shows active listings have risen to nearly 1.2 million, only 2% below pre-pandemic levels. In many Sunbelt and West Coast states, supply has reached its highest point in over a decade.

This creates a dangerous combination: the lowest demand on record paired with the highest supply in ten years. According to basic economic principles, this imbalance necessitates lower prices. While some markets like San Francisco County remain hot due to localized tech sector growth (Anthropic, OpenAI, xAI), broader trends indicate widespread stagnation or decline. Tech-dependent hubs like Seattle, Austin, and Denver are seeing significant price corrections as companies reduce workforces and remote work policies normalize.

The Affordability Crisis and Buyer Psychology

The primary driver of this demand depression is the widening gap between renting and buying costs. Currently, the monthly cost to own a home (mortgage, taxes, insurance) is approximately 40% higher than renting an equivalent property in the US average. In some markets, this premium exceeds 100%. For first-time buyers, who typically require median incomes of $110,000–$120,000 to qualify for mortgages, this financial reality is prohibitive.

Furthermore, white-collar job insecurity in sectors like tech, finance, and HR has made potential buyers hesitant to take on massive debt. Demographic shifts also play a role; birth rates have declined by 35% over the last 30 years, leading to fewer family formations and reduced long-term housing demand. Investors are similarly retreating, as high interest rates make cash-flow positive returns on rental properties difficult to achieve.

The “La La Land” Seller Phenomenon

Despite falling transaction volumes, national median home prices remain stubbornly high. Gerli attributes this to two factors:

  1. Luxury Buyer Dominance: A disproportionate share of remaining sales are conducted by cash-rich baby boomers and luxury buyers purchasing homes over $1 million. This skews the median price upward, masking declines in the starter-home market.
  2. Seller Delusion: Many sellers, particularly those with 3% mortgage rates from 2020–2021, anchor their listing prices to peak values rather than current market clearing prices. These sellers are often unwilling to cut prices to meet buyer expectations, leading to extended days on market (DOM).

Gerli advises buyers to look for properties listed below the previous owner’s purchase price, which signals a motivated seller. He personally secured a 35% discount on a home in Atlanta by targeting such listings.

Reventure’s 2027 Price Forecasts

Reventure Consulting provides one of the few granular forecasts down to the zip-code level. Their analysis suggests that most US states will see price declines over the next year, driven primarily by oversupply and rising inventory.

Markets Forecasted to Decline:

  • Washington State: -6.7% (Driven by supply 50-60% above normal levels)
  • Utah, Arizona, Colorado: -5% to -6%
  • Tennessee: Significant drops expected due to massive inventory gluts
  • Florida: Continued decline, though improving from previous overvaluation. The state passed Amendment 3, which reduces property taxes for owners.

Markets Forecasted to Rise:

  • Illinois: +5.3% (Due to severe supply shortages)
  • Connecticut: +6%
  • New Jersey: +3.4%
  • New York: Mixed, but certain sub-markets remain strong due to lack of new construction and high demand for legacy urban locations.

Investment Strategy: Land vs. Structure

For investors with capital (“dry powder”), Gerli recommends targeting single-family homes in markets that have already corrected significantly, such as Austin, Texas. Austin values are down nearly 30% from their peak and are now considered undervalued relative to income growth.

The key investment thesis is the appreciation of land versus structures. Home value appreciation is driven by the land, while the structure depreciates over time based on construction costs. Therefore, buying single-family homes in desirable locations with strong intrinsic land value offers better long-term potential than condos or townhomes, which suffer from high HOA fees and oversupply. Outlying suburban subdivisions built during the pandemic boom are viewed as risky due to low relative land value and easy future supply expansion by builders.

Conclusion: Buy or Sell?

The decision to buy or sell in 2027 depends heavily on individual circumstances and mortgage rates.

For Buyers: It is not necessarily a “good time” to buy if seeking immediate financial appreciation, but it is an excellent time to make aggressive offers. With sellers competing for scarce buyers, those willing to negotiate hard—potentially 25–30% below previous peak sale prices—can find exceptional deals.

For Sellers:

  • Low Mortgage Rates (3%): Consider holding. The present value of a low-rate mortgage is high, and selling may mean losing that financial advantage.
  • High Mortgage Rates (7%+): Sell now. Waiting likely means facing a worse market next year. Success requires pricing realistically, often below recent comps, to attract buyers in a thin market.

Ultimately, the US housing market is undergoing a necessary correction where prices must fall to align with local incomes. While mortgage rates may remain elevated at 7%, creating a “new normal,” the path forward for prices is downward in most jurisdictions until affordability improves.
