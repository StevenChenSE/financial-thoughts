Is This Selloff a Pause Before the Equity Meltup? David Hunter on TradeGATEHub

November 27, 2026 970 words TradeGATEHub
Watch Source Video
TradeGateHub Live Trading | Is this Selloff a Pause for The Equity Meltup? · TradeGATEHub

David Hunter (@DaveHcontrarian) sat down with Dale Pinkerton on TradeGATEHub Live on November 26, with the S&P and Nasdaq nursing sharp losses and metals in a violent shake-out. Hunter’s read: the damage is an unwind of leverage, not a trend change — and the last leg of a generational bull market is still ahead.

The Bull Case: 10,000 Still Stands

Hunter reiterated his long-term S&P target of 10,000, reading the recent action as a gap-fill and a retest of support rather than a breakdown. He is explicit about what would falsify him: “if I’m all wrong and this thing’s rolling over, I can see the S&P down to 5,800, but I think much more likely this is going to prove to be a successful retest.”

Sentiment is doing the work he expects at a low: “there’s risk. I don’t pretend to say I know, but boy, sentiment is pretty, pretty negative” — to which Pinkerton replies, “and now it’s a contrarian trade.”

Deleveraging, and the 1987 Parallel

“what we just saw was a good old-fashioned deleveraging… whether it be hedge funds or retail that were leveraged up and buying options” on momentum names in AI and semiconductors. The concentration made the same names fragile, which is why the unwind was so sharp.

He compares it to his own experience “when I was at Fidelity running money… I remember that year… and that crash, the move on Friday and Monday” — the caption drops the year, but the Friday/Monday sequence is Black Monday 1987, and the lesson he took then was about over-owned, thinly held stocks being hit hardest. The parallel today is the crowded AI/semiconductor trade.

Metals went through the same process. “I was looking for $50 silver all year,” he says, noting he owns physical — and the shake-out below it was deleveraging, not a change in the thesis.

Metals: The Targets Have Not Changed

  • Gold: “my gold target is 7,000.”
  • Silver: “my silver target is 200. So if you do the math, obviously silver outperforms gold there” — the gold-silver ratio, he argues, can still be read as a retracement of what it did back in 2025, and silver has to go next: “and then silver will begin to outperform.”
  • Copper: he would “be happy with nine” — with the caveat that he is shorting the dollar to get there. On copper’s recent behavior he quotes the line that “Dr. Copper was sued for malpractice for trying to impersonate a precious metal.”

On the silver bottom itself he is careful: the current low “feels more like a sold-out bottom,” and he watches miners for signs they stop reacting negatively to metal-price drops — a divergence that usually precedes a reversal.

The Dollar Is the Fulcrum

Hunter treats the US Dollar Index as the pivot around which stocks, bonds and commodities turn, and he admits the recent strength caught him: “I wasn’t surprised by 101, 103 even. But this last move surprised me… if you draw the downtrend line on a longer chart, you can even go to 106.” Yesterday’s low “looks like it could have finally been the turn, but we’ll see. It’s faked us out before.”

His view is unchanged: “I think we’re going to have a big drop in the dollar over the course of the next year” — he gives no index number in this session, so treat the size of the move as his conviction rather than a quoted target. A falling dollar is the tailwind for both metals and equities.

Bonds: One More High, Then the Rollover

“I’m with you now on yields. I think the 10-year peaked up there at 5.36… I’m not as aggressive as you are to like zero rates, but I can make a case for us being back down in the low threes on the 10-year down the road, especially if we take out this 4.75 level.” He had earlier flagged the pattern himself: “this looks like it goes back to 2007. It’s a double top and you get a rollover… my call isn’t in sync. It’s still intact.”

He goes further on the tail scenario: “I believe we’re going to have one higher high, which means you could get a 0% 10-year in the bust. It’s a crazy call. It may not happen, but I think it’s quite likely given the leverage in the system.”

Rotation: Small Caps and Semis

  • Russell: “I think 4,000 is my target and I feel pretty good about that… this should lead because these are really the spec stocks.” His evidence is relative strength: in a downside scenario he has the Russell at 2,300, about 23% off, versus roughly 34% off for the larger index — “so it would outperform.”
  • Semiconductors (SMH): the number he gives is 850. The ETF has already filled the gap from last April, which he counts as part of the washout completing.

The Endgame

“we’re in a once-in-a-lifetime, generational secular bull market that I think is not far from the end, and that last run can be crazy.” If support holds, that is how a 10,000 S&P gets into view. But the same leverage that produced this selloff sets up what comes after: “I still believe there’s a much bigger downturn coming, a global bust, and it’s going to require a bigger response than we saw in” the last crisis.

His advice is unglamorous: manage emotions, avoid leverage, and accept that forecasts are only forecasts — the way to benefit from a final meltup is to stay in the market without taking the kind of risk that gets you stopped out before it happens.
