Introduction

In a recent market update on October 16th, Christopher Vermeulen, chief market strategist at The Technical Traders, shared critical insights regarding the current state of global markets. With tech stocks facing increased selling pressure and investors rotating toward defensive assets, Vermeulen revealed significant changes to his portfolio management strategy. He has closed out NASDAQ positions due to a confirmed downtrend signal and warns that both real estate and precious metals remain in bear markets despite recent price action. This article details his analysis of bond yields, sector rotations, and the impending correction in the housing market.

The Interconnected Web of Market Assets

Vermeulen emphasizes that financial markets are not isolated entities but are linked by a singular wave of sentiment shifting across five key asset classes:

  • The Stock Market (specifically the equal-weighted S&P 500, RSP)
  • 10-Year Treasury Yields
  • Bond Prices
  • The US Dollar
  • Gold

Recently, the 10-year yield peaked and began to pull back. This technical move triggered a sharp bounce in bond prices and caused the US dollar to retreat slightly. Consequently, gold found some bid support as the dollar weakened. Vermeulen notes that while the stock market saw defensive sectors like utilities rise, this was part of a broader rotation out of technology stocks rather than an isolated event.

Bond Yields: A Temporary Pullback, Not a Top

A common question among investors is whether bond yields have peaked. Vermeulen’s answer is a definitive no. While short-term charts show a pullback from multi-year highs, he identifies this as a standard correction within a larger uptrend in yields.

  • Technical Analysis: Using Fibonacci extensions, the recent breakout in the 10-year note suggested a measured move that has now paused. The yield pulled back to the 38.2% retracement level, which is typical for such moves.
  • Long-Term Trend: On monthly charts, the trend remains up. Vermeulen views the current bond rally as merely an oversold bounce or counter-trend rally within a clear downtrend in bond prices (which corresponds to rising yields).
  • Future Outlook: He expects yields to find a bottom in the coming sessions and then resume their climb toward much higher levels, eventually returning the market to its original bearish bias for bonds.

Exiting the NASDAQ: Managing Risk in Tech

The most significant action taken by Vermeulen recently was closing out all positions in the NASDAQ (QQQ and QLD) after holding them since early 2025. This decision was driven by strict risk management and technical signals rather than fear.

The Signal

Vermeulen’s proprietary strategy flagged a new downtrend for the NASDAQ. Despite the index looking insignificant on long-term charts compared to previous years, the short-term technicals indicated that money flow was moving out of growth stocks. Holding these positions would have risked turning profitable trades into break-even or losing scenarios.

Critical Technical Levels

The decision to exit centered around a critical pivot low on the QQQ chart:

  • Support Level: Approximately 660. This level aligns with a 50% retracement of the recent run-up and was a previous profit-taking zone.
  • Downside Target: If the QQQ closes below 660, Vermeulen warns it could drop significantly further, potentially all the way down to 555.

The Philosophy: Active Trading vs. Buy and Hold

Vermeulen distinguishes between passive long-term investing and active trading. While buy-and-hold works over decades, it exposes traders to significant “dead money” periods where capital is tied up in markets that spend years recovering from peaks. For active traders using margin or leveraged ETFs (like QLD), a 25% pullback can feel like a 50-75% crash. Therefore, protecting capital and stepping aside when the trend turns negative is crucial for survival and compounding gains.

Sector Rotation: Utilities vs. Healthcare

As money flows out of tech, it hasn’t necessarily found safe harbor in all defensive sectors. Vermeulen analyzes the divergence between utilities and healthcare:

  • Utilities (XLU): Despite recent price increases, utilities remain in a technical bear market. The long-term moving average is sloping down, and price action shows lower highs and lower lows. The recent rally is viewed as a temporary bounce within a downtrend.
  • Healthcare: This sector presents a starkly different picture. Healthcare exhibits a strong uptrend with rising long-term moving averages and bullish chart patterns like bull flags. Biotech and specific health stocks are driving this strength, making healthcare the preferred defensive sector for those looking to trade against the tech sell-off.

Precious Metals: Insurance, Not Speculation

Gold and silver are currently in bear markets from a technical standpoint (daily charts show prices below long-term moving averages). However, Vermeulen advocates for holding precious metals as insurance against currency devaluation and systemic failure, not necessarily as short-term speculative plays.

  • Buying Strategy: He is prepared to “catch the falling knife” only for physical metals if they hit specific Fibonacci measured move targets.
  • Target Levels: He plans to buy gold around $3,600 and add more at $3,100. For silver, he looks to buy as it drops toward lower technical levels (previously cited around $39).
  • Rationale: Unlike stocks, physical metals have intrinsic value and serve as a hedge against government or currency collapse. Catching these dips is seen as acquiring insurance at a discount rather than gambling on a rebound.

Real Estate: The Looming 20% Correction

Vermeulen issues a stark warning for the real estate market, particularly in Canada, though he notes the US may be more volatile. He predicts a minimum 20% drop from current pricing levels.

Current Warning Signs

  • Canadian Data: National average sold prices are down ~18-20%. Transactions are falling, and listings are stagnant because buyers are offering 10-20% below ask prices.
  • Distress Signals: In Vancouver, court-ordered foreclosures (power of sale) are at a 20-year high. Only 38% of these distressed properties are selling, compared to a typical 60%. High-end homes are seeing discounts of up to 50% (e.g., a $17 million home selling for $10 million).
  • REITs Warning: Real Estate Investment Trusts (REITs) like IYR are not bear-proof. In past bear markets, REITs have dropped 60-80%, far outperforming the general stock market’s decline.

The Catalyst for the Drop

The housing market is a “slow slug,” but financial pressure is building. As mortgage rates rise and economic softness leads to layoffs, homeowners will eventually be forced to sell at lower prices. Once one property in a neighborhood sells at a discount, it triggers a cascade of sales (“opening the floodgates”).

When to Buy Real Estate

Vermeulen suggests waiting for maximum distress:

  1. High Inventory: A surge in homes for sale and multifamily properties.
  2. Tight Credit: Banks becoming extremely reluctant to lend due to their own financial stress.
  3. Negative Sentiment: When media coverage is uniformly negative and mortgages are hard to obtain.

At this point, buyers with cash (“dry gunpowder”) can negotiate aggressively or use creative financing like seller takebacks. The bottom is likely near when it becomes nearly impossible to get a mortgage yet properties are abundant and cheap.

Summary and Outlook

Chris Vermeulen’s current stance is one of defensive positioning. He has exited NASDAQ positions due to confirmed technical downtrends, expects bond yields to rise further after a brief pause, and identifies healthcare as the strongest defensive sector. For real estate investors, he advises patience, predicting a significant correction ahead that will present major opportunities for those holding cash. Meanwhile, precious metals should be viewed through the lens of long-term insurance, with specific lower-price targets set for accumulation.

  • Key Action: Protect capital by stepping aside from assets showing negative trend signals.
  • Key Watch Level: QQQ support at 660; a break below opens the door to 555.
  • Key Opportunity: Real estate and precious metals offer high-probability entry points once maximum fear and distress are visible in the market.
