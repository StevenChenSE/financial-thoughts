🔴 Something Weird Is Happening With The US Stock Market... · CapitalCosm

For decades, the US stock market has been considered the world’s premier destination for capital. The S&P 500 (SPX) has historically delivered a “bottom left to top right” trajectory, consistently outperforming international markets. However, recent data suggests this dominance faced a significant challenge in early 2025, as capital rotated into international equities. This article examines how the US market compares to major global indices—including Europe, Asia, Latin America, and emerging markets—and explores whether the trend is reversing back toward US outperformance.

The S&P 500: A Historical Benchmark

The S&P 500 has long been the benchmark for US equity performance. Historically, its chart reflects a consistent upward trajectory dating back to the 1800s, punctuated by occasional bubbles and crashes, such as the dot-com bust and the 2008 financial crisis. While there was a notable consolidation period between 2000 and 2011—often referred to as a “dead period”—the market has generally been “all gas, no brakes” relative to global peers.

Even through recent volatility, including the drop from 4,792 to 3,436 during the 2020-2021 correction and the COVID-19 crash, the long-term trend remains bullish. To understand current market dynamics, however, one must look beyond absolute performance and analyze relative performance against other global asset classes.

International Stocks vs. The S&P (VXUS)

The ETF VXUS serves as a proxy for international stocks excluding US equities. Data for this basket only goes back to 2011, but it reveals a stark contrast: while the S&P surged, international markets largely moved sideways until a breakout in May 2025.

When plotting the ratio of SPX/VXUS, the data shows that US stocks have historically dominated. However, a “hiccup” occurred starting around December 2024 or January 2025, where international stocks began to outperform the S&P. This trend persisted for approximately two years but appears to be reversing. Recent technical patterns, such as a descending wedge or bull flag on the ratio chart, suggest a potential resumption of US outperformance.

It is important to note that this is a comparative ratio. An upward move in SPX/VXUS means US stocks are outperforming international stocks relative to each other; it does not necessarily mean both markets are rising, nor that one is falling. However, the shift in momentum since August of the current year indicates a change in capital flow direction.

Regional Breakdown: Europe and Emerging Markets

Europe (IEV)

Using the ticker IEV for European stocks, the analysis mirrors the broader international trend. US stocks outperformed European equities significantly starting after the 2008 crisis. A top formed in December 2024, followed by sideways movement throughout early 2025.

A critical pivot point occurred with the onset of the Iran war. Prior to the conflict, US stocks were underperforming European stocks. Once the war began, the S&P saw a significant pop relative to IEV, marking a clear trend reversal where US equities regained dominance over their European counterparts.

Emerging Markets (EEM)

The ticker EEM represents emerging markets, including Southeast Asia and Latin America. Similar to Europe, US stocks dominated from 2008 through early 2025. In January 2025, the trend reversed as emerging markets began to outperform US stocks.

However, a double bottom formed in February of this year, coinciding with the start of the Iran war. Since then, US stocks have begun rallying against EEM. While some technical analysts might view the current chart pattern as a potential bear flag or head-and-shoulders formation suggesting downside risk for US relative performance, the immediate momentum favors US outperformance following the geopolitical shock.

The Japan Anomaly (DXJ)

Japan presents a unique case. Using DXJ to represent Japanese stocks, the data shows that while US stocks dominated until late 2021, a trend reversal began in December 2021. Since then, Japanese stocks have consistently outperformed the S&P.

Unlike other regions, the Iran war did not trigger a significant reversal in favor of US stocks for Japan. The ratio chart remains within a downward channel, indicating that Japanese equities retain momentum relative to American stocks. There are currently no technical indicators suggesting a sharp reversal back to US dominance in this specific pair.

Asia Excluding Japan (AAXJ) and China (FXI)

For Asian stocks excluding Japan (tracked by AAXJ), the S&P outperformed since roughly 2010, with a top forming in January 2025. Interestingly, the initial phase of the Iran war saw Asian stocks continue to outperform US stocks. However, this trend reversed around June, and US stocks have since resumed outperformance against Asian equities.

China (FXI) shows an inverse pattern compared to some other regions. Chinese stocks briefly outperformed US stocks starting in early 2024. By late 2024 into early 2025, the trend reversed back to US dominance. Unlike Europe or emerging markets, the Iran war had little impact on the China-US ratio; the S&P has continued to outperform FXI throughout this period.

Commodity-Heavy Markets: Brazil (EWZ), Australia (ASX), and Canada (TSX)

The performance of US stocks against commodity-rich nations is heavily influenced by global commodity cycles.

  • Brazil (EWZ): After a period where Brazil outperformed the US during the 2002–2010 commodity bull market, US stocks dominated from 2010 onward. A recent top formed in early 2025, followed by a brief reversal in April that is now reversing back down, favoring Brazilian stocks again.
  • Australia (ASX): US stocks have generally outperformed Australia, with a notable dip around 2021–2022. Currently, the ratio is at all-time highs, suggesting strong US relative performance. However, if a major commodity super-cycle emerges, ASX could potentially break this trend and outperform SPX.
  • Canada (TSX): The Toronto Stock Exchange has seen periods of underperformance and outperformance tied to resource cycles. Canadian stocks outperformed the US during the last commodity super-cycle (ending around 1999). Recently, US stocks dominated until June 2024. This top coincided with gold beginning its rally in February 2024. As gold broke out, TSX began to outperform SPX again, highlighting the sensitivity of resource-heavy markets to precious metals and commodity prices.

Conclusion: Geopolitics as a Market Catalyst

The central narrative emerging from this comparative analysis is that early 2025 was a period of capital rotation away from US stocks, with international markets catching a bid. However, the onset of the Iran war acted as a secondary pivot point, causing capital to flow back into US equities for most regions (excluding Japan).

Key Takeaways:

  1. US Dominance Resumed: For Europe, Emerging Markets, and broader International indices, the trend has reversed from international outperformance back to US outperformance following the geopolitical conflict.
  2. Japan is an Exception: Japanese stocks continue to outperform US equities, unaffected by recent geopolitical shifts in terms of relative strength.
  3. Commodities Matter: Markets like Canada and Australia are tied to resource cycles. A resurgence in commodities (like gold) can lead these markets to outperform the S&P again.

Investors should monitor whether this post-war rotation back into US stocks is sustainable or if the underlying momentum of international and commodity-driven markets will eventually reclaim dominance.
