Andy FindValue: Copper Scarcity, Oil Decline, and the Role of Disciplined Habits

April 01, 2026 2322 words
Watch Source Video

In today’s volatile financial landscape, the interplay between long-term discipline, commodity scarcity, and market cycles defines the investment horizon. In a recent deep dive into social media discussions, Andy FindValue breaks down three critical topics: wealth-building habits, the looming copper and oil supply crunches, and the timeless lessons of Warren Buffett.

Here is a detailed look at his analysis, complete with key data points and actionable insights.

1. The Foundation of Wealth: Habits Over Goals

The discussion begins with a fundamental truth about personal finance, highlighted by a viewer comment: “Your future is shaped by the habits you repeat, not the goals that you set.”

Andy agrees, stating, “That is 100% right. Uh that’s all signal. and uh no noise.” He emphasizes that while people obsess over “what goal, what goal, what goal,” the reality is: “well, you are what your habits are.”

The Discipline of Accumulation

To build wealth, one must focus on repetition rather than distant targets:

  • Physical Health: “You want to go get a better body? Well, start working out. Uh change your diet and then repeat it over and over. That’s called discipline.”
  • Financial Health: Andy applies the same logic to money. “So, uh I I’m very good at at exercising discipline. I work out every single week, three times a week. I go on walks all the time. I save money repeatedly over decades.”

He highlights specific financial habits that drive success:

  • Low Spending: “I don’t spend a ton of money.”
  • Debt Management: “I don’t borrow money uh at at high interest rates or anything like that. I pay down debt. Uh I don’t really have debt, but I would pay it down if I had it.”
  • The Core Mechanism: “So, I guess uh not taking uh on debt that has uh interest tied to it, I think, is a is a good one. And and then you just repeat it over a long period of time. That’s how you get rich. That is exactly how you get rich.”

Key Takeaway: “It’s the habits that you repeat on a day in dayout basis or week- toeek basis that um sets you up for the rest of your life and that’s your future.”

2. The Copper Crunch: Supply vs. Demand

Turning to commodities, Andy analyzes the current scarcity of copper, noting a significant drop in production from the world’s largest producer.

Chile’s Production Low

  • Event: “Chile posted its lowest monthly copper output in almost nine years, highlighting the challenges facing the world’s top producer as ore grades decline and key mines underperform.”
  • Trend: Copper is down 20% from its February highs, but the long-term outlook is tighter.

Future Deficits and the “Never” Scenario

When asked about future supply, Andy projects massive deficits:

“Yeah, I was looking at the uh future deficits of copper and they’re they just get massive the further you get into the future.”

He anticipates that supply will likely never meet demand for several commodities, including copper, silver, oil, natural gas, and potentially coal.

“Now, that’s going to happen probably across a few different commodities. We could see that happen in silver. We could see that happen in uh oil and natural gas, potentially even coal.”

The Inflationary Question

A major uncertainty lies in whether money printing (inflation) will outpace supply creation.

“Every time in history, what we had is we had demand go up from money printing and then supply would eventually catch back up. Now, the big question that I have is we’re going to print a bunch of money. It’s going to come into the system. It’s going to be highly inflationary. What is the supply actually do? Are we going to meet that demand or is supply going to lag for the rest of my life?”

Andy also questions technological substitution:

“Is is technology going to find new commodities or is technology going to replace copper and aluminum and all these different things with say graphine or something else. Is graphine even going to be viable?”

Despite the uncertainty, his conclusion is clear:

“I just I just know things are going to get really tight uh for a bunch of different commodities and it’s going to get tight starting like now forward.”

3. Oil: Secular Decline vs. Short-Term Surge

While copper faces long-term deficits, oil presents a complex picture of secular decline versus short-term volatility.

Kathy Wood’s Bearish Forecast

Kathy Wood of ARK Invest predicts that oil demand has hit a secular peak and is now in decline due to Electric Vehicles (EVs).

“Oil demand probably hit a secular peak last year and thanks to EVs, now is in secular decline, says Kathy Wood.”

She forecasts oil prices falling to $12 per barrel, a level last seen after the 1973 oil cartel crisis.

“I believe that oil prices are on their way back to $12 a barrel, the level reached after the 1973 oil cartel crisis or lower now that EVs are taking off.”

Andy critiques this prediction as potentially flawed given current market dynamics:

“Now, let’s let’s do this. She’s got her ARK fund. Let’s throw some ARK fun. So, let’s do um XOP divided by RK… Oh, yeah. Real strong there, Kathy. Real good outperformance.”

He notes that $12 is likely below the cost curve of most producers:

“$12. $12 is underneath the cost curve, I think, of every single oil producer in the world. How are we going to get that low?”

Technical Analysis: XOP vs. ARK

Analyzing the XOP (Oil Select Sector SPDR) against ARK (ARK Invest’s speculative fund), Andy finds that oil equities are outperforming ARK’s speculative bets.

  • Double Bottom Pattern: “We have a double bottom that’s about XOP is about to outperform Kathy Wood here.”
  • Commodity Comparison: When comparing other sectors like COPX (Copper miners) and Steel against ARK, the commodity sectors show stronger relative strength, suggesting they could be “launching pads” against speculative funds.

“God, is she so wrong. Sorry, guys. I just I just have to do this. This is so dumb. How is this person making money?”

Short-Term Outlook: Perian’s $100 Oil Target

Despite the long-term decline, short-term indicators suggest higher prices.

  • Rig Count: Expected to rise from 240 to 300 by the end of 2027.
  • Production: Projected at 7.3 million barrels per day (vs. 6.8 million base case).
  • Infrastructure Constraint: “Here’s the catch. Infrastructure is already tight. Which means when we try to get this rig count going up, it’s going to increase day rates and pricing.”
  • Utilization: End-of-year utilization is projected at 91%, indicating near-capacity constraints.

Additionally, OPEC+ exports dropped significantly in March, mirroring the 2020 COVID crash but even deeper.

“Historic drop in OPEC plus oil exports in March, mirroring the drop at the height of COVID. Complacency being fueled by lag effect yet imminent production or imminent product shortages in much of the world about to serve as a reality check.”

Andy raises his oil target to $369, citing technical patterns:

“The 4 and a half year red bullish falling wedge is probably a halfway pattern with a price target of 369 and that’s the green measured move.”

4. Defining Financial Freedom: Beyond the $3 Million Net Worth

A viewer asked: “Once you have $3 million in net worth, it no longer makes sense to keep investing aggressively. You won the game. You’re set for life.”

Andy refines this definition, emphasizing cash flow over static net worth:

“Well, I I think some of it is net worth, but some of it is also uh cash flow. So, it’s not necessarily 3 million in net worth. uh I think it is what is the cash coming in and what is the cash going out and that that is what you want not to meet each other.”

He outlines his criteria for financial independence:

  1. Income > Expenses: “You want more income coming in than you have cash going out with a buffer.”
  2. Diversification: “So I don’t think the 3 million net worth… but I also think you need cash flow and whether that’s dividend uh or whatnot. And and it would be great if it was diversified income streams because if you lose an income stream… you’re not impacted it impacted by it all that much.”
  3. Resilience: “So, that that’s the game that I’m basically playing right now. I’m setting that all up uh for myself to to play the the income dividend game.”

5. Warren Buffett’s Lessons: Patience and Selection

Warren Buffett’s approach offers further wisdom on market timing and asset selection.

The Power of Patience

Buffett stated: “If there is a big market decline, Berkshire will deploy cash. I’m still involved in making investments at Berkshire. We have north of 350 billion in cash on hand. Things don’t look substantially deeper. Not in it to make five or 6%, which is what indices are down.”

Andy interprets this as:

“He’s looking for a big decline. He’s looking to deploy money. He just doesn’t want to do it at overvalued prices. He’s extremely patient and it’s in my opinion people should learn from this. What should you learn? He’s patient to purchase. He waits for good entry points that are massively undervalued and then he deploys capital.”

Why Buffett Avoids Commodities

Buffett prefers “boring companies that compound over long periods of time” (e.g., candy, food, insurance, banks) because they offer stable, long-term compounding without the need to sell.

“That that’s really what they are. But you can take some of his uh some of the things that he applies like patience and waiting for fear in the market and buying when things are very cheap. You can apply that to commodities.”

However, commodities cycle up and down viciously, lacking the long-term stability of Buffett’s preferred assets.

“The reason he doesn’t like commodities is because it cycles up and down quite viciously. you don’t get that long compounding uh year afteryear return that lasts for 50 60 70 years that you never have to sell and and that’s kind of what he deems to be the greatest efficiency of capital allocation.”

He also avoids most technology companies due to rapid obsolescence, except for those with massive moats like Apple or Google.

“Uh the they’re not technology companies where technology companies cycle in cycle out. Uh the they get replaced with new technology. So he basically avoids technology… Uh he may take a a a a position in just something that’s got a massive moat that’s a technology company like Apple or Google or something like that.”

The AI Bubble

Currently, the AI sector is trading at 41% concentration levels, similar to the tech bubble of 2000.

“Uh bubbles tend to peak at 40% concentration levels. The current AI bubble is at 41%.”

6. Market Cycles and the “Rat Race”

Looking at the broader commodity bull market, Andy suggests we are in the early stages of a massive multi-decade trend.

Technical Patterns

  • S&P 500: Recently hit 40% concentration.
  • Japan: Hit 44%.
  • Tech & Telecom (2000): Hit 41%.
  • AI Big 10: Currently at 41%.

Andy references a “three-hump consolidation” pattern, suggesting we are transitioning from Wave 2 into Wave 3, which could last for decades.

“This is the old paradigm. This is the consolidation and if you look at this this three hump consolidation is the end of wave two on a wave one wave two pullback. We’ve started wave three which is the biggest wave and this is going to go on for a very long time this move. lots of inflation and commodities are are just this is like 1970s on steroids.”

Sector Rotation

Currently, the explorers and producers (higher beta) within the energy sector are lagging behind the broader sector index (XLE).

“One thing that stands out is that the more offensive higher beta side of the sector, explorers and producers with greater leverage to the underlying commodities has not even broken out on a relative yet.”

This lag suggests the full bull market hasn’t started yet.

“And that means that we haven’t even entered the bull market. Like money’s not really rotating. Another thing that you can also look at if you do SIJ versus silver futures pricing, that’s also really really low… So we still have a huge move ahead of us uh in in Silj and it’s like that for all the juniors. The juniors are are all lagging because we haven’t really hit full stride of the bull market.”

Conclusion: Play the Long Game

As we navigate these complex markets, Andy’s final advice is to focus on discipline, patience, and understanding the long-term trajectory of commodities.

“When the second pink head and shoulders pattern broke down just before the COVID crash, I understood that the huge blue head and shoulders pattern was probably going to play out and it very much did.”

He encourages investors to look beyond the short term:

“People are focusing in too much. This is too close of a view. You have to focus back out from the 1800s uh to today to see the big patterns uh and and and where we’re at in the big picture view.”

Ultimately, whether building wealth through disciplined habits or navigating commodity cycles, the key is patience and a clear understanding of what drives value over time.
